It is hard to think of another person in Indian history who did so much in so few years. Shankara is traditionally said to have lived only 32 years. In that time he wrote some of the most studied texts in Indian philosophy, travelled from Kerala to Kashmir and Kedarnath, and left behind monasteries that still run today.

## A boy in Kaladi
He was born in Kaladi, a village on the banks of the Periyar in Kerala, to Shivaguru and Aryamba. Most accounts place him in the 8th century CE, though the monasteries themselves give earlier dates, and historians still discuss it. His father died when he was young. The stories say he mastered the Vedas by the age of eight.

## The crocodile
The most famous story is about the river. Young Shankara wanted to become a sannyasi, a renunciant, but his mother, a widow, would not allow it. One day, as he was bathing in the river, a crocodile caught his leg. He called to his mother that he was going to die, and that if she would agree to let him take sannyasa, he would be allowed to live as a monk at the last moment. She agreed, the crocodile let go, and he left home.

It is a legend, but notice what it says. Even this great teacher left home with a promise to come back to his mother when she needed him, and he did.

## The teacher by the Narmada
He walked north to the banks of the Narmada, where he found his guru, Govinda Bhagavatpada, who was himself a student of Gaudapada, the author of an early text on Advaita. Under him, Shankara studied and began writing.

## What he wrote
His major works are commentaries, called *bhashyas*, on three sets of texts: the principal Upanishads, the Bhagavad Gita and the Brahma Sutras. Together these form the base of Advaita Vedanta. He also wrote shorter works, including the Upadesha Sahasri, and many devotional hymns, such as [Bhaja Govindam](/bhaja-govindam-shankaras-song-for-the-confused-mind/), the Kanakadhara Stotram and the Kalabhairava Ashtakam.

## The debates
Shankara travelled the length of India debating scholars of other schools. In the best-known story, he met the Mimamsa scholar Mandana Mishra, whose wife Ubhaya Bharati judged the debate. After Mandana lost, he became Shankara's disciple Sureshwaracharya.

## The four monasteries
He established four mathas in the four directions: Sringeri in the south, Dwaraka in the west, Jyotirmath in the north and Puri in the east. We will look at them more closely later in this series.

## The end
According to tradition, Shankara left the world at Kedarnath, in the Himalayas, at the age of 32. Other accounts say Kanchi.

## Why he still matters
He gave a clear and logical form to the teachings of the Upanishads. He travelled across a country with no trains. And the lesson of his life, as his admirers see it, is that the search for truth can be a way of living: walking, listening, debating, and writing it down.
