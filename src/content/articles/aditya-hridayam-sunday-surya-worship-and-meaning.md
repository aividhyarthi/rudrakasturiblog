Sunday has a different feel from the other days. The house moves slowly. Someone is cooking something that takes time. In many families, this is the day that begins, quietly, with the Sun.

This is a plain guide to the day: who Surya is, what people do on Sunday, the one hymn that Rama himself received, and a routine you can follow in ten minutes.

## Why Sunday belongs to Surya
The word for Sunday in Telugu is *Adivaram* and in Sanskrit *Ravivara* or *Bhanuvara*. All of them name the Sun. In Hindu tradition Surya is the visible god, the one deity everybody can see every day. The Rig Veda has hymns to him, and the Gayatri mantra, which millions chant at dawn, is addressed to the Sun as the giver of light and understanding.

In astrology the Sun is the planet of the self, authority, health and fathers. Families with a weak Sun in the chart are often told to worship him on Sundays. The simpler reading is this: Sunday is a good day to give thanks for light.

## The Aditya Hridayam: Rama's lesson on the battlefield
The most famous Sunday text comes from the Ramayana. In the Yuddha Kanda (chapter 107), Rama is exhausted in the war with Ravana. The sage Agastya arrives and says: *Rama, listen to a secret that will bring you victory over every enemy.* He then teaches a hymn of 31 verses to the Sun, the *Aditya Hridayam*, "the heart of the Sun".

What is striking is what the hymn asks for. Rama is told to chant it three times, with a calm mind. Agastya does not give him a new weapon. He gives him a way to steady himself. Many people read it for the same reason: before an exam, an interview, a long journey or a difficult conversation.

## A simple Sunday routine
1. **Wake early,** if you can by sunrise. Bathe and wear clean clothes. Red, orange or saffron are traditional.
2. **Offer arghya.** Stand facing east with a small vessel of water and pour it slowly while the Sun rises. Say the Gayatri or *Om Suryaya Namah*.
3. **Light a lamp** at your shrine, with a red or orange flower.
4. **Read or listen to the Aditya Hridayam,** or say the Surya mantra 12 or 108 times: *Om Hram Hreem Hraum Sah Suryaya Namah*.
5. **Offer something simple.** Wheat halwa, sweet pongal or jaggery with a little ghee.
6. **Share a meal** with the family. Sunday is for this too.

## Surya Namaskar
If you want movement in the practice, [Surya Namaskar, the twelve steps, with a mantra for each](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/) is the natural companion. Even three rounds at sunrise count.

## Temples of the Sun
- **Arasavalli, near Srikakulam, Andhra Pradesh.** The Surya Narayana temple, believed to be over a thousand years old. Twice a year, the rising Sun's rays are said to touch the feet of the deity. Thousands come for the sight.
- **Konark, Odisha.** The great chariot-shaped Sun temple of the thirteenth century, now in ruins and a World Heritage Site.
- **Modhera, Gujarat.** An eleventh-century Sun temple with a beautiful stepped tank.

## Ratha Saptami
The seventh day after the new moon in the month of Magha is Ratha Saptami, the day the Sun is said to turn his chariot toward the north. It is the great Sun festival of the year, with a bath at dawn and an offering of milk boiled in the open until it overflows.

## Questions people ask
**Can I do this if I cannot be up at sunrise?** Yes. The practice is the thanks, not the clock. Do it when you can, and from a balcony or window if you have no courtyard.

**Must I fast on Sundays?** Many families keep a light fast and avoid salt on this day. It is optional. If you have a health condition, do not fast.

**Is it all right to do it without Sanskrit?** Of course. A sentence of thanks in your own language is a real prayer.

## The Gayatri, word by word
Many people begin Sunday with the Gayatri mantra, which is a prayer to the Sun as the source of understanding. It is from the Rig Veda (3.62.10).

> **om bhūr bhuvaḥ svaḥ, tat savitur vareṇyaṁ**
> **bhargo devasya dhīmahi, dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt**

*We meditate on the radiant light of the divine Sun, the best to be worshipped. May it inspire our understanding.* Notice what it asks for: not wealth, not victory, but *clear thinking*.

## The chariot of seven horses
In pictures Surya rides a chariot drawn by seven horses, driven by Aruna, who has no legs. The seven horses are read as the seven colours of light, the seven days of the week or the seven metres of the Vedas. The single wheel is the year. It is a lovely image to keep in mind when you stand facing the dawn: the whole day is on its way, and you are at the beginning of it.
