Sunday belongs to Surya, the Sun, the one deity everyone can see. Many families begin the day with arghya, a vessel of water offered to the rising Sun, and some recite the Aditya Hridayam.

## The story
In the Yuddha Kanda of the Valmiki Ramayana, Rama is exhausted and worried in the middle of the battle with Ravana. The sage Agastya, who has come to watch the war with the gods, tells him there is an ancient and secret hymn, the Aditya Hridayam, "the heart of the Sun". He teaches it and tells Rama to recite it three times. Rama sips water, repeats it with attention, looks at the Sun, and goes into battle with renewed strength.

## What it contains
The hymn has about thirty verses. It praises the Sun by many names (Aditya, Savitar, Bhaskara, Divakara, Prabhakara and others), describes him as the source of the gods, the seasons and time itself, and promises that one who recites it will overcome fear, trouble and enemies. It ends with Rama's resolve.

The hymn is long, and some people learn it over several weeks. If you prefer something short, the Surya mantras below do the same job.

## A simple Sunday morning
1. Bathe and stand facing the rising Sun.
2. Offer water from a copper or clean vessel, slowly, while chanting *Om Suryaya Namah* (ఓం సూర్యాయ నమః).
3. Chant the **Surya Gayatri**: *Om Bhaskaraya Vidmahe Mahadyutikaraya Dhimahi, Tanno Adityah Prachodayat*.
4. If you can, do a few rounds of [Surya Namaskar](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/).
5. Offer red flowers and a naivedyam of jaggery pongal or wheat sweets.

All three mantras, with their meanings, are on the [Surya mantras page](/mantras/surya/).
