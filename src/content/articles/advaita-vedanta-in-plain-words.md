Advaita sounds like a heavy word, but the idea behind it is easy to say and hard to live. It says that the thing you are looking for outside yourself is what you already are.

## What the word means
*Dvaita* means two. *A-dvaita* means not two. In Advaita Vedanta, the school of Adi Shankaracharya, the claim is that there is one ultimate reality, Brahman, and that your innermost self, the Atman, is not separate from it.

Brahman is described as *sat-chit-ananda*: existence, consciousness and bliss. These are not three things, but three ways of pointing to one.

## Where does the idea come from?
It comes from the Upanishads, the last part of the Vedas. Four short sentences, the Mahavakyas or great statements, sum it up:
- *Prajnanam Brahma*: consciousness is Brahman.
- *Aham Brahmasmi*: I am Brahman.
- *Tat tvam asi*: you are that.
- *Ayam Atma Brahma*: this self is Brahman.

Each one comes from a different Veda. The most famous, *Tat tvam asi*, is a father's reply to his son Shvetaketu in the Chandogya Upanishad.

## An easy way to picture it
Think of space inside a clay pot and the space outside it. We call one the pot-space and the other the sky, but the space is the same. Break the pot and the difference is gone. In the same way, the body, mind and personality are like the pot. Within them is the same awareness that is everywhere.

Another picture is a wave and the ocean. The wave has a shape and a name, but it is water, and when it falls, it is still the ocean.

## So is there no God?
This is the question people ask most. Advaita does not say there is no God. It says that God, the world and you are not three separate things at the deepest level. Plenty of Advaitins worship Shiva, Vishnu or Devi with full devotion. Shankara himself wrote beautiful hymns to them. Devotion is seen as a way to purify the mind until the teaching can be understood.

## How do people practise it?
The classical path has three steps:
1. **Shravana:** listening to the teaching from a teacher.
2. **Manana:** reflecting on it until the doubts are cleared.
3. **Nididhyasana:** meditating on it until it becomes natural.

Before this, a student is expected to develop discrimination between the lasting and the passing, a certain calm about pleasures, self-control and a real longing for freedom.

## What it is not
It is not saying that your problems do not exist, or that suffering is just imagination. It does not tell you to walk away from your life. It asks you to see who it is that is living it.

## Why it still matters
People find the idea comforting when they are afraid of loss. If what you are is not the thing that can be lost, that changes how you hold success and failure. It is close to what Krishna says in the Gita about being steady in pleasure and pain, as in [Gita 2.14](/gita-2-14-heat-and-cold-pleasure-and-pain-come-and-go/).
