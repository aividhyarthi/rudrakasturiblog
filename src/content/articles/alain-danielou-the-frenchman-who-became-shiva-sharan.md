Alain Danielou belongs to a small group of Europeans who did not just study India but tried to live in it. He is also a figure who divides opinion, so this story comes with both sides.

## A musician from France
Born in 1907 in a well-off family in France, Danielou trained as a singer and dancer, and was a restless traveller. In 1932, with his friend, the Swiss photographer Raymond Burnier, he set out for India. The trip was meant to be a long journey, and it turned into a stay of more than twenty years.

## Banaras
After touring the country, including a visit to Rabindranath Tagore's school at Santiniketan, where he taught French and was influenced by Tagore's circle, Danielou settled in Banaras in the mid-1930s. He lived in a house on the Ganga, learned Hindi and Sanskrit, and studied music, particularly the veena, with the musician Shivendranath Basu.

## Shiva Sharan
He was drawn to Shaivism, and he took initiation as a Shaiva, with the new name of Shiva Sharan, one who has taken refuge in Shiva. Local pandits and teachers accepted him, and he was allowed to take part in ceremonies. He often described himself as having become a Hindu, which was unusual for a European, and in his writing, he said that he did so by choice and not by fashion.

## The scholar
In 1949 he became the director of the Adyar Library and Research Centre in Madras, a leading institution for Sanskrit manuscripts, which he held until 1953. He translated Sanskrit works, and wrote on Indian music, including a book on the theory of ragas that is still read.

## The books
His best-known writings on religion are *Hindu Polytheism*, from 1964, and *The Myths and Gods of India*, a later edition of the same book. They are readable and detailed, and they take the Hindu deities seriously, not as symbols of something else. Many readers outside India first learned about Shiva, Vishnu and the Goddess from them.

## Back to Europe
He left India in the 1950s and worked on music in Europe, setting up the International Institute for Comparative Music Studies in Berlin in 1963, and travelling widely. He died in Switzerland in 1994.

## The debate
People disagree about Danielou. His supporters say that he respected Indian traditions at a time when few Europeans did. Critics point out that he was strongly opposed to modern ideas, and that some of his later views are controversial. Readers should look at his work with that in mind.

## What it shows
He is a good reminder that the encounter between Europe and India produced many different kinds of people. Some came to rule, some came to teach and some came to listen. Danielou, in his own way, came to listen.
