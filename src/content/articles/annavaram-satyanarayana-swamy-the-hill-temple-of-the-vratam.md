If you ask almost any Telugu family what they do at a house-warming, a new job or a wedding, the answer may be: *Satyanarayana vratam*. A pandit arrives, a kalasam is set, a story is read, and sweet prasadam is shared. The temple where that vratam is said to have its greatest home is a few hours from Rajahmundry, on a hill.

## The hill and the river
Annavaram stands on **Ratnagiri**, a hill of about 100 metres, on the bank of the **Pampa river** in the Kakinada district of Andhra Pradesh. The temple is dedicated to **Sri Veera Venkata Satyanarayana Swamy**, a form of Vishnu. Some call it the Badrinath of the South, and it is visited by pilgrims in the tens of thousands, especially on Pournami and during the wedding season.

## How the temple began
According to local tradition, in the late nineteenth century a devotee, in some tellings a local zamindar, had a dream. The Lord told him that he was lying buried in a particular place on the hill and asked to be found. The place was dug, an image of the Lord was uncovered, and a temple was built around it. The present temple is usually dated to 1891, built by the local ruler Raja Inuganti Rajagopala Jagapati Bahadur, with later additions.

These are the temple's own accounts, and they are believed by the faithful. Historians would add that the site has been a place of worship for longer.

## The Lord of the temple
The main deity is shown as a tall form that, tradition says, represents the three great gods together: the lowest part is Brahma, the middle Shiva, and the top Vishnu. The Lord is attended by Goddess Anantalakshmi, whose shrine is on the hill. It is a lovely idea, to find the three in one, and is one reason Annavaram is loved by both Vaishnavas and Shaivas.

## The Satyanarayana vratam
The vratam is described in the *Skanda Purana*, in the section called Reva Khanda. It begins with the sage Narada asking Vishnu how people can be freed of suffering. Vishnu tells him of the vratam of Satyanarayana, "the lord who is truth". The story is told in five chapters, each of which has a tale: a poor Brahmin who did the vratam and prospered, a woodcutter, a merchant who forgot his vow and lost his ship, and a king who ignored the prasadam.

The tale is not subtle. It says that *a vow to truth that is forgotten has consequences*, and that those who keep it are blessed. Families do it at the time of a new beginning, as a way of promising themselves to live honestly.

At Annavaram the vratam is performed in the temple every day, in groups, and thousands attend on Pournami. It is considered auspicious to do it at the hill itself, and many couples come to do it just after their wedding.

## The prasadam
The Annavaram prasadam is made of wheat rava (*godhuma ravva*), jaggery, ghee and cardamom, and wrapped in broad leaves. Anyone who has tasted it will remember it, and families who cannot visit ask neighbours to bring some back. It is one of the best-loved temple foods in the Telugu country.

## How to visit
- **The route.** From Rajahmundry it is about 70 km by road. There are buses and taxis, and the hill can be climbed by steps or reached by road.
- **Timings.** The temple opens early and has several darshans. Pournami and the wedding months are crowded. Check the temple's official site for hours.
- **What to wear.** Modest clothes, as at any temple.
- **For the vratam.** Book a slot at the temple counters, and bring a new set of clothes if you wish to wear them for the puja.
- **The Pampa river** can be visited after darshan; many families bathe there.

## A home version
If you cannot go, you can do the vratam at home on a Pournami or any day you choose. Set a picture of the Lord, a kalasam, fruit and the prasadam of rava, jaggery and ghee. Read or listen to the five chapters of the story. If you do not know a priest, there are good recordings in every Indian language. Share the prasadam with everyone.

## What the story is really about
*Satya* means truth, and *Narayana* is the name of the Lord. So the vratam is a promise of truth made before God. If a family does it, and it makes them a little more honest with each other, then it has done its work.

## The five chapters in brief
The tale of the vratam is told in five chapters, and each one carries a lesson that a child can remember. In the first, a poor Brahmin does the vratam and his poverty ends. In the second, a woodcutter who does it sells his wood at a better price. In the third, a merchant promises the vratam if he has a child, but forgets, and his ship is stuck until he remembers. In the fourth, his daughter and wife make the same mistake. In the fifth, a king throws away the prasadam in pride and suffers until he repents. The point is repeated, gently: *a promise made to the Lord is also a promise to yourself, and forgetting it has a cost.*
