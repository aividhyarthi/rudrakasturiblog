This is a different kind of faith story. These two Englishmen did not become Hindus or write hymns. But they have become a part of local devotion in a way that few foreigners have, and the reason is water.

## The delta before the dam
In the 1830s and 1840s, the Godavari delta was a place of floods and famine. The river swelled in the monsoon and ran low in summer, and many people left. Arthur Cotton, a young engineer in the Madras service, believed the river could be controlled.

## Arthur Cotton and Dowleswaram
Cotton designed an anicut, a long low barrage, across the Godavari at Dowleswaram, near Rajahmundry. It was finished in 1850, and it turned the delta into one of the richest rice-growing areas in India. He built other schemes, such as the Cauvery anicut improvements and the Krishna anicut at Vijayawada, which was finished after his time. He lived to the age of 96, and campaigned for irrigation and canals all his life.

In the delta, farmers call him *Cotton Dora*, the lord Cotton. They put up his statues, hang his portrait in their homes and, in some villages, celebrate his birthday on 15 May. There is a memorial at Dowleswaram and a museum.

## John Pennycuick and Mullaperiyar
Further south, in the dry districts around Madurai, Theni, Dindigul, Sivaganga and Ramanathapuram, farmers have a similar story. The Periyar river rose in the hills of Kerala and flowed west to the Arabian Sea, while the land to the east stayed dry. In the 1800s the idea came up of turning the river to the east, through a tunnel in the mountains.

The engineer John Pennycuick took the task. The Mullaperiyar Dam was built between 1887 and 1895, and the water of the Periyar, through a tunnel, began to flow into the Vaigai river. It changed farming in five districts.

There is a story, told by many families, that when the money ran short, Pennycuick sold his own property in England to complete the dam. Whether or not every detail is right, it is a story that people tell with pride, and he is remembered for it.

## How they are remembered
Farmers of the area still celebrate his birthday, 15 January, which coincides with Pongal. People name their children Pennycuick, and garland his statue on that day. In Theni and Madurai there are those who put his picture beside their gods.

## A note on complexity
It is worth being honest about the other side. These works were built under colonial rule, and they served the colonial government as well. The Mullaperiyar Dam is also at the centre of a bitter dispute today between Tamil Nadu, which uses the water, and Kerala, which is worried about the dam's age. But for the families who farm the land, the original gift is not forgotten.

## What it tells us
Devotion, in India, has always included gratitude. A river is a goddess in the Hindu tradition, and someone who makes her serve people has, in the eyes of the farmers, done something sacred.
