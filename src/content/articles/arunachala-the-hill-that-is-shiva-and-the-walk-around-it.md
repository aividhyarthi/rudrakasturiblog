Most temples have a god in a building. At Tiruvannamalai, the god is the hill. People do not just visit it. They walk around it, and some do it every month for years.

## The story
We told the story of the Lingodbhava in [our piece on Shiva](/who-is-shiva-five-things-most-people-get-wrong/). Brahma and Vishnu argued over who was supreme, and a pillar of fire appeared between them, so large that neither could find the top or the bottom. The pillar was Shiva. At the request of the gods, Shiva cooled the pillar into a hill, and that hill is Arunachala. The name means the red hill, or the hill of dawn.

## The hill
Arunachala is about 814 metres high, a modest granite hill rising on a flat plain. It looks different from every side. From one angle it appears as a single peak, and from another it seems to have two, which locals link with Shiva and Parvati. Devotees say it is among the oldest hills in the world, and some say the Siddhas, the perfected beings, still live in its caves.

At its foot stands the Arunachaleswarar temple, one of the five Pancha Bhoota Shiva temples, for the element of fire. It is among the largest temples in Tamil Nadu, with nine gopurams, the tallest at about 66 metres.

## Girivalam
The central practice is the Girivalam, the circumambulation of the hill, about 14 km. Pilgrims walk barefoot, in silence or chanting, past eight lingams, one in each direction, and many ashrams and tanks. It takes about four to six hours. The best time is on a full-moon night, when lakhs of people join. It is believed that the walk is equal to circling the whole world.

If you can't do it, even a small part has value, say the locals. Do it slowly, and wear a hat in the day.

## Karthigai Deepam
The big festival is the Karthigai Deepam, in the Tamil month of Karthigai (November or December). Over ten days, the temple holds processions. On the final evening, at dusk, a huge lamp, Maha Deepam, is lit at the top of the hill, using many hundreds of kilograms of ghee, and can be seen for kilometres. Lakhs of people watch from below, and the cry of *Annamalaiyarukku Arohara* fills the town. Houses all over Tamil Nadu and Andhra light rows of lamps that evening, which links it to [Karthika Masam](/karthika-masam-2026-dates-deepam-and-fasting/).

## Ramana Maharshi
In 1896, a 16-year-old boy named Venkataraman came to Tiruvannamalai, after a sudden experience of the death of the body that left him with no fear. He sat in silence in the temple and then in caves on the hill, and he stayed here until his death in 1950. As Ramana Maharshi, he taught a short question, *Who am I?*, which is Advaita in its simplest form. See [Advaita in plain words](/advaita-vedanta-in-plain-words/) and the [Nirvana Shatakam](/nirvana-shatakam-i-am-not-the-mind-meaning/). His ashram, Sri Ramanasramam, is on the south side of the hill.

We will meet a few of the Westerners who came to meet him in our [faith stories](/faith-stories/).

## Planning
Tiruvannamalai is about 200 km from Chennai, and well connected by road. The weather is mild from November to February. Book accommodation early for Karthigai Deepam and full moon days.
