If you ask a Tamil family where they go for Murugan, they will name a hilltop or a seashore. And if you ask which are the most important, the answer is always the same six.

## The list
These are the Arupadai Veedu, the six abodes. The poet Nakkirar, in the ancient poem Thirumurugatruppadai, praised them.

**1. Thiruparankundram.** Near Madurai, set into a rock hill. This is where Murugan married Deivanai, the daughter of Indra, after he defeated the demon Surapadma. The sanctum is carved into the rock itself, and the wedding is celebrated here every year.

**2. Tiruchendur.** On the seashore in Thoothukudi district. It is the only one of the six that is not on a hill. This is where Murugan defeated the demon Surapadma, and the Kanda Sashti festival, with a re-enactment of the battle on the beach, draws huge crowds every year. Many people bathe in the sea before darshan.

**3. Palani.** On a hill rising over the town, with a flight of steps and a winch train. Here Murugan stands as Dandayudhapani, as an ascetic with a staff, shaved and dressed only in a loincloth, after leaving home angry because his brother Ganesha had won a fruit contest. The idol is said to have been made by the sage Bogar from a mixture of nine minerals, called Navapashanam, and the abhishekam water is believed to have healing power.

**4. Swamimalai.** Near Kumbakonam, on a small artificial hill with 60 steps, one for each Tamil year in the 60-year cycle. This is where Murugan, as a child, explained the meaning of Om to his own father, Shiva, who sat to listen. That is why he is called Swaminatha, the teacher of the lord.

**5. Thiruthani.** In Tiruvallur district, near Chennai, on a hilltop reached by 365 steps, one for each day of the year. After the battle, Murugan came here to calm down, and married Valli here. People say that, unlike at the other abodes, the anger is gone: it is the place of peace.

**6. Pazhamudircholai.** In the hills of Alagar Koil, north of Madurai. The name means the grove of ripe fruits. It is the place of the famous story of the Tamil poet Avvaiyar, who sat tired under a rose-apple tree. A shepherd boy, who was Murugan in disguise, asked her, "Would you like a roasted fruit or an unroasted one?" She was puzzled, and said she wanted a roasted fruit. He shook the tree, and the fruits fell in the dust. When she blew the dust off, he asked, "Is the fruit too hot?" She understood, and bowed.

## Planning the pilgrimage
- The six places are all in Tamil Nadu, in a rough loop from Chennai to Madurai to the south coast. Many people do them in two or three days by car.
- The most crowded are Palani, Tiruchendur and Thiruthani, especially on Kartikai and on Thai Poosam in January or February.
- Palani's hill has steps, a winch train and a ropeway. Pick whichever you prefer.
- Check each temple's timings, because most close in the afternoon.

## A closer look at the poem
The old poem that praises these places, the *Thirumurugatruppadai*, is part of the early Tamil collection known as the Pathupattu, the Ten Idylls. The word *aruppadai* in its title means "guiding", as if the poem were a signpost. In the poem, a poet who has already been blessed by Murugan meets another traveller and tells him which road to take to find the same grace.

That framing is lovely. The poem is not a boast. It is one traveller helping another, and the six abodes are the stops on the road.

## What each place seems to teach
It is not part of any rulebook, but many devotees feel that each abode has its own mood.

- **Thiruparankundram:** joy, a wedding, a home that is welcoming.
- **Tiruchendur:** courage and victory by the open sea.
- **Palani:** letting go, the ascetic who gave up everything.
- **Swamimalai:** the humility of a teacher who is also a child.
- **Thiruthani:** peace after a struggle.
- **Pazhamudircholai:** the humbling of cleverness, as Avvaiyar found.

If you are going through a hard time, you might find one of these moods speaks to you more than the others. Starting with that one is a perfectly good way to begin.

## Kavadi and Thai Poosam
At Palani and other Murugan temples, many devotees carry a *kavadi*, a decorated frame or pot carried on the shoulders as a vow. It is most visible at Thai Poosam. If you see kavadi-bearers, give them room. They often walk long distances, sometimes barefoot, and fast or keep a simple diet beforehand.

## Questions people ask
**Do I have to visit all six in one trip?**
No. Many families visit them over several years, one or two at a time. A pilgrimage is a long relationship, not a checklist.

**Is there a right order?**
The list above is the one commonly given, but travel plans differ. Go in whatever order your journey allows.

**What should I carry?**
Water, light clothes you can walk in, and a little cash for offerings. Footwear is left outside, so wear something easy to slip off. Hill temples can be hot by midday, so go early.
