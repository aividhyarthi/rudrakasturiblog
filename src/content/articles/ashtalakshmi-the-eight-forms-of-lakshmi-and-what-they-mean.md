Ask someone what Lakshmi gives and they will say: *money*. It is a fair answer, and a very small one.

The tradition has a longer list, and it is a wiser one.

## Eight, not one
The Ashtalakshmi Stotram describes eight forms of the goddess, each standing for a kind of abundance:

1. **Adi Lakshmi,** the original Lakshmi, the source of the rest, who is the goddess of the *beginning*.
2. **Dhana Lakshmi,** the giver of wealth, the one we usually mean when we say Lakshmi.
3. **Dhanya Lakshmi,** the giver of grain and food, the abundance of a harvest and of a full kitchen.
4. **Gaja Lakshmi,** shown with two elephants pouring water over her, who gives strength and royal authority.
5. **Santana Lakshmi,** the giver of children and of a continuing family.
6. **Veera Lakshmi,** also called Dhairya Lakshmi, the giver of courage and the strength to face difficulty.
7. **Vijaya Lakshmi,** the giver of victory, in the field, in the exam hall, in a hard fight.
8. **Vidya Lakshmi,** the giver of learning and wisdom.

Look at that list again. Food. Children. Courage. Learning. Victory. If a person had all of these and little money, we would not call them poor.

## What it means for how we pray
When we pray to Lakshmi only for money, we ask for one-eighth of her. The Ashtalakshmi list invites us to ask which kind of abundance is missing. Some people need courage more than cash. Some need a child's laughter. Some, a quiet mind and the ability to learn.

This is also a useful question to ask yourself on a Friday evening: *which Lakshmi do I need most this week?*

## Friday worship
- **Clean the entrance** of the house and light a lamp at dusk. Many draw a small *muggu* or *kolam* at the doorway.
- **Offer flowers** (lotus or whatever you have) and a simple sweet, such as sweet pongal or kheer.
- **Say a stotram.** The Ashtalakshmi Stotram, the Lakshmi Ashtottaram, or *Om Shreem Mahalakshmyai Namah* said 108 times.
- **Share something.** Giving food or a small gift to someone who needs it is considered the truest offering to Lakshmi.

## A temple of the eight
In Besant Nagar, Chennai, the Ashtalakshmi Kovil, built in the 1970s, has all eight forms in separate shrines on several levels, near the beach. It is a lovely place to see the list in stone.

## One form a Friday
If you want a gentle way to use the list, try keeping one form for each Friday over eight weeks. Week one, Adi Lakshmi: sit quietly and be thankful for where you began. Week two, Dhana Lakshmi: look at your accounts honestly and make one sensible change. Week three, Dhanya Lakshmi: give food to someone. Week four, Gaja Lakshmi: take responsibility for something that needs leadership. Week five, Santana Lakshmi: spend time with a child or a young person. Week six, Dhairya Lakshmi: do the thing that frightens you a little. Week seven, Vijaya Lakshmi: finish something that has been pending. Week eight, Vidya Lakshmi: learn something new. After eight Fridays, you will have worshipped each form in action, which is perhaps what the goddess wanted.

## The first stanza
The Ashtalakshmi Stotram opens with a verse to Adi Lakshmi, beginning *sumanasa vandita sundari madhavi*, "O beautiful one, praised by good-hearted people, beloved of Madhava". The stotram has a verse for each of the eight. It is easy to find in Telugu, Tamil, Kannada and Sanskrit, and takes about ten minutes to read.

## Questions people ask
**Do I need to worship all eight?** No. Choose the one you feel closest to, or the one you most need.

**Is the Ashtalakshmi worship only for women?** No, it is for everyone.

## A thought to keep
The Gita has a line that says something similar: *yoga-kshemam vahamy aham*, "I carry what you lack and keep what you have." (9.22) Lakshmi, in the tradition, is the grace in that carrying.

*Which of the eight is the one you would ask for first? If you want to tell us, we would love to know.*

## A note on money
The tradition does not despise money. It simply refuses to put it first. A person with money and no courage, no food, no learning and no family is called poor in this list, and a person with enough, with friends and a calm mind, is called rich. It is a healthy way to read a bank balance.
