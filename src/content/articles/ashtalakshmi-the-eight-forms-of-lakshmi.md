Ask most people what Lakshmi gives and they will say money. Ask an older grandmother, and she may list something else: a full granary, healthy children, a house with a lamp lit every evening, a son who can read. She is closer to the tradition than she may realise. The goddess is worshipped in eight forms, and only one of them is about gold.

## The eight forms

Lists of Ashtalakshmi vary slightly between temples and hymns, and some include Aishwarya or Maha Lakshmi in place of another. The most commonly given eight are these.

**Adi Lakshmi.** The first or primal Lakshmi, the source from whom the others come. She is sometimes seen as Lakshmi at Vishnu's side in Vaikuntha. She stands for the root of all auspiciousness.

**Dhana Lakshmi.** The giver of wealth, shown pouring coins. She stands for money honestly earned and wisely used.

**Dhanya Lakshmi.** The giver of grain. In an agricultural country, a full store of food was the real wealth, and this form is honoured at harvest time.

**Gaja Lakshmi.** Lakshmi flanked by two elephants pouring water over her. She stands for royal honour, authority and the strength of a stable household or kingdom.

**Santana Lakshmi.** The giver of children and continuity. She is the family's future, as a mother holding a child.

**Veera Lakshmi or Dhairya Lakshmi.** The giver of courage. She is shown with weapons in some images. She stands for the inner strength to face difficulty.

**Vijaya Lakshmi.** Victory. Not only victory in contest but success in a task one has taken up.

**Vidya Lakshmi.** The giver of knowledge. She is often shown with the white of Saraswati. Many families treat this as the highest form, because learning cannot be taken from you.

## Wealth is eight things

Read the list again. It asks: do you have the source of blessing (Adi)? Money (Dhana)? Food (Dhanya)? Standing (Gaja)? Family (Santana)? Courage (Veera)? Success (Vijaya)? Knowledge (Vidya)?

A person can have one and lack the others, and know that something is missing. The tradition teaches that a life is called prosperous only when these are held together. A rich man with no peace at home, or a scholar with nothing to eat, is missing a form of Lakshmi. This is a teaching well worth keeping in mind next time you pray for something.

## Lakshmi and the lotus

In most images Lakshmi sits or stands on a lotus, and holds lotuses in her hands. The lotus grows in mud but stays clean, which is a quiet lesson in itself: wealth can come from ordinary, messy work and still be pure if it is earned honestly. Gold coins pour from one hand, showing generosity. Wealth that is only held and never shared is not counted in the tradition as true Lakshmi. Elders often say that the goddess does not stay long where money is hoarded or earned by harming others, and while that is a saying rather than a rule, it carries the sense of the whole teaching.

## The temple at Besant Nagar

In Chennai, at Elliot's Beach in Besant Nagar, stands a well-known Ashtalakshmi temple, built in the 1970s in the shape of a tall tiered structure facing the sea. Pilgrims move upward through the shrines of the eight forms, one level at a time. The sea breeze, the climb and the quiet make the visit memorable. There are Ashtalakshmi shrines elsewhere too, and many Vishnu and Lakshmi temples show the eight forms in carved panels.

## Friday and Varalakshmi Vratam

Friday is Lakshmi's day in most Telugu and Tamil homes. Families clean the house, light a lamp at dusk, draw a kolam or muggu at the door, and offer flowers and a simple sweet. For a fuller guide, see [Friday Lakshmi worship: mantras and how to do it](/friday-lakshmi-worship-mantras-and-how-to-do-it/).

Varalakshmi Vratam is the great Friday of the year, held before the full moon of the month of Shravana, usually in July or August. Women set up a kalasam with a coconut and the face of the goddess, offer fruits and sweets, tie a sacred thread, and invite other women to receive tambulam. The exact date changes each year, so please check [the festivals page](/festivals/).

For a wider reading of the goddess, [the eight forms of Lakshmi and what they mean](/ashtalakshmi-the-eight-forms-of-lakshmi-and-what-they-mean/) offers another view.

## Differences in the lists

You may meet a slightly different list in your own family or temple. Some versions give Aishwarya Lakshmi, the goddess of fortune and prosperity, in place of Vijaya. Others place Maha Lakshmi in the first position and treat Adi Lakshmi as a separate, older form. Some Tamil and Telugu hymns use slightly different names for the same figure. None of these is wrong. They are different ways of saying the same thing, that the goddess has many faces and her gifts are many.

## How to use this in your own prayer

You do not need elaborate rituals. A simple practice for a Friday evening is:

1. Light a lamp at dusk, facing the picture or idol of Lakshmi.
2. Name the eight forms slowly, one by one.
3. For each, think honestly: where am I strong, where am I lacking?
4. Offer a flower, and sit quietly for a few minutes.

You may find that the prayer changes from a request for money into a review of your whole life. That is a good change. No ritual can promise results, but the habit of looking at all eight is itself a kind of wisdom.
