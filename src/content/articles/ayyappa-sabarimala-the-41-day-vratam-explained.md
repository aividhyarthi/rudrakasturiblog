If you have been in Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra or Telangana between mid-November and mid-January, you have seen them: men in black or dark blue dhotis, with a tulasi bead mala around the neck, walking barefoot and greeting everyone with *Swamiye Saranam Ayyappa*.

## Who is Ayyappa?
Ayyappa, also called Hariharaputra, Manikanta and Dharmasastha, is the son of Shiva and Vishnu in his Mohini form. The story goes that the gods and Shiva were taken with Mohini after the churning of the ocean, and their child was found as a baby by the king of Pandalam, who raised him. Ayyappa grew up to fight the demoness Mahishi, and then went to Sabarimala, where he remains as a celibate yogi.

The idea of Hari and Hara, Vishnu and Shiva, joining in one child is a reason Ayyappa devotion crosses so many boundaries.

## The 41-day vratam
The pilgrimage season, called the Mandala season, begins in the Malayalam month of Vrischikam, in mid-November, and runs for 41 days to the Mandala Pooja in late December. Devotees follow a vratam of the same length before they go up.

The common rules are:
- Wear a tulasi or rudraksha mala given at a temple or by a guru swami, and black or blue clothes.
- Bathe twice a day and pray in the morning and evening.
- Eat simple vegetarian food. Avoid alcohol, tobacco and non-vegetarian food.
- Practise celibacy and avoid anger and harsh speech.
- Sleep on the floor, go barefoot where possible, and address every other devotee as *Swami*.

The point of the vratam is to prepare the mind. By the time you reach the hill, you have already spent forty days thinking about something other than yourself.

## The climb and the irumudi
On the day of the pilgrimage, the devotee fills the irumudi, a two-compartment cloth bundle. One side carries the coconut filled with ghee for the temple's abhishekam. The other has food and offerings for the journey. It is carried on the head, which is considered a sign of surrender.

At the top, devotees climb the 18 holy steps, the Pathinettampadi. Each of the steps has a meaning, and it is customary to touch each step as you go up. Only those carrying the irumudi who have kept the vratam are traditionally allowed to climb them.

## What to know before you go
- Sabarimala is in Pathanamthitta district, Kerala. The usual route is by road to Pamba and then a trek of about 5 kilometres uphill.
- Crowds are heavy. The Kerala and temple authorities run an online queue system, so book as advised on the official site.
- Wear a mask in the rush, carry water, and walk at your pace. People of all ages do it, but heart and breathing conditions deserve a medical check first.
- Rules for the entry of women of certain age groups have been debated in the courts and by the temple. Check the current position before planning.

## Where else to see Ayyappa
Temples to Ayyappa exist in almost every city in the South, and in many of them the season begins with a ceremony called Mala Dharana, taking the mala, on the first day of Karthika or Vrischikam.
