*బలి పాడ్యమి నాడు వామనుడికి మూడు అడుగుల నేలను దానం చేసిన బలి చక్రవర్తిని గుర్తు చేసుకుంటారు. భాగవతం ఆయన దానాన్ని చెబుతుంది; ఏడాదికి ఒకసారి ఆయన తన ప్రజలను చూడవస్తాడనే నమ్మకం ప్రాంతీయ సంప్రదాయం.*

Imagine you have given away everything you own, in front of a crowd, to a small boy who asked politely. Would you stand there smiling, or would you ask for it back? King Bali (బలి చక్రవర్తి) is the person who stood there and gave it.

On the day after the Deepavali new moon, many homes remember him. That day is Bali Padyami (బలి పాడ్యమి).

## Who was Bali?

Bali was the grandson of Prahlada, the child-devotee of Narasimha's story, and the son of Virochana. He was an asura, but a king people loved. By the telling of the Bhagavata Purana (Book 8), he conquered the three worlds and drove Indra from his throne, while keeping up his sacrifices. His teacher Shukracharya guided him.

The devas, who had lost their seats, went to Vishnu. Vishnu was born as Vamana (వామనుడు), a small Brahmin boy, to Aditi.

## The three steps

Vamana came to Bali's sacrifice and asked only for what he could cover in three steps of ground. Shukracharya saw who the boy was and warned Bali not to promise. Bali's answer is the heart of the story: he had given his word, and a king who goes back on a gift is no king.

Then the boy grew until he filled the sky. One step crossed the earth. The second crossed the heavens. There was no room for a third. Bali offered his own head. The Bhagavata says Vishnu placed his foot there, and sent Bali to the realm of Sutala, a place described as more beautiful than heaven. Vishnu himself, the text says, stood guard at his door.

Notice what the text does. It does not call Bali's fall a punishment. It calls it a boon with a hard edge: he is promised a future seat as Indra in a later age.

## The boon of the yearly visit

The idea that Bali is allowed to come back once a year to see his people is not in the Bhagavata's account as such. It is a tradition. In Kerala it is Onam, when Mahabali is believed to visit, and flower carpets (pookalam) are laid for him. Onam falls in the month of Chingam, around August or September, so the date differs. The Bali tradition is the same.

Other regions remember him at Deepavali. In Maharashtra, Bali Pratipada is also called Padwa. A saying is often heard on this day, wishing that sorrows go and the kingdom of Bali come. That is the memory of a king under whom, tradition holds, the people lacked nothing.

## How the day is kept

Practices vary by region and family. Here are some you may recognise:

- A rangoli or a small figure of Bali is drawn at the door, sometimes made of cow dung or rice flour.
- Lamps are lit in the evening, and in some places the figure is worshipped.
- In Kannada homes, Bali Padyami may include Balindra Pooja.
- In Maharashtra, a wife often puts the tilak on her husband and receives a gift, and many traders begin a new year of accounts.

If your family does none of these, there is no need to worry. Light a lamp in the evening and tell the story to a child. That is a complete observance.

## A simple way to keep the evening

If you would like something to do, keep it small. Draw a rangoli of a crown or a few steps at the threshold, three of them if you like, to recall Vamana's strides. Light one lamp on the front step. Tell the story to the youngest person at home, and let them decide whether Bali was foolish or great. Do not hurry them. Children tend to have strong views on this one.

## Why does a Vaishnava story honour a king Vishnu humbled?

This is the question behind the story. If Vamana was right, why do we honour Bali? Because the Bhagavata does not teach that giving is easy. It teaches that Bali was tested with his own word, and did not turn away. The pride that fell was real, but so was the surrender. The same festival that gives Vishnu his triumph gives a donor his honour.

## What Shukracharya saw

It is easy to read the story from Bali's side only. But think of Shukracharya, who loved his student and tried to save him. He was right on the facts: the boy was Vishnu, and the gift would cost Bali his kingdom. Bali did not argue the facts. He said the word had been given.

So the story sets two goods against each other: prudence, and a promise. Do you always know which one your family would choose? The honest answer in most homes is that elders disagree. That disagreement is part of why the story is told every year.

## Where this comes from

The Vamana and Bali story is in the Bhagavata Purana (Book 8, the chapters on Bali and Vamana), and is told also in the Vishnu Purana and the Ramayana in brief. Onam's link with Mahabali is from Kerala's own tradition and its Malayalam songs. The yearly return and the festival customs on Bali Padyami are regional tradition, not text. Tellings differ over whether Bali is an asura-hero or a fallen king.

When did you last keep a promise that cost you something, and who knew?
