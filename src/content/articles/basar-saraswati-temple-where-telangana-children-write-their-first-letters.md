Most temples in South India are dedicated to Shiva, Vishnu or a form of the Devi. Saraswati, the goddess of knowledge, has far fewer of her own. That is one reason why the temple at Basar is so well loved. Every year, thousands of children begin their education here.

## Where it is
The Gnana Saraswati temple stands at Basar, on the bank of the Godavari river in Nirmal district of Telangana. It is about 200 km from Hyderabad and a short drive from Nizamabad. It is one of only a few temples dedicated to Saraswati in the country, and the best known in the Telugu states.

## The story
The tradition says that the sage Vyasa, who composed the Mahabharata, was troubled in mind after the great war and came south in search of peace. He found it on the banks of the Godavari at this spot. He then installed the three goddesses, Saraswati, Lakshmi and Durga, here. Vyasa is said to have collected sand from the river and formed the idols from it.

The name Basar is a shortening of *Vyasapuri* or *Vasara*, according to local belief. A popular story also links the place with the sage Valmiki, who is said to have installed an image here before beginning the Ramayana.

## Aksharabhyasam
The big tradition at Basar is **aksharabhyasam** (also called *vidyarambham* or *akshara swikaram*). It is the ceremony in which a child, usually between three and five, begins learning letters.

How it is done:
1. The family comes in the morning, often on a day considered auspicious.
2. The priest invokes Ganesha and Saraswati.
3. The child is seated on the parent's lap, and the first letters are written on a plate of rice or with the finger, often *Om* and the first letters of the alphabet.
4. A little turmeric paste is placed on the child's tongue or head.
5. Sweets are shared.

Thousands of families attend on Vasant Panchami (in January or February) and Dasara, when queues are very long.

## The turmeric paste
The temple's prasadam is called *bandaru*, a paste made of turmeric. Devotees apply it on their forehead, and many take it home for children preparing for exams. Teachers, students and parents often visit before important exams.

## Other things nearby
- The **Godavari ghat** in Basar, where people take a dip.
- The **Mahalakshmi and Mahakali shrines** within the complex.
- The nearby town of **Nirmal**, famous for its toys and paintings.

## Planning a visit
- Go on a weekday. Vasant Panchami and Dasara are packed.
- Make an early start. The journey from Hyderabad takes about four hours.
- Book accommodation in advance if you are staying overnight, since the temple has its own guest facilities.
- Dress modestly and take little with you.
- Check rules for the aksharabhyasam, which usually has a ticket, with the temple office.

## If you can't go
Many families do the aksharabhyasam at home, with a family priest and a small lamp, or at a local Saraswati or Ganesha temple. The significance is the beginning, and the intention. If you want a simple mantra to say before study, see [Saraswati mantras](/mantras/saraswati/).

## Something to remember
The point of this ceremony is love of learning. It is a way of telling a child that knowledge is worth honouring. A parent who sits with the child every evening, after the ceremony, does the second half.

## A closer look at Vasant Panchami
Vasant Panchami falls on the fifth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Magha, around the start of spring. In many parts of India it is also Saraswati's day, and children place their books and pens before her. At Basar, it is one of the busiest days of the year. If you want a calmer visit, choose a weekday in another month.

## Questions people ask
**How old should my child be?**
Families commonly do aksharabhyasam between the ages of three and five, but there is no strict rule. Many families simply choose a time when the child is ready to sit and enjoy it.

**Do I need to go to Basar?**
No. The ceremony can be done at home or at any Saraswati, Ganesha or local temple. For the home version, see [Saraswati and the first letter: aksharabhyasam at home](/saraswati-and-the-first-letter-aksharabhyasam-at-home/).

**What should the child wear?**
Something simple and comfortable. Many families choose new clothes. Remember that the child may have to sit for a while.

**Can adults visit and pray for their own studies?**
Yes. Students of all ages come before exams and interviews. It is a place where people ask for clarity of mind.

## Making the day easy for a small child
- Feed the child beforehand so that they are not hungry during the ceremony.
- Bring water, a small snack and a change of clothes.
- Keep the visit short. Little ones tire quickly in queues.
- Let the child hold the pen or chalk and enjoy the moment, even if the letters are wobbly.
- Take a photograph for the family album, if the temple allows it.

## What comes after
The ceremony is a doorway. What matters most is what the family does next: reading to the child, keeping books at home, praising effort and not only marks. Many parents choose a short evening routine of reading together for ten minutes. It is a modest practice, and it may be the best gift of all.
