*కంచర్ల గోపన్న భద్రాచల రామదాసుగా ప్రసిద్ధుడు; పదిహేడవ శతాబ్దంలో గోల్కొండ రాజ్యంలో తహశీల్దారు. భద్రాచల ఆలయం కట్టించినందుకు పన్నెండేళ్ళు చెరసాలలో ఉన్నాడని సంప్రదాయం చెబుతుంది.*

Imagine a government officer, honest in every way except one: he gave public money to a temple. Would you call him a thief or a saint? The people of the Telugu country have been arguing happily about Ramadasu for four hundred years, and they still sing his songs on Sri Rama Navami.

## The man called Gopanna

Kancherla Gopanna (కంచర్ల గోపన్న) was born, by the usual account, in the village of Nelakondapalli in present Khammam district, around the early 1620s. His uncles Akkanna and Madanna held high posts in the court of Abul Hasan Qutb Shah, the last Golconda ruler, called Tanisha (the "gentle" or "easy-going" one). Through them, Gopanna was made tahsildar of Palvancha and Bhadrachalam.

Dates are uncertain, and Tanisha's reign (1672 to 1687) gives the surest frame for the central events.

## The temple on the hill

At Bhadrachalam, on the bank of the Godavari, there was already a small shrine to Rama, said by tradition to stand near where Rama rested during his forest years. Gopanna found it in poor repair and was moved to rebuild it. He raised money from the villagers, and tradition says he also used a part of the revenue he had collected.

The temple gained its idols as well as walls: Rama, Sita and Lakshmana. Gopanna had ornaments made for them, including the Chintaku Pathakam, and the Ramamudra, a coin of Rama. Whether these were all made at that time is uncertain. For the festival that now fills the town, see [Bhadrachalam Sri Rama Navami Kalyanam and the story of Ramadasu](/bhadrachalam-sri-rama-navami-kalyanam-and-the-story-of-ramadasu/).

## Twelve years in Golconda

When the money was missing from the treasury, the Sultan's officers demanded an account. Gopanna is said to have answered openly that he had spent it on Rama. Tanisha had him taken to the Golconda fort and put in prison, where, tradition says, he stayed for twelve years.

Some say the sentence was shorter, and some that it was harsher. Many of the best-known songs are said to come from this period. They are bold and personal: he scolds Rama, he asks if the Lord forgot him, he reminds him of the temple built with his own risk, and then he asks forgiveness for his own impatience. These are the most human prayers in the Telugu tradition.

## Rama repays the debt

The best-known tale is this. One night, tradition says, two young men came to the Sultan's chamber. They gave him a bag of gold coins, six lakh varahas as the debt, asked for a receipt, and left. The Sultan woke and found the coins and the receipt. When he looked closely, he saw the coins bore an image of Rama. Convinced that Rama and Lakshmana had come in disguise, he released Gopanna and sent him home, with honour.

This is a tradition, not a historical record. We have no court document for the visit. But the story tells us what the people felt about their saint: that the Lord himself would not allow a debt unpaid.

## The songs

Ramadasu's name is joined to two famous bodies of work. One is the *Dasarathi Satakam*, a hundred-odd verses ending with the same address to Rama, "Dasarathi", the son of Dasaratha. The other is a set of *keertanas* in Telugu, sung in Carnatic concerts and at bhajans. Many are in simple language, the kind a village person would use. As ever, some songs sung in his name may be later.

## What to take from it

The story invites two readings and both are honest. One is the saint's: no sacrifice for God is too large. The other is the clerk's: he used what was not his. Our tradition does not hide this. Gopanna himself, in his songs, does not pretend he was without fault.

If you want to go to Bhadrachalam, the main festival is Sri Rama Navami in Chaitra, and the town gets very crowded. Plan a stay well in advance. For the festival calendar see [the festivals page](/festivals/).

## The temple today

If you visit Bhadrachalam, keep ready a few simple things. Reach early in the morning, as queues grow quickly. Bring a small offering such as flowers or fruit and, if you wish, a note of the name and gotra for the archana. Dress simply, as in any Telugu temple. Ask the temple staff about the Ramadasu relics and the nearby Parnasala, where Rama is said to have lived during exile, for it is a short drive and many pilgrims go on to see it.

Do not worry if you cannot sing Ramadasu's songs. Even a single line, sung badly and with feeling, belongs in the tradition that began with a man in a cell. His keertanas were first sung, say the stories, not on a stage but on the prison floor, to an audience of one.

## Where this comes from

The main sources are the songs and the satakam, oral and temple tradition at Bhadrachalam, and later biographies in Telugu. Contemporary Golconda records do not clearly describe the jailing or the coins, so the details, including twelve years and six lakhs, come from tradition and differ in tellings. Scholars generally accept that Gopanna lived under Tanisha and built the temple.
