For the Telugu people, there is no weddings-and-Rama connection stronger than Bhadrachalam. Every year on Sri Rama Navami, the whole state watches the live broadcast of the wedding of Sita and Rama, and for decades the Chief Minister has brought silk clothes and pearls to the temple.

## Where it is
Bhadrachalam is a town on the left bank of the Godavari in Bhadradri Kothagudem district of Telangana, about 315 km from Hyderabad. The Sri Sita Ramachandra Swamy temple stands on a small hill above the river.

## Why here?
The Ramayana says that Rama, Sita and Lakshmana stayed at Panchavati, on the Godavari, during their exile. The Telugu tradition places this area, Dandakaranya, near present-day Bhadrachalam. The place where Rama is said to have crossed the river is nearby, and the village of Parnasala, where the hut is said to have stood, is about 35 km away.

The temple's story begins with a woman named Pokala Dammakka, who found an old idol buried in an anthill on the hill and began to worship it. She was soon joined by the villagers.

## The story of Ramadasu
The most-loved part of the story is about a man. Kancherla Gopanna, born in 1620, was a tax collector for the Golconda Sultan Abul Hasan Tana Shah at Palvancha, a town near Bhadrachalam. He was a devout man, and when he saw the dilapidated state of the temple, he was moved to restore it.

When the villagers could not raise enough money, Gopanna used the taxes he had collected, six lakh gold coins, as the story goes, to build the temple, including the shrine, the gopuram and the jewellery for the idols.

When the Sultan learnt of it, he was furious and imprisoned Gopanna in the Golconda fort. Gopanna remained there for twelve years, and in prison, he composed hundreds of songs in Telugu to Rama, in a mood of pain and love. They are among the best-known Telugu devotional songs, such as *Paluke Bangaramayena* and *Ikshvaku kula tilaka*. He wrote of his prison pain, and the line "Rama, are you not listening?"

According to the story, one night two young men, Rama and Lakshmana, arrived at the Sultan's palace, paid the six lakh gold coins in gold coins stamped with Rama's name, called *Rama Mudra*, and demanded Gopanna's release. The Sultan found the coins in the morning, and was moved. He freed Gopanna. From that day, Gopanna was called Bhakta Ramadasu.

## Sri Rama Navami Kalyanam
The temple's big day is Sri Rama Navami, on the ninth day of the bright fortnight of Chaitra, in March or April. The ceremony of the divine wedding is held at noon, in a pavilion near the river, with thousands in attendance. The idols are carried in a procession, and pearls (talambralu) are showered on Rama and Sita, a tradition in the Telugu country.

The next day is the coronation of Rama, *Pattabhishekam*.

Many families, especially unmarried youth and couples, pray for a good marriage on this day. The Government of Telangana brings silk clothes and pearls to the temple, as a tradition.

## Visiting
1. **Plan well ahead for Rama Navami.** Book accommodation months before. Lakhs of people attend.
2. **On other days,** the temple is quieter, and the view of the Godavari from the hill is lovely.
3. **Make a day trip** to Parnasala, and a boat ride on the river.
4. **Use official counters** for tickets, and avoid agents.
5. **Dress modestly.**

## A closer look at the pearls
The Telugu word *talambralu* refers to rice, often mixed with turmeric or coloured red, that is showered on a bride and groom in a Telugu wedding. At Bhadrachalam, the same custom is followed for the divine couple, and the rice is mixed with pearls. Families who watch the ceremony on television or at the temple often take a few grains home as prasadam, and some keep them for a wedding in the family. It is a good example of how the temple's ritual mirrors the wedding customs in homes across the Telugu country.

## Why Ramadasu is remembered
Ramadasu's songs are still sung today in homes, temples and concerts. What makes them last is how honest they are. He does not only praise Rama. He complains, he pleads and he asks questions out of pain. Many people find comfort in this, because it shows that a devotee does not have to hide their feelings from God.

The tale of the coins is told with affection in Telugu families, as a story of how the Lord stands by a devotee who has trusted him. It is a tradition and a story, so we tell it as one. What is beyond doubt is that Bhadrachalam is a living centre of Rama devotion, and that Ramadasu's name is part of its identity.

## Questions people ask
**Why is Bhadrachalam sometimes called Dakshina Ayodhya?**
Because for many Telugu devotees it is the Ayodhya of the south, the place where Rama is closest to them.

**Do I need to attend in person on Rama Navami?**
No. The ceremony is widely broadcast, and many families watch it together and hold their own small Kalyanam at home with a picture of Sita and Rama.

**Is it a good place to take children?**
Yes, if you go on a calm day. They will enjoy the river, the hill and the stories. On Rama Navami itself, the crowd can be overwhelming for small ones.

**What else can I read to understand the Rama stories?**
Try the tale of [Guha, the boatman king who hosted Rama](/guha-the-boatman-king-who-hosted-rama/) and of [Shabari, who waited for Rama at Pampa](/shabari-the-old-woman-who-waited-for-rama-at-pampa/). Both show the same warm hospitality that Bhadrachalam stands for.

## Keeping Rama Navami at home
On the day, offer a drink of jaggery and pepper water (panakam) and a simple pulse dish, which many Telugu families make. Place a picture of Sita and Rama on a clean cloth, light a lamp and read a few verses from the Ramayana or sing a song of Ramadasu. Even a short, quiet puja is a fine celebration.
