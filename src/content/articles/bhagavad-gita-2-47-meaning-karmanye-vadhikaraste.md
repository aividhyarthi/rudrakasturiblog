*Karmanye vadhikaraste, ma phaleshu kadachana. Ma karma-phala-hetur bhur, ma te sangostv akarmani.*

This is verse 47 of Chapter 2 of the Bhagavad Gita. Almost everyone knows the first line. Here is the full thought, in plain words.

## The four parts of the verse
1. **You have a right to action alone.** Your choice and effort are yours.
2. **Never to its fruits.** The outcome depends on many things you do not control: other people, timing, luck, circumstances.
3. **Do not let the fruit be your motive.** If the only reason you work is the reward, you will be anxious at every step.
4. **Do not be attached to inaction either.** Since results are uncertain, it is tempting to do nothing. Krishna rules that out.

## What it does not mean
It does not mean "do not care about the result" or "do not plan". Krishna is speaking to Arjuna on a battlefield, where planning and strategy matter a great deal. The verse is about your **state of mind** while you act: give the task your full attention, and accept that the outcome is not entirely yours to command.

It also does not mean working without pay or without ambition. It means that pay and ambition should not be the only things that decide how well and how honestly you work.

## Using it in daily life
- **Exams.** Your part is the preparation and the paper you write. Marks and rank are the fruit. Spending the night before worrying about the result steals time from the work itself.
- **A job search.** Send the applications, prepare for the interviews. Whether a particular company says yes is not in your hands. This makes rejection easier to carry without making you stop.
- **Sport and fitness.** Train for the process. The medal is the fruit.
- **Business.** Do good work and serve customers well. Profit follows, but a decision driven only by fear of loss tends to be a worse decision.

## The next verse
Verse 2.48 continues the idea: *yogasthah kuru karmani*, "act while established in yoga". Krishna explains that yoga here means evenness of mind, staying steady in success and failure alike.
