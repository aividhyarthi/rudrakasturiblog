Anger is the subject the Gita returns to more than once. It treats it less as a moral failing and more as a process, one that can be understood and interrupted.

## The chain in Chapter 2
In verses 2.62 and 2.63 Krishna describes how a person is led to ruin, step by step:

1. **Dwelling on an object of the senses.** A thought keeps repeating in the mind.
2. **Attachment** grows to it.
3. **Desire** grows out of the attachment.
4. **Anger** arises when the desire is blocked.
5. **Delusion**: anger clouds the mind.
6. **Loss of memory**: you forget what matters and what you stand for.
7. **Loss of discernment**, and with it, ruin.

The practical message is that anger is the fourth link of seven. The better chance to stop it is early, at the thought you keep dwelling on, not at the moment of shouting.

## Anger as an enemy
In 3.37 Krishna calls desire and anger the great enemy, born of the restless quality called rajas. In 16.21 he names three gates to self-destruction: desire, anger and greed. These verses are blunt, but they are about what the habit does to you, not about condemning you for having feelings.

## The person Krishna admires
In the second chapter (2.56) the steady-minded person, the *sthitaprajna*, is described as one who is free from attachment, fear and anger, and who is not shaken by sorrow or carried away by pleasure. That is a description of a quality to grow into, not a standard to judge yourself by on a bad day.

## A practical way to apply it
- **Notice the first link.** When a thought starts to loop ("how could they", "this is unfair"), name it as a loop.
- **Create a gap.** Take a few slow breaths, drink water, or step away before replying. The Gita's point about losing memory is exactly what a short pause protects.
- **Ask what is blocked.** Anger is usually a blocked desire or an expectation. Naming the desire loosens it.
- **Act, don't just suppress.** If the issue is real, address it calmly. The Gita does not ask you to ignore injustice; Arjuna himself is told to act.
- **Look to the long view.** A few months from now, which choice will you be glad you made?
