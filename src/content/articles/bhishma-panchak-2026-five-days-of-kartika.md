At the end of the Mahabharata war, Bhishma lies on a bed of arrows. The battle is over, but his life has not yet ended. He has been given the boon of choosing the moment of his departure, and he waits for the auspicious transition of the Sun. During that long wait, the man known as one of the greatest elders of the Kuru family becomes a teacher. His final days give the name and moral weight to Bhishma Panchak.

In 2026, the New Delhi panchang lists Bhishma Panchak from **Friday, 20 November to Tuesday, 24 November**, beginning on Kartika Shukla Ekadashi and ending on Kartika Purnima. Some communities follow a different Ekadashi observance. Check the calendar followed by your temple.

## The story of Bhishma: the vow that shaped his life

Bhishma was born Devavrata, the son of King Shantanu and the river goddess Ganga. He was trained in warfare, statecraft and sacred learning. The Mahabharata describes him as a warrior of extraordinary skill and a person of formidable self-control.

His life changed when his father fell in love with Satyavati. Her father agreed to the marriage only if Satyavati's children inherited the throne. Devavrata was the rightful heir. To remove that obstacle, he gave up his claim to the kingdom. He then took a second vow: he would never marry or have children, so that no descendants of his could challenge Satyavati's line.

The vow was so severe that the gods are said to have called him Bhishma, the one who made a terrible or formidable pledge. Shantanu granted him the boon of *iccha mrityu*, the ability to choose the time of his death.

That vow protected his father's happiness, but it also tied Bhishma to the Kuru throne for the rest of his life. He served successive kings and remained loyal to the kingdom even when its rulers made deeply questionable choices. This is one of the epic's difficult lessons: a noble promise can become a burden when loyalty is placed above justice.

## Why did Bhishma lie on a bed of arrows?

During the Kurukshetra war, Bhishma led the Kaurava army for the first ten days. The Pandavas knew that defeating him in a direct fight would be extremely difficult. With Krishna's guidance, they placed Shikhandi before Arjuna. Bhishma had resolved not to fight Shikhandi, and Arjuna used that opening to bring him down.

Bhishma did not die when he fell. His body rested on a bed of arrows, with the shafts supporting him above the ground. He asked for a pillow worthy of a warrior, and Arjuna created one with three arrows. When Bhishma asked for water, Arjuna shot an arrow into the earth and brought a stream of water to him. The epic describes this moment as an act of respect between warriors on opposing sides.

Bhishma chose to wait for Uttarayana, the Sun's northward course. The epic links his departure with this auspicious period. The important point is not that every person must wait for a particular calendar date. It is that Bhishma used the time remaining to pass on knowledge.

## The final teachings to Yudhishthira

After the war, Yudhishthira is overwhelmed by the destruction and the deaths of relatives, teachers and friends. He is reluctant to rule a kingdom won at such a cost. Krishna takes him to Bhishma, who explains the responsibilities of a ruler and the difficult nature of dharma.

The Mahabharata places these teachings mainly in the Shanti Parva and Anushasana Parva. Bhishma speaks about good governance, justice, duties, charity, conduct, compassion and the responsibilities of different stages of life. These are not merely instructions for a king. They are reflections on how a society should be held together.

Bhishma's final position is complicated. He had great knowledge, yet his loyalty to the throne kept him from intervening decisively in every injustice. His silence during Draupadi's humiliation remains a moral question in the epic. That tension makes his final teachings more meaningful: knowing dharma and acting on it are not always the same thing.

## Why is the five-day observance called Bhishma Panchak?

The word *panchak* means a group of five. The observance covers the final five days of the bright fortnight of Kartika in traditions that follow this vrata. It is associated with Bhishma and with devotion to Vishnu.

The Padma Purana's Uttara Khanda includes a description of the five-day Bhishma Panchaka vrata. In that account, Bhishma receives the observance from Vishnu, and the text describes a disciplined five-day practice. This is a vrata tradition connected with the epic figure, rather than a claim that the Mahabharata itself gives one universal five-day festival calendar.

Vaishnava communities explain the observance through their own texts, teachers and ritual lineages. The exact rules should therefore be attributed to the tradition that follows them.

## Bhishma Panchak 2026 dates

For New Delhi, the five days are listed as follows:

| Day | Date | Calendar note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Friday, 20 November | Bhishma Panchak begins; Kartika Shukla Ekadashi |
| 2 | Saturday, 21 November | Ekadashi observance in some Vaishnava calendars |
| 3 | Sunday, 22 November | Kartika Shukla Dwadashi |
| 4 | Monday, 23 November | Vaikuntha Chaturdashi |
| 5 | Tuesday, 24 November | Kartika Purnima; Bhishma Panchak ends |

The Ekadashi tithi and the day chosen for fasting are not always the same in every tradition. A family may begin its observance on 20 November while another follows a different Vaishnava calendar.

## How devotees observe the five days

There is no single routine followed by every household. Common practices include Vishnu worship, recitation of the Vishnu Sahasranama or Bhagavad Gita, simple food, charity and remembering Bhishma's teachings. Some lineages prescribe specific offerings or dietary restrictions for each of the five days.

The point is not to compete over who can keep the hardest fast. The observance is meant to create space for prayer, self-control and service. A person who cannot fast can still read, pray, help someone or practise restraint.

## Who should be careful with fasting?

A strict fast is not suitable for everyone. Older adults, children, pregnant people and anyone taking medicines or living with a medical condition should not undertake a demanding fast without advice from a qualified clinician. A simple prayer, a regular meal and an act of kindness are also meaningful ways to observe the days.

## Questions people ask

**Is Bhishma Panchak the same as Bhishma Ashtami?**

No. Bhishma Panchak is a five-day observance at the end of Kartika. Bhishma Ashtami is a separate day in the month of Magha associated with Bhishma.

**Does everyone worship Vishnu during Bhishma Panchak?**

The observance is especially important in Vaishnava traditions. The exact prayers and practices differ across communities.

**Can I observe Bhishma Panchak without fasting?**

Yes. Prayer, reading, restraint and charity are meaningful parts of the observance. Follow your family's practice and your health needs.
