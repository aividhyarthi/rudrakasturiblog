Five days sit between Kartika Ekadashi and the full moon. In many Vaishnava traditions, these days are observed as Bhishma Panchak. The name brings to mind Bhishma Pitamaha, the grand old warrior of the Mahabharata, who spent his final days teaching dharma from a bed of arrows.

In 2026, the New Delhi panchang lists Bhishma Panchak from **Friday, 20 November to Tuesday, 24 November**, beginning on Devutthana Ekadashi and ending on Kartika Purnima. Some communities follow a different Ekadashi observance, so check the calendar followed by your temple.

## Why is it called Bhishma Panchak?

The word *panchak* means a group of five. The observance covers the last five days of the bright fortnight of Kartika. It is associated with Bhishma, who is remembered for his devotion, discipline and final teachings.

In the Mahabharata, Bhishma falls in battle on the tenth day of the war. Because he has the boon of choosing the time of his death, he remains on the bed of arrows until the sun turns north. During this period, he teaches Yudhishthira about kingship, duty, conduct and dharma. These teachings are found mainly in the Shanti and Anushasana Parvas.

The five-day observance is also strongly associated with worship of Vishnu. Different Vaishnava communities explain its origin and rules through their own texts and teachers. It is better not to treat every regional explanation as one identical account.

## Bhishma Panchak 2026 dates

For New Delhi, the five days are listed as follows:

| Day | Date | Calendar note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Friday, 20 November | Bhishma Panchak begins; Devutthana Ekadashi |
| 2 | Saturday, 21 November | Tulasi Vivah and the Vaishnava Ekadashi observance in some calendars |
| 3 | Sunday, 22 November | Ravi Pradosh |
| 4 | Monday, 23 November | Vaikuntha Chaturdashi |
| 5 | Tuesday, 24 November | Kartika Purnima; Bhishma Panchak ends |

The Ekadashi tithi and the day chosen for fasting are not always the same in every tradition. That is why a family may begin its observance on 20 November while another follows the Vaishnava date on 21 November.

## How devotees observe the five days

There is no single routine followed by every household. Common practices include:

- **Prayer to Vishnu.** Families recite the Vishnu Sahasranama, Bhagavad Gita or prayers taught by their acharya.
- **Simple food.** Some devotees avoid grains or follow a more limited diet. The rules vary by sampradaya.
- **Restraint.** The five days are used to reduce distractions and spend more time in prayer.
- **Charity.** Food, clothing or other help may be offered to people in need.
- **Remembering Bhishma.** His teachings on duty, truth and responsibility are read or discussed.

The point is not to compete over who can keep the hardest fast. It is to make a little room for prayer, self-control and service.

## What Bhishma's final days teach us

Bhishma's story is not simple. He was a great warrior and teacher, but his loyalty to the Kuru throne also placed him in situations where he failed to stop injustice. His silence during Draupadi's humiliation remains one of the hardest questions in the epic.

That tension makes his final teachings worth reading. Knowledge is not enough by itself. It has to guide action, especially when doing the right thing is uncomfortable.

## Who should be careful with fasting?

A strict fast is not suitable for everyone. Older adults, children, pregnant people and anyone taking medicines or living with a medical condition should not undertake a demanding fast without advice from a qualified clinician. A simple prayer, a modest meal and an act of kindness are also meaningful ways to observe the days.

## Questions people ask

**Is Bhishma Panchak the same as Bhishma Ashtami?**

No. Bhishma Panchak is the five-day observance at the end of Kartika. Bhishma Ashtami is a separate day in the month of Magha associated with Bhishma's departure.

**Does everyone worship Vishnu during Bhishma Panchak?**

The observance is especially important in Vaishnava traditions. The exact prayers and practices differ across communities.

**Can I observe Bhishma Panchak without fasting?**

Yes. Prayer, reading, restraint and charity are meaningful parts of the observance. Follow your family's practice and your health needs.
