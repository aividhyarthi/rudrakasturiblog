Brahma Muhurtham is the quiet window just before dawn, long considered the best time of day for prayer, study and meditation.

## What it is and how to find it
The tradition divides the day and night into 30 muhurtas, each about 48 minutes long. Brahma Muhurtham is the muhurta that ends 48 minutes before sunrise, so it runs from **96 minutes to 48 minutes before sunrise**.

For example, on 8 November 2026 in Hyderabad, sunrise is at 6:17 AM, so Brahma Muhurtham is from 4:41 AM to 5:29 AM. Because sunrise changes by season and city, so does this window. The [panchangam page](/panchangam/) lists it for your location every day.

## Why it is valued
The name means "the time of Brahma", the creator, or "the time of the sacred knowledge". Traditional texts recommend waking at this time for:
- Prayer, mantra recitation and the Vishnu Sahasranama
- Meditation and [pranayama](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/)
- Study, since the mind is fresh and the house is quiet
- Yoga, including [Surya Namaskar](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/) at sunrise

People who wake early often say the main benefit is simple: no phone, no traffic and no interruptions.

## Practical tips
- **Sleep is the real key.** Waking at 4:30 AM only works if you go to bed early. Adults generally need seven to eight hours.
- **Shift gradually.** Move your wake-up time earlier by 15 minutes every few days.
- **Prepare the night before.** Keep the lamp, mat and book ready.
- **Do not force it.** If you work night shifts, are unwell or are caring for a baby, sleep first. Tradition itself places health before ritual.

## Other timings in the panchangam
Brahma Muhurtham is the best of the day's timings. The ones traditionally avoided for new work are [Rahu Kalam](/rahu-kalam-today/), Yamagandam and Gulika Kalam. For a full picture of a day see [how to read a panchangam](/how-to-read-a-panchangam-tithi-nakshatra-yoga-karana/).
