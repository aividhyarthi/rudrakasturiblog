In Tamil it is the Peruvudaiyar Kovil, the temple of the Great Lord. People simply call it the Big Temple, and the name fits. Over a thousand years after it was built, the tower still dominates the skyline of Thanjavur.

## Who built it, and when
It was built by Rajaraja Chola I, the king who made the Cholas the greatest power in South India. The temple was consecrated in 1010 CE, about a quarter of a century after he began. Inscriptions on its walls record the gifts he made and the names of the dancers, musicians and workers who served it, a precious list of ordinary people.

## The tower
The vimana, the tower above the sanctum, rises about 66 metres (216 feet) in thirteen levels. It is made of granite blocks, fitted without cement. The surprising fact is that there is no granite near Thanjavur. The stone had to be brought from quarries many kilometres away.

A story that is often told is that the shadow of the tower never falls on the ground at noon. It is a nice story, and it is not true: the shadow does fall, and if you visit you can see it for yourself. Another popular claim is that the capstone at the top weighs 80 tonnes and was dragged up a ramp from a village several kilometres away. Historians treat the exact numbers as legend, but nobody disputes that raising such a stone was a remarkable feat.

## What to see
- **The Nandi.** At the entrance sits a large monolithic Nandi, the bull of Shiva, about six metres long, carved from a single piece of stone.
- **The big lingam.** The sanctum has an enormous Shiva lingam, which gives the temple its name, Brihad-Isvara, the great lord.
- **The frescoes.** In a dim passage around the sanctum, Chola paintings were found under later layers of paint. They are among the oldest surviving temple murals in India.
- **The carvings.** The walls of the sanctum are covered in sculptures of Shiva in many forms, and of dancers in the 108 poses described in the Natya Shastra.

## Great Living Chola Temples
UNESCO named the temple a World Heritage Site in 1987. Later it was joined by two others of the same dynasty: Gangaikonda Cholapuram, built by Rajaraja's son Rajendra, and the Airavatesvara temple at Darasuram. The word *living* matters: all three are still places of worship.

## A living temple
The temple had its Kumbhabhishekam, the consecration ceremony held every few decades, in February 2020, and the ceremony was held in Sanskrit and Tamil together, which pleased many people.

## Visiting
- Thanjavur is about 55 km from Tiruchirappalli and well connected by train.
- The temple is open from early morning, with a break in the afternoon. Check timings for the day.
- Allow at least two hours. Early morning light on the tower is beautiful.
- Nearby: the Thanjavur Royal Palace and the Saraswathi Mahal Library, and the Thanjavur painting workshops.
