It is the night before an exam, or before an interview, or before the first day in a new office where you will have to speak up. A parent quietly presses a little sacred ash on your forehead, or asks you to say a short prayer before you sleep. Almost every family has some version of this. In the language of the Navagrahas, that small moment belongs to Budha.

Budha, called Budhan in Tamil and Budhudu in Telugu (బుధుడు), is the planet we call Mercury, and he is the fourth in the usual order of the nine. If you want a wider look at how the nine fit together, our overview of [the Navagrahas in Vedic astrology](/navagraha-the-nine-planets-in-vedic-astrology/) is the place to begin. Our separate piece on [Mercury, mind, speech and business](/budha-mercury-in-vedic-astrology-mind-speech-and-business/) goes deeper into the astrological side.

## Who is Budha in the tradition?

Tradition holds that Budha is the son of Chandra, the Moon, and Tara, and is therefore sometimes called Saumya, the gentle son of Soma. He is shown as youthful and green, seated on a lion or a chariot drawn by lions, holding a sword, a shield and sometimes a mace, with one hand in blessing. Do not be surprised if the descriptions differ between temples and texts; images vary by region.

He is called the prince of the planets, and the tradition paints him as quick, curious and adaptable, the kind of student who is always asking one more question.

## What Budha is said to govern

In the symbolic language of astrology, Mercury is associated with:

- buddhi, the discerning intellect
- speech, writing and the art of conversation
- learning, mathematics and logic
- trade, accounts and commerce
- friendship, humour and adaptability

As always, these are themes, not guarantees. A prominent Mercury in a birth chart is read as a mind that likes words and numbers; it does not promise high marks, and a modest Mercury does not doom anyone. Many astrologers will tell you the same. A child who struggles with studies is not a child with a bad planet. They may need a different way of being taught.

## Day, colour, grain and mantra

- **Day:** Wednesday, Budhavaram in Telugu, Budhan-kizhamai in Tamil.
- **Colour:** green.
- **Grain:** green gram or moong dal (pachai payaru in Tamil, pesalu in Telugu).
- **Flower and gem:** green leaves and flowers; emerald is traditionally named, though it is not needed.
- **Mantra:** Om Budhaya Namah.
- **Deity linked with him:** Vishnu, often as Perumal, Venkateswara or Narayana.

Why green? Because green is the colour of fresh growth, the colour of a young shoot. A family might wear green or cook a moong dish on Wednesday as a small way of remembering him. Nothing more is required.

## Vishnu worship and Budha

In many Vaishnava homes Wednesday is a day to pray to Vishnu. The practice varies, but it often includes lighting a lamp in the evening, reading a few verses from the Vishnu Sahasranama, or offering tulsi leaves. If you are drawn to this, our article on [why the Vishnu Sahasranama begins with Vishvam and how to read it](/vishnu-sahasranama-why-it-begins-with-vishvam-and-how-to-read-it/) will help you start gently.

The link between Budha and Vishnu is a matter of family and region. Some households instead pray to Saraswati, the goddess of learning, on Wednesdays, and some to Hayagriva, the horse-faced form of Vishnu who is the god of knowledge. Any of these is fine.

## Prayers for students

Students and parents often ask about this, so here is a plain way to do it. Before study, many families recite the simple verse to Saraswati or say Om Budhaya Namah three times. Others touch their books to their forehead before opening them. If your child is nervous, the most useful prayer may simply be a calm hand on their shoulder and the words: do your best, and I am with you.

Please keep one honest note in mind. Prayer can steady a nervous mind, but it does not replace study, rest and good teaching. The tradition itself says that effort and grace go together.

There is also a small teaching hidden in Budha's nature. Speech is a gift that can heal or wound. Many elders say that before you say something important, you should ask three questions: is it true, is it kind, is it needed? You may find that this simple habit does more for your standing at work and at home than any ring or ritual.

## The Budha sthalam

The Navagraha temple for Budha is Thiruvenkadu, near Sirkazhi in Tamil Nadu, where Shiva is worshipped as Swetharanyeswarar. The village is also known for its ancient Shiva temple tradition, and devotees visit on Wednesdays. Our guide to [the nine Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam in order](/navagraha-temples-near-kumbakonam-the-nine-in-order/) shows where it sits among the others.

## What you can leave alone

You may be told that a weak Mercury needs a costly emerald or a special ritual before exams or business deals. The tradition does not insist on this, and no one has to pay to pray. Honest speech, careful learning and a short daily prayer are the heart of Budha's worship.

## A simple practice for Wednesday

This Wednesday, try this. Before you start your work or study, sit for one minute. Say Om Budhaya Namah three times. Then choose one thing to learn properly today, one page, one word, one skill, and give it your whole attention. If you are speaking with someone difficult, try saying only what is true and kind.

That is what a quick mind is for.
