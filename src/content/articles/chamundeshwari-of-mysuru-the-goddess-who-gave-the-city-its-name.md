Cities are often named for kings. A few are named for demons. Mysuru is one of them, and the goddess who gave it the name still sits on the hill above.

## The land of the buffalo demon
The Sanskrit *Devi Mahatmya*, part of the Markandeya Purana, tells of Mahishasura, a demon who took the form of a buffalo and had been granted a boon that no man could kill him. He conquered heaven and drove out the gods. The gods, in despair, combined their powers and a great goddess appeared, Durga, armed with the weapons of all of them.

In the local tradition of Karnataka, Mahishasura ruled the area called Mahishamandala, and the goddess killed him on a hill. *Mahishuru*, the city of Mahisha, became *Mysuru*, in English *Mysore*. The hill is called Chamundi Hill, after the goddess Chamundi, a fierce form of Durga who slew the demons Chanda and Munda.

## The temple on the hill
The Chamundeshwari temple stands on top of Chamundi Hill, about 13 km from the city. It is among the more famous goddess temples of Karnataka, and some traditions count it among the eighteen *Maha Shakti Peethas*. Its gopuram is a tall tower, in the style of southern temples, that is visible from far away. The goddess in the sanctum is shown as Mahishasura Mardini.

The way up is by road, or on foot by a stairway of about a thousand steps. About halfway up is a huge monolithic Nandi, around five metres tall, carved from a single rock in the seventeenth century. Nearby, the old temple of Mahabaleshwara is one of the oldest on the hill.

## The royal goddess
The Wodeyar kings of Mysuru, who ruled for centuries, regarded Chamundeshwari as their *kuladevata*, their family goddess. They gave generously to the temple, and its sanctum bears their patronage.

The royal Dasara began when Raja Wodeyar in 1610 held it at Srirangapatna, continuing the tradition of the Vijayanagara kings. In time it moved to Mysuru. The ten-day festival ends on Vijayadashami with a procession, in which the idol of the goddess is carried on a golden howdah on an elephant, from the palace through the city. The palace is lit with thousands of bulbs, and the festival draws millions of visitors.

## What it teaches
- **A place remembers its story in its name.** The demon is in the city's name, and so is the victory.
- **Strength can be a mother's.** The goddess is a protector of the kingdom.
- **Royal patronage kept the festival alive.** Dasara in Mysuru is among the oldest continuous festivals in India.
- **The hill is part of the story.** Climbing the thousand steps is part of the devotion for many.

## Visiting
The temple is open daily and is busiest on Fridays and during Ashadha, when the goddess is especially honoured, and during Dasara. Cars and buses go up from the city. Walking up in the early morning is a popular choice. Photography is not allowed inside the sanctum.
