Have you ever noticed that the same worry feels heavier at night than in the morning? Or that on a full moon night the whole street seems a little more restless, a little more tender? Long before anyone wrote textbooks, people noticed that the Moon and the mind seemed to keep similar company. That observation sits at the heart of how the tradition understands Chandra.

Chandra, Chandran in Tamil and Chandrudu in Telugu (చంద్రుడు), is the second of the nine grahas. If you want the whole set first, start with our overview of [the Navagrahas in Vedic astrology](/navagraha-the-nine-planets-in-vedic-astrology/).

## What Chandra is said to govern

In Vedic astrology the Moon is read with unusual care. Your Moon sign, the rashi where the Moon stood at your birth, is often given more weight than the Sun sign. Your nakshatra, the lunar star, is used for naming ceremonies, for choosing auspicious dates and for matching horoscopes in many families.

Tradition associates Chandra with:

- the manas, the mind, and its moods
- emotions, sensitivity and imagination
- the mother, and motherly care in general
- nourishment, home and the feeling of being looked after

These are symbolic themes, not verdicts. A strong or weak Moon in a chart is read by astrologers as a tendency, and honest astrologers add that no chart fixes your character. Please do not let anyone use a Moon reading to frighten you.

## Daksha's curse and why the Moon waxes and wanes

The Puranic story, which tradition holds in many versions, goes like this. Chandra was married to twenty-seven daughters of the sage Daksha, who are the twenty-seven nakshatras. But he favoured one of them, Rohini, above the rest. The other sisters complained to their father, and Daksha cursed Chandra to fade away.

As the Moon grew thinner, the world lost his cool light. Chandra turned to Shiva, who gave him shelter. Shiva placed the sliver of the Moon on his head, which is why he is called Chandrashekhara, and softened the curse so that Chandra would wane for half the month and wax for the other half.

Whether you take this as history or as a story that explains the sky, it carries a gentle teaching: the mind has phases, and none of them is permanent. A dark fortnight is followed by a bright one. Many people find comfort in that.

## Day, colour, grain and mantra

What families commonly associate with Chandra:

- **Day:** Monday, Somavaram in Telugu and Tamil.
- **Colour:** white or cream.
- **Grain:** rice, especially raw rice offered or cooked as payasam.
- **Flower and gem:** white flowers; pearl is traditionally named, though no one needs to buy one.
- **Mantra:** Om Chandraya Namah, or the Chandra beeja mantra if your family elder has taught it.
- **Deity he is linked with:** Shiva and Parvati, Goddess Gauri.

You will notice that Monday is a day for Shiva too. Our article on [Monday worship of Shiva and Pournami](/monday-shiva-worship-panchakshari-and-pournami/) shows how the two run together in many homes. A person who fasts or prays to Shiva on Monday is, in effect, also honouring the Moon on his head.

## Pournami and the full Moon

The full moon day, Pournami in Telugu and Pournami or Pournamasi in Tamil, is Chandra's brightest moment. Many families observe a simple vrat, light a lamp in the evening, and some offer a little milk or payasam to the Moon when it rises. If you want to track the dates, our page on [Pournami](/pournami/) lists them.

The practice need not be elaborate. Step out in the evening, see the Moon, fold your hands, say his name once. That is a whole worship in itself.

## Questions worth asking yourself

When the tradition says the Moon governs the mind, what is it really inviting you to notice? Perhaps just this: that your moods have a rhythm, and that you are not your mood. Do you feel more tender at certain times of the month? Do you sleep better when the evening is calm? These are honest questions, and answering them is closer to the spirit of Chandra than any chart table.

## The Chandra sthalam

Among the nine Navagraha temples around Kumbakonam, the one for Chandra is Thingalur, with the Kailasanathar temple, near Thanjavur. The name Thingal is Tamil for Moon, so the village itself carries the name. Tradition holds that devotees visit on Mondays. Our guide to [the Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam in order](/navagraha-temples-near-kumbakonam-the-nine-in-order/) explains how the nine are traditionally visited.

## What not to worry about

You may meet advice that a weak Moon in a chart needs an expensive pearl ring or a costly homam. The tradition does not demand this. Many astrologers themselves will say plainly that the simple things are enough: a lamp, a prayer, kindness to your mother or an elder woman, and some quiet in the evening.

Please also remember that astrology is a tradition of reading symbols, not a promise. If you are feeling low for a long time, the loving thing is to speak to a doctor or a trusted person; prayer sits beside that, and does not replace it.

## A simple practice for Monday

On the coming Monday, wear something white if you have it. In the evening, look for the Moon. If it is hidden by clouds, that is fine; say Om Chandraya Namah three times anyway. Call your mother or the oldest woman in your family, even for two minutes. If your mother is no longer with you, remember her with a lamp.

That is the Moon's own teaching: the mind is soothed by light, by rhythm, and by being remembered.
