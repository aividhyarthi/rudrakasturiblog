Most people go to a temple to see an image. At Chidambaram, one of the most important things you see is a curtain.

## What the temple is
Chidambaram, in Cuddalore district of Tamil Nadu, is the temple of Nataraja, Shiva as the Lord of the Dance. It is one of the five Pancha Bhoota Shiva temples, each linked with an element: the one at Chidambaram is for *akasha*, space or ether. Where the other temples have a lingam of earth, water, fire or air, Chidambaram has an empty space.

## The Nataraja
The bronze image of Nataraja in the Chit Sabha, the hall of consciousness, shows Shiva dancing the *ananda tandava*, the dance of bliss. His upper right hand holds the damaru drum of creation, his upper left holds the fire of destruction. His lower right hand is raised in a sign of protection, and his lower left points to the raised foot, which shows the way to freedom. He dances on a small figure, the dwarf of ignorance, within a ring of flames. The image is so full of meaning that scientists and artists have also been drawn to it.

## The secret: Chidambara Rahasyam
To the right of the Nataraja there is a curtain, the colour of dark cloth, hung with garlands of golden bilva leaves. When the priests draw the curtain aside during the pooja, you see no image. You see an empty space, with the garland hanging in the air. That emptiness is the secret, the *Chidambara Rahasyam*. The idea is that the divine is not an object but space, awareness, which cannot be seen but is everywhere. It matches what we wrote about [Advaita](/advaita-vedanta-in-plain-words/), the idea that the real is not a thing among things.

## The towers and the poses
The temple has four tall gopurams in the four directions. On the east and west towers, you can see the 108 karanas, the dance poses described in the Natya Shastra, carved in stone in a line. For dancers, a visit here is almost a pilgrimage. Every year, a dance festival called Natyanjali is held around Maha Shivaratri, where dancers from all over India offer a performance in front of the Nataraja.

## The priests
The temple is run by the Dikshitars, a community of hereditary priests, who also have the unusual right to manage it themselves. Their rules and customs are old, and many disputes have been taken to court over the years.

## Arudra Darshan
The big day is Arudra Darshan, held on the day of the Arudra nakshatra in Margazhi, in December or January. The Nataraja is taken out in a chariot procession and brought back for a long and elaborate ritual at dawn. It draws thousands.

## Visiting
- The temple is open in the morning and evening with a break in the afternoon.
- The Chidambara Rahasyam is shown at set times during the day, so ask the priests and be patient.
- Dress should be modest, with traditional wear preferred for men inside the sanctum.
- Nearby: the Gangaikonda Cholapuram temple and the Annamalai University campus.
