On any weekend morning, the road to Chilkur is full of cars. Students with files of documents, families, software engineers with passport photocopies. They have come, in the popular phrase, to ask Balaji for a visa. It is probably the only temple in the country with a nickname like that.

## Where it is
Chilkur Balaji temple stands on the bank of the Osman Sagar lake, about 25 km from central Hyderabad, on the road to Gandipet. It is a small, old temple dedicated to Lord Venkateswara, known locally as Balaji.

## The story
The temple is believed to be about 500 years old. The local story says that a devotee who used to walk every year to Tirumala grew too old to make the journey. One night, the Lord appeared in a dream and told him that he would find an image under an anthill close to his home. The image was found, and the temple was built.

It is worth noting that this is a local tradition. The temple is not part of the Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams.

## The 11 and 108 rounds
The practice that made the temple famous is simple. A devotee goes round the sanctum eleven times, while holding a wish in the mind and praying to Balaji. When the wish is fulfilled, the devotee comes back and goes round 108 times.

Many say the first wish to be granted was for a visa, hence the name. Today people also come for exam results, jobs, weddings and health.

There is no booking or fee. The pradakshinas are done in a queue on a covered path, and it takes a few minutes for eleven rounds.

## What is unusual about it
- **No hundi.** The temple does not have a donation box, and it does not accept money from devotees. That is why people say it is a place with no commerce.
- **No VIP darshan.** Everyone queues in the same line.
- **No tickets.** Entry is free.
- **Strict rules about offerings.** You are asked not to bring in a lot of items.

It is run by a family of hereditary priests, and it is known for its focus on simple worship.

## Another temple on the same road
The Hanuman temple at Gandipet and the Osman Sagar lake make for a pleasant half-day. The area is crowded on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays.

## Planning a visit
1. **Go on a weekday morning.** Weekends see very long lines.
2. **Dress modestly.**
3. **Don't carry large bags.**
4. **Take your own water.**
5. **Check opening hours.** The temple has a long afternoon break.
6. **Do not pay anyone** who offers to skip the line. There is no skip.

## A reality check
A visa depends on documents, funds, interviews and policy. Prayer can give calm and confidence, but it does not replace the paperwork. Many pilgrims treat the visit as the last part of a well-prepared application. That is a sensible way to see it.
