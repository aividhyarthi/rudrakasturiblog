**Source:** Historical accounts of Dakshineswar and the life of Sri Ramakrishna (including the *Sri Sri Ramakrishna Kathamrita* and Swami Saradananda's *Lila Prasanga*). This is a mix of history and devotional tradition, and is marked as such.

We have been telling stories about the South, but this one comes from Bengal, and it is among the best documented stories of any goddess temple.

## A temple few would touch
Rani Rasmani was a widow of Calcutta, wealthy, strong-willed and devout. She belonged to a fishing community, a caste that conservative society ranked low. In 1847, she planned a pilgrimage to Varanasi. On the eve of the journey, the story says, the goddess Kali appeared to her in a dream and told her to build a temple on the river instead.

She bought land on the east bank of the Hooghly, at Dakshineswar, and built a great temple complex, with a main shrine to Bhavatarini, a form of Kali, twelve Shiva temples and a Radha-Krishna temple. It was consecrated in 1855.

The problem was priests. Some Brahmins said they could not serve in a temple built by a woman of her caste, and could not eat food offered there. Finally a learned brahmin, Ramkumar Chattopadhyay, agreed to become the head priest. He brought his younger brother, Gadadhar.

## The young priest
Gadadhar, who would become Sri Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, was about nineteen. After his brother's death in 1856, he took over the role of priest. What began as duty soon became something very different.

The accounts say that he would sit in front of the image for hours, crying out to Kali as a child cries for its mother: *Mother, is it true you exist? If you do, why do I not see you?* He could neither eat nor sleep. The day came when, in despair, he took the sword hanging in the shrine to end his life. At that moment, as he later described, a vision of boundless light overwhelmed him, and he fell unconscious. He described it as the Mother revealing herself.

## What happened next
For years, Ramakrishna lived in an altered state of devotion. He practised many paths, including Tantra, Vaishnava devotion, and even Islam and Christianity, and later said that all led to the same God. The young priest became the teacher of a generation, among them Narendranath Datta, who became Swami Vivekananda.

The Dakshineswar temple, with Ramakrishna's room, the panchavati and the Nahabat, is now one of the best-visited places in Kolkata.

## What Kali means here
In Bengal, Kali is not primarily a goddess of fear. Poets such as Ramprasad Sen sang to her as a mother. *Kali* means *time* and also *black*. She is the one who ends things, and so the one who frees. Ramakrishna's approach was that of a child with a mother, who may be angry or loving, but is always the mother.

## What it teaches
- **Devotion can overcome a social barrier.** The temple was built despite caste prejudice, and became a place of pilgrimage for all.
- **Longing is a form of prayer.** Ramakrishna's tears are the image of sincere seeking.
- **The goddess is a mother.** In this tradition, fear gives way to intimacy.
- **Many paths, one goal.** A message that came from the very same place.

## Visiting
The temple is on the east bank of the Hooghly, in North Kolkata, easily reached by metro. Early morning and evening arati are the best times. It can be extremely crowded on weekends and during festivals such as Kali Puja.
