The Ayodhya Kanda of the Ramayana tells a story that shows how karma can wait quietly for years.

## The arrow in the dark
As a young prince, Dasharatha was hunting at night near the river Sarayu. Hearing a sound like an elephant drinking, he shot an arrow at it. The sound was a young man named Shravana filling a pot with water for his blind parents, who were waiting thirsty nearby. Dasharatha found the dying boy, who asked him to take water to his parents.

The old couple, hearing what had happened, cursed Dasharatha: as they were dying of grief for their son, so would he die of grief for his own.

## The curse comes due
Many years later, Dasharatha was an old king with four sons. When Kaikeyi demanded that Rama be sent to the forest for fourteen years, Dasharatha was bound by his own promise and could not refuse. As Rama left, he remembered the curse of Shravana's parents and died of grief for his son.

## What the story teaches
- **Haste and assumption cause harm.** Dasharatha did not look before he acted. A sound is not proof of what makes it.
- **Intention does not cancel outcome.** He did not mean to hurt anyone, yet a life was lost, and he carried it.
- **Karma has its own timing.** The result came decades later, at the most painful moment, in exactly the form foretold.
- **Honesty about the past.** Dasharatha does not hide what happened. Telling Kausalya the truth is part of his last hours.

## For today
Pause before acting on a guess, a rumour or a first reaction: in traffic, at work, on a message you have only half read. A few seconds of checking is the cheapest karma you will ever buy.
