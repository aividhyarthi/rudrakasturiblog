Deepavali, the festival of lights, is celebrated over several days. The dates below are worked out from the tithis in Hyderabad. Calendars differ by a day in some places, so confirm with your local panchangam or temple.

## The days
| Day | Date | Tithi |
|---|---|---|
| Dhanteras (Dhana Trayodashi) | Friday, 6 November | Trayodashi, 10:30 AM on 6 Nov to 10:48 AM on 7 Nov |
| Naraka Chaturdashi | Sunday, 8 November (before sunrise) | Chaturdashi until 11:28 AM on 8 Nov |
| Deepavali Amavasya, Lakshmi Puja | Sunday, 8 November (evening) | Amavasya, 11:28 AM on 8 Nov to 12:31 PM on 9 Nov |
| Bali Padyami, Govardhan Puja | Tuesday, 10 November | Shukla Pratipada |
| Bhai Dooj (Yama Dwitiya) | Wednesday, 11 November | Shukla Dwitiya |

In Hyderabad, sunset on 8 November is at 5:41 PM. Some calendars place Naraka Chaturdashi on 7 November, so check your local panchangam.

## What each day is for
- **Dhanteras:** the day to honour Lakshmi and Dhanvantari, and to buy something new, traditionally a metal item. Light a lamp at the entrance in the evening.
- **Naraka Chaturdashi:** an early oil bath before sunrise, remembering Krishna's victory over the demon Narakasura, followed by new clothes and lamps.
- **Lakshmi Puja:** on the evening of Amavasya, after sunset and within the pradosha period of about two hours, the home is lit with lamps and Lakshmi and Ganesha are worshipped. Your priest or local panchangam will name the exact auspicious muhurtham.
- **Bali Padyami:** the day after, remembering King Bali, and the first day of Karthika Masam in the Telugu calendar.
- **Bhai Dooj:** sisters pray for their brothers, who visit them.

## Preparing for Lakshmi Puja
1. Clean and decorate the home, with rangoli at the entrance.
2. Place a kalasha, a picture of Lakshmi and Ganesha, and fresh flowers.
3. Light lamps at dusk, including at the doorway.
4. Chant *Om Shreem Mahalakshmyai Namah* and the verses on our [Lakshmi mantras page](/mantras/lakshmi/).
5. Share sweets and prasad.

## Deepavali Amavasya
Because Lakshmi Puja is on Amavasya, this is also the day when families remember ancestors. See the [Amavasya timings for November 2026](/amavasya/november-2026/). The next day begins [Karthika Masam](/karthika-masam-2026-dates-deepam-and-fasting/).

## Questions people ask
**Which day is the main Deepavali?**
For most families it is the evening of Amavasya, the day of Lakshmi Puja, which the table above puts on Sunday, 8 November. Many South Indian homes also give great importance to the early morning of Naraka Chaturdashi, with the oil bath and new clothes.

**Why an oil bath before sunrise?**
It is a long-standing custom of the festival. Families take sesame or coconut oil, rub it in, and bathe before sunrise, often followed by a prayer and sweets. In Tamil homes it is commonly called the Ganga snanam, and elders bless the younger ones. If you have a medical condition or sensitive skin, do what suits your body. The custom is meant to be pleasant, not hard.

**Do all regions tell the same story?**
No. The Narakasura story is told widely, but the details differ. In many Telugu retellings Satyabhama has a prominent role in the battle. North Indian families often connect the festival with Rama's return to Ayodhya. These are different traditions, and they sit side by side.

## The stories behind the days
**Dhanteras and Dhanvantari.** Dhanvantari, the physician of the gods, is remembered in the story of the churning of the ocean (the Bhagavata Purana tells it in canto 8). This is why the day is also associated with health and not only with wealth.

**Bali Padyami.** The same canto tells of Vamana, who asked King Bali for three steps of land. Bali's generosity is remembered at this festival.

**Bhai Dooj.** The tradition says Yama, lord of death, visited his sister Yamuna on this day, which is why it is also called Yama Dwitiya. Sisters pray for their brothers' long life.

## A simple plan for the week
- **Before Dhanteras:** clean the home, wash the lamps, and buy oil, wicks and flowers.
- **On Dhanteras:** light a lamp at the entrance in the evening. A small purchase, even a steel spoon, is enough.
- **On Naraka Chaturdashi:** wake early, take the oil bath, wear fresh clothes and light lamps.
- **On Amavasya evening:** do the puja after sunset, and keep time to remember family members who have passed.
- **After the puja:** share sweets with neighbours, including those you do not often speak to.

## Keeping it safe
Keep lamps away from curtains and children's clothes. Place them on a plate or tray, never directly on a wooden surface. If you light fireworks, keep a bucket of water nearby and keep a safe distance. Older people and pets often find the noise hard, so a little thought goes a long way.
