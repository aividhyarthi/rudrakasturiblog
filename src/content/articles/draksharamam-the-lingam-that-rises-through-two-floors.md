A Shiva temple where the sanctum seems to have two floors is unusual enough to make a visitor stop. At Draksharamam, in the Konaseema region of Andhra Pradesh, that architectural detail sits beside an even more striking local story: devotees say the Shiva Lingam kept growing.

This is not a story to read as a measurement report. It is a temple tradition, carried through generations, and it gives the shrine a character that is difficult to forget.

## The temple with an unusual sanctum

Draksharamam is home to Sri Manikyamba Sametha Bhimeswara Swamy. It is one of the five Pancharama Kshetras, the group of Shiva temples associated in tradition with the story of Tarakasura and the sacred fragments of the Shiva Lingam.

The temple is known for its tall, distinctive structure and a sanctum arranged across two levels. The arrangement is part of what makes a visit here different from the familiar experience of entering a single-level garbhagriha.

## The story of the growing Lingam

The local account says that the Shiva Lingam at Draksharamam did not remain at one height. It continued to rise, and people feared that it would keep growing. In the version told by devotees, a nail was driven into the Lingam to check its growth.

Some tellings connect the nail with a mark or point on the Lingam that devotees are shown. Details can vary between oral accounts, so the story should be presented as the sthala-purana tradition of the temple rather than as a verified physical event.

That distinction does not make the story less meaningful to a devotee. A sthala-purana is the sacred memory of a place: the way a community explains why this particular shrine is special.

## Why the two floors matter

The two-level sanctum gives the story a physical setting. The temple is not simply remembered for a miracle; its architecture asks visitors to look carefully at how the shrine is arranged and how worship is connected to that space.

When visiting, follow the temple's instructions about access to the different levels. Do not assume every area is open to all visitors at every hour.

## Manikyamba and Bhimeswara

The presiding Shiva is worshipped as Bhimeswara Swamy, with Goddess Manikyamba as the consort. The temple is also revered as an important Shakti Peetha in local and regional traditions. Devotees therefore come not only for Shiva worship but also to seek the grace of the Goddess.

Draksharamam is part of a wider sacred geography in coastal Andhra. It is often visited along with other Pancharama temples, though each shrine has its own history, form of worship and local memory.

## What the story asks us to notice

The growing-Lingam story is easy to reduce to a question: did it happen exactly as told? A better first question may be why generations chose to preserve this account.

For devotees, the Lingam is not an object made meaningful by its height. It is the presence of Shiva. The story expresses that presence as something that cannot be contained by ordinary limits. The temple's unusual sanctum gives that belief a place in stone.

You can visit Draksharamam as an architectural and historical site, or as a place of worship. Either way, approach the story with respect and keep faith tradition separate from claims that can be measured or independently verified.

## Before you visit

Draksharamam is in the Konaseema region of Andhra Pradesh. Check the temple administration or district tourism information for current darshan timings, festival schedules, dress expectations and photography rules before travelling. Timings can change on festival days.

The most useful way to experience the temple is to give yourself time. Look at the structure, listen to the local account from a knowledgeable temple guide, and allow the place to explain why it has remained important to devotees.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Shiva Lingam at Draksharamam really growing?

The growing-Lingam account is a local sacred tradition. It should not be presented as a scientifically established physical claim.

### What is special about the Draksharamam temple?

It is one of the Pancharama Kshetras and is known for the Bhimeswara Swamy shrine's unusual two-level sanctum, as well as the worship of Goddess Manikyamba.

### Which deities are worshipped at Draksharamam?

The principal deities are Sri Bhimeswara Swamy and Goddess Manikyamba.
