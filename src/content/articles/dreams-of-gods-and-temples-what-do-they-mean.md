You dream that you are standing before a temple you have never seen, lamps burning, a tall figure at the door. You wake, your heart full. Or you dream of a snake, or a god who is angry, and the day begins with unease. What do such dreams mean?

## What tradition says
Indian texts take dreams seriously. The Upanishads speak of the dream state as one of the three states of experience, along with waking and deep sleep. The *Mandukya Upanishad* describes it as the second of four states. In classical texts on omens and dreams, there are lists of auspicious and inauspicious dreams.

- **Dreams of gods, temples, lamps, flowing water, a cow, or a flower** are generally considered auspicious.
- **Dreams of falling, being chased or losing teeth** are often considered signs of worry.
- **Dreams of snakes** carry many readings, depending on the region and the teacher.

These lists differ between texts and families, and nobody can say with certainty what any dream means.

## Stories that began with a dream
Some of our temples have stories that start with a dream. At [Chilkur](/chilkur-balaji-the-visa-temple-of-hyderabad/), the old devotee who could no longer walk to Tirumala was told in a dream where to find the idol. At Bhadrachalam, [Ramadasu's story](/bhadrachalam-sri-rama-navami-kalyanam-and-the-story-of-ramadasu/) has a night of mystery. In the Indian tradition, a dream can be a call.

## What psychology says
Modern science sees dreams as part of how the brain processes memory and emotion during sleep. A dream of a temple may come because you have been thinking of a visit, or because you are craving peace. A nightmare may come after a stressful day. In this view, dreams are a mirror of the mind, not a message from outside.

## A way to hold both
You do not have to choose. A balanced approach:
- **Take comfort from a good dream.** If you wake feeling peaceful, enjoy it, and say a prayer of thanks.
- **Don't be frightened by a bad one.** One bad dream says nothing about what will happen.
- **Ask what it stirred.** What emotion did it leave? What was on your mind that day? That is often the real message.
- **Write it down.** A dream diary helps you see patterns over weeks.

## What not to do
- **Don't decide a marriage, a job or a big purchase based on a dream.** Use facts.
- **Don't pay anyone who says a dream means you are cursed** and offers to remove it for a fee.
- **Don't blame yourself** if you dream something troubling.

## If dreams are disturbing you
Frequent nightmares, dreams that repeat a trauma, or sleep that is disturbed most nights can be a sign of stress or anxiety. Try the wind-down in [yoga for sleep](/yoga-for-sleep-an-evening-routine-that-helps-you-wind-down/). If it continues, speak to a doctor or counsellor.

## A simple evening prayer
Many families have a verse to say before sleep. *Shantakaram Bhujagashayanam* is a good one. See [which god to pray to for peace and sleep](/which-god-to-pray-to-for-peace-of-mind-and-sleep/).

## Dreams in the epics
The old stories treat dreams with respect, but they never treat them as the last word.

In the Ramayana (Sundara Kanda), the demoness Trijata, who guarded Sita in Lanka, told the other guards about a dream she had had: Lanka falling and Rama victorious. It gave Sita some comfort. You can read her story in [Trijata, the demoness who dreamed of Rama](/trijata-the-demoness-who-dreamed-of-rama/).

In the Ayodhya Kanda, Bharata, far away in his mother's family home, has a disturbing dream before any news arrives from Ayodhya. His worry is real, and it does turn out that something terrible has happened. The poet uses it to show Bharata's close bond with his father, not to teach us to read omens in our own sleep.

## Questions people ask
**Is a dream just before waking more meaningful?**
There is a traditional belief that dreams near dawn are the ones that "bear fruit". It is a belief and not a certainty. If you rise early for prayer, see [Brahma Muhurtham: time and meaning](/brahma-muhurtham-time-and-meaning/).

**I keep dreaming of the same temple. What does that mean?**
Perhaps you miss it, or perhaps it stands for the quiet you want. Consider simply going, or visiting a similar temple nearby. If a dream repeats and troubles you, speak to someone you trust.

**Does dreaming of a god mean I am being called?**
Many people feel it that way, and it can be a lovely feeling. A good test of whether to act on it is whether it makes you kinder and steadier in daily life.

## Four states of mind
The Mandukya Upanishad names four states: waking, dreaming, deep sleep, and a fourth, called *turiya*, which is the awareness behind them all. It is a very short text, and it is read in many traditions. You will find it in our [beginner's map of the ten principal Upanishads](/the-ten-principal-upanishads-a-beginners-map/).

The lesson is not that dreams are tricks. It is that the one who watches the dream is the same one who watches the waking world, and that watcher stays calm when the scenery changes. This thought can comfort you when a dream has shaken you.

## If you want to try a dream diary
Keep a notebook by your bed. On waking, write three lines before you stand: what happened, how you felt, and what was on your mind the day before. After two weeks, read it back. You may find that the dreams follow your days more closely than you expected.
