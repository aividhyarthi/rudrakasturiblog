Ekadashi means the eleventh. It is the 11th tithi of both the bright and the dark halves of the lunar month, so it comes twice a month, about 24 times a year. It is sacred to Vishnu, and for many families it is the most regular fast of the year.

## How people observe it
- **Wake early and bathe.** Offer a lamp and prayers to Vishnu, with tulasi leaves if you have them.
- **Avoid grains and pulses.** Rice, wheat and dal are avoided in most traditions. Fruit, milk, nuts, sabudana and root vegetables are commonly eaten. Some families keep a stricter fast and some a lighter one.
- **Spend time in prayer.** Reading the Vishnu Sahasranama, the Gita or stories of Vishnu is common.
- **Stay up with devotion** on important Ekadashis, if you are able.

## Parana: breaking the fast
The fast is broken on **Dwadashi**, the day after, after sunrise. Panchangams give a parana window, which falls within the Dwadashi tithi. If the Dwadashi is short, breaking the fast should be done before it ends. Many people break the fast with a little water, tulasi leaf and a simple meal, and offer food to someone else first if they can.

## Important Ekadashis
- **Tholi Ekadashi** (Ashadha Shukla), considered the start of Chaturmasya.
- **Nirjala Ekadashi** (Jyeshtha Shukla), a waterless fast that is very demanding and not for everyone.
- **Prabodhini or Utthana Ekadashi** (Kartika Shukla), when Vishnu is said to wake. In 2026 it falls around 20 to 21 November, so check the panchangam.
- **Vaikuntha (Mukkoti) Ekadashi** (Margashira Shukla), the biggest of all at Tirumala and Srirangam. In 2026 it falls around 20 December.

## Who should not fast
Fasting should be a choice made for your health, not against it. Children, elderly people, pregnant and nursing women, and anyone who is unwell or on medicines, especially for diabetes or blood pressure, should not fast without asking a doctor. A fruit-and-milk meal or a simple meal without grains is entirely acceptable. The tradition values devotion and restraint, not suffering.

## Finding the right day
Because the tithi starts and ends at different times, Ekadashi can fall across two days. Many families keep the day on which Ekadashi is present at sunrise, though some traditions, especially Vaishnava ones, follow stricter rules. Check [your city's panchangam](/panchangam/) and confirm with your temple.

*Traditions vary by region and family.*
