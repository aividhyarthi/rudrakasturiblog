Most Western horoscopes are written for your **Sun sign**, the sign the Sun was in on your birthday. Vedic astrology, which is what rasi phalalu uses, goes by your **janma rashi**, the sign the **Moon** was in when you were born. The Moon moves through a sign in about two and a half days, so your rashi depends on your birth date and time, not just the month.

## Why the Moon
The Moon stands for the mind, mood and daily experience. Since daily and weekly horoscopes are about how a period feels and unfolds, they are read from the Moon sign. The Moon's constant movement is also why the [daily horoscope](/rasi-phalalu/) changes so often.

## Three ways to find your rashi
1. **Your horoscope (jatakam).** If your family has your birth chart, the rashi is written on it. In many families it is on the first page.
2. **Your birth nakshatra.** If you know your janma nakshatra (the star you were born under), use the table below. Each rashi covers nine quarters ("padas") of the 27 nakshatras.
3. **Your birth date, time and place.** An astrologer or a free horoscope tool can calculate the Moon's position. Use the Lahiri (Chitrapaksha) setting, which is the standard in India.

## Janma rashi and name rashi
Many families also use a name rashi: the rashi that matches the first letter of the name, which is chosen from the birth nakshatra pada. If your birth details are not known, the name rashi is sometimes used as a stand-in. It is better to use the janma rashi whenever you can.

## Which one should I read?
For rasi phalalu, read your **janma rashi**. Once you know it, open your sign on the [rasi phalalu page](/rasi-phalalu/) for today, [tomorrow](/horoscope-tomorrow/), this week and this month.
