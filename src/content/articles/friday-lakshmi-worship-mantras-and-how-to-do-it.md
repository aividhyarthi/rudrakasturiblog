On Friday evenings, in many homes across the South, something small and certain happens at dusk. A lamp is lit at the entrance. A fresh rangoli appears on the threshold. Someone changes into a clean sari or a fresh shirt, and the house, for a few minutes, feels like it is expecting a guest.

The guest is Lakshmi.

## Why Friday?
*Shukravaram*, Friday, belongs to Shukra, the planet Venus, who stands in astrology for beauty, comfort and the good things of life. That is why it is also the day of Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity. In many homes Friday is also kept for Durga and other forms of the Divine Mother.

## Who Lakshmi is
Lakshmi appears from the churning of the ocean, rising on a lotus with the Moon's coolness and the sea's depth. She sits beside Vishnu. In pictures she holds a lotus and pours coins from one hand: the picture says *prosperity comes with grace, not grabbing*.

In the tradition she is not only money. The [eight forms of Lakshmi](/ashtalakshmi-the-eight-forms-of-lakshmi-and-what-they-mean/) include food, courage, children, learning and victory. When someone wishes you *Lakshmi kataksham*, they are wishing you her glance in all of these.

## A simple Friday puja
1. **In the morning,** sweep and clean the house, especially the entrance. Many draw a *muggu* or *kolam* with rice flour at the door.
2. **Bathe** and wear fresh clothes before the puja.
3. **At dusk,** light lamps at the entrance and at the shrine, with ghee or oil.
4. **Offer flowers,** lotus if you have it, and turmeric and kumkum.
5. **Naivedyam:** kheer, sweet pongal, or any sweet you can make. Fruit is also fine.
6. **Say a mantra:** *Om Shreem Mahalakshmyai Namah*, 108 times, or the Lakshmi Ashtottaram (the 108 names).
7. **Share the prasadam** with everyone at home.

## Texts for Lakshmi
- **The Sri Suktam,** an old Vedic hymn that begins *hiranya-varnam harinim*, "golden-hued, deer-like", in praise of the goddess.
- **The Lakshmi Ashtottaram,** the 108 names.
- **The Kanakadhara Stotram,** composed by Adi Shankara as a boy, according to the story, in gratitude to a poor woman who had given him a single amla fruit. We have the [Kanakadhara story](/kanakadhara-stotram-the-golden-amla-story/).
- **The Ashtalakshmi Stotram,** for the eight forms.

## Varalakshmi Vratam
On the Friday just before the full moon in the month of Shravana (usually August), women in the Telugu states, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka perform the Varalakshmi Vratam. A kalasam is decorated as the goddess, with a coconut and a mukhavatam (face), and a yellow thread is tied on the wrist. It is one of the most loved festivals of the year.

## Temples of Lakshmi
- **Mahalakshmi temple, Kolhapur,** one of the Shakti peethas, where the Sun's rays fall on the goddess twice a year.
- **Ashtalakshmi Kovil, Chennai,** with shrines for all eight forms.
- **Padmavathi (Alamelu Mangapuram), near Tirupati,** where the consort of Venkateswara is worshipped.

## What the goddess does not ask
She does not ask for expensive rituals. The old stories insist on this. Lakshmi is said to stay where there is cleanliness, truthfulness, generosity and hard work, and to leave where there is quarrelling and carelessness. In that sense the Friday puja begins with sweeping the floor.

## Why Lakshmi left, and came back
In the Vishnu Purana, the sage Durvasa once gave Indra a garland, and Indra carelessly let it fall under an elephant's feet. The angry sage cursed him that the gods would lose their prosperity. Lakshmi left the heavens, and the gods grew weak. To bring her back, they churned the ocean of milk, and she rose from it on a lotus. The story says something plain: *carelessness and pride drive Lakshmi away, and patient, shared effort brings her back.*

## A longer Friday, if you have time
If you want to do the full puja on a Friday evening, set a small silver or brass kalasam with water, mango leaves and a coconut, and treat it as the goddess. Offer turmeric, kumkum and flowers. Read the Sri Suktam, or listen to it, and then the Lakshmi Ashtottaram. Finish with an aarti, and offer the prasadam first to the women of the house and then to everyone. Many families add a short prayer for those who have no food tonight.

## A few gentle rules
- **Cleanliness comes first.** A tidy entrance is the best invitation.
- **Do not borrow or lend money at dusk on Friday,** says an old custom. Whether or not you follow it, the thought behind it is to start the evening calm.
- **Give something.** Many families give a coin, a sweet or a handful of rice to someone on Friday, because the goddess is said to love a giving hand.
- **Do not worry about perfection.** If you could only light a lamp today, that is a puja.
