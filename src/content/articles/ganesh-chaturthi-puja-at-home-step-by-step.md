The idol has come home. It is still wrapped in a cloth, the cook is asking whether there is enough jaggery, and someone has just realised nobody remembers how the puja goes. If that sounds like your house at Chaturthi, relax. This is a simple order you can follow, in plain words. In Telugu homes the festival is Vinayaka Chavithi (వినాయక చవితి); in Tamil Nadu it is Vinayagar Chaturthi.

Dates change every year, so check the [festivals page](/festivals/) for this year's day and the best time for the puja.

## What to keep ready

Gather these the day before:

- A clay Ganesha idol, ideally unpainted or painted with natural colours.
- A kalasha: a small pot of water with mango leaves and a coconut on top.
- 21 blades of durva grass, flowers, turmeric, kumkum, sandal paste and unbroken rice.
- Incense, a lamp, camphor, betel leaves and nuts, and fruit.
- Sweets: modaka (kozhukattai in Tamil), and in Telugu homes kudumulu and undrallu, steamed rice-flour dumplings.

Clay is preferred because it returns to the earth and water without harm. Many families also prepare a small raised seat, a clean cloth and a bit of rice to place the idol on.

## The sixteen upachara in simple words

The sixteen steps are a way of treating Ganesha as an honoured guest. Families and priests vary slightly, so take this as the common shape.

1. **Dhyana.** Sit quietly, bow, and bring Ganesha to mind.
2. **Avahana.** Invite him: please be present in this idol.
3. **Asana.** Offer him a seat, the rice or a flower.
4. **Padya.** Offer water to wash his feet.
5. **Arghya.** Offer water for his hands.
6. **Achamana.** Offer water to sip.
7. **Snana.** Bathe him by sprinkling water, or a little milk and honey; for a clay idol, a symbolic sprinkling is wise.
8. **Vastra.** Offer cloth, often a thread or a small piece of new cloth.
9. **Yajnopavita.** Offer the sacred thread.
10. **Gandha.** Apply sandal paste.
11. **Pushpa and durva.** Offer flowers and the 21 durva, naming him as you go.
12. **Dhupa.** Show incense.
13. **Dipa.** Show the lamp.
14. **Naivedya.** Offer the sweets and fruit, with a little water.
15. **Tambula.** Offer betel leaf and nut.
16. **Namaskara and aarti.** Show camphor, circle the lamp, bow and offer your prayers.

Many families recite the Ganesha ashtottara (108 names) while offering durva or flowers. You can also read [the Atharvashirsha](/ganapati-atharvashirsha-the-upanishad-of-ganesha/) if it is part of your home practice.

## Setting up on the day

Clean the corner where he will sit, and wash your hands and feet before you begin. Place the seat, spread a cloth, and set the idol facing east or west if you can, though many apartments simply have to use what space there is. Put the kalasha beside him and light the lamp first. Many families begin with a short prayer to Ganesha himself for permission to start, and a sankalpa, a quiet statement of the day, your name and why you are worshipping. If a family priest is available he will guide this; if not, say it in your own language.

Some families also do the pranapratishtha, the ritual of invoking life into the idol, with a priest. Without one, the avahana step above is the simple form most households use.

## The 21 durva

Durva is a fine grass with three blades at the tip. The usual custom is 21 small bunches offered one by one with a name of Ganesha. Elders will tell you the story of why durva is dear to him, and it varies by telling. If you cannot learn 21 names, offer them with one simple line, such as Om Gam Ganapataye Namah.

## The sweets

Modaka is the best known offering: a sweet dumpling of rice flour with coconut and jaggery filling. In Andhra and Telangana, kudumulu (steamed, no filling or with a little) and undrallu (small steamed balls) are common. Make what your family makes. If the kitchen is overwhelmed, store-bought sweets offered with a clean heart are accepted by most elders.

## How long to keep him

Families keep Ganesha for one and a half days, three, five, seven or eleven, and some community pandals go up to ten days. The number follows family custom, so do what your elders did. If you are starting new, one or three days is perfectly fine.

Each day you keep him, offer at least a lamp, a flower and a sweet morning and evening, and speak to him as you would to a guest who stayed.

## Visarjan, the farewell

On the last day, do a short puja and aarti. Offer a little curd rice or something sweet, ask forgiveness for any lapse, and request him to return to Kailasa. Then immerse the idol in a bucket or tub at home, or at an arranged place, never in a lake already polluted. Many families keep the clay water for the garden, a gentle way to let the earth receive him.

Do not leave the idol broken on a roadside or beside a tree. If you are unsure, ask your local temple or municipality for the nearest proper immersion arrangement.

## If you cannot do everything

Maybe you live alone, or the day is a workday, or you have no idol at all. What then? Tradition says Ganesha is easily pleased. A lamp, one flower, a few blades of durva and a heartfelt prayer is a full puja. A picture works as well as a statue. Chanting "Vakratunda Mahakaya" three times, with attention, is a worship in itself. Better a small puja done calmly than a long one done in a panic.
