Ask a visitor from abroad what they see in the Ganga and they will say, a river. Ask a pilgrim on the steps at Varanasi and they will say, my mother. That difference is the whole story.

## How she came to earth
The Ramayana and the Puranas tell it this way. King Sagara had sixty thousand sons, who were burnt to ashes by the anger of the sage Kapila after they accused him wrongly of stealing a horse. Their souls could not rest until the ashes were touched by the waters of the Ganga, who was in heaven.

Generations later, a descendant named Bhagiratha went to the Himalayas to do penance, to bring her down. After years of effort, Brahma agreed. But there was a problem. The force of the river falling from the sky would shatter the earth. So Bhagiratha prayed to Shiva, who agreed to hold her. Ganga fell into his matted hair, wandered there for a long while, and was then released in a gentle stream. Bhagiratha led her to the place where the ashes lay, and the sixty thousand were freed.

That is why a hard, long effort is called *bhagiratha prayatna* in many Indian languages.

## The river on the map
The actual river begins in the Himalayas, from the Gangotri glacier, where it is called the Bhagirathi. At Devprayag, it meets the Alaknanda and becomes the Ganga. It flows past Haridwar, Prayagraj and Varanasi, and enters the Bay of Bengal through the delta in Bengal and Bangladesh. Over two thousand kilometres.

## The holy places along her
- **Haridwar**, where she leaves the mountains, and where the evening aarti at Har ki Pauri draws large crowds.
- **Prayagraj**, where the Ganga meets the Yamuna, and the invisible Saraswati is said to join them. The Kumbh Mela is held here and in Haridwar, Nashik and Ujjain.
- **Varanasi**, the oldest living city, where the ghats run along the river for miles.

## Ganga Dussehra
The day of her descent, the tenth day of the bright fortnight of Jyeshtha, in May or June, is Ganga Dussehra. People take a dip in the river, or in any river if they cannot get to her, and give charity.

## In everyday life
Even people who have never been to the Ganga keep a small bottle of Gangajal at home. It is used in prayers, sprinkled to purify a house, and placed on the lips of a person at the end of life. It is a link to something bigger.

## The honest part
It is hard to write about the Ganga without mentioning the pollution. Sewage and industrial waste have damaged long stretches, and government programmes such as Namami Gange have worked to clean it, with mixed results. Many devotees say that this is the same story: the mother is giving, and her children are neglecting her. A good way to honour a goddess is to keep her clean.
