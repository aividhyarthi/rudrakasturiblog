**Source:** Mahabharata, Drona Parva (the fourteenth night of the war, the Ghatotkacha-vadha section).

It is the middle of the night, in the middle of a war, and a giant is destroying an army. The people on his side, the Pandavas, are about to lose their loved one. And Krishna, who loves him as much as anyone, will smile.

## The son of the forest
Ghatotkacha was the son of Bhima and a rakshasi, Hidimbi, whom Bhima met in the forest during the Pandavas' hiding after the house of lac. He was born with great gifts: he was huge, strong, and could use illusions. When the war came, he fought with the Pandavas.

On the fourteenth night, the fighting went on past dark, which was unusual. Ghatotkacha, in his full strength, began to destroy the Kaurava army in a rain of weapons and tricks. The Kauravas were in disarray.

## Duryodhana's demand
Duryodhana came to Karna. He said that if the night went on like this, the whole army would be wiped out. *Use your spear,* he said. *Use the weapon that Indra gave you.*

The Vasavi Shakti was a divine spear Karna had been given by Indra, who had come disguised as a Brahmin to ask for Karna's armour and earrings, which made him invincible. Karna gave them without hesitation, and Indra, ashamed of tricking him, gave the spear in return. It could be used once, with certain kill. Karna had saved it for Arjuna, the one he wished most to face.

He hesitated, and then did what Duryodhana asked. The spear struck Ghatotkacha. As the giant fell, he made himself as large as he could, so that his body would crush part of the Kaurava army as it fell. It did.

## The grief and the smile
The Pandavas were in tears. Yudhishthira, Bhima and the others wept for Ghatotkacha. Krishna, however, was seen to laugh and to embrace Arjuna.

Arjuna was shocked. He asked how Krishna could smile when his nephew was dead. Krishna's reply was this: *Karna had in his hand a weapon that could not miss. Every day I was afraid of the moment he would turn it on you. Now it is gone. Your life is safe.*

The text says Ghatotkacha was dear to Krishna, and that Krishna did not take his death lightly. But in the arithmetic of the war, a life had been given for a life.

## A hard story
This is not an easy passage to read. A young man died, and in the text it is Krishna who sends Ghatotkacha to face Karna. Many readers feel uneasy about a god who smiles. Others see in it the way that the war forced its participants into impossible calculations, and that Krishna carried the weight of them.

## What it teaches
- **Not every sacrifice looks like one.** Ghatotkacha did what he could do, and it turned the war.
- **Duryodhana's panic cost Karna his only protection.** Fear makes us spend what we should save.
- **Grief and relief can occupy the same night.** The epic does not simplify it.
- **Dharma is hard.** The story does not make the war look glorious.

## Remembered today
Ghatotkacha is worshipped as a protective figure in some regions, and a temple in Himachal Pradesh near Manali is dedicated to Hidimba, his mother.
