*Uddhared atmanatmanam natmanam avasadayet, atmaiva hy atmano bandhur atmaiva ripur atmanah.*

Verse 5 of Chapter 6, the chapter on meditation, is one of the most quoted lines in the Gita.

## The meaning
Let a person lift themselves by their own self. Let them not lower themselves. The self alone is the friend of the self, and the self alone is the enemy of the self.

In the next verse (6.6) Krishna explains: for the one who has conquered the mind, the mind is a friend, and for the one who has not, it behaves like an enemy.

## Why it matters
Krishna has been speaking of yoga and meditation, and here he makes the point that nobody else can do this work for you. A teacher can show the road, a family can help, a good book can inspire, but the decision to get up every morning and do the practice is yours.

It is also an unusual comfort. The enemy is not outside. The same mind that procrastinates, panics or sulks can be trained to focus, calm down and do what is needed.

## In daily life
- **Habits.** The alarm, the walk, the study hour: you are the one who decides whether they happen.
- **Self-talk.** The voice that says "you always fail" is a poor friend. Notice it and answer back.
- **Blame.** It is tempting to wait for circumstances or people to change. The verse invites you to start with what you can control.
- **Rest.** Being your own friend also means not driving yourself into the ground. Chapter 6 in the next verses speaks of balance in eating, sleeping and effort.

## A practice for this week
Pick one small act of self-friendship: sleeping at a fixed time, ten minutes of [pranayama](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/) in the morning, or a daily walk. Do it for seven days, and note how you feel.
