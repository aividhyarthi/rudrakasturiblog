The battlefield is ready. Conches have sounded. Two armies stand facing each other. Yet Arjuna, one of the greatest warriors of his time, lowers his bow. He sees teachers, relatives and friends on both sides. How can he fight them? What is the right action when every choice seems painful?

It is in this moment that Lord Krishna speaks. His words become the Bhagavad Gita, one of India's most influential spiritual texts. Gita Jayanti remembers this teaching. In 2026, the Hyderabad and New Delhi calendar references consulted for this guide list Gita Jayanti on **Sunday, 20 December**, coinciding with Mokshada Ekadashi.

## What is Gita Jayanti?

Gita Jayanti is traditionally observed on Margashirsha Shukla Ekadashi, the eleventh lunar day of the bright fortnight in the month of Margashirsha. Devotees remember the moment when Krishna taught Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra.

The Bhagavad Gita forms part of the Bhishma Parva of the Mahabharata. It contains a dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna on duty, the self, action, devotion, knowledge and liberation. Its 18 chapters bring together several paths of spiritual understanding.

## Why did Arjuna put down his bow?

Arjuna's crisis is not simple fear. He is caught between his duty as a warrior and his love for the people standing before him. He does not want victory if it means killing those he respects and loves.

Krishna does not dismiss his sorrow. He asks Arjuna to look more deeply at the nature of the self, the responsibility of action and the difference between attachment and duty. The conversation moves from Arjuna's confusion towards a clearer understanding of how to act.

This is why the Gita remains relevant beyond the battlefield. People face difficult choices in family life, work, public responsibility and personal relationships. The text asks them to examine not only what they do, but why and how they do it.

## Three teachings readers often return to

**Karma Yoga: act without being controlled by the result.** The Gita does not ask people to abandon work. It teaches that action should be performed with responsibility, while avoiding an unhealthy attachment to the reward.

**Jnana: understand the nature of the self.** Krishna explains that the body changes, while the deeper self is not destroyed in the way the body is. Different philosophical traditions interpret this teaching in distinct ways.

**Bhakti: make devotion a path.** The Gita presents loving devotion to the Divine as a path accessible to people in ordinary life. Devotion is not limited to ritual; it shapes attention, humility and conduct.

These teachings are connected. The Gita does not present a life of responsibility as separate from spiritual life.

## Why does Gita Jayanti coincide with Mokshada Ekadashi?

Mokshada means that which grants liberation. Mokshada Ekadashi is a sacred day dedicated to Vishnu, and it falls on the same lunar tithi as Gita Jayanti in the commonly followed tradition. The association gives the day two related themes: remembering Krishna's teaching and reflecting on liberation.

The observance is especially significant at Kurukshetra, where Gita Jayanti celebrations and Gita recitations take place. Temples and spiritual organisations elsewhere also arrange readings, discourses and group chanting.

## How to observe Gita Jayanti at home

1. Place a copy of the Bhagavad Gita or an image of Krishna in the puja space.
2. Light a lamp and begin with a short prayer.
3. Read one chapter, or even a few verses with their meaning.
4. Discuss one teaching with family and consider how it applies to daily life.
5. If you observe Mokshada Ekadashi, follow the fasting practice of your family and local tradition.

A complete fast is not necessary to honour the Gita. Anyone with a medical condition, medication schedule or other health concern should not undertake fasting without medical advice.

## Questions people ask

**Who spoke the Bhagavad Gita?**

Lord Krishna spoke it to Arjuna on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, before the great war began.

**Where is the Bhagavad Gita found?**

It is part of the Bhishma Parva of the Mahabharata.

**How many chapters are in the Bhagavad Gita?**

The Bhagavad Gita has 18 chapters.

**Do I need to read the whole Gita on Gita Jayanti?**

No. Reading a few verses carefully and reflecting on their meaning is a good beginning.

The Gita begins with a person who cannot see a way forward. It does not promise that life will become free of difficult decisions. It offers a way to meet those decisions with understanding, responsibility and devotion. That is worth remembering every day, not only on Gita Jayanti.
