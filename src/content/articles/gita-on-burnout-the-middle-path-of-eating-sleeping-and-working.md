You are tired in a way sleep does not fix. The work you once liked feels pointless. You are short with people. You keep going because there is no choice, and a small voice says: how long can this last?

This has a name now, burnout. But the Gita described the cure in two lines, many centuries ago.

## The verses
In the sixth chapter, Krishna is explaining yoga to Arjuna, and in verses 16 and 17, he gives a practical rule for the practitioner:

> Yoga is not for one who eats too much, or one who does not eat at all; not for one who sleeps too much, or who stays awake too much.
> For the one who is moderate in food and recreation, moderate in effort in actions, moderate in sleep and wakefulness, yoga becomes the destroyer of sorrow.

The key word in Sanskrit is *yukta*, which means regulated, fitting, balanced. *Yukta ahara vihara*: right food and right recreation.

## Look at what is listed
Five things:
1. **Food**
2. **Recreation**
3. **Effort at work**
4. **Sleep**
5. **Wakefulness**

Krishna is not talking about a lofty meditation technique. He is talking about the plain structure of a day: what you eat, how you play, how hard you work and when you sleep.

## Why this prevents burnout
Burnout is rarely caused by hard work alone. It comes from hard work without recovery. A person who works fourteen hours, eats at the desk, sleeps five hours and has no play is going to break, however much they love the job.

The Gita's advice treats rest and recreation as part of the work, not a reward for finishing it.

## A middle-path check-up
Look honestly at your week:
- **Food:** Do you eat regular meals, or skip and snack?
- **Sleep:** Seven to eight hours, most nights?
- **Work:** Do you finish at a reasonable time?
- **Recreation:** When did you last do something just for fun?
- **Rest:** Is there one day or half-day with no work?

If two or more are off, you have found your starting point.

## Small changes
- **Fix a stop time** for work and stick to it three days a week.
- **Eat lunch away from the screen.**
- **Sleep and wake at the same time.**
- **Protect one evening** for something that is yours: music, a walk, friends, a temple.
- **Say no** to one thing this week.
- **Take your leave.** It is not a luxury.

## The other extreme
The verse also warns against too little effort, too much sleep. Laziness is its own extreme. The Gita's answer is the middle, not rest at all costs. See [the Gita on procrastination](/gita-on-laziness-and-procrastination-just-start-the-task/).

## When it is more than tiredness
If you feel numb, hopeless or very low for weeks, if you can't sleep or sleep all day, or if you think of harming yourself, please see a doctor or counsellor. Burnout and depression can overlap, and both respond to help. See [yoga when you feel low](/yoga-when-you-feel-low-small-practices-for-a-heavy-mood/).
