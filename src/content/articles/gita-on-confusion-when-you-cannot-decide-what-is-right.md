Should I take the job in another city? Should I tell my parents? Should I leave, or stay and try again? There are decisions where each choice has a cost, and every day of not deciding costs something too.

It might help to know that the most famous conversation in Indian thought begins exactly there.

## The moment of confusion
In the second chapter, verse 7, Arjuna says something very frank. My nature, he says, is overcome by weakness and pity. My mind is confused about what is right. I ask you: tell me clearly what is better for me. I am your student, I have come to you for refuge. Teach me.

It is a remarkable verse. The greatest archer of his time says, "I do not know, and I need help."

## What Arjuna does right
Look at the steps in that one verse:
1. **He admits the confusion.** He does not pretend to know.
2. **He names what is happening in him.** Pity, weakness, a muddled mind.
3. **He goes to someone.** He picks a person he trusts and respects.
4. **He asks for a clear answer,** not for comfort.
5. **He declares that he will listen.** "I am your student."

Most of us stay at step 1. We carry the confusion alone and replay it.

## Making your own decision
Here is a practical process, in the spirit of the Gita:
1. **Write down the decision,** in one sentence.
2. **List your values.** What matters most here: family, health, honesty, growth, money, duty? Rank them.
3. **List the facts you are missing,** and find out. Often half the confusion is lack of information.
4. **Ask one or two people** who are wise, honest and not too invested in the outcome. A teacher, an elder, a mentor.
5. **Ask what you would regret.** Imagine yourself in ten years. Which choice would you be more at peace with?
6. **Look at the downside.** Could you recover if it went badly? If so, you can afford to try.
7. **Sleep on it.** A night often clears things.
8. **Decide, and act.** Then stop reopening it.

## What Krishna does next
He does not answer at once. He first talks about the self, about action, about duty, giving Arjuna a frame to decide within. At the end, in verse 63 of the last chapter, he says: I have told you what is most secret. Reflect on it fully, and then do as you wish.

Notice the last words. *Yatha ichchhasi tatha kuru*. Do as you choose. After all the teaching, the decision is still Arjuna's.

## A caution
Do not make the decision to avoid the discomfort of not knowing. Some choices benefit from time. But do not use "I need more time" as a hiding place for months.
