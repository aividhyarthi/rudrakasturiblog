The exam did not go the way you hoped. The interview did not lead to a job. The business is not working. You did what you were supposed to do, and it did not work. Now there is shame, and the quiet question: what was the point?

The Gita speaks to that moment more directly than you may expect.

## Fire and smoke
In the last chapter, verse 48, Krishna says something that sounds almost comforting in its honesty. One should not abandon the work that is natural to one, even if it has flaws, because all undertakings are covered by fault, as fire is by smoke.

Think about that. Fire always has smoke. You cannot have the one without the other. In the same way, no human action is perfect or clean. There is always some mistake, some cost, some part that does not work. If you wait for a perfect, flawless action, you will never act.

So a flaw in your effort is not proof that you should not have tried. It is the normal condition of trying.

## Equal in gain and loss
In the second chapter, verse 38, Krishna tells Arjuna to regard pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat alike, and then to engage. He is not asking for a robotic indifference. He is saying that if your whole sense of self rises and falls with each result, you will not be able to act well.

## Four things to do after a failure
1. **Let yourself feel it.** Disappointment is normal. A day or two of low mood is fair.
2. **Separate the result from yourself.** "The exam went badly" is not the same as "I am a failure." Say the first sentence only.
3. **Find one lesson.** Not ten. One. What would you do differently?
4. **Take a small next step.** Write to the interviewer for feedback. Study one chapter. Fix one thing in the plan.

## What failure does for you
Almost every person whose story we admire has a list of failures. Some of the most-loved characters in the epics fail again and again. Arjuna himself freezes at the start. The Pandavas lose everything at dice. What makes them who they are is what comes next.

## The role of results
The Gita does not say results do not matter. It says you do not control them fully, as in [Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/). Other people, timing, luck, the economy all play a part. So hold your effort to a high standard, and hold the outcome lightly.

## Talking to yourself
The way you speak to yourself after a failure matters. [Gita 6.5](/gita-6-5-lift-yourself-by-yourself/) says you are your own friend or your own enemy. Ask: how would I talk to a friend in this situation? Then do that.
