The Gita begins with a warrior who is afraid. Arjuna's mouth is dry, his hands tremble, his bow slips. He is not afraid of dying. He is afraid of what the war will do to everyone he loves. It is a very human fear, and Krishna does not mock it.

## "Not a single step is wasted"
In the second chapter, after explaining the idea of acting without clinging to results, Krishna gives a reassurance in verse 40. On this path, he says, no effort is wasted and there is no loss. Even a little of this practice saves one from great fear.

Read that again. Not a lot. A little. For a fearful mind, that is a relief. You do not need to become a saint. You need to take small steps, and the steps count.

## Fearlessness is the first quality
In the sixteenth chapter, Krishna lists the qualities of a person with a divine nature. The first quality on the list, in verse 1, is *abhayam*, fearlessness. It comes before generosity, self-control and truth.

Why first? Because most of our bad choices come from fear. We lie because we are afraid of consequences. We do not speak because we are afraid of looking foolish. We cling because we are afraid of loss. Without fear loosening its hold, the other virtues are hard to practise.

But notice that fearlessness does not mean having no fear. Arjuna is afraid, and Krishna asks him to act anyway. It means that fear does not make the decisions.

## The last words of the Gita
In the last chapter, verse 66, after eighteen chapters of teaching, Krishna says the lines that many people carry with them. Give up all other supports, he says, and come to me alone for refuge. I will free you from all sins. Do not grieve.

*Ma shuchah.* Do not grieve, do not be afraid. That is how it ends. It is a gentle sentence, and it suggests that the whole of the teaching was about helping a frightened person find a place to stand.

## A small daily practice
You do not need a dramatic change. Try this for four weeks:
1. **Two minutes of slow breathing** each morning, with a longer exhale than inhale.
2. **A short prayer or mantra,** said ten times. Choose *Om Namo Narayanaya*, a prayer to Hanuman or any one that feels comforting.
3. **One small thing you are avoiding.** Do the first step: write the email, make the call, open the file.
4. **Notice and write down one thing** that went better than you feared.

This is the "little practice" of the verse. It works because fear shrinks when you act in small steps, not when you wait to feel brave.

## For specific fears
- Fear of the future: write down what you can do today, and do that.
- Fear of speaking: practise one sentence, then say it.

## When fear is more than a thought
If you have panic attacks, if you avoid places or people because of fear, or if fear is taking over your days, please see a doctor or counsellor. There is good treatment, and getting it is a strength.
