If you have lost someone, you may have been told, kindly or clumsily, that it was their time, that they are in a better place, that you should be strong. These sentences can feel like a door closing. So it is worth asking what the Gita, the book that many people turn to at a funeral, actually says.

## Where the Gita starts
The whole of the Gita begins with grief. Arjuna looks at the people on the other side, his teachers, his cousins, his grandfather, and says he cannot fight. His body shakes. He sits down in the chariot. He is mourning people who are still alive, because he knows what is about to happen.

Krishna does not begin with a lecture. In the first verses of the second chapter, he says that Arjuna is speaking words of wisdom but grieving for those who should not be grieved for. He then spends the next verses explaining what he means.

## What he says about the soul
- **2.12:** There was never a time when I, you or these kings did not exist, nor will there be a time when we cease to exist.
- **2.13:** As the soul passes in this body through childhood, youth and old age, so it passes to another body. The wise are not confused by this.
- **2.20:** The soul is never born and never dies. It is unborn, eternal, ancient. It is not killed when the body is killed.
- **2.22:** As a person puts on new clothes and throws away worn-out ones, the soul takes on new bodies.
- **2.27:** For one who is born, death is certain, and for one who dies, birth is certain. So you should not grieve over what cannot be avoided.

These verses offer a way of seeing death as a change of form and not an end. Many people find it comforting, and many Hindu families chant these verses during funeral rites.

## What he does not say
It is important to say what the Gita does not do. It does not say that grief is a sin or a lack of faith. It does not tell you to hide your tears. Arjuna cries, and Krishna remains with him for the rest of the chapter. And the teaching has to be heard slowly, not as a thing to be quoted at someone who has just lost a parent.

Many Hindu traditions give grief a structure: thirteen days of mourning, the rites at the river, remembering on the death anniversary (*shraddha*), the offerings to ancestors at [Amavasya](/amavasya/). These rites are not just rituals. They give grief a place to go.

## How to carry grief
If you are grieving, these are the things that most people find help:
1. **Allow it.** There is no right timetable. Months and even years are normal.
2. **Keep a few routines.** Meals, a walk, sleep.
3. **Talk about the person.** Say their name. Share stories.
4. **Do something in their memory.** Feed someone, light a lamp, plant a tree.
5. **Let people help.** Accept the food and the company.
6. **Be careful with alcohol** and with big decisions in the first months.

## If you want to comfort someone
Do not rush to explain. Sit with them. Say, "I'm sorry. I'm here." Offer to do something specific, such as cooking a meal. Return after the funeral, when others have gone.

## When to get help
Grief is not an illness. But if months later you cannot function, you feel that life is not worth living, or you cannot sleep or eat, please speak to a doctor or counsellor. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is free and open at all hours.
