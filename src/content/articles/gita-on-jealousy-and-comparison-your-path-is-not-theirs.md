You open your phone and a college friend has bought a house, a cousin has a new job abroad and a stranger has a body you will never have. A small, sour feeling rises in your chest. Then, because you are a good person, you feel guilty about feeling it.

Jealousy is common. It is also very old. The Gita has something to say about it.

## Better your own path, even if imperfect
In the third chapter, verse 35, Krishna says: better is one's own dharma, even if done imperfectly, than the dharma of another done well. Better to die in one's own dharma. The dharma of another brings fear.

Dharma here means your duty and your path, your nature, your place in life. You are a student, a parent, a nurse, a farmer, a software engineer. The verse says to put your effort into the life that is yours.

Why? Because the moment you measure yourself against another person, you are judging your life by someone else's rules. And you cannot win, because you only see the polished part of their story.

## What comparison does
- **It takes your attention.** The time you spend watching others is time you are not building your own.
- **It tricks you.** You see their highlights and your own behind-the-scenes.
- **It hides your own progress.** You stop noticing how far you have come.
- **It poisons friendships.** It is hard to love someone you envy.

## The person Krishna is fond of
In the twelfth chapter, Krishna describes the devotee who is dear to him. In verse 13, the first quality is *adveshta sarva bhutanam*: no hatred for any being, friendly and compassionate. The following verses go on to say that this person is free of the sense of "mine" and of ego, equal in pleasure and pain. Not once does the list include being better than other people.

## What to do with envy
1. **Treat it as information.** Ask what it is pointing at. Sometimes envy shows a thing you really want, which you can plan for. Sometimes it shows a hurt.
2. **Say something kind.** Congratulate the person, with words. It breaks the spell surprisingly fast.
3. **Limit the feed.** Mute the accounts that make you feel small. You are allowed to.
4. **Compare with your own past.** Where were you a year ago? That is the fair measurement.
5. **Do your own work.** Give an hour today to your own path.
6. **Be grateful.** Write down three things you have. It sounds cheap and it works.

## An important distinction
The Gita is not telling you to stop wanting to grow, or to stay in a role that does not fit you. Krishna is speaking of your own nature and duty, which includes learning and change. It is telling you not to measure yourself by another person's journey.

## For a long-term view
The idea of acting without obsessing about the outcome helps here. See [Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/) and [Gita 6.5](/gita-6-5-lift-yourself-by-yourself/).

## When it is more than jealousy
If comparison is making you feel worthless, if you cannot stop checking, or if you feel low most days, please talk to someone. A counsellor or doctor can help.

## A closer look at verse 3.35
The Sanskrit begins *shreyan sva-dharmo vigunah para-dharmat sv-anushthitat*. The word *vigunah* means "lacking in quality" or imperfect. Krishna is not saying your path is lesser. He is saying that even a clumsy version of your own life is worth more than a polished copy of someone else's. The word for fear in the verse, *bhayavaha*, points to something many of us know: living another person's life is frightening because you are always afraid of being found out.

## Questions people ask

**Is it wrong to feel envious?**
It is human. The feeling arrives on its own. What matters is what you do with it. You can look at it honestly, learn from it and let it pass.

**What if my envy is about a sibling or close friend?**
These are the hardest, because they sit close to love. Try telling a trusted person what you feel, and say a kind word to the sibling or friend when you can. Over time the knot often loosens.

**How can I stop comparing on social media?**
Mute the accounts that leave you feeling small, set a limit on time, and check in with real people. A message to a friend does more for you than an hour of scrolling.

**Can envy ever be useful?**
Sometimes. It can point to something you value, such as more time with family, creative work or good health. Once you know that, you can plan a small step for yourself.

## Stories that sit well with this verse
In the Mahabharata, Karna spent much of his life comparing himself with Arjuna and nursing his hurt. His generosity was famous, but the comparison cost him. You can read about him in [Karna: curses and choices](/karna-curses-and-choices-karma-of-the-generous/). Another story is of Ekalavya, who trained alone in the forest and became skilled by his own effort, though his story ends in sorrow. See [Ekalavya, the archer who gave his thumb](/ekalavya-the-archer-who-gave-his-thumb/). Both remind us that the pain of unfair comparison is old, and that people have survived it.

## A small weekly habit
Once a week, write down three things you did that you are glad of, however small, and one thing you are learning. When envy rises, read the list. It is not a cure, but it reminds you that your life has its own story.
