In the sixth chapter of the Gita, Krishna has been explaining how to sit in meditation and steady the mind. Then Arjuna interrupts. He says what many of us have thought: this is all very well, but I cannot do it.

## What Arjuna said
In verse 34, he says: the mind is restless, turbulent, strong and obstinate, Krishna. I think it is as hard to control as the wind.

Arjuna was a great warrior. He could control a bow, a chariot and an army. And he is telling Krishna that the mind is harder than all of them. If that sounds like you at midnight, you are in good company.

## What Krishna said
Krishna does not tell him to try harder or to be ashamed. In verse 35 he says: yes, without doubt, the mind is hard to control and restless. But it can be held through two things: *abhyasa*, practice, and *vairagya*, detachment.

It is a surprisingly modern answer. Not a trick. A routine and a way of loosening your grip.

## Abhyasa: practice
Abhyasa means repetition, doing the same thing again and again. A mind that has been allowed to run all day will not become quiet in one sitting. It needs a short, regular practice. Five minutes of sitting with a mantra, ten minutes of slow breathing, a short walk without the phone: the content matters less than the regularity.

Try this: choose one practice and a fixed time. Do it for a week. Judge nothing.

## Vairagya: loosening the grip
Overthinking often means holding on, to a past mistake, a future worry, an argument you want to win. *Vairagya* is the skill of loosening that hold. It is not coldness or giving up. It is the ability to say, "That is a thought, I do not have to follow it."

Some ways to practise:
- Name the loop. "This is the worry about the meeting again."
- Ask what is in your hands today. Do that. Let the rest go.
- Write the worry down, with a time to look at it tomorrow.

## The instruction for when it wanders
In verse 26 of the same chapter, Krishna gives the practical rule. Wherever the restless, unsteady mind wanders, he says, bring it back and place it under the control of the Self.

Notice what it does not say. It does not say that the mind should never wander. It says that when it does, you bring it back. A beginner may do that a hundred times in ten minutes. That is not failure. That is the practice. Each return is a repetition, like a lift at the gym.

## A five-minute practice from the Gita
1. Sit comfortably. Pick one anchor: the breath at your nostrils, a mantra, or the flame of a lamp.
2. When the mind wanders, notice it without scolding.
3. Bring it back. That is one rep.
4. At the end, take one slow breath and say thank you.

## What this teaching does not say
It does not say that anxious thoughts are a sign of weak faith. If your mind will not rest, if you cannot sleep, if the worry takes over your days, a doctor or counsellor is part of the answer. Krishna himself sends Arjuna to practice and also gives him a teacher.
