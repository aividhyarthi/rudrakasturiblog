A message left on read. A voice that sounded different on the phone. A friend who didn't call. And the mind builds a story: they are hiding something, they don't care, they are against me. Suspicion grows in small gaps like damp in a wall.

Krishna talks about this in the fourth chapter of the Gita, and what he says is more balanced than you might expect.

## First, Krishna welcomes questions
In verse 34, he tells Arjuna how to gain knowledge: approach a teacher, bow, ask questions with sincerity and offer service. The word he uses for questioning is *pariprashna*, a deep, respectful asking. The Gita itself is a conversation that begins with doubt. Krishna never says doubting is a sin.

So the problem is not questions.

## Then he warns about something different
In verse 40, he says: the one who is ignorant, who has no faith and whose mind is full of doubt, perishes. For the doubting soul there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness.

It sounds harsh, but read it carefully. He is not talking about a person who asks questions to learn. He is talking about someone who has made doubt a habit, who trusts nothing and nobody, and so can never rest. The mind of such a person is always on guard.

## Honest question versus suspicion
Here is a simple way to tell them apart.

| An honest question | Suspicion |
|---|---|
| Wants an answer | Wants a confirmation |
| Asks the person | Asks the mind |
| Can be satisfied | Is never satisfied |
| Opens a conversation | Builds a case |

## What suspicion does to us
- It makes every neutral thing look hostile.
- It makes us act in ways that push people away, so that we create what we fear.
- It exhausts us. Watching all the time is heavy.
- It makes us unable to enjoy what is good.

## What to do instead
1. **Separate fact from story.** What do you actually know? What are you adding?
2. **Ask directly and kindly.** "I felt a bit hurt when you didn't call. Is everything okay?" Most worries shrink when spoken.
3. **Give the benefit of the doubt when there is no evidence.** Most people are busy, not plotting.
4. **Notice old wounds.** Sometimes the suspicion belongs to someone who hurt you years ago.
5. **Look at the pattern, not the moment.** One missed call is not a pattern.
6. **Keep sensible boundaries.** Trust is built in steps, and you are allowed to be careful with people who have shown they cannot be trusted.

## The verse before it
Verse 39 says that the person who has faith, who is devoted and has mastery of the senses gains knowledge, and soon finds peace. Faith here does not mean blind belief. It means a basic willingness to trust life and people enough to act.

## When suspicion is part of something bigger
If you find that you suspect almost everyone, or that you cannot stop checking, or if it is destroying a relationship, a counsellor can help. Persistent suspicion is sometimes linked with anxiety, past trauma or other conditions, and these are treatable. And if your suspicions are about real behaviour that worries you, trust your safety first and talk to someone you trust.

## Arjuna's doubt, and how Krishna answered
It helps to remember how the Gita begins. Arjuna is full of doubt, so much so that he puts down his bow. Krishna does not scold him. In chapter 2 Arjuna says, in effect, that he is confused about his duty and asks to be taught as a disciple. That honest asking is where the teaching begins.

At the end of chapter 4, Krishna comes back to doubt, and speaks of cutting through it with the sword of knowledge (verse 42) and then standing up to act. Notice the order. Doubt is not simply ignored. It is met with understanding, and then you move.

## Questions people ask
**How do I know if my suspicion is right?** Look for evidence you could show another person. If you have only a feeling, treat it as a question, not a conclusion.

**What if I asked and they denied it?** Then you have two choices: to believe them for now, or to watch calmly over time. Either is more peaceful than interrogating them again and again.

**Is trusting people foolish?** Trust is not the same as giving up judgement. The Gita values clear sight. You can trust in steps, as the article says, and still be kind.

**What about suspicion of God, or of teachers?** The same advice applies. Ask your question with respect, look for a good answer, and be patient with yourself.

## A small exercise for the next time
When the story starts in your mind, try this:
1. Write one line: *What I know is...*
2. Write another: *What I am guessing is...*
3. Write a third: *One kind question I could ask is...*
4. Wait one night before you act.

You will often find the guess column longer than the know column. That is not a flaw. It is just information about how the mind works.
