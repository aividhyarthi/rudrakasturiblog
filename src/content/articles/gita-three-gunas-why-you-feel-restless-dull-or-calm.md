Some days you wake up clear and kind. Some days you are wound up and snappish. Some days you are heavy and can't start. The Gita has a very old way to describe this: three qualities of nature, called *gunas*.

## The three
In the fourteenth chapter, Krishna says that all of nature is made of three gunas: sattva, rajas and tamas. They are in everything, including your mind, and they rise and fall like weather.

**Sattva** (14.6): luminous, clear, healthy. It brings a feeling of calm, understanding and well-being. It can also bind through attachment to happiness and knowledge.

**Rajas** (14.7): passion, craving, activity. It comes with restlessness and attachment to results. It binds through the urge to act and get.

**Tamas** (14.8): born of ignorance, it deludes. It shows up as heaviness, laziness, confusion and sleep. It binds through negligence and inertia.

## How they feel
| | Sattva | Rajas | Tamas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mind | Clear, steady | Restless, wanting | Foggy, dull |
| Mood | Peaceful, kind | Irritable, anxious | Low, heavy |
| Action | Thoughtful | Hurried, driven | Delayed, avoided |
| Sleep | Refreshing | Short, disturbed | Too much, still tired |

None of us is only one. Most people feel all three in a day.

## What shifts them
The Gita offers several clues. In chapter 17, verses 8 to 10, Krishna describes food by the guna.
- **Sattvic food** increases life, strength, health and cheerfulness. It is fresh, nourishing and pleasant. Think of fruit, grains, milk, vegetables.
- **Rajasic food** is bitter, sour, salty, very hot and pungent. It leads to pain and restlessness.
- **Tamasic food** is stale, tasteless, left over or impure. It dulls the mind.

You do not need to follow this rigidly, but most of us notice that after a heavy, oily late dinner, we feel tamasic, and after a lot of coffee and spice, rajasic.

Other things that shift the balance:
- **Sleep:** Too little makes you rajasic and irritable. Too much makes you tamasic.
- **Company:** People who are calm help you calm. People who are always in drama pull you toward rajas.
- **Work:** Doing something meaningful, without panic, brings sattva.
- **Practice:** A little prayer, breathing or quiet in the morning builds sattva over time.
- **Screens:** Endless scrolling tends toward both rajas and tamas.

## What to do on a rajasic day
Slow down. Longer exhales, a walk, less caffeine, do one thing at a time. See [yoga for anxiety and stress](/yoga-for-anxiety-and-stress-a-ten-minute-reset/).

## What to do on a tamasic day
Move. Open the curtains, wash your face, walk, eat something light and do the smallest task. See [the Gita on procrastination](/gita-on-laziness-and-procrastination-just-start-the-task/).

## A warning about sattva
It is tempting to think of sattva as the goal. The Gita says that even sattva binds, when you become attached to your own peace or knowledge. The aim is to go beyond all three, to be steady whatever the weather is (14.22 to 14.25). For now, it is enough to notice.

## A simple practice
Each evening, ask: which guna was strongest today? What fed it? That one question, asked every day, teaches more than a book.
