If you were born in Andhra or Telangana, there is a good chance the Godavari is the river you know. She is also called the Dakshina Ganga, the Ganga of the South, and the devotion she gets is similar.

## Her journey
The Godavari begins at Trimbakeshwar, near Nashik in Maharashtra, in the Brahmagiri hills, by one of the twelve Jyotirlingas. She flows about 1,465 km, through Maharashtra, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, and enters the Bay of Bengal, which makes her the second longest river in India after the Ganga.

On the way she passes Basar in Telangana, home to the famous Saraswati temple, and Bhadrachalam, where [Sita and Rama](/temples/bhadrachalam-sita-ramachandraswamy-temple/) are worshipped on her bank. Then she runs through Rajahmundry, where the river is nearly three kilometres wide, and splits into seven streams before the sea, called Sapta Godavari.

## How she came
The Gautama story is the Godavari's own. The sage Gautama, according to the tradition, was falsely accused of killing a cow, and was told to purify himself by bringing the Ganga to the area. Shiva, pleased with his penance, released the Ganga at Brahmagiri, and she flowed down as Gautami, another name for the Godavari.

## Pushkaralu
Every twelve years, when the planet Jupiter enters Simha rashi, the Godavari holds her Pushkaralu festival, lasting twelve days. Millions of people take a ritual dip, perform ancestral rites and see the aarti on the banks at Rajahmundry and other ghats. The last was in 2015. The next is due in 2027.

## The Englishman the farmers thank
This part of the story is less well known outside the delta. In the 1840s the Godavari delta was prone to floods and famine. The British engineer Arthur Cotton (1803 to 1899) designed a barrage at Dowleswaram, across the river near Rajahmundry. It was finished in 1850 and turned the delta into one of the most productive rice-growing areas in India.

Farmers there still call him *Cotton Dora*, which means Cotton, the lord. In villages near Dowleswaram, he is remembered with statues and a memorial, and some families hang his picture at home. His birthday, on 15 May, is observed by farmers in the area. There is a museum on the site.

It is a reminder that devotion in India does not only come from scripture. A river that feeds a million fields is a goddess in its own way, and the person who made her work for people is not forgotten.

## Rivers and prayer
The Godavari has many sacred stretches, from Trimbak to Bhadrachalam to Antarvedi, where the river meets the sea, and the famous Lakshmi Narasimha temple stands. If you travel in the delta, a boat ride on the river at sunset is one of the quietest things you can do.
