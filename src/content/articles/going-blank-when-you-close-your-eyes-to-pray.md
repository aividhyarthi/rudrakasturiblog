You light the lamp, fold your hands and close your eyes. You have so much to say. And then there is nothing. No words, no picture of the deity, no feeling. Just a grey blank. After a minute, you wonder if you are doing it wrong.

Many people have this experience and very few mention it, because they assume it means their faith is weak. It rarely does.

## Why it happens
There are a few ordinary reasons.
- **You are tired.** Prayer at the end of a long day often meets a worn-out brain.
- **You are trying too hard.** When you expect a feeling, such as peace or tears, and it does not come, the effort itself makes you stiff. The mind goes quiet from pressure, like a student in an exam who forgets everything.
- **You have nothing to say today.** Sometimes there is no request and no thanks, and that is fine.
- **Closing the eyes is hard for some people.** Without anything to look at, the mind has nothing to hold.
- **It might be real stillness.** After a busy day, the first moments of quiet can feel like a blank. For some, it is the start of calm.

## What the tradition says
Indian traditions do not treat silence as a failure.
- **Ramana Maharshi** taught mostly through silence, and said that silence is the highest teaching.
- The idea of *shunya*, emptiness, and *shanti*, peace, is spoken of as a high state, not a gap.
- But the teachers also say that not every blank is meditation. They distinguish between a mind that is quiet and alert (*sattva*) and a mind that is dull and sleepy (*tamas*). If you are calm and awake, it is a gift. If you are nodding off, you need rest, not more effort.

Also, prayer and meditation are different. Prayer is a conversation with God. Meditation is a quiet gaze inward. You might be expecting one and getting the other.

## What to do
1. **Open your eyes.** Look at the deity, a picture, a lamp or a flame. This is called *darshan*, seeing, and it is the oldest way. Many people find it easier than closing the eyes.
2. **Talk in your own language.** Tell God about your day in Telugu, Tamil, Kannada or Malayalam. It does not have to be Sanskrit or polished. Many saints prayed in the language of the street.
3. **Use a mantra.** A short one such as *Om Namo Venkatesaya* keeps the mind busy in a good way. See [how to begin japa](/mantra-japa-how-to-start-count-and-keep-going/) and our [mantras with meaning](/mantras/).
4. **Sing or listen.** A stotra, an abhangam or a song counts as prayer.
5. **Start with thanks.** Name three small things you are grateful for. It warms the mind.
6. **Keep it short and regular.** Five minutes every day is better than half an hour once a week.
7. **Try the early morning.** The mind is usually clearer at [Brahma Muhurtham](/brahma-muhurtham-time-and-meaning/).
8. **Breathe first.** A few slow breaths, as in [this simple practice](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/), settle the mind before you begin.

## When it is not about prayer
Sometimes the blankness is not about God at all. If you feel numb to everything, not only prayer, if you have lost interest in things you used to love, if you sleep too much or too little, or if you feel low for weeks, please speak to a doctor or counsellor. It can be depression or burnout, and these are very treatable. It is not a spiritual failure.

## A last thought
A well-known saying among teachers is that the mind is like muddy water: it clears if you leave it alone. A blank, then, may be the water settling. You do not have to produce something. You only have to turn up.
