Many of Krishna's stories take place in the open, in Vrindavan, with cows and rivers. The best known is a story of a hill and a storm.

## What happened
The people of Vraj, the cowherd community, used to offer worship to Indra, the king of the gods, every year, so that he would send rain. Krishna, who was a boy, asked his foster father Nanda why. The answer was that Indra gives rain, and rain gives grass for the cows.

Krishna said that it was the hill of Govardhana, with its grass and streams, that fed their cattle, and that they should worship the hill and the cows instead. The villagers agreed. Indra, angry and insulted, sent a terrible storm to Vraj that went on for days.

Krishna lifted Govardhana on the little finger of his left hand like an umbrella, and everybody, with all the cows, took shelter under it. For seven days and nights, the storm raged. Then Indra realised who Krishna was, and gave up. Krishna put the hill down.

## What it teaches
Many readers take it as a story about humility and pride. Indra's pride in being worshipped is broken by a child. Other readers see a kind of early environmental message: honour what really feeds you, the land, the grass, the cow. In both readings the point is the same.

## The hill today
Govardhan Hill is not tall. It is a low ridge, about 8 km long, near Mathura in Uttar Pradesh, and is said to be shrinking each year. A local story says that a sage once cursed it to sink a little every day. Pilgrims treat every stone of it as sacred, and most will not step on it with shoes.

## The parikrama
Pilgrims walk around the hill, a path of about 21 km, called Govardhan Parikrama. It takes five or six hours. Many do it barefoot, in groups, singing. On the way are the Manasi Ganga lake, Daan Ghati and Radha Kund. Devotees on a vow may roll along the ground or take it one prostration at a time. Before the walk, people usually bathe at the Manasi Ganga and make an offering.

## Govardhan Puja and Annakut
On the day after Deepavali, the first day of the bright fortnight of Kartika, Govardhan Puja is celebrated. People make a small hill from cow dung or of food and decorate it, then circle it. In temples, a tradition called Annakut, a mountain of food, is offered to Krishna. Dozens or hundreds of dishes are arranged in front of the deity and then shared with devotees as prasad.

In 2026 Govardhan Puja is on **Tuesday, 10 November**, the day after Deepavali Amavasya. The full list of Deepavali dates is in [our Deepavali guide](/deepavali-2026-dates-dhanteras-naraka-chaturdashi-lakshmi-puja/). Bali Padyami, the same day, begins Karthika Masam in the Telugu calendar.
