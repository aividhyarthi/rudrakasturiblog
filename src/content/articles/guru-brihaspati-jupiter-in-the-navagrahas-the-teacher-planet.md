Who was the first person who really taught you something? Perhaps a school teacher, perhaps an uncle who explained how to ride a bicycle, perhaps a grandmother who taught you a verse without ever calling it a lesson. Most of us carry that person in memory with a quiet, unpaid gratitude. In the tradition of the Navagrahas, that feeling has a name and a place in the sky: Guru.

Guru, called Guru or Viyazhan in Tamil and Guruvu or Brihaspati in Telugu (బృహస్పతి), is the planet Jupiter, the fifth in the common order. If you want to see all nine together, begin with our overview of [the Navagrahas in Vedic astrology](/navagraha-the-nine-planets-in-vedic-astrology/).

## Who is Brihaspati?

Tradition holds that Brihaspati is the preceptor of the devas, the gods, and a son of the sage Angirasa. When the gods need advice, they go to him. He is shown in a golden or yellow form, often seated, holding a rod, a rosary and a book, sometimes with a lotus.

The word guru means one who removes darkness, gu being darkness and ru the one who dispels it. That is the heart of what this graha stands for. It is not about being strict; it is about guiding.

## What Guru is said to govern

In astrology, Jupiter is associated with:

- wisdom, learning and teachers
- dharma, sound judgement and good counsel
- children and the blessings of elders
- generosity, optimism and faith

Of the nine, Guru is considered the most benefic, which is to say the most kindly. Even so, no honest teacher of astrology will tell you that Jupiter's placement guarantees good fortune, or that a weak Jupiter dooms you. A chart is a set of symbols to reflect on, nothing more.

## Day, colour, grain and mantra

- **Day:** Thursday, Guruvaram in Telugu, Viyazhakizhamai in Tamil.
- **Colour:** yellow.
- **Grain:** chana dal or Bengal gram (kadalai paruppu in Tamil, senagapappu in Telugu).
- **Flower and gem:** yellow flowers such as chrysanthemum or jasmine; yellow sapphire is traditionally named, though it is never needed for prayer.
- **Mantra:** Om Gurave Namah, or Om Brihaspataye Namah.
- **Deities he is linked with:** Dakshinamurthy, and in many homes Sai Baba, Raghavendra Swami and other gurus venerated on Thursday.

Yellow turmeric, banana and yellow flowers are common on this day. In some homes a sweet made of chana dal is prepared. If you want a fuller picture of how families keep this day, our article on [Thursday and Guru worship](/thursday-is-for-the-guru-how-to-worship-on-guruvaram/) goes through it step by step.

## Dakshinamurthy and Brihaspati

Here is a point that confuses many people. Brihaspati is the graha, the teacher of the gods. Dakshinamurthy is Shiva himself, seated facing south under a banyan tree, teaching through silence. The tradition treats them as different, yet in practice many families pray to both on Thursdays, since both stand for knowledge that frees.

Adi Shankara's Dakshinamurthy Stotram praises that silent teacher, and many people recite it on Thursdays. If it is too long, simply saying Om Namah Shivaya before the shrine of Dakshinamurthy, usually found on the south wall of Shiva temples, is a fine start. If you are also wondering how to find a living teacher, our article on [searching for a guru and recognising a true one](/searching-for-a-guru-how-to-find-one-and-how-to-know-a-real-one/) speaks to that.

## The Guru sthalam

Among the nine Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam, the one for Guru is Alangudi, with the Apatsahayeswarar temple. Apatsahayeswarar means the one who helps in times of danger. Devotees traditionally visit on Thursdays, and it is often counted in the pilgrimage described in our guide to [the Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam in order](/navagraha-temples-near-kumbakonam-the-nine-in-order/).

## What is Guru peyarchi?

If you read Tamil calendars, you will see a notice about Guru peyarchi, which means the transit or change of place of Guru. Jupiter takes roughly a year to move from one rashi, or zodiac sign, to the next. Tamil panchangams announce the date and time, and many people go to a temple, especially to Dakshinamurthy or to Guru shrines, on that day.

Different families and astrologers give different readings of what each transit means for each rashi, and the readings published in newspapers are very general. Please take them lightly. They are traditions of reflection, not predictions you must plan your life around. If a stranger tells you to pay for a special puja because of your rashi, you are free to refuse.

## What you can leave alone

Expensive rings, costly homams and warnings that your children's future depends on a ritual are not part of what the tradition asks of an ordinary householder. A teacher's greatest blessing is knowledge, and the most honest way to honour Guru is to learn something and to thank the person who taught you.

## A simple practice for Thursday

On Thursday, wear something yellow if you like. Light a lamp, say Om Gurave Namah eleven times, and then do one small thing for a teacher: call a former teacher to say thank you, or touch the feet of an elder, or share something you know with a younger person. If your teacher has passed on, remember their name aloud.

That is worship in the spirit of Guru: gratitude, passed on.
