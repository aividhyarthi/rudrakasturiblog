A community kitchen serves a meal to anyone who arrives. No one is asked about their wealth, background or status. For many visitors, this is one of the most visible expressions of the teachings remembered on Guru Nanak Jayanti.

Also called Gurpurab, the day commemorates Guru Nanak, the first of the Sikh Gurus and the founder of the Sikh tradition. In 2026, Guru Nanak Jayanti falls on **Tuesday, 24 November**, which is Kartik Purnima in the New Delhi Hindu calendar.

## Who was Guru Nanak?

Guru Nanak was born in 1469 at Rai Bhoi di Talwandi, now known as Nankana Sahib in present-day Pakistan. His teachings questioned empty ritual, social divisions and the idea that the Divine belongs to one community alone.

He travelled widely, according to Sikh tradition, sharing his message through conversation, hymns and spiritual teaching. His words form a central part of the Guru Granth Sahib, the Sikh scripture.

Guru Nanak's message begins with the oneness of the Divine. The opening words of the Sikh scripture, known as the Mool Mantar, describe the One as timeless, without fear and without enmity. The teaching is not only an idea to repeat. It asks a person to change how they live.

## Three principles often used to explain his teaching

Sikh ethical teaching is commonly explained through three connected principles:

- **Naam Japna:** Remember and meditate on the Divine Name.
- **Kirat Karni:** Earn a living through honest work.
- **Vand Chhakna:** Share what you have with others.

These principles connect spiritual life with everyday responsibility. Prayer is not separated from work, family or care for people around us. Honest earning matters. So does sharing the fruit of that work.

The three-part summary is widely used in Sikh education and community life. Scholars also note that the way these principles are grouped and named has a history of its own, so it should not be presented as a verbatim list from one single verse.

## Why is Guru Nanak Jayanti on Kartik Purnima?

The SGPC Nanakshahi calendar lists Guru Nanak Dev Ji's Parkash Gurpurab on 9 Maghar. In 2026, that date falls on 24 November and coincides with Kartik Purnima in the Hindu calendar. Sikh communities follow the calendar and practices recognised by their own institutions and local gurdwaras. The Gregorian date can change from year to year.

In 2026, the New Delhi panchang lists the full moon on 24 November. Sikh communities around the world observe Gurpurab with their own calendar and local programmes. For exact event timings, check the nearest gurdwara.

## How is Gurpurab observed?

The observance varies by gurdwara and community. Common elements include:

- **Akhand Path:** A continuous reading of the Guru Granth Sahib, completed over a set period.
- **Kirtan:** Singing of hymns from Sikh scripture.
- **Nagar Kirtan:** A community procession in some towns and cities.
- **Langar:** A free community meal prepared and served to everyone.
- **Seva:** Voluntary service, from preparing food to helping visitors.

The langar is especially important because it makes equality visible in an ordinary act: sitting together and sharing a meal.

## What can we learn from Guru Nanak today?

His teachings are practical. Remember the Divine, earn honestly and share. They ask for a life in which faith is visible in conduct, not only in words.

For a person who is busy with work, the message is not to abandon daily responsibilities. It is to bring honesty and care into them. For a community, it is a reminder that dignity should not depend on status or wealth.

## Questions people ask

**Is Guru Nanak Jayanti the same as Gurpurab?**

Gurpurab is the name used for a Sikh commemorative festival associated with a Guru. Guru Nanak Jayanti is one of the most widely observed Gurpurabs.

**Where was Guru Nanak born?**

He was born at Rai Bhoi di Talwandi, now Nankana Sahib in Pakistan.

**Can people of other faiths attend langar?**

Yes. The langar is open to all, and sharing a meal without discrimination is central to its practice.
