Many parents in Kerala, and many from the Telugu and Tamil states too, have the same plan for their baby: the first rice must be eaten at Guruvayur. It is also a temple of wonderful stories.

## Where it is
Guruvayur is a town in Thrissur district of Kerala, about 30 km from Thrissur city. The Guruvayur Sri Krishna temple is often called the Dwaraka of the South. The deity is Guruvayurappan, Krishna in the form of a four-armed child, holding the conch, discus, mace and lotus.

## The story of the name
According to tradition, the idol was worshipped by Brahma, then by Vishnu's devotees, and eventually installed by the guru of the gods, Brihaspati, and the wind god Vayu, in a spot chosen after the end of Dwaraka. Hence *Guru* plus *Vayu* plus *ur* (place): Guruvayur.

## Annaprasanam
The best-known ritual is *choroonu*, the first feeding of rice (in Telugu *annaprasana*). A baby of about six months is brought to the temple dressed in new clothes. The priest or a relative offers the first spoon of sweet rice, and the family offers a prayer. Every day there are hundreds of such ceremonies, and the temple has a separate area for it.

For families, it is a joyful moment, with grandparents, photographs and sweet payasam.

## Thulabharam
Another ritual unique to the temple is the *thulabharam*. A devotee sits on one side of a large scale and is weighed against offerings, such as bananas, sugar, jaggery, coconuts, rice or even gold. The offerings are given to the temple. It is done in fulfilment of a vow, for good health or thanks.

## The elephants
The temple is also known for its elephants. A sanctuary called Punnathur Kotta, close by, is home to dozens of elephants kept for the temple. The famous elephant Guruvayur Keshavan, who died in 1976, is still honoured with a statue and an annual event. Visitors can go to the sanctuary, subject to timings and rules.

## The Narayaneeyam
The best-loved story is of a poet. In 1586, Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri, a Sanskrit scholar from Kerala, was suffering from a painful rheumatic ailment. On the advice of the poet Thunchaththu Ezhuthachan, the story goes, he came to Guruvayur and began to compose the Narayaneeyam, a poem of about a thousand verses retelling the Bhagavata Purana. He composed a hundred verses a day and offered them to the Lord. When he finished, he was cured.

The work is still recited in the temple, and in many homes in Kerala, and is known for its devotional power and its use of language.

## Customs to know
- **Dress code.** Men are expected to wear a mundu (dhoti), without a shirt. Women wear a saree, a skirt and blouse, or a salwar with a dupatta, depending on the current rules. Check before you go.
- **Non-Hindus.** Entry to the sanctum is restricted by temple rules, though visitors can see the exterior.
- **Phones and cameras** are not allowed in the temple.
- **Darshan** usually starts around 3 am. Evenings are crowded.

## Visiting
1. **Book accommodation in advance,** especially during festivals such as Ekadashi, Vishu and the temple festival in February and March.
2. **Go early.**
3. **Use official counters,** and avoid agents.
4. **Try the temple's payasam and meals.**
