**Source:** later and folk tradition, not Valmiki. The story differs from place to place.

On Saturday evenings, in lane after lane of the Telugu states, you will see a small crowd outside a Hanuman temple. Many hold a lamp of sesame oil. Many are in black or dark blue. They have come for the same reason: *Shani*.

## The story people tell
In the popular telling, when Ravana was at the height of his power, he held the nine planets captive in his court and made them lie in a row, so that their gaze would be turned downward. Hanuman, searching Lanka for Sita, found them and released them. Shani, grateful, offered a boon. Hanuman asked for nothing for himself. Shani promised that he would not trouble any person who worshipped Hanuman.

In other versions, Hanuman allows Shani to climb on his shoulders and discovers that the weight is great, and so on. There are many such tales, and they do not agree.

It is worth being clear: this is a folk story. It is not in the Valmiki Ramayana. Nothing in the great texts promises that Hanuman will turn aside Shani's effects. What exists is a belief, held with great warmth by many families.

## What Shani is
In Vedic astrology Shani is the planet Saturn, the slowest of the visible planets, taking about thirty years to go around the Zodiac. He is the planet of time, discipline, hard work and consequence. When Saturn passes through the sign before, the sign of, and the sign after your Moon, the tradition calls the period *Sade Sati*, "seven and a half years". It is often described as a hard time. The more thoughtful teachers add: it is also a time of building character.

## What people do
- **Worship Hanuman on Saturdays.** Many read the Hanuman Chalisa, or the Sundara Kanda.
- **Light a sesame oil lamp** for Shani.
- **Offer simple charity,** such as sesame, black clothes, food or oil to those in need.
- **Be disciplined.** Shani is said to reward honest work and fairness, and to punish carelessness.

## A balanced view
If you are in Sade Sati, you may hear frightening things. Be careful of anyone who sells fear. In our [Sade Sati guide](/sade-sati-2026-which-rashis-are-affected/) we have explained which rashis are affected now, and how traditions read it.

The honest teaching is this: planets do not act on us as a weather system. What they offer is a language to think about time, patience and work. Many people find that a hard season brought out their best.

## Saturdays that help
If you want a simple Saturday practice: light a lamp at sunset, read a few verses of the Chalisa, give something to someone who cannot repay it, and spend ten minutes thinking about what you have been putting off. That is probably what Shani wants anyway.

## Shani temples
- **Thirunallar,** near Karaikal, in Tamil Nadu, has a famous Shani shrine within the Darbaranyeswarar temple. Pilgrims bathe in the Nala tank, linked to the story of King Nala, who was troubled by Shani and recovered.
- **Mandapalli,** in the East Godavari region of Andhra Pradesh, has a well-known Shani temple visited on Saturdays.
- **Shani Shingnapur,** in Maharashtra, is known for its open-air Shani stone and its famous village without doors.

If you want the story of Nala, we have it in our [karma series](/nala-damayanti-and-kali-karma-and-patience/): it is a story of patience through a hard period.

## What to actually do in a hard stretch
If you are told you are in Sade Sati, or any hard period, three things matter more than any ritual.
1. **Do your work well and on time.** Shani is said to reward steady effort.
2. **Look after your health and your money.** Save a little, keep checkups, avoid risky bets.
3. **Treat people fairly,** especially those who work for you. Shani is considered the planet of justice.

Light the lamp, say the Chalisa, give charity: all fine. But the true Shani puja is a well-kept life.

## Questions people ask
**Do I have to be in Sade Sati to worship Hanuman on Saturday?**
Not at all. Many families go every Saturday for strength and calm, whatever the planets are doing.

**Should I buy a ring or an expensive remedy?**
You do not need to. Be wary of anyone who asks for large sums to "remove" Shani's effect. The traditional practices above cost almost nothing.

**Is it right to be afraid of Shani?**
Most teachers say no. Shani is described as strict but fair. If you are honest and hardworking, there is little to fear in that picture.

## How to read a story like this
Folk stories carry a feeling, and the feeling here is trust. You are not alone in a hard season; there is someone strong who has promised to stand with you. Whether you take it as history or as poetry, it does what stories are meant to do: it gives courage. The wise thing is to hold it lightly. Let it comfort you, and let your own effort do the rest.
