The Hanuman Chalisa was composed by the poet-saint Tulsidas, in Awadhi, in the sixteenth century. *Chalisa* means forty, for its forty verses (chaupais). Tuesday and Saturday are Hanuman's days, and many people recite it on both.

## Opening doha
*Shri Guru Charan Saroj Raj, Nij Man Mukur Sudhari*
*Baranau Raghubar Bimal Jasu, Jo Dayaku Phal Chari*

**Meaning:** Cleaning the mirror of my mind with the dust of my guru's lotus feet, I describe the pure glory of Raghuvar (Rama), which gives the four fruits of life: dharma, wealth, desire and liberation.

## The first chaupais
**1.** *Jai Hanuman Gyan Gun Sagar, Jai Kapis Tihun Lok Ujagar*
Victory to Hanuman, ocean of knowledge and virtue. Victory to the lord of the monkeys, who lights up the three worlds.

**2.** *Ram Doot Atulit Bal Dhama, Anjani Putra Pavan Sut Nama*
Messenger of Rama, abode of matchless strength, known as the son of Anjani and the son of the Wind.

**3.** *Mahabir Bikram Bajrangi, Kumati Nivar Sumati Ke Sangi*
Great hero, mighty, with limbs strong as a thunderbolt. Remover of bad thoughts and companion of good ones.

**4.** *Kanchan Baran Biraj Subesa, Kanan Kundal Kunchit Kesa*
Golden in colour, beautifully dressed, with earrings and curly hair.

**5.** *Hath Bajra Aur Dhvaja Biraje, Kandhe Moonj Janeu Sajai*
In your hand a mace and a flag, and across your shoulder a sacred thread of munja grass.

## What the opening teaches
Notice the order. Before asking Hanuman for anything, Tulsidas cleans his own mind, honours his guru and praises Rama's glory. And among Hanuman's titles, the ones he picks first are knowledge, virtue and the removal of bad thoughts, not only strength.

## How to recite it
- Recite on **Tuesdays and Saturdays**, at sunrise or dusk, after a bath.
- Light a lamp and offer sindoor, red flowers or a garland of betel leaves.
- Recite once, three, seven or eleven times, as time allows. Many people recite it before an exam, journey or interview.
- Understanding the meaning matters as much as the count.
