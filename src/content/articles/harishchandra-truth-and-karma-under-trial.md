Harishchandra is the king whose name has become a byword for truthfulness in India. The story is told in the Markandeya Purana and in many regional retellings, including popular Telugu and Kannada versions.

## The test
The sage Vishwamitra, testing the king's reputation for truth, asked him for his entire kingdom as a gift, and Harishchandra gave it without hesitation. Vishwamitra then demanded the customary additional payment (dakshina). With no kingdom left, Harishchandra left Ayodhya with his wife Shaibya (Chandramati) and young son Rohitashva and was given a deadline to pay.

## The fall
Unable to pay, he sold his wife into service and himself to a keeper of a cremation ground in Kashi. He worked in the burning ground, collecting the fee from every family who brought their dead. One day his own son Rohita died of a snakebite, and his wife came to the cremation ground carrying the body. Harishchandra, bound by his duty to his master, asked her for the fee, even for his own child.

## The ending
At that moment the gods appeared. The whole thing had been a test. Rohita was restored to life, the kingdom was returned, and the king was honoured. In the retelling, truth and karma meet: the one who kept his word through every loss was lifted above all of them.

## What the story teaches
- **Character shows in loss.** Harishchandra's truth was not tested while he was a king, but when everything had been taken.
- **Do what is right, not what is rewarded.** Harishchandra did not know the ending. The story is a picture of *nishkama karma*, acting without calculating the result.
- **Duty can be hard.** The cremation ground scene is painful on purpose. The tradition does not say that truth is easy, only that it is worth it.

## For today
Few of us will lose a kingdom for a promise. But we all have small tests: the true receipt, the honest answer, the work done properly when nobody checks. Those are our Harishchandra moments.
