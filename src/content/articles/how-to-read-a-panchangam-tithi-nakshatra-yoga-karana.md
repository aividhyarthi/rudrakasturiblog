The panchangam is the Hindu almanac. *Pancha* means five and *anga* means limb. Each day it lists five things, all worked out from the positions of the Sun and Moon.

## 1. Tithi: the lunar day
A tithi is the time it takes the Moon to move 12 degrees ahead of the Sun. There are 30 tithis in a lunar month, in two halves of 15:

- **Shukla paksha**, the bright half, from Amavasya to Pournami as the Moon grows.
- **Krishna paksha**, the dark half, from Pournami to Amavasya as it shrinks.

Tithis start and end at any time of day, so one tithi often covers parts of two calendar days. The tithi in force at sunrise usually gives its name to the day. Special days such as [Amavasya](/amavasya/), [Pournami](/pournami/) and Ekadashi are tithis.

## 2. Vara: the weekday
The weekday is ruled by a planet: Sun for Sunday, Moon for Monday, and so on. Each weekday has its own Rahu Kalam, because the three bad periods of the day shift with the weekday.

## 3. Nakshatra: the Moon's star
The sky is divided into 27 nakshatras of 13 degrees 20 minutes each, such as Ashwini, Rohini and Pushya. The nakshatra is the star group the Moon is in. Your birth nakshatra (janma nakshatra) is used for naming, matching and many vratams.

## 4. Yoga
A yoga is found by adding the longitudes of the Sun and Moon and dividing the total into 27 parts. There are 27 yogas, such as Siddhi and Vyatipata. Some are considered favourable and some are avoided for starting new work.

## 5. Karana
A karana is half a tithi, so there are 60 in a month. Of the eleven karanas, seven repeat in a cycle. One of them, **Vishti** (Bhadra), is traditionally avoided for new beginnings.

## The timings around the five limbs
Almost every panchangam also lists:
- **Sunrise and sunset** for the place. Every other timing is measured from them.
- **[Rahu Kalam](/rahu-kalam-today/), Yamagandam and Gulika Kalam:** periods of about 90 minutes traditionally avoided for starting new work.
- **Abhijit Muhurtham:** a window around midday traditionally good for beginning things.
- **[Brahma Muhurtham](/brahma-muhurtham-time-and-meaning/):** the pre-dawn period for prayer and study.

## How to use it
1. Check your city's timings, since sunrise changes with location. The [panchangam page](/panchangam/) shows them for your place.
2. For a routine task, avoid Rahu Kalam if you can.
3. For an important ceremony such as a wedding or housewarming, ask your family priest, who also looks at your horoscope, not just the day.

*Traditions vary by region and family. These are guides, not rules.*
