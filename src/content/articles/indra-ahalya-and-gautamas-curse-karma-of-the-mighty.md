The Bala Kanda of the Ramayana tells the story to Rama and Lakshmana on the way to Mithila, at the site of Gautama's old hermitage.

## The story
Ahalya, the wife of sage Gautama, was known for her beauty. Indra, king of the gods, came to the hermitage in Gautama's form while the sage was away at his morning ritual. Gautama returned and found him, and cursed them both.

In the Valmiki Ramayana, Indra is cursed to lose his manhood, which the gods repaired afterwards, and Ahalya is cursed to live unseen, fasting and doing penance, until Rama comes to the hermitage. When Rama arrives, she is freed and rejoins Gautama. Later retellings, especially the Puranas, add details, including Indra's body becoming marked with a thousand signs that turned into eyes. The details vary, but the structure is the same.

## What the story teaches
- **High rank gives no protection from consequences.** Indra was the king of the gods. The story does not excuse him for it.
- **Karma is tied to intention.** Indra acted by deception. Ahalya's role is read differently in different tellings, and her long penance and eventual release is the story's central picture of repentance.
- **There is a way back.** The curse is not final. After a long period of penance, Ahalya is freed. The tradition treats her as one of the five virtuous women to remember every morning (Panchakanya).
- **Rama's arrival is grace.** Release comes both from effort and from something beyond effort.

## For today
Rank, wealth and authority tempt people to believe the rules do not apply to them. The story says they do. And if you have made a serious mistake, the way back is a long, quiet effort, and it exists.
