A worried message arrives in many inboxes. I woke up late and had no bath. Can I pray? I'm travelling and cannot cook. Can I offer something? I ate onion yesterday. Is my prayer spoiled? I wore leather. Did I offend God?

These worries come from a good place, a wish to be respectful. But they can also grow into anxiety.

## What the tradition values
There is no doubt that Hindu practice values cleanliness. A bath before puja, clean clothes, a clean place of worship, hands washed before cooking for the deity: they are ways of preparing the mind. When you wash and dress with attention, you are telling yourself that something important is about to happen.

For a formal ritual such as a puja with offerings, many families keep these rules, and it makes sense to follow your family's custom.

## But there is another side
There is a verse that many Hindus know by heart and recite as part of the purification at the start of rituals:

*Apavitrah Pavitro Va Sarvavasthan Gato Api Va,*
*Yah Smaret Pundarikaksham Sa Bahyabhyantarah Shuchih.*

*Meaning:* Whether pure or impure, or in any state whatsoever, whoever remembers the lotus-eyed Lord is clean, both outside and inside.

In other words, the old tradition itself says that remembering God is never blocked by your condition.

## Putting the two together
A useful way to see it:
- **Formal puja** has its rules, and you can follow them if you like.
- **Remembering God** has no rules. You can do it any time, anywhere, in any state.

So if you woke up late, say a prayer in your heart on the way to work. If you are travelling, say a mantra in the train. If you are ill in bed, you can still remember. Nobody is turned away.

## Specific worries
- **No bath.** A silent prayer is fine. If you like, wash your face and hands.
- **Eating onion, garlic or non-vegetarian food.** Many families avoid these on festival or puja days, but they do not make prayer invalid. Follow your family's custom for festivals.
- **Leather.** Many people remove leather items in a temple. That is a courtesy of the place, not a judgement on you.
- **Travel.** Offer a mental puja. The tradition calls it *manasika puja*, a puja done in the mind, which is considered as good as an external one. You can do it by imagining the offerings.
- **Illness.** Do what you can. A prayer in bed is enough.
- **Periods.** See [our piece on periods on festival days](/periods-on-festival-days-why-it-happens-and-what-to-do/).
- **Work and shift schedules.** If you work nights, adapt the timing. Many people do a short prayer when they wake, whatever the hour.

## When it becomes a worry
For most people, these questions are small. But if you find yourself washing repeatedly, afraid to pray or constantly worried that you have made a mistake, and it takes a lot of your time or causes distress, it can be a sign of obsessive-compulsive tendencies, which are common and treatable. Please speak to a doctor or counsellor. It is not a spiritual failure.

## What matters
Teachers say that bhava, the feeling in the heart, is the main thing. Rules are the shape of the cup. The devotion is what is poured in.

For a simple way to pray, see [how to chant a mantra](/mantra-japa-how-to-start-count-and-keep-going/).

## What Krishna says about a simple offering
In the Gita, Krishna says that if someone offers him a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water with devotion, he accepts it, because it was offered with love. The emphasis is on the devotion and not on the grandness or the correctness of the offering. We look at this in [A leaf, a flower, a fruit, water](/read-the-line-gita-9-26-a-leaf-a-flower-a-fruit-water/). It is one of the gentlest answers to the worry that you have not done everything perfectly.

## A short routine for busy mornings
If you often feel you have no time to prepare properly, try this:
1. Wash your face and hands, if you can. If you cannot, skip it.
2. Stand or sit where you are, and close your eyes for a breath.
3. Say the verse above, or just the name of God you love.
4. Ask for one thing: for example, steadiness through the day.
5. Go on with your day.

That takes less than a minute, and no one can say you have done it wrongly.

## Questions people ask
**If I miss my usual puja, have I sinned?** There is no such rule in the texts that we know of. Missing a routine is not a sin. Start again the next day without guilt.

**Is a puja without a bath worthless?** For a formal puja with offerings, bathing is the family custom, and it is good to follow. For a silent prayer or a mantra in the mind, it is not required. Many elders themselves say so.

**What do I tell a relative who scolds me for these things?** You can say, politely, that you respect the custom and are doing what you can. You are not obliged to argue.

**How do I help a child who worries about these rules?** Tell them that God loves them in every state. Teach the rules as habits of care, not as threats.

## A closer look at manasika puja
The mind-puja is worth trying even when you are at home and have time. Sit quietly, imagine the deity before you, and offer in your mind a seat, water to wash the feet, flowers, a lamp and food. Do each slowly, one at a time. Many people find that it brings more attention than the outward ritual does on a rushed day.
