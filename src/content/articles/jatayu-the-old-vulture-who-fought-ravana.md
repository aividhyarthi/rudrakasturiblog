**Source:** Valmiki Ramayana, Aranyakanda (chapters 14, 49, 50 and 67 to 68). The Lepakshi connection is a local tradition.

Imagine you are old, and you see a great wrong being done by someone ten times stronger than you. You know you cannot win. Do you try?

That is the story of Jatayu.

## The old friend at Panchavati
When Rama, Sita and Lakshmana were settling at Panchavati on the Godavari, a huge vulture came up to them and introduced himself (Aranyakanda 14). He said he was Jatayu, the son of Aruna, who is the brother of Garuda, and a friend of Rama's father Dasharatha. He offered to guard Sita when the brothers were away. Rama, who had lost his father, embraced him as he would an elder.

So when Ravana came in his flying chariot and seized Sita, there was one old bird in the forest who recognised what was happening.

## The fight
Jatayu woke and called out to Ravana: that he was an old bird, but that he would not stand by while this was done. He fought. The Ramayana tells us that for a time he tore at the chariot and wounded Ravana. But Ravana was in the prime of his powers, and he cut off Jatayu's wings. The old bird fell to the ground.

Sita, in the sky, watched all of this. It was the first thing in her captivity that told her someone had tried.

## What happened next
When Rama and Lakshmana found him, Jatayu was still alive. With the last of his strength he told them that Ravana had taken Sita, and in which direction. Then he died with his head in Rama's lap.

Rama's response is one of the most affecting in the epic. He tells Lakshmana to gather wood, and he performs the funeral rites himself, with water offered and mantras said, as for a father (Aranyakanda 68). A bird, in the middle of a forest, had a prince as his chief mourner. The Ramayana says that Rama gave him the status of a family elder.

## Why did he do it?
Jatayu had every reason not to. He was old, he was outmatched, and he had no obligation to a stranger's wife. The Ramayana gives no speech to explain it. The old bird saw something wrong, and acted.

The tradition says that it was *dharma*, but not the kind that needs a large argument. It was the kind that sees what is in front of it. And, as the text says, he gave Rama the first clue to find Sita, so his fight was not in vain.

## Lepakshi
In Anantapur district of Andhra Pradesh stands the village of Lepakshi, famous for its Veerabhadra temple, its hanging pillar and the huge Nandi. According to local tradition, this is where Jatayu fell, and where Rama touched him and said *Le pakshi*, "rise, bird". This explains the name of the village. It is a tradition rather than a line in Valmiki, but it shows how the story lives in local memory.

## What it teaches
- **You do not have to win to act rightly.** Jatayu lost the fight and still did what mattered.
- **Small acts leave useful clues.** His last words set Rama on the road.
- **Honour belongs to what a person does, not to what they are.** A bird received a prince's funeral.
- **Old people have a great deal of courage that no one notices.**
