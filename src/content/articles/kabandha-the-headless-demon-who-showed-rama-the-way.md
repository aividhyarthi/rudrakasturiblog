**Source:** Valmiki Ramayana, Aranyakanda (chapters 69 to 73).

After Jatayu's funeral, Rama and Lakshmana go south in search of Sita. They are tired, grieving and lost. And in the middle of a dense forest, something seizes them.

## The monster
It is a creature unlike any they have seen: huge, headless, with a single eye in its chest and a mouth in its belly, and arms several miles long that sweep through the forest, catching everything that passes. This is Kabandha. He grabs the two brothers and begins to drag them toward his mouth.

Lakshmana, seized, tells Rama to offer him to the monster and escape, so that Rama can find Sita and rule. Rama refuses, and tells him to be calm. Together they draw their swords and cut off Kabandha's arms.

## The curse and its end
The monster, now in pain, asks who they are. When he hears the name Rama, he is overjoyed. He tells them his story. He had once been a handsome being named Danu, a son of Shri. He had made a mistake: he took the form of a demon to frighten a sage called Sthulashira, and was cursed to stay in that form. Another version of the story adds that Indra struck him and drove his head into his body. The curse would end, he was told, when Rama cut off his arms.

So, in a strange way, Kabandha had been waiting for them. He asks them to burn his body. They light a pyre, and out of the fire he rises in a radiant form, in a chariot, and tells Rama how to find his friend.

## The advice
What he tells Rama is the first real lead the brothers have received. He says:
- Go to **Sugriva**, a vanara king exiled by his brother Vali, who lives on the hill of **Rishyamukha** near Lake **Pampa**.
- Make friends with him *before the fire*, that is, with a formal pact.
- Sugriva knows every corner of the earth and will help in the search.
- On the way, visit the ashram of **Matanga**, where the old woman Shabari lives. (Aranyakanda 73)

So the monster who attacked them is the one who sends them on their path. Without Kabandha, there is no Sugriva, no Hanuman, no army. The shortest road in the epic goes through the ugliest face.

## What it teaches
- **Help can arrive in terrifying forms.** Rama was in no doubt that the thing grabbing him was an enemy, and it turned out to be the person who knew the way.
- **Every curse has an end.** The tradition insists, again and again, that no condition lasts forever.
- **Do not judge the road by its first obstacle.** What attacks us is sometimes what frees us.
- **People in disguise are waiting to be recognised.**

## Where to think of it
Pampa is the lake and river at Hampi in Karnataka, which gave Hampi its name. The Anjanadri hill, said to be Hanuman's birthplace, and Rishyamukha are close by, and the whole landscape is part of the Kishkindha of the story.
