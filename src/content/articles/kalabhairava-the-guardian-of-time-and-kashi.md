Ask anyone who has been to Varanasi about the city's guardian and they will say Kal Bhairav. Nobody enters the city without paying respects to him, they say, because he is the kotwal, the police chief of Kashi.

## Who is he?
Kalabhairava, also just Bhairava, is a fierce form of Shiva. *Bhairava* is often explained as the one who is beyond fear or the one who makes fear itself afraid. *Kala* means both time and death. He is the lord of time, who guards the cycle of birth and death.

## The story
The Puranas tell the story in more than one way. In the most common telling, Brahma, who had five heads, grew proud and claimed to be supreme. Shiva created Bhairava from his anger, and Bhairava cut off Brahma's fifth head.

The skull stuck to his hand as the mark of the sin of killing a Brahmin. Bhairava wandered the world with it, begging, until he reached Kashi, where the skull fell from his hand and he was freed. He stayed there as the guardian. We told the longer version in [our karma story on Shiva and the skull](/shiva-brahmahatya-and-the-skull-karma-of-the-lord/).

## What he looks like
Bhairava is shown dark and fierce, with a trident, a damaru drum, a skull cup and a dog beside him. The dog is his vehicle, and many families feed dogs on his days. His fierce look is protective. To his devotees he is the one who watches the door.

## When people worship him
The special day is **Kalashtami**, the Ashtami of the dark fortnight each month. The Ashtami of the dark fortnight in November or December is the big one, called Kalabhairava Jayanti or Kalabhairavashtami. Sundays and Tuesdays are also his days in many traditions.

Common offerings include black sesame, oil lamps, urad dal vada and a garland of red flowers. People pray to him for protection from fear, legal trouble and bad influences, and for safe travel.

## The temples
The Kal Bhairav temple in Varanasi is the guardian of the city. The Kal Bhairav temple in Ujjain is known for the tradition of offering liquor, which the deity is believed to accept. Many Shiva temples across the South have a Bhairava shrine of their own, and in some of them the keys are traditionally handed over to Bhairava at night, as if to the guard who keeps watch till morning.

## A verse to know
The Kalabhairava Ashtakam, a set of eight verses traditionally attributed to Adi Shankaracharya, begins by describing him as the lord of Kashi, worshipped by the gods. If you want to read his story along with Shankara's own verses, this is the one.

## A caution
You will see Bhairava worship linked with tantric practices in videos and articles online, and it makes the god sound more dangerous than most families treat him. Most families do nothing so dramatic: a lamp, black sesame, feeding a dog and a short prayer on Kalashtami is the usual form. If you are curious about stricter practices, speak to a teacher rather than copying something you read online.
