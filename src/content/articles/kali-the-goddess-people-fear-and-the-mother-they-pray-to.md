Most people feel two things when they first see Kali: a little shock and a little curiosity. Dark skin, a red tongue, a garland of skulls, a severed head in one hand. Why would anyone pray to that?

The answer is that every part of the picture is a teaching, and that her devotees see something quite different from what the picture first suggests.

## What is she?
Kali is a form of the Divine Mother, Shakti. The name comes from *kala*, which means both black and time. She is the goddess who stands beyond time, and who ends what has to end.

## Reading the picture
- **Dark skin.** Not an insult, but infinity. All colours merge into black, as all things merge into her.
- **Garland of heads.** The letters of the Sanskrit alphabet, or the cycle of births and deaths, depending on who is explaining.
- **Red tongue.** In some readings it shows her fury; in others it is shame at stepping on Shiva by mistake, which brings her back to herself.
- **The sword and the severed head.** The sword cuts away ignorance, and the head is the ego.
- **The raised hand.** Often one hand gives a boon and another says do not fear. She frightens the evil and protects the devotee.

## Why she stands on Shiva
The story says that after Kali slew the demons, her fury would not stop, and the world began to shake. Shiva lay down in her path. When she stepped on him, she realised what she had done, and stopped.

Philosophically, the picture says that power without awareness is destructive. Shiva is the stillness; Kali is the force. You need both.

## The story of Raktabija
The Devi Mahatmya tells of a demon named Raktabija, every drop of whose blood became another demon. The goddess Durga could not win until Kali drank the blood before it fell. Kali is the part of the Divine that goes where others cannot.

## The mother
What often surprises visitors is how tender her devotion is. The great saint Ramakrishna Paramahamsa served her as the priest of the Dakshineswar temple near Calcutta. He sang to her, begged and wept for her vision, and called her Ma, mother. The Bengali poet-saints, such as Ramprasad Sen, wrote songs that scold her like a child scolds a mother who has been away too long.

## Kali in the Telugu country
The Bhadrakali temple near Hanamkonda in Warangal is among the oldest and best-loved Shakti temples in Telangana. It sits on a hill by a lake and is crowded during Navaratri and Ashadha. In Andhra and Karnataka, Kali is often worshipped as a village guardian under local names.

## When people worship her
Amavasya, the new moon, is traditionally her night, and so are Fridays and Navaratri. Kali Chaudas, the day before Deepavali Amavasya, is also linked to her in some regions.
