Most great temples show you a face. Kamakhya does not. On a hill above the Brahmaputra, in Guwahati, the goddess is worshipped as a rock.

## Where it is and why it matters
The temple stands on Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, Assam. It is one of the Shakti Peethas, the sites where, in the story, parts of Sati's body fell to earth. In the most widely told account, Shiva carried Sati's body in grief after she gave up her life in her father's yagna, and Vishnu's discus cut it into pieces that fell in different places. Tradition counts 51 of them. Kamakhya is where her yoni, the symbol of creation, fell, which makes it one of the most important of all.

## What is inside?
There is no statue. In a small dark chamber below the main shrine, a natural stone shaped like a yoni is kept moist by a spring of water. Devotees come down narrow steps to offer flowers and water. Photography is not allowed.

The temple's roof is a beehive-shaped dome built in the 16th century by the Koch king Naranarayana, over older remains. Around the main temple there are smaller shrines for the ten Mahavidyas, the ten great forms of the goddess, including Kali, Tara, Bhuvaneshwari and Kamala.

## The Ambubachi Mela
In June, around the time the monsoon arrives, the temple is closed for three days. It is said that the goddess is going through her annual cycle, as the earth does before the rains. On the fourth day the doors open, and lakhs of pilgrims gather for the festival, including sadhus and tantric practitioners from across India. Farmers have long linked the season with the fertility of the land.

## A centre of tantra
Kamakhya is also one of the oldest centres of Shakta tantra in India. If you read about tantra in the mainstream press, you are likely to get something dramatic. In practice, most visitors come for the same reasons people go to any goddess temple: to ask for protection, for children, for health, for a way forward. You will see families, not only ascetics.

## What the pilgrims say
Talk to people who have been and the word you hear again and again is *energy*. Some say the dark chamber is overwhelming, some feel nothing in particular and some cry without knowing why. That is true of most strong places of worship. What stays with most people is the humility of the place: queues, wet steps, flowers, the sound of water, and the sense of having come to the oldest idea of the divine, the mother as the source of life.

## Visiting
Guwahati is well connected by air and rail. The temple is a short drive up the hill. Queues are very long in June and during Navaratri, so many people go on ordinary days. Check the current rules for entry and offerings with the temple administration before travelling.
