**Source:** The sthala purana (temple legend) of Kanchipuram, as told at the Ekambareswarar and Kamakshi temples. It is a tradition, not a historical record.

If you stand in front of Kamakshi in Kanchipuram, one of the first things you may notice is how still she is. She sits in a yogic posture in the sanctum, holding her sugarcane bow and flower arrows, a noose and a goad. Her face is calm. The story of how she came there is a story of resolve.

## The mango tree
According to the temple legend, Parvati came to Kanchi to do penance so that she might marry Shiva. She sat under a mango tree on the bank of the river Kampa, and made a lingam of earth, the Prithvi lingam, one of the five elemental lingams of Shiva. She worshipped it every day.

Shiva wished to test her. The tradition says that he tested her first with fire, and then with a great flood down the river Kampa. The water rose until the lingam of sand was about to dissolve.

## The embrace
Parvati threw herself over the lingam and held it to her chest, so that it would not be washed away. The marks of her bangles and her breasts are said to be visible on the lingam of Ekambareswarar to this day, which is why the lingam is traditionally not bathed with water.

Shiva, moved by her devotion, appeared and married her. The tree under which she sat is said to be the ancient mango tree in the Ekambareswarar temple, thousands of years old by tradition, said to bear four kinds of fruit from four branches that stand for the four Vedas.

## Kamakshi in her own temple
Kamakshi has her own temple a short distance from the Ekambareswarar temple. She is often named together with Meenakshi in Madurai and Visalakshi in Kashi as one of the great goddesses of the land. Her name, *kama-akshi*, is traditionally read as *the one whose eyes bring love*, or as the one who fulfils desire.

It is said that Kamakshi was once a fierce form. Tradition links Adi Shankaracharya to the temple, saying that he installed a Sri Chakra in front of her and calmed her so that she became the gentle mother that devotees know. The Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham, a monastic seat, is associated with Shankara's name. Historians treat the details with caution, but they show how deeply the goddess is part of the city.

## The city of a thousand temples
Kanchipuram is traditionally counted among the seven sacred cities of India. Pilgrims commonly visit Kamakshi, the Shiva temple of Ekambareswarar and the Vishnu temple of Varadaraja Perumal. It is also the birthplace of the famed Kanchipuram silk.

## What the story says
- **Devotion is tested.** The flood is a test, not a punishment.
- **Holding on is a form of worship.** Parvati's embrace of the sand is the image of not letting go.
- **Marriage in the Shaiva tradition is the union of power and stillness.** Shiva and Shakti are two sides of one reality.
- **A goddess may be fierce and gentle.** The tradition does not think the two are opposed.

## Visiting
The Kamakshi temple opens early and gets crowded on Fridays and during Navaratri. Dress modestly. Many families visit Ekambareswarar first, then Kamakshi. The lunar festival of Phalguna Uttiram, which marks the wedding of Shiva and Parvati, is celebrated at Ekambareswarar.
