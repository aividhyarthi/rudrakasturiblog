Vijayawada is known today for its barrage, its food and its traffic. But for the people of coastal Andhra, it is the city of the goddess on the hill.

## The hill of Indra
The temple of Kanaka Durga stands on Indrakeeladri, a rocky hill on the north bank of the Krishna river. The name, *Indrakila*, points to a tradition. The sage Indrakila, said to be a devotee of the goddess, did penance and asked her to settle on his hill. She agreed, and the hill took his name.

The Mahabharata (Vana Parva) tells us that Arjuna came to the Indrakila mountain to do penance to Shiva and obtained the Pashupata weapon, after a contest with a hunter, Kirata, who turned out to be Shiva. The temple tradition identifies the hill with that mountain.

## Durga and Mahishasura
The temple tradition says that on this hill Durga, with the powers of all the gods, killed the demon Mahishasura. The icon of the goddess in the sanctum shows her as Mahishasura Mardini, with many arms, slaying the buffalo demon with her trident. Her name, *Kanaka*, golden, speaks of her radiance. The sanctum image is said to be self-manifested.

There is also a tradition that Adi Shankaracharya visited Indrakeeladri and installed a Sri Chakra here. It is among the claims linking Shankara to temples across the South, and is held by tradition.

## Dasara in Vijayawada
The temple is at its busiest during Dasara, the nine nights of Navaratri. Each day the goddess is decorated as a different form: Bala Tripurasundari, Gayatri, Annapurna, Lalita Tripurasundari, Mahalakshmi, Saraswati, Durga, Mahishasura Mardini and Rajarajeshwari, among others. On the last day, the *teppotsavam*, the goddess is taken in a decorated boat on the Krishna river, as the hill is lit with lamps.

## The red devotees
In Karthika and Margashira, thousands of men, women and children wear red clothes and a garland, and take the *Bhavani Deeksha*, a period of discipline lasting up to 41 days. At the end they climb the hill with a bundle of offerings on their heads, in a procession that fills the streets. The sea of red is one of the sights of Vijayawada.

## What it teaches
- **Strength and radiance go together.** Her name, Kanaka, is gold.
- **A place can hold two stories.** Arjuna's penance and the goddess's victory share one hill.
- **Discipline is a form of devotion.** The Bhavani devotees give up comforts for weeks.
- **A river city has a goddess.** The temple, the river and the hill are one landscape.

## Visiting
The temple is easily reached from Vijayawada's railway station. Expect long queues during Dasara and Bhavani Deeksha periods. The climb can be done on foot, by car, or on the ghat road. Visit early. Footwear is not allowed in the temple.
