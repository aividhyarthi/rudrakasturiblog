Kanchipuram is about 70 km from Chennai, and it is the kind of town where a lane can have a Shiva temple at one end and a Vishnu temple at the other. It is counted among the seven Moksha Puris, the cities that are said to grant liberation: Ayodhya, Mathura, Haridwar, Kashi, Kanchi, Ujjain and Dwaraka.

## Four temples to start with
**Ekambareswarar.** The Shiva temple here represents the element of earth, Prithvi, among the five Pancha Bhoota temples. The lingam is made of sand. The story says that Parvati made it and worshipped it by the river, and when the river flooded, she held the lingam close to protect it. The lingam still shows the marks of her bracelets. The temple has a very old mango tree, said to be thousands of years old, with four branches representing the four Vedas, and a very tall gopuram of 11 storeys.

**Kamakshi Amman.** One of the three main Shakti temples of the south, along with Madurai and Kasi. Kamakshi's name comes from *kama* and *akshi*, the one whose eyes bring love. Tradition says that Adi Shankaracharya installed the Sri Chakra here and brought calm to a goddess who had been fierce. The goddess is seated in the lotus pose, with a sugarcane bow and a parrot.

**Varadaraja Perumal.** The Vishnu temple, which is huge, and has a hundred-pillared hall carved with a stone chain. The temple's special mystery is Athi Varadar, an old fig-wood image of Vishnu kept in a tank under water. It is brought out once every 40 years for 48 days. The last time was in 2019, when millions of people came. The next will be in 2059.

**Kailasanathar.** A smaller Shiva temple from the 8th century, built by the Pallava king Rajasimha. Made of sandstone, it is among the oldest stone temples in Tamil Nadu, and its walls are covered in detailed sculptures of Shiva and Shakti. It is less crowded than the others and a good place to see the early Dravidian style that later grew into the Chola towers.

## Silk and a long history
Kanchipuram is also known for its silk saris, woven by families of weavers who have been here for generations, and is a place to see the looms at work. Look for the Silk Mark certificate if you buy.

Historically, Kanchi was the capital of the Pallava dynasty, and a centre of learning that drew Buddhists, Jains and Hindus. The Chinese traveller Xuanzang visited it in the 7th century.

## The Kanchi Mutt
The Kanchi Kamakoti Peetham, whose heads are called Shankaracharyas, has its seat here, and many people visit it. It is one of the traditions that trace themselves to Adi Shankara. We wrote about this in [our article on the four mathas](/shankaras-four-mathas-sringeri-dwaraka-jyotirmath-puri/).

## Planning a visit
- Start early. Temples close for several hours in the afternoon.
- If you have only a day, see Ekambareswarar, Kamakshi and Varadaraja, with Kailasanathar if time allows.
- Use auto-rickshaws or hire a car for the day, and agree on a rate beforehand.
- Dress modestly and carry change for shoe stands.

Kanchi is also on the list of [Pancha Bhoota temples](/temples/pancha-bhoota-sthalams/), and the Shakti worship ties in with the [Shakti Peethas](/temples/shakti-peethas/).
