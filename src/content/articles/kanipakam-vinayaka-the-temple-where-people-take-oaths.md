There is a saying in Andhra that if you want a dispute settled, ask the other side to swear in front of Kanipakam Vinayaka. It is still said in some villages, half in jest and half in earnest. The temple is known for its truth.

## Where it is
Kanipakam is a village on the bank of the Bahuda river in Chittoor district of Andhra Pradesh, about 12 km from Chittoor town and 75 km from Tirupati. The temple is dedicated to Varasiddhi Vinayaka, Ganesha who grants boons.

## The legend of the well
The story is well known. Three brothers lived in the village: one was mute, one was blind and one was deaf. They had a small plot of land and a dry well. When the water level fell, they decided to dig the well deeper.

As they dug, a spade struck something hard. Blood began to flow from the spot. The brothers panicked. When they looked, a stone idol of Ganesha was coming out of the water. The mute spoke, the blind saw and the deaf heard. The villagers came to see, and a temple was built.

The name Kanipakam is said to come from *kani*, wetland, and *pakam*, flow, a land watered by flow of water.

## A well that never dries
The idol of Vinayaka is still said to be sitting in the well. The water around it is believed never to dry up, and devotees take it as holy water. Another popular belief is that the idol is slowly growing in size. An armour was made for it, the story goes, when the old one became too small. Whatever one makes of the claim, the belief is widely repeated.

## The tradition of oaths
Kanipakam became famous for its truth oath. In the past, when two people had a dispute that could not be settled, they would be brought to the temple. The person accused would take a bath in the pond, and swear in front of the idol that he or she was telling the truth. The belief was that a person who lied would face harm.

This custom is not part of the courts today, and nobody should use an oath to replace the legal process. But it carries a message. In the presence of Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, truth is the thing that clears the way.

## What people do
- **Offer coconuts** and break them in front of the shrine.
- **Take a dip** in the temple pond.
- **Perform the Vinayaka Chaturthi** festival, which is the largest event, with a procession for 21 days.
- **Pray before beginning a new venture**, an exam or a trip.

## Visiting
1. **Go early in the morning.** It gets very crowded on Chaturthi and Sundays.
2. **Combine it** with Tirupati, Tirumala and the Srinivasa Mangapuram temple, or the Golden Temple at Vellore, which is 90 km away.
3. **Dress modestly.**
4. **Use official counters,** and avoid agents.

## The name Varasiddhi
The deity here is called Varasiddhi Vinayaka. *Vara* means a boon, *siddhi* means accomplishment or success, and Vinayaka is a name of Ganesha that means "the leader" or "the remover of obstacles". So the name asks for a boon and success together. That is why many people come here with a plain request: help me begin this well and see it through.

## Questions people ask
**Do I have to take an oath to visit?**
No. Most visitors come for ordinary prayers, to break a coconut or to thank the Lord. The oath is part of the temple's reputation and the old tradition, not a requirement.

**Is it true the idol grows?**
It is a belief held by many devotees, and we have mentioned it as a belief. We cannot confirm it, and the temple's own explanation is the best source if you are curious.

**Why would truth matter so much at a Ganesha temple?**
Because Ganesha is the one who clears the path. A path built on a lie is full of hidden stones. Speaking truthfully is the first obstacle to remove.

## What the scriptures say about truth
Several of the old texts put truth at the centre of life. Two short teachings go well with a visit here:
- *Satyameva jayate*, "truth alone triumphs", from the Mundaka Upanishad. See [Read the line: Mundaka Upanishad, satyameva jayate](/read-the-line-mundaka-upanishad-satyameva-jayate/).
- *Satyam vada*, "speak the truth", from the Taittiriya Upanishad. See [Read the line: Taittiriya Upanishad, speak the truth, do your duty](/read-the-line-taittiriya-upanishad-speak-the-truth-do-your-duty/).

## An honest promise at home
You do not need to travel to practise what Kanipakam stands for. Some families keep a simple custom before a big step, whether a new job or a business:
1. Light a lamp before an image of Ganesha.
2. Say aloud, in your own words, what you promise to do and not to do.
3. Say it to yourself, not to impress anyone.
4. Write it on a small slip and keep it in your prayer shelf.

It is a promise to yourself, in front of the one who removes obstacles. Do not make a promise you cannot keep, and do not use it against anyone else.

## On the matter of disputes
If you are in a real dispute, a family property matter or a quarrel with a neighbour, an oath in a temple does not replace talking, mediation or the law. A truthful conversation, with an elder or a lawyer present, is the practical step. The temple can give you calm to go into that conversation.
