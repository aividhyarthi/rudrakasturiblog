A wrestling match is announced in Mathura. The king expects a public display of power. Instead, the arena becomes the place where his rule ends.

Kansa Vadh remembers the killing of Kansa by Krishna. The observance is especially associated with Mathura and the Braj region. Drik Panchang lists **Friday, 20 November 2026** as Kansa Vadh for New Delhi. Local programmes and dates should be checked against the calendar followed in Mathura.

## Why did Kansa fear Krishna?

The Bhagavata Purana tells the story of Kansa, the ruler of Mathura and the brother of Devaki. A prophecy warns him that Devaki's eighth child will be the cause of his death. Kansa responds by imprisoning Devaki and her husband Vasudeva, and by killing the children born to them.

Krishna is the eighth child. Vasudeva carries the newborn across the Yamuna and leaves him in Gokul, where he grows up among the cowherds. Kansa repeatedly sends forces to kill him, but the attempts fail.

When Krishna and Balarama are invited to Mathura, the invitation is not an act of hospitality. Kansa has arranged a wrestling contest and expects the brothers to be killed by his strongest fighters.

## What happens in the wrestling arena?

The Bhagavata Purana describes the match between Krishna and Chanura, and between Balarama and Mushtika. The women watching from the arena question the fairness of placing young visitors against powerful royal wrestlers.

Krishna defeats Chanura. Balarama defeats Mushtika. Kansa then orders further violence and tries to have the brothers arrested. Krishna leaps onto the royal platform, seizes Kansa and kills him. Balarama deals with Kansa's brothers when they rush forward.

The story does not end with the defeat of the king. Krishna and Balarama release Vasudeva and Devaki from prison and restore Ugrasena, Kansa's father, to the throne.

## What does Kansa Vadh mean?

For devotees, the episode is a story of Krishna protecting his family and ending an unjust rule. It is also remembered as the moment when the fear that had shaped Mathura for years finally breaks.

There is another detail worth noticing. The women in the arena speak against an unfair contest even before the outcome is known. Their concern is about the rules of the fight, not simply which side will win. The episode gives space to that voice too.

## How is Kansa Vadh observed?

Kansa Vadh is particularly associated with Mathura and the Braj region. Local observances may include readings or enactments of Krishna's story, temple worship and community programmes. The form is not identical in every town or household.

If you are visiting Mathura, check the local temple or district administration for the current year's programme. Do not assume that a date listed for one city automatically gives the timing of a local performance.

## The lesson in the story

Kansa's power depends on fear. He tries to control the future by imprisoning a family and attacking children. The story says that power built on cruelty cannot make itself secure.

Krishna's return to Mathura is not presented only as a personal victory. The release of his parents and the restoration of Ugrasena matter just as much. Ending an injustice also means repairing what it damaged.

## Questions people ask

**Is Kansa Vadh mentioned in the Bhagavata Purana?**

Yes. The killing is described in the Tenth Canto, Chapter 44, after the wrestling match in Mathura.

**Did Balarama also fight Kansa's wrestlers?**

Yes. The text describes Balarama defeating Mushtika, while Krishna fights Chanura and then Kansa.

**Is Kansa Vadh a major festival everywhere in India?**

No. It is especially associated with the Braj region and Mathura. Other regions may remember the episode within the wider Krishna story.
