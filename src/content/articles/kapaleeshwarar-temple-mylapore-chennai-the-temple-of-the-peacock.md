Chennai is not a city people usually think of as a temple town. But in Mylapore, there is a tank, a tower and a market, and everything runs to the rhythm of one temple.

## What the name says
*Mayil* means peacock and *ur* means place, so Mylapore is the place of peacocks. The story says that the goddess Parvati, in a moment of playfulness, once taught Shiva a lesson by turning her attention away, and he cursed her to be born as a peahen. She came to this spot and worshipped Shiva as a lingam under a punnai tree until he accepted her again. The Shiva here is Kapaleeshwarar, the Lord with the skull, and the goddess is Karpagambal. Inside the temple, a peahen worships a Shiva lingam in a sculpture.

## Old poems, newer walls
The temple is praised in the *Tevaram*, the hymns of the Nayanmars, the Shaiva saints, who lived over a thousand years ago. The present temple was mostly rebuilt in the 16th century, after the old temple, which stood closer to the sea, was damaged. The tall east gopuram, about 40 metres, is covered with colourful figures.

## A miracle in the poem
One of the best-known stories belongs to the child saint Sambandar. A devotee named Sivanesa Chettiyar had a daughter, Poompavai, who died of a snake bite. Her ashes were kept in a pot, waiting for Sambandar. When he arrived, he sang a hymn of ten verses at this temple, and the girl, according to the story, rose from the ashes alive. The verses are sung each year at the festival.

## The Panguni festival
The main festival is the Panguni Peruvizha, held in March or April. It lasts ten days, and on the eighth day the great chariot, the *ther*, is pulled through the streets. Then comes the festival's best-loved moment, the Arubathu Moovar procession, in which images of the 63 Nayanmars are carried round the four streets. Thousands of people come out to see it.

## The tank
The big temple tank, Kapaleeswarar Kulam, is a quiet place. On festival days the temple floats a decorated raft on it at night, and the whole area is lit.

## The neighbourhood
Mylapore is also a cultural district. It has old agraharam houses, the December Music Season concerts in nearby halls, the Santhome Church and the Thiruvalluvar Temple, which is said to mark the home of the poet Thiruvalluvar, the author of the Thirukkural. Early morning, with flower sellers and filter coffee, is the best time to walk through.

## Visiting
- The temple opens early and closes in the afternoon, then reopens in the evening.
- Fridays and Pradosham evenings are busy. Weekday mornings are quiet.
- Footwear stays outside, and dress should be modest.
