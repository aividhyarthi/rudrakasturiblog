We have spent two weeks with the epics, watching karma find kings, sages and even the gods. The question that every reader asks sooner or later is: if all this is so, how much is left to me?

## The five factors
The Gita answers directly in Chapter 18, verses 14 and 15. Whatever a person does, it says, five factors are at work:
1. **The seat** of action, the body and the place.
2. **The doer**, the one who acts.
3. **The instruments**: senses, mind, tools.
4. **The many kinds of effort.**
5. **Daiva**: the unseen, which is often translated as fate or the divine.

Four of the five are in our hands, in whole or in part. Only the fifth is not. The Gita does not say fate is nothing, and it does not say you are helpless. It says it is one factor among five.

## Action cannot be avoided
In Chapter 3 (verse 5), Krishna says no one can remain without action even for a moment, since the qualities of nature make everyone act. So "not choosing" is also a choice. The only question is whether the action is done with awareness.

## The course of karma is hard to understand
In Chapter 4 (verse 17), Krishna admits that the way of karma is difficult to grasp. This is an honest warning. It means we should not be sure we understand why things happen to us or to anyone else. It is a reason for humility, and against judging others for their misfortune.

## What to focus on
1. **The present action.** That is kriyamana karma, the only part you hold.
2. **The quality of the action**, not only its result: honest, careful, kind.
3. **Acceptance of what has already happened.** The arrow that has left the bow has left it.
4. **Without attachment to the fruit**, as in [Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/).
