Karna is the most debated figure of the Mahabharata: the first-born of Kunti, raised by a charioteer, the greatest of givers, and the loyal friend of Duryodhana.

## What he gave
Karna would never refuse a request made of him in the morning. He gave away his natural armour and earrings to Indra, who came in disguise as a Brahmin, though he knew it would make him vulnerable. When Kunti revealed that he was her eldest son, he refused to leave Duryodhana, who had given him honour when no one else would.

## What he carried
The epic also counts the other side. Karna, hiding his birth, learned the Brahmastra from Parashurama while pretending to be a Brahmin. When it was found out, his teacher cursed him: he would forget the mantra when he needed it most. On another occasion, he accidentally killed a Brahmin's cow, and was cursed that his chariot wheel would sink in the earth at the moment of his greatest need. Both curses came true on the last day of the war.

He also stood in the assembly when Draupadi was insulted, and spoke words of contempt. That act the epic does not forget.

## What the story teaches
- **Good qualities do not cancel actions.** Generosity and loyalty are real, and so are the costs of the other things.
- **One lie to a teacher has a long shadow.** The cost of an early deception came due on the battlefield.
- **Loyalty is not a substitute for judgement.** Karna's faithfulness to a friend did not make Duryodhana's cause right.
- **Resentment is a choice.** Karna's life was hard and unfair. The epic still holds him responsible for what he did with it.

## For today
If you are kind, do not assume that is enough, and if you were wronged, do not assume that gives permission. Each action is weighed on its own.
