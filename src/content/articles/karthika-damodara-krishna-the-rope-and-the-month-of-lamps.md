Everywhere in India, Krishna is loved for his mischief. But one story of his childhood has a special place in the month of Karthika, and it is about a rope that was too short.

## The story
Little Krishna, in Gokul, had been stealing butter again. One day his mother Yashoda was churning curd when he came for a feed, and then left to attend to milk on the stove. Krishna, annoyed, broke the pot of curd and went off to feed butter to the monkeys.

Yashoda came back, saw the broken pot and went after him with a stick. He ran, and she, a mother in a hurry, ran after him. At last she caught him, panting, and decided to tie him to a heavy wooden grinding mortar so he could not run away again.

She took a rope and began to tie it around his waist. It fell short by two fingers. She brought another rope and joined it. It was still short by two fingers. She joined another, and another, all the ropes in the house. Always two fingers short.

Then, according to the Bhagavata Purana, Krishna saw his mother's tired face, her sweat and her loosening hair. He let himself be tied. The Lord of the world, bound by a mother's love.

## The meaning of the name
*Dama* means rope, and *udara* means belly. Damodara is the one bound round the belly. In Karthika, it is this form of Krishna that is worshipped. It is called the month of Damodara.

## The two fingers
Teachers have read the missing two fingers in many ways. A common one: the Lord cannot be bound by effort, however much you work, there is always a little missing. He allows himself to be tied only by love, and when the devotee has done all they can. It is a story about grace, not about getting right.

## The mortar and the trees
There is a sequel. The child, dragging the mortar behind him, went between two *arjuna* trees. The mortar got stuck, and the trees fell. Out of them came two celestial beings, Nalakuvara and Manigriva, who had been cursed to be trees for their arrogance. They bowed to Krishna and were freed.

## What people do in Karthika
- **Offer a lamp to Damodara** every evening, in front of an image of Krishna or Vishnu. In some traditions, the lamp is offered for the whole month. It is called *deepadana*.
- **Recite the Damodarashtakam**, a hymn of eight verses that describes the rope story and ends with a prayer for devotion. It is traditionally recited each evening in the month.
- **Offer tulasi** to Vishnu.
- **Observe Ekadashi.** Both Ekadashis of the month are especially important.

## How to offer a lamp
1. In the evening, clean a small place before the picture or idol of Krishna.
2. Light a ghee or sesame oil lamp, with a cotton wick.
3. Fold your hands and say: *Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya*, or recite the Damodarashtakam.
4. Offer a flower or a tulasi leaf, and a little sugar or fruit.
5. Sit for a minute in quiet.

## A phrase to remember
The great verse of the hymn says that the child's mother ran after him, and "the Lord who is feared by Brahma and Shiva is tied by a rope." It is a statement of how love can hold what power cannot.
