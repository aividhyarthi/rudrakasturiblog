In Karthika, you hear it in every conversation: have you lit the lamp? The deepam is the soul of the month. Here is what it means and how to do it properly.

## What the lamp stands for
A lamp in Hindu worship is not just decoration. It stands for three things:
- **Knowledge**, which drives out the darkness of ignorance.
- **A witness.** The flame is lit as a witness to whatever you do before it, a prayer, a promise, a meal.
- **Warmth and welcome.** A lighted home is a home that is open to the divine and to the guest.

The prayer said while lighting a lamp is old: *Deepajyoti parabrahma, deepa sarva tamopahah*, the light of the lamp is the highest reality, the lamp removes all darkness.

## What you need
- **A clean lamp (agal):** clay, brass or silver. Clean it first.
- **Oil or ghee.** Sesame oil is traditional in the South. Ghee is offered for Vishnu and Lakshmi. Avoid cheap, smoky oils.
- **A cotton wick.** Twist it from clean cotton. Many families make wicks from raw cotton wool, or use ready-made ones.
- **A matchstick or lighter,** not the flame of another lamp.
- **A plate or tray,** to keep the lamp safe.

## How to light it
1. Wash your hands and, if possible, your feet.
2. Place the lamp on a tray before the image of the deity, and put a flower under it.
3. Pour in oil, and place the wick.
4. Light it, and fold your hands. Say the prayer above or a short mantra.
5. Offer a flower, a little sugar and a moment of silence.
6. Let the lamp burn on its own. In many homes, the lamp is allowed to go out by itself. Don't blow it out with your mouth. Gently cover it or use a flower.

## When
In Karthika, the main times are dawn and dusk. Many families light a lamp at the shrine, one at the Tulasi, and one at the doorway. On Mondays and Pournami, many light more.

## The amla (usirika) lamp
A tradition of the month, especially in the Telugu country, is the *Usirika Deepam*. A lamp is made on a halved amla (Indian gooseberry) or in a hollowed-out fruit. It is placed on a plate and lit, often at the Tulasi plant or a Shiva temple. The amla is dear to Vishnu and Lakshmi in tradition, and the month is linked with the amla tree.

Place it carefully, away from anything that can catch fire, and never leave it burning on its own. Make fresh ones each day.

## Floating lamps
In rivers and ponds, women float small lamps on leaf boats or on flowers. If you do this, use biodegradable materials such as leaf cups and clay, and collect them afterward if possible. Many rivers are suffering from plastic and metal lamps.

## Akasha Deepam
A lamp raised on a long pole, high above the house or temple, is called Akasha Deepam, the lamp of the sky. It is meant to guide ancestors and travellers, especially during Karthika.

## Safety
- Keep lamps away from curtains, sarees and dry leaves.
- Keep children and pets away.
- Never leave a lamp burning in an empty house.
- Put a metal tray under every lamp.
- Don't pour oil near the flame.

## Looking after your lamps
A lamp that is clean is a joy to light. Over a month of daily use, brass and silver lamps turn dark with oil and soot.

- **Wipe after each day.** Once the lamp has cooled, clear the old wick and extra oil with a soft cloth.
- **Wash weekly.** Many families scrub brass with tamarind, lemon or a little ash, then rinse with water. Dry well before the next use.
- **Clay lamps** can be soaked in water for a short time before the first use, which reduces how much oil they drink. Use each clay lamp for the day and replace it.
- **Keep a small box** with wicks, matches, a spoon for oil and a cloth, so lighting is quick and calm.

## A simple dawn and dusk routine
If you are new to the month, here is an easy way to begin.

1. Before sunrise, bathe or at least wash your face and hands.
2. Clean the place where the lamp will stand, and draw a small kolam or place a flower.
3. Light a single lamp. Say the verse you know, or simply a few words of thanks.
4. Sit for one minute and watch the flame.
5. At dusk, repeat the same steps. A second lamp at the doorway is a lovely addition.

Keep it small and keep it daily. A steady habit through the month is worth more than a grand effort on one day.

## Questions people ask
**Do I need a priest?**
No. This is a home practice. Anyone in the family can light the lamp.

**Can I use an electric lamp?**
If an open flame is not safe where you live, a small electric lamp placed with a prayer is better than no lamp at all. Many people also light a real lamp at a temple.

**What if the lamp goes out?**
Don't worry. Relight it calmly. A lamp that goes out in a draught is not a bad sign.

**Which lamp do I light on which day?**
Follow your family's custom. For the dates of the special days, see [Karthika Masam 2026](/karthika-masam-2026-dates-deepam-and-fasting/), and for the Monday observance, see [Karthika Somavaram](/karthika-somavaram-the-monday-fast-and-the-worship-of-shiva/).

It is the intention. A single clay lamp with a few drops of oil, lit with attention, is as good as a hundred.
