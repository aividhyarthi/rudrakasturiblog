Karthika Masam is the most lamp-filled month of the Telugu calendar. Homes, temples, rivers and tulasi platforms glow every evening, and the month is considered dear to both Shiva and Vishnu devotees.

## Karthika Masam 2026 dates
- **Starts:** Tuesday, 10 November 2026, the day after Deepavali Amavasya (8 November).
- **Ends:** Tuesday, 8 December 2026, on [Kartika Amavasya](/amavasya/december-2026/).
- **Kartika Pournami:** Tuesday, 24 November 2026. The tithi runs from late night on 23 November to the evening of 24 November. See the [Pournami timings](/pournami/november-2026/).
- **Karthika Somavaram (Mondays):** 16, 23 and 30 November, and 7 December.

Dates follow the amanta calendar used in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka and much of the south. Calendars in north India, which count months from the full moon, name this month differently.

## What families usually observe
**A bath at dawn.** Many people bathe before sunrise through the month, in a river where possible, and some do it only on Mondays.

**Lighting lamps.** The deepam is the heart of the month. A lamp is lit at dawn and at dusk in front of the home shrine and the tulasi plant. Many families also light a lamp on an amla (usirika) fruit or leaf, or float a lamp in a river or pond. Some raise a lamp on a pole, called akasa deepam, above the house.

**Karthika Somavaram fast.** Mondays are special for Shiva. Many people keep a fast until the evening, visit a Shiva temple, and break the fast after lighting a lamp. Fasting is a personal choice. Children, elders, pregnant women and anyone with a health condition should not fast, and a fruit-and-milk fast is perfectly acceptable.

**Reading the Karthika Puranam.** The text is traditionally read one chapter a day across the thirty days of the month.

**Vana bhojanam.** On a Sunday or another day of the month, communities share a meal under an amla tree, a custom that is especially popular in the Telugu states.

## Kartika Pournami and Kartika Deepam
Kartika Pournami is the high point of the month. Lamps are lit in great numbers at temples and by rivers, and the day is also known as Dev Deepawali and Tripuri Purnima. The Kartika Deepam festival, famous at Tiruvannamalai, falls close to this date. The exact day depends on the Krittika nakshatra, so check the temple's own announcement before you plan a visit.

## Which temples draw crowds
Shiva temples such as [Srisailam](/temples/srisailam-mallikarjuna-temple/) and Vemulawada see their biggest crowds of the year on Karthika Mondays and Pournami, so plan travel and darshan well ahead.
