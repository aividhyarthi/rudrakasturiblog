If you want to understand why Krishna is loved by so many different kinds of people, start by noticing that he is three different people in one life.

## The child of Gokul
The first Krishna is a baby who steals butter. You know the pictures. A pot, a crawling child, butter all over his face and a mother pretending to be angry.

Why does this image matter so much? Because it is the most approachable form of God there is. You do not fear a naughty child. You laugh, you scold him a little and you love him. Many South Indian homes keep a small Bala Krishna idol for exactly this reason, and some feed him first before eating their own meal.

The flute belongs to the same Vrindavan years. The stories say that cows, birds and the gopis all stopped to listen. The music is a picture of the call that draws the mind toward God.

## The king of Dwaraka
When Krishna grows up he leaves Vrindavan, defeats the tyrant Kamsa in Mathura and later builds the city of Dwaraka on the western coast. Now he is a king and a strategist, a man who gets things done.

He is also the friend of the Pandavas. In the Mahabharata he does not fight, because he promised not to take up arms, but he drives Arjuna's chariot and guides the Pandavas through the war. The epic does not hide that some of his advice was hard, and that people argue about it to this day.

## The teacher on the battlefield
The third Krishna is the one in the Bhagavad Gita. On the field of Kurukshetra, Arjuna puts down his bow, overwhelmed and unable to act. Krishna spends eighteen chapters talking him back to his feet.

What he teaches is surprisingly practical: do your duty without being ruled by the result, keep your mind steady in success and failure, and offer your actions to God. The famous line is in [Chapter 2, verse 47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/).

## How the three fit together
People sometimes ask which one is the real Krishna. The tradition's answer is that all of them are. The child shows that God can be loved like family. The king shows that God works in the world, not away from it. The teacher shows that God also speaks to the confused mind. Each of us tends to meet him in the form we need.

## Where Krishna is worshipped in the South
Udupi in Karnataka has the famous Krishna temple founded by the saint Madhvacharya, where the idol is seen through a window with nine holes. Guruvayur in Kerala is called the Dwaraka of the South. Many Telugu homes celebrate Janmashtami, which falls on the dark Ashtami of Shravana, with little footprints drawn on the floor leading to the shrine.
