If you have ever sat with an astrologer over a horoscope and heard the words *sarpa dosha*, there is a good chance the next sentence was, "go to Kukke." It is one of the best-known temples in South India for this reason, and also one of the most misunderstood.

## Where it is
Kukke Subramanya temple is in Subramanya village in Sullia taluk, Dakshina Kannada district of Karnataka, in the Western Ghats. It stands beside the river Kumaradhara, in thick green hills, a few hours by road from Mangaluru and about 280 km from Bengaluru.

## Why snakes, in a Subramanya temple?
Subramanya, whom Tamil devotees call Murugan, is closely linked in many traditions with snakes. At Kukke he is worshipped as the lord of serpents. The local story says he came here after defeating the demon Tarakasura and washed his spear in the Kumaradhara. The serpent king Vasuki, it is said, was given refuge in a cave nearby, in the presence of Garuda, and Subramanya protected him.

The result is a place where the god and the snake are one image. Devotees do not see the serpent as an enemy but as a sacred being.

## What is sarpa dosha?
In astrology, it is a belief that certain positions of the nodes Rahu and Ketu in a person's horoscope, or harm done to snakes in this life or earlier lives, bring obstacles. The problems usually named are delay in marriage, difficulty in having children, repeated failures or skin problems.

It is important to say plainly that this is a belief tradition. There is no scientific evidence that a chart can show such a thing, and a person who has been told they are afflicted should not accept fear. Delay in marriage or difficulty with children has many ordinary causes and, in the case of fertility, treatments. Please see a doctor for health problems.

For people who do hold the belief, Kukke is where they go to settle it.

## What people do there
- **Ashlesha Bali.** A ritual done on the Ashlesha nakshatra day, the star associated with serpents, to propitiate the serpent deities. It takes place at fixed times.
- **Sarpa Samskara.** A rite meant to atone for harm to snakes.
- **Naga Prathishta.** Installing a stone image of a serpent.
- **Regular worship** of Subramanya with milk, flowers and lamps.

The temple has a large annual festival, Champa Shashti, in the month of Margashira, when Subramanya's chariot is pulled through the streets.

## How to visit
1. **Go through the official temple office** or website for booking. The temple publishes rates and timings.
2. **Avoid touts.** Outside any popular temple, middlemen sell packages at high prices. Ask at the temple counter.
3. **Follow the dress code.** Men are asked to remove shirts in certain areas, a common rule in coastal Karnataka temples.
4. **Plan for crowds** on weekends, Shashti days and holidays.
5. **Check the nearest accommodation.** The temple and the village have many lodges, and the temple offers free meals.
6. **Combine it** with the nearby Kumara Parvatha trek and the temple of Dharmasthala if you want a longer trip.

## Other temples for the same belief
- **Mopidevi** in Krishna district, Andhra Pradesh, a well-known Subramanya temple where devotees worship snake images, especially around Nagula Chavithi.
- **Srikalahasti** in Andhra Pradesh, for Rahu-Ketu worship (see [our guide](/srikalahasti-the-rahu-ketu-temple-and-the-story-of-kannappa/)).
- **Thirunageswaram** in Tamil Nadu, a Rahu temple.

For the god himself, see [Murugan, Kartikeya, Subramanya](/murugan-kartikeya-subramanya-one-god-many-names/).

## A balanced word
Many people go to Kukke out of fear, and come back with a sense of calm. For some, the visit is simply a way of saying, "I did what tradition asks." That is a good outcome. If you are anxious about a horoscope, remember that astrology is a belief, that you are free to choose, and that kindness to living beings is the best practice of all.

*Beliefs and customs differ from family to family and temple to temple. Check timings, rules and costs on the temple’s official channels before you travel, and be careful with agents who offer to arrange rituals for large sums.*
