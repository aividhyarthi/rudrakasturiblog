Can a punishment be fair if the person receiving it does not remember committing the offence? The Mahabharata raises this difficult question through Mandavya Maharishi, a sage who suffers a terrible punishment and then questions the very authority responsible for justice.

## The sage who was wrongly punished

Mandavya was a sage who spent his time in spiritual practice. One day, thieves fleeing from the king's men hid their stolen goods near his hermitage. The guards found the goods and assumed that the sage was involved.

Mandavya remained silent, absorbed in his practice. The king's men took him to the court, and he was punished by impalement. The sage survived this terrible ordeal, and the truth eventually came to light.

## Mandavya questions Yama

After the king recognised the mistake and sought forgiveness, Mandavya turned to Yama, the lord associated with justice and death. He asked why he had been made to suffer such a severe punishment.

Yama connected the suffering to an act from Mandavya's childhood. The sage challenged the fairness of assigning such a grave consequence to an action performed when he was too young to understand its moral weight.

## A difficult question about karma

The episode is important because it does not offer a simplistic explanation that every suffering person must have deserved it. Instead, it presents a debate about intention, age, knowledge and proportionate consequences.

Mandavya's challenge is remembered in the tradition as a turning point in how responsibility should be understood. The story also connects Yama's earthly birth as Vidura with the consequences of the episode.

## What the story teaches

Karma is often discussed as if it were a simple system in which every event has an obvious explanation. The Mandavya episode asks us to be more careful. It raises questions about intention, understanding and justice.

It also warns against judging another person's suffering from the outside. We do not know the full story of another life.

## The deeper meaning

Mandavya Maharishi does not accept authority without question. His story reminds us that even a question about divine justice can be part of a serious spiritual tradition.

For readers, the lesson is to act with care, avoid cruelty and remember that justice must consider intention and understanding.

### Who was Mandavya Maharishi?

### Why did Mandavya question Yama?

He questioned why a severe punishment had been linked to an act committed during childhood.
