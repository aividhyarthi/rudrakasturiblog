A mother is sitting with her son's horoscope and a matching form for a prospective bride. The astrologer frowns at one line and says a single word: Mangal. Suddenly the room goes quiet. If you have ever been in that room, or known someone who was, you know how heavy one word can feel.

This article is partly about that word. But first, who is Mangala, and why do we honour him? Let us start with the planet himself, and then come back to the worry gently.

## Who is Mangala?

Mangala is the third of the nine grahas in the common order, and the planet we call Mars. His names are many: Angaraka, meaning the one with the colour of glowing coals; Kuja, the one born of the earth; Sevvai in Tamil; Mangaludu or Kujudu in Telugu (అంగారకుడు). In temple images he is red, often seated on a ram, holding a spear or trident and a mace.

The word mangala itself means auspicious, which tells you something. The tradition did not set out to fear this planet. It saw in him a fiery, protective, driven force, one that needs to be pointed toward good use.

## What Mangala is said to govern

Tradition associates Mars with:

- courage, boldness and the willingness to act
- energy, stamina and competitive spirit
- land, property and building
- brothers, especially younger brothers
- the soldier, the protector, the one who stands at the door

These are symbols, not forecasts. A strong Mars in a chart is described as a person with drive; whether that drive becomes patience or anger depends on the person, which is something the chart cannot decide.

## Day, colour, grain and mantra

- **Day:** Tuesday, Mangalavaram in Telugu, Sevvai-k-kizhamai in Tamil.
- **Colour:** red.
- **Grain:** toor dal or red gram (thuvaram paruppu in Tamil, kandipappu in Telugu).
- **Flower:** red flowers such as red hibiscus or arali.
- **Mantra:** Om Angarakaya Namah, or Om Mangalaya Namah.
- **Deity linked with him:** Kartikeya, Subramanya or Murugan, and also Hanuman in many homes, since Tuesday is a day for both.

## The link with Kartikeya

Many families connect Mangala with Murugan, the red-hued warrior son of Shiva, who is also called Kartikeya and Skanda. Both are linked with courage and with the power to protect. If you want to know more about this god, our article on [Murugan, Kartikeya and Subramanya](/murugan-kartikeya-subramanya-one-god-many-names/) explains why one deity carries so many names.

Tuesday is therefore a natural day to light a lamp before Murugan or Hanuman. This is a family custom, not a rule, and it varies by region.

## Angaraka Sankashti

When Sankashti Chaturthi, the fourth day after the full moon, falls on a Tuesday, it is called Angaraka Sankashti. In many families it is considered especially auspicious for praying to Ganesha. The two are said to meet there: Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, on the day of the red planet. If you want to see how a Sankashti is usually observed, our note on [Sankashti Chaturthi, its moonrise and vrat](/sankashti-chaturthi-october-2026-date-moonrise-and-vrat/) covers the steps.

Notice what the tradition is really pointing at. Courage is not the same as anger. A protector stands between a child and a danger without raising his voice. When you pray to Mangala, you might ask yourself: where in my life do I need steady courage, and where am I only being loud? Only you can answer that, and it is a better question than any chart can ask.

## The Angaraka sthalam

Among the nine Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam, Mangala's place is Vaitheeswaran Koil, in Tamil Nadu, where the main deity is Shiva as Vaidyanatha, the divine healer, and Angaraka is worshipped in a shrine there. Tradition holds that people go there on Tuesdays. Our guide to [the nine Navagraha temples in order](/navagraha-temples-near-kumbakonam-the-nine-in-order/) shows where it falls in the pilgrimage.

## About Mangal dosha, spoken gently

Now let us return to that quiet room. Mangal dosha, also called Kuja dosha, is a notion in which Mars standing in certain houses of a birth chart is thought by some astrologers to affect marriage. Many families worry, and the worry is real.

Here is what can honestly be said. Astrologers differ a great deal. Some say the dosha applies only in particular combinations; some say it cancels in many cases; some give it little weight at all; and many modern astrologers think it has been overused. The rules differ between regions and between teachers. Because of that disagreement, no one should use Mangal dosha to frighten a young person or their parents, or to push them into a costly ritual, a quick marriage, or a rejection of a good match.

If a match is being considered, a calm conversation with an elder you trust, and with the people involved, is worth more than any single line in a chart. Marriage rests on how two people treat each other. If the chart matters to your family, ask a respected astrologer, ask what they base it on, and do not feel bound to pay for anything.

## A simple practice for Tuesday

You need no money to honour Mangala. On a Tuesday morning:

1. Light a small lamp before whichever form you pray to, Murugan, Hanuman or Ganesha.
2. Say Om Angarakaya Namah, or simply the name of your deity, eleven times.
3. Do one brave small thing you have been avoiding: a difficult conversation, a task you keep postponing.

That last one may be the truest worship of Mars. Courage is exercised, not bought.
