Kashi is a city where prayer, daily life and the presence of death sit close to one another. At Manikarnika Ghat, the Ganga flows beside one of Hindu tradition's most important cremation grounds. The same sacred landscape is also connected with pilgrimage bathing, the worship of Shiva and Vishnu, and stories about the origin of the ghat.

Drik Panchang's Varanasi calendar lists **Monday, 23 November 2026** for Manikarnika Snan, on Kartika Shukla Chaturdashi. This confirms the calendar observance, not the availability of a particular bathing area or temporary access arrangements. Check local notices before visiting.

## What is Manikarnika Ghat?

Manikarnika Ghat stands on the western bank of the Ganga in Varanasi. It is widely known as a major Hindu cremation ground, but it is not only that. The ghat has also been recognised as a *tirtha*, a sacred place of pilgrimage, with areas connected to bathing, worship and ritual offerings.

The Kashi district portal identifies it as one of the city's significant ghats. The Incredible India description also notes its dual association with bathing and cremation. The northern section is associated with bathing activity, while the southern area is used for cremation. Actual access and use can be affected by local arrangements.

## The story of Parvati's lost earring

One well-known legend explains the name Manikarnika through an earring belonging to Goddess Parvati. In this account, Vishnu creates or prepares a sacred pool near the place where Shiva and Parvati come to bathe. During the visit, Parvati's earring falls into the water.

The word *mani* can mean a jewel, while *karnika* refers to the ear ornament. The place becomes known as Manikarnika because of the lost earring. The nearby pond is remembered as Manikarnika Kund.

The story is told in more than one form. The Kashi district portal refers to a tradition in which Sati's earring falls here, while the Incredible India account describes Parvati's earring falling into the pool prepared by Vishnu. These are sacred explanations of the place's name, not historical records of a single event.

## Vishnu's connection with the sacred pool

Another part of the local sacred geography connects Manikarnika with Vishnu's devotion. The tradition remembers Vishnu performing austerities in Kashi and leaving his footprints near the kund. Some accounts say that the pool was created through his effort and that Shiva and Parvati visited it.

This connection is significant because Kashi is strongly associated with Shiva, yet its sacred stories also give Vishnu an important place. The city's religious life has long included Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions, shrines and pilgrimage routes.

The Skanda Purana's Vaikuntha Chaturdashi account also places Vishnu at Manikarnika before he goes to worship Shiva with a thousand lotuses. This is another reason the ghat appears in stories connecting the two deities.

## Why is Manikarnika associated with liberation?

Kashi is often described in Hindu tradition as a place where the boundary between life and death is understood differently. A widely held belief says that Shiva grants the *Taraka Mantra* to those who die in Kashi, helping the soul move beyond the cycle of rebirth.

This is a matter of religious faith, not a claim that can be measured or guaranteed. For devotees, the belief gives the city a special place in the journey towards *moksha*, or liberation.

Manikarnika's cremation activity makes this belief especially visible. The ghat is not treated only as a place of death. It is part of a larger understanding of the body, the soul, impermanence and the hope of liberation. Visitors should approach these beliefs with respect, whether or not they share them.

## What does Manikarnika Snan mean?

A *snan* is a ritual bath. In Hindu pilgrimage, bathing in a sacred river or at a recognised tirtha is often an act of purification, prayer and remembrance. Manikarnika Snan is listed in the Kartika calendar as a specific observance at this important Kashi site.

The meaning is not simply physical cleanliness. The bath is accompanied by an intention: to remember the Divine, offer gratitude and reflect on the temporary nature of life. Ritual details vary by family and local tradition.

A calendar listing does not mean that every part of the ghat is available for bathing. Visitors should use only areas designated by local authorities and ghat staff.

## Visiting Manikarnika respectfully

Manikarnika is an active cremation ground. The ceremonies taking place are private moments of grief and religious duty for families. Visitors should not treat them as a tourist attraction.

Follow local police, ghat staff and temple instructions. Do not photograph or film grieving families or cremation rites. Do not obstruct the movement of mourners, priests or workers. Use only designated bathing areas and follow safety advice around the river. Avoid leaving plastic, flowers wrapped in plastic or other waste in the Ganga.

The purpose of a pilgrimage is respect. A camera or social media post should never come before the dignity of people who are grieving.

## Questions people ask

**Is Manikarnika Snan the same as Kartika Purnima snan?**

No. The Varanasi calendar lists Manikarnika Snan on 23 November 2026 and Kartika Purnima on 24 November. They are separate calendar observances.

**Can anyone visit Manikarnika Ghat?**

The ghat is a public religious site, but visitors must respect cremation activity, local restrictions and the instructions of the authorities.

**Why is Manikarnika important to both Shiva and Vishnu devotees?**

Its sacred stories connect the site with both deities. The ghat is often described as a meeting point of Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions.
