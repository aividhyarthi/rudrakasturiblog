Kashi is a city where prayer, daily life and the presence of death sit close to one another. At Manikarnika Ghat, the Ganga flows beside one of Hindu tradition's most important cremation grounds. The same place is also connected with pilgrimage bathing and sacred stories about Shiva and Vishnu.

Drik Panchang lists **Monday, 23 November 2026** as Manikarnika Snan in the Varanasi calendar, on the day of Vaikuntha Chaturdashi. The date should be checked against the local panchang and any instructions issued by the city administration.

## What is Manikarnika Ghat?

Manikarnika Ghat stands on the western bank of the Ganga in Varanasi. It is widely known as a major Hindu cremation ground, but it is not only that. The ghat also has a history as a pilgrimage site, with nearby places connected to bathing, worship and ritual offerings.

The Kashi district portal describes it as one of the city's most significant ghats. It is associated with the belief that Goddess Parvati's earring, or *mani-karnika*, fell here. The nearby Manikarnika Kund is part of this sacred geography.

## The stories behind the name

There are several traditional explanations for the name and sanctity of the place. One account says that Vishnu created a sacred pool for Shiva and Parvati. During their visit, an earring belonging to Parvati fell there. Shiva searched for it, and the place came to be known as Manikarnika.

Other tellings connect the site with Vishnu's austerities and with Shiva's presence in Kashi. These are sacred narratives, not historical descriptions of a single event. They explain why the place has such importance in the religious imagination of the city.

## Why is the ghat associated with liberation?

Kashi is often described in Hindu tradition as a place where the boundary between life and death is understood differently. A widely held belief says that Shiva grants the *Taraka Mantra* to those who die in Kashi, helping the soul cross beyond the cycle of rebirth.

This is a matter of religious faith, not a claim that can be measured or guaranteed. For devotees, the belief gives the city a special place in the journey towards moksha.

The ghat is also a meeting point of Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions. Vishnu's footprints and nearby shrines are part of the sacred landscape, while Shiva is worshipped as Tarakeshvara and in other forms.

## What does Manikarnika Snan mean?

A *snan* is a ritual bath. In Hindu pilgrimage, bathing in a sacred river or at a recognised tirtha is often an act of purification, prayer and remembrance. Manikarnika Snan is listed in the Kartika calendar as a specific observance at this important Kashi site.

The meaning is not simply physical cleanliness. The bath is accompanied by an intention: to remember the Divine, to offer gratitude and to reflect on the temporary nature of life. Ritual details vary by family and local tradition.

## Visiting Manikarnika respectfully

Manikarnika is an active cremation ground. Visitors should remember that the ceremonies taking place are private moments of grief and religious duty for families.

- Follow local police, ghat staff and temple instructions.
- Do not photograph or film grieving families or cremation rites.
- Do not obstruct the movement of mourners, priests or workers.
- Use only designated bathing areas and follow safety advice around the river.
- Avoid leaving plastic, flowers wrapped in plastic or other waste in the Ganga.

The purpose of a pilgrimage is respect. A camera or a social media post should never come before the dignity of people who are grieving.

## Questions people ask

**Is Manikarnika Snan the same as Kartika Purnima snan?**

No. The Varanasi calendar lists Manikarnika Snan on 23 November 2026 and Kartika Purnima on 24 November. They are separate calendar observances.

**Can anyone visit Manikarnika Ghat?**

The ghat is a public religious site, but visitors must respect cremation activity, local restrictions and the instructions of the authorities.

**Why is Manikarnika important to both Shiva and Vishnu devotees?**

Its sacred stories connect the site with both deities. The ghat is often described as a meeting point of Shaiva and Vaishnava traditions.
