*Japa* is the repetition of a mantra, aloud, in a whisper or silently. It is among the simplest and oldest forms of spiritual practice, and needs only a quiet place and a few minutes.

## 1. Choose one mantra
Pick one that your family uses or that you feel drawn to, and stay with it for a while. For beginners, short name mantras work well: *Om Namah Shivaya*, *Om Namo Narayanaya*, *Om Namo Venkatesaya*, *Om Gam Ganapataye Namah*. Our [mantras page](/mantras/) lists them for each god, with meanings. Knowing the meaning helps the mind stay with it.

## 2. Choose a time and a place
The pre-dawn hour, [Brahma Muhurtham](/brahma-muhurtham-time-and-meaning/), is traditional, but any regular time works: after your bath, before sleep, or at your desk on a break. A fixed time is easier to keep than a long one.

## 3. Sit comfortably
Sit on a mat or chair with your spine straight and shoulders relaxed, ideally facing east or north. A lamp and a flower in front of you are helpful but not required.

## 4. Use a mala
A mala has **108 beads** plus one larger bead called the sumeru. Hold the mala in your right hand, rest it on your middle or ring finger and move each bead with the thumb. The index finger is traditionally not used. When you reach the sumeru, do not cross it. Turn the mala around and go back the other way. One full round is 108 repetitions.

If you do not have a mala, count on the finger-joints of one hand: each hand gives 12 or 10 counts, and a tally of rounds can be kept by a small pile of seeds.

## 5. Start small
Begin with **11 or 21 repetitions** a day, then add. Many families do 108 once a day. A steady, short practice done daily is better than a long one done once.

## 6. When the mind wanders
It will. Do not scold yourself. Notice that you drifted, and return to the next repetition. That return is the practice.

## 7. Say it with understanding
Slowly, clearly, without rushing. If you cannot pronounce a longer mantra well, use the short one. Devotion matters more than perfection.

## A 21-day plan
- Days 1 to 7: 11 repetitions each morning.
- Days 8 to 14: 21 repetitions.
- Days 15 to 21: one full round of 108.

Pair it with a few minutes of [breathing practice](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/) beforehand to settle the mind.
