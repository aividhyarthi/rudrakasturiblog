If you have heard one thing about Advaita, it is probably this: the world is an illusion. People say it with a shrug, as if it explains everything. It does not, and it is not quite what the teachers say.

## The famous line
A verse often attributed to Shankara says: *Brahma satyam, jagan mithya, jivo brahmaiva naparah.* Brahman is real, the world is *mithya*, and the individual self is none other than Brahman.

Most people translate *mithya* as false or illusory. But in Advaita, the word has a more careful meaning.

## What mithya means
Mithya is something that does not have an existence of its own and depends on something else. A wave depends on water. A gold ring depends on gold. The name and shape change, but the gold is the same. The ring is not fake. You can wear it. But it is not separate from the gold.

In the same way, the world is real enough to live in, but it depends on Brahman, the one reality that does not change.

## The rope and the snake
Shankara's classic example goes like this. You are walking at dusk and see a snake on the path. Your heart races, you jump back. Then someone brings a lamp, and you see it is a piece of rope.

Notice three things. The snake was never there. But your fear was real, and your reaction was real. And once you saw the rope, the fear disappeared, even though nothing about the rope changed.

Advaita says our ordinary experience of the world is like seeing the snake. The one reality is the rope. When we see it, the fear and clinging that come from the mistake go away.

## Two levels of truth
Advaita speaks of levels. At the ordinary level, called *vyavaharika*, the world and its rules are real. Money has value, medicine works and what you do matters. At the highest level, called *paramarthika*, only Brahman is truly real.

Shankara does not say you should ignore the first level. In fact, the whole of ethics, family and work belongs there.

## The dream
Another example is a dream. While you are dreaming, the people and places are real, and your joy and fear are real. When you wake, you know that the dream did not exist outside of you. Advaita asks whether waking life might be similar, not meaning that it does not matter, but that something deeper holds it.

## Why this matters in daily life
If you do not take the world to be the final reality, you can treat it more lightly. You still work, but you are less afraid of losing what you cannot hold anyway. It is not a recipe for giving up. It is closer to what Krishna says when he asks Arjuna to act without clinging to results, in [Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/).

## A common mistake
Some people say that if the world is illusory, then suffering is imaginary. Teachers reject that. Compassion and service are part of Advaita life. Shankara himself spent his short life working for others.

## A closer look at one verse
In the Gita, Krishna gives a line that Advaita teachers love to quote: of the unreal there is no being, and of the real there is no non-being (Gita 2.16). In plain words, what truly is cannot cease to be, and what is only a passing form does not have lasting existence. You can see why Advaita readers hear mithya in that line. Bodies, houses and seasons come and go. The awareness in which all of it appears does not.

## Questions people ask

**If the world is mithya, why did Shankara bother to teach?**
Because the person who suffers from the mistake is real at their own level. The teacher, the student and the lamp that shows the rope all belong to the ordinary level. Teaching is the lamp.

**Do all schools of Vedanta agree?**
No. Ramanuja and Madhva both disagree strongly, and hold that the world and the souls are real and are not merely dependent on Brahman in the way Advaita says. You can compare them in [Shankara, Ramanuja and Madhva](/shankara-ramanuja-madhva-three-ways-to-see-god-and-the-soul/).

**Is Maya the same as a trick or a fraud?**
Not in this teaching. Maya is the power by which the one appears as many. It is not a lie told to you. It is more like the way a single actor can play many parts and you still enjoy the play.

**Do I have to believe this to follow Hindu practice?**
No. Millions of devotees worship God with form and never study Advaita. It is one way of seeing, an old and respected one, but not the only one.

## Trying it in daily life
Here is a gentle way to test the idea without any belief at all:
1. Notice a worry that is taking over your evening.
2. Ask: what is solid here, and what is my mind adding?
3. Like the rope in the dusk, the fear may shrink once you look at it with a lamp.

This does not make real problems vanish. The bills still need to be paid. It only shows how much of our suffering is the extra story we add. For a short hymn that expresses the same freedom, see [Nirvana Shatakam](/nirvana-shatakam-i-am-not-the-mind-meaning/).
