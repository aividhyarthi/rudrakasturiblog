Most Indian cities grew around a river or a port. Madurai grew around a temple. The streets are laid out in squares around it, and the city's festivals are timed to its calendar.

## Who lives here
The main shrine is for Meenakshi, a form of Parvati, with eyes shaped like a fish, which is what her name means. Her husband Sundareswarar is a form of Shiva. In most Shiva temples the god is the first to be worshipped. Here it is the goddess. Pilgrims are traditionally supposed to see Meenakshi first, then Sundareswarar.

## The story
The Tamil tradition says that Meenakshi was born to the Pandya king Malayadwaja, who had prayed for a son. The child was born with three breasts, and a voice from heaven said the third would vanish when she met her husband. Raised as a warrior queen, she conquered the world, and then met Shiva at Mount Kailash. The third breast vanished, and she knew him. They were married in Madurai, and Sundareswarar became the king.

## The towers
The temple has fourteen big gopurams, the towers at the gateways, with the south tower being the tallest at around 50 metres. They are covered with thousands of colourful painted figures of gods, goddesses and animals, repainted every few years. The present complex was built mostly in the 16th and 17th centuries, especially under Thirumalai Nayak.

## What to see
- **The Golden Lotus Tank (Potramarai Kulam).** A square pool with steps, where pilgrims sit quietly. Tradition says that no fish live in it, because the poet's judgement of a Tamil text is said to have been thrown into it.
- **The Hall of a Thousand Pillars.** It has about 985 carved pillars and now houses an art museum.
- **The musical pillars.** Some of the pillars in the Ayirakkal Mandapam give out musical notes when tapped. Guides usually show one or two, though tapping is now limited.
- **The nightly ceremony.** Every night, an image of Sundareswarar is carried in a palanquin to Meenakshi's chamber for the Palliyarai pooja, a ritual much loved by devotees.

## The wedding festival
Chithirai Thiruvizha, in April or May, is the great festival of Madurai. It lasts about two weeks, with the coronation of Meenakshi, her conquering of the world and then the wedding, Thirukalyanam, which draws lakhs of people. The next day is the chariot procession. The festival is linked with a second event, when Lord Alagar of Alagar Koil comes from the hills to the Vaigai river, and many people see the two as one celebration.

## Planning a visit
- Early morning, before about 8, is the quietest. Evenings are crowded.
- Mobile phones are not allowed inside, and there are lockers at the gates.
- Dress modestly. Footwear is left at the entrance.
- Fridays and festival days are very busy.
- Allow about two to three hours.
