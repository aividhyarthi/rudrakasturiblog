**Source:** The Madurai sthala purana, in the *Thiruvilaiyadal Puranam* of Paranjothi Munivar and the Sanskrit *Halasya Mahatmya*. A temple legend, not a record of history.

There is a goddess in Tamil Nadu whom the tradition remembers first as a queen. She ruled a city, led armies, conquered the worlds, and only then met the god she was meant to marry. That is the legend of Meenakshi of Madurai.

## A child of the fire
Malayadhwaja Pandya, king of Madurai, and his queen Kanchanamalai had no children. They performed a great sacrificial rite to ask for a son. From the fire rose a three-year-old girl. She had three breasts, and the king and queen were troubled. A voice from heaven said: *do not be afraid. Raise her as a prince. Her extra breast will vanish when she meets her husband.*

They named her Tatatakai, and then Meenakshi, *fish-eyed*, for the long beautiful shape of her eyes.

## The queen of Madurai
When Malayadhwaja died, Meenakshi took the throne. She ruled with strength and justice, and, as the legend says, set out to conquer the world. She defeated the kings of the earth, then the guardians of the directions, and finally the gods. She marched to Kailash, the home of Shiva, where his armies, the *ganas*, were routed.

Then she saw Shiva. The legend says that as soon as she looked at him, her third breast vanished and she lowered her eyes in shyness. She knew who he was. Shiva announced that he would come to Madurai in eight days, and marry her.

## The wedding
Shiva came as Sundareswarar, the beautiful lord. Vishnu, in the role of Meenakshi's brother, gave her in marriage. The wedding of the goddess and the god was celebrated with all the pomp of the city, and Sundareswarar ruled with her as king of Madurai.

That event is the heart of the Chithirai Thirukalyanam, held every year in April or May. Hundreds of thousands of people attend the wedding and the processions, and the entire town turns out for the festival. A fascinating part of the story is that Vishnu as Kallazhagar rides from the temple at Alagar Koil to attend the wedding, but is said to arrive too late, turning back at the river Vaigai. His arrival at the edge of the city is welcomed as the *Ethir Sevai*, and he is said to turn back when he hears the wedding is over.

## The temple today
The Meenakshi Sundareswarar temple in Madurai is one of the great temples of India. Its four towering gopurams, covered in thousands of painted figures, rise over the city. Meenakshi has her own sanctum, where she stands with a parrot on her hand, and pilgrims traditionally visit her first. The thousand-pillared hall and the Potramarai Kulam, the golden lotus tank, are among the sights.

## What the story says
- **A woman can lead.** Meenakshi is the sovereign of Madurai and her husband the consort. This is a reversal that many traditions preserve.
- **Power meets its match.** She conquered everything, and then met Shiva.
- **Dharma includes the household.** The wedding is as sacred as the war.
- **Cities have goddesses.** In the Tamil country, a city is not just a place but a body of a goddess.

## Visiting
Visit early in the morning, or after sunset when the evening *palliyarai* procession takes place. Many visitors start with the Potramarai tank, then Meenakshi and then Sundareswarar. Dress modestly, and expect crowds on Fridays and during festivals.
