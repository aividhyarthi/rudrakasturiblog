Monday morning is when most of us feel the week begin. In millions of homes, it is also when something older begins: a small lamp, a few bilva leaves, five syllables said under the breath.

This is a plain guide to Monday as Shiva's day: why it is his, how to worship him at home in ten minutes, what to offer, and the special Mondays of the year.

## Why Monday?
The word for Monday in Telugu is *Somavaram*. *Soma* is the Moon, and the Moon rests on Shiva's head as a thin crescent. So Monday, the day of the Moon, became the day of Shiva. In Tamil it is *Thingal*, "the Moon day", and in Sanskrit *Somavara* or *Indu-vasara*.

You can see the reason in the picture too. Shiva is the god of stillness, of the mind brought to rest, and the Moon is the planet of the mind. Worshipping Shiva on a Monday is a way of cooling the thoughts at the start of a busy week.

## A simple Monday puja
1. **Bathe** and wear clean clothes, white if you have them.
2. **Light a lamp** at your shrine, before a lingam or a picture of Shiva.
3. **Abhishekam.** Pour water, and milk if you wish, slowly over the lingam, saying *Om Namah Shivaya*. If you have no lingam, do it in your mind in front of a picture.
4. **Offer bilva leaves.** The three-leaflet leaf of the bilva tree is dear to Shiva. Place it with the stem toward the lingam.
5. **White flowers and a little vibhuti,** the sacred ash, on your forehead and on the lingam.
6. **Chant the Panchakshari** (*Om Namah Shivaya*) 108 times, or as many as you have time for.
7. **Offer fruit,** milk or a sweet, and share it.

## What Om Namah Shivaya means
The five syllables, *na-mah-shi-va-ya*, are linked in the tradition to the five elements: earth, water, fire, air and space. The sentence means *I bow to Shiva*, and the word *Shiva* itself means *the auspicious one*. Of all mantras it is the shortest to say and one of the longest to understand. The Shiva Purana and the Rudram both honour it.

If you want more than five syllables, the [Maha Mrityunjaya mantra](/maha-mrityunjaya-mantra-word-by-word-and-what-it-really-asks/) is the next step. We have set it out word by word.

## What not to offer
Tradition says that **tulasi** leaves and **ketaki** flowers are not offered to Shiva. There are stories behind both. Tulasi is for Vishnu, and ketaki, in the tale, was made to tell a lie about Shiva's beginning and end. If you do not have bilva, offer water and flowers. A heart is the real offering.

## Fasting on Mondays
Many people keep a fast until the evening, called *Somavara vratam*, and eat after lighting the lamp at dusk. It is entirely optional. Do not fast if you are unwell, elderly, pregnant or on regular medication.

## The special Mondays
- **Karthika Somavaram.** Each Monday in the month of Karthika is especially sacred. See our [Karthika Somavaram guide](/karthika-somavaram-the-monday-fast-and-the-worship-of-shiva/).
- **Somavati Amavasya.** When the new moon falls on a Monday, it is considered very auspicious. See [Amavasya dates](/amavasya/).
- **Soma Pradosham.** When the evening Pradosham falls on a Monday, many devotees visit Shiva temples at dusk. See [Pradosham dates](/pradosham/).
- **Maha Shivaratri,** the great night of Shiva, in the month of Magha.

## Temples to remember
In the Telugu states you will find Shiva at Srisailam (Mallikarjuna), Srikalahasti (Vayu lingam), Vemulawada (Raja Rajeshwara) and Draksharamam. In Tamil Nadu: Chidambaram, Rameswaram, Tiruvannamalai. For the Five Elements temples, see [Pancha Bhoota Sthalams](/pancha-bhoota-sthalams-five-temples-five-elements/).

## What the lingam stands for
The word *linga* means a mark or a sign. The lingam is Shiva without a face: a simple rounded form that stands for the formless. It is often set on a base called the *peetha*, and the two together are read as Shiva and Shakti. If you have no lingam at home, a picture, a smooth stone from a river or a simple vibhuti mark is enough. Shiva has always been the god who does not need much.

## If you cannot go to a temple
You do not have to. A Monday at home with a lamp and the Panchakshari is a full worship. If you can, add one small act of kindness: feed a stray, give water to a bird, call someone who lives alone. Shiva is called *Bhola*, the simple one, and the old stories say he is pleased by sincerity far more than by grandness.

## A line from the Rudram
The *Namakam*, the first part of the Rudram, repeats the word *namo*, "salutations", to a long list of forms of Shiva: to the one in the forest, the one in the mountains, the cook, the carpenter, the one at the crossroads. It is a beautiful reminder that Shiva is not away in a temple. He is in all of life.
