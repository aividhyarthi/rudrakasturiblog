The story of Nala and Damayanti is told in the Vana Parva of the Mahabharata, to comfort Yudhishthira in his own exile.

## The lapse
Nala, king of Nishadha, and Damayanti, princess of Vidarbha, married after she chose him over the gods themselves. Kali, the spirit of the dark age, had come to the swayamvara too late and was furious. He waited for any gap in Nala's conduct, and found it years later: Nala once failed to wash his feet properly before evening prayers. Kali entered him.

## The fall
Under Kali's influence Nala gambled with his brother Pushkara, lost everything, and went into the forest with Damayanti. In his confusion he abandoned her while she slept. Damayanti searched for him through forests and cities with courage and cleverness. Nala, transformed into the misshapen Bahuka, worked as a charioteer for King Rituparna, learned the secret of dice from him, and was freed from Kali's hold. He regained his kingdom and his wife.

## Shani and Tirunallar
In the Mahabharata it is Kali who afflicts Nala. In Tamil tradition, and at the temple of Tirunallar in Tamil Nadu, the story is told with Shani, the planet Saturn, as the force that tested him and was finally propitiated. Many people visit the Shani shrine there in difficult times.

## What the story teaches
- **Small lapses matter.** A slight failure of discipline gave Kali his opening. Habits are where large falls begin.
- **Hard times end.** Nala's exile lasted years, and the recovery came through self-mastery, not luck.
- **Steadfastness is a form of strength.** Damayanti never doubts Nala's worth, even when he has failed her.
- **Do not blame the planets.** The tradition itself is clear that Nala's actions let the difficulty in, and his learning ended it.
