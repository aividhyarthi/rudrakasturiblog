There is an old saying about three rivers. The Ganga cleanses you when you bathe in her. The Yamuna cleanses you when you drink her water. The Narmada cleanses you just by being seen. That is how holy the Narmada is considered.

## The river
The Narmada begins at Amarkantak, a plateau in Madhya Pradesh, and flows westwards for about 1,300 km through Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Gujarat, to the Arabian Sea in the Gulf of Khambhat. Most rivers of the peninsula run east to the Bay of Bengal. The Narmada, with the Tapti, runs west.

## The story
The Puranas say Narmada was born from Shiva's body during his penance, and is often called Shankari, daughter of Shankara. Another legend says she was the daughter of the moon. In both stories, the river is linked to Shiva, and many pilgrims call her Mother Narmada, Reva.

## Stones of Shiva
The stones of the river are famous. The smooth, oval stones that roll in her bed, called Bana lingas or Narmadeshwar, are worshipped as lingas, and traditionally need no consecration. There is a saying that every pebble of the Narmada is a Shankara. Families keep them at home, and shops in Omkareshwar sell them.

## Holy places
- **Amarkantak**, the source, with a temple and a kund.
- **Omkareshwar and Mamleshwar**, two of the twelve Jyotirlingas, on an island shaped like the sacred syllable Om.
- **Maheshwar**, with its fort and ghats, the capital of Queen Ahilyabai Holkar, who built or restored temples all over India.
- **Jabalpur**, with the Marble Rocks gorge.

## Where Shankara met his teacher
If you read our piece on [Adi Shankaracharya](/adi-shankaracharya-the-boy-from-kaladi-who-walked-across-india/), you will remember that the boy from Kerala walked to the banks of the Narmada to find his guru Govinda Bhagavatpada. This is the river where he studied.

## The Parikrama
The most famous practice is the Narmada Parikrama, walking around the river. Pilgrims start at the source or at Omkareshwar, walk along one bank to the sea, cross by boat at the mouth, and return along the other bank. By tradition, they never cross the river. The route is about 3,500 km and traditionally takes three years, three months and thirteen days. Many people do shorter sections now, by foot or vehicle.

Local villagers along the route often offer food and shelter to the parikramavasis, as the walkers are called, and people say that is part of the blessing.

## A modern concern
The river has been at the centre of a long debate on dams, such as the Sardar Sarovar, and on displacement of villages. Many families worship her and also worry about what is happening to her. Both are part of the story today.
