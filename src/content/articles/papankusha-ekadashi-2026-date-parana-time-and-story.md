Papankusha Ekadashi is the Ekadashi of the bright fortnight of Ashwayuja, the day after Vijayadashami (Dasara). The name means "the goad that controls sin": the tradition says that devotion on this day keeps a person on the right path, as a goad (ankusha) guides an elephant.

## Date and timings (Hyderabad)
- **Ekadashi tithi:** begins on Wednesday, 21 October at 2:12 PM and ends on Thursday, 22 October at 2:48 PM.
- **Fast day:** Thursday, 22 October 2026, since Ekadashi is present at sunrise.
- **Parana (breaking the fast):** Friday, 23 October, after sunrise and before Dwadashi ends at 2:36 PM.

Thursday is also the day of the guru and of Vishnu in many homes, so the combination is considered especially suitable for prayer. Some calendars and Vaishnava traditions follow stricter rules for the day, so confirm with your own temple.

## How to observe it
- **Wake early, bathe, and light a lamp** before an image of Vishnu or Venkateswara.
- **Offer tulasi leaves**, flowers and a simple naivedyam.
- **Avoid grains and pulses**, and eat only fruit, milk and similar foods if you are fasting. A lighter meal without grains is also fine.
- **Chant** *Om Namo Narayanaya* or read the Vishnu Sahasranama. See [Vishnu mantras with meaning](/mantras/vishnu/).
- **Break the fast** the next morning, after sunrise and within the Dwadashi window.

## The story
According to the Padma Purana tradition, Krishna told Yudhishthira about a cruel hunter named Krodhana, who had spent his life in violence. As his end approached, he was terrified of the Lord of Death and fled to the hermitage of the sage Angira. The sage told him to fast on Papankusha Ekadashi with faith. The hunter did so, and was freed of his fear and his sins.

The message the tradition draws is not about magic. It is that sincere effort to turn around is always possible, however long the past, which is also what [our karma series](/karma-stories/) explores every day.
