Parikshit was the grandson of Arjuna and the king who ruled after the Pandavas. His story appears in the Mahabharata and, in greater depth, in the Bhagavata Purana.

## The moment of anger
Hunting in the forest, the king grew tired and thirsty and came upon the sage Shamika, who was sitting in deep meditation. Parikshit asked for water and received no answer, because the sage was absorbed in silence. Offended, he picked up a dead snake with his bow and placed it round the sage's neck, and rode away.

## The curse
Shamika's young son Shringi heard of it and, in anger, cursed that the king would die within seven days of a bite from Takshaka, the king of snakes. The sage himself was dismayed by his son's haste and sent word to the king. Parikshit accepted the news without protest.

## The seven days
Parikshit placed his son Janamejaya on the throne, went to the banks of the Ganga, and sat down to fast. The sage Shuka came and, over seven days, told him the Bhagavata Purana. At the end of the seventh day Takshaka came, and the king left the world at peace.

## What the story teaches
- **A single moment of anger can start a chain.** Parikshit's offence was small, and Shringi's response was larger. Both were products of haste.
- **Karma is not always proportionate.** The tradition does not say the curse was fair. It says the king met it with grace.
- **We all have a known end, even if we do not know the date.** Parikshit's week is a picture of how to spend the time we have: letting go of what does not matter and listening to what does.

## For today
If you had seven days, what would you stop doing, and what would you listen to? You do not need a curse to ask that question. Ask it this week.
