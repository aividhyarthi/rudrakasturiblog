The drums start slowly. The thappu, the dappu, the urumi, a nadaswaram in the background. The beat quickens, the crowd presses in, the camphor is lit. And then a woman near the front begins to sway. Her eyes close, her head shakes, her voice changes. People step back and fold their hands. *Saami vachindi*, they say. The goddess has come. In Telugu this is *poonakam*, in Tamil *saami aadudhal* or *arulvaakku*.

Many people watching feel something between awe and unease. Is it real? What is the energy? And why does it seem to happen to women more?

## What the tradition says
In the village temple traditions of the South, the deity is not far away. She is the *grama devata*, the guardian of the village, and she can be called to speak. A person who becomes the vessel is treated with deep respect. People ask for advice, for blessings, for the cure of a child. The trance is seen as grace, not illness.

The same idea is old and wide. The Alvars and Nayanmars wrote of being overcome by the Lord. The Bhagavata Purana speaks of trembling, tears and hair standing on end as signs of devotion.

## What the drums do
The tradition says the drum calls the goddess. Researchers who study trance across the world say something close, in other words. A steady, rising beat holds attention. When it is joined by a crowd, heat, fasting, a lack of sleep and strong expectation, the mind can slip out of its normal state. People shake, lose track of time and often remember little afterward. Studies of drumming and ritual have found this in many cultures, from Africa to Brazil to Bali. This is not a proof or a disproof of the divine. It tells us the body is wired for it.

## Why more women?
Not only women. Men are among the oracles and the priests of many of these temples. But you see women more, and there are two plain reasons.
- **Permission.** In many homes, a woman has few places to cry, shout or be heard. A temple festival gives her one, and the community will not judge it. It can be a release of years of strain.
- **Expectation.** If you grow up watching your mother and aunts receive the goddess, the doorway is already open.

Neither makes the experience fake. It makes it human.

## A teacher's view
Shankara would say: the real sign of the presence of God is not shaking but steadiness. In the Gita Krishna describes the devotee as one who is the same in praise and blame and free of fear (12.13 to 12.19). A trance may open the heart. What lasts is what you carry out of the temple: kindness, steadiness, a lighter mind.

So, hold it with respect. Do not mock it. Do not run after it either.

## If you feel it
- **Let it pass, in safe company.** Sit down, with someone you trust nearby.
- **Drink water and eat something afterward.** Trances in crowds and fasting can leave you drained.
- **Do not make big decisions from it.** Give it a few days.
- **Do not push for it.** A forced trance helps no one.

## When to seek help
If it happens unasked, outside the temple, if you feel afraid or out of control, or if it comes with fits, fainting or low mood, please see a doctor.
