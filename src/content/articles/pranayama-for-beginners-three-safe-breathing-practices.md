*Pranayama* is the yogic practice of regulating the breath. Done gently, it is a simple way to slow down and settle the mind. Start small and keep every breath comfortable.

## Before you start
- Sit on a chair or the floor with the spine tall and shoulders relaxed.
- Practise on an empty stomach, in a quiet place, ideally in the morning.
- Breathe through the nose unless instructed otherwise.
- Stop if you feel dizzy, breathless or uncomfortable, and go back to normal breathing.

## 1. Slow belly breathing
This is the base of every other practice.
1. Place one hand on the belly.
2. Breathe in through the nose for a count of four, letting the belly rise.
3. Breathe out for a count of four to six, letting the belly fall.
4. Continue for two to five minutes.

## 2. Nadi Shodhana (alternate nostril breathing)
1. Rest the left hand on the knee. Fold the index and middle fingers of the right hand and use the thumb and ring finger to close the nostrils.
2. Close the right nostril with the thumb. Breathe in through the left for four counts.
3. Close the left nostril with the ring finger, release the right and breathe out for four counts.
4. Breathe in through the right, close it, and breathe out through the left. That is one round.
5. Do five to ten rounds. Keep the breath even, with no holding of the breath in the beginning.

## 3. Bhramari (humming bee breath)
1. Sit tall with the eyes closed. Place the index fingers lightly on the ears or just rest the hands in your lap.
2. Breathe in through the nose.
3. As you breathe out slowly, make a steady, low humming sound like a bee.
4. Repeat five to seven times.

## Cautions
- **Avoid breath holds** if you have high blood pressure, a heart condition, glaucoma, epilepsy, or if you are pregnant, unless your doctor and a trained teacher say otherwise.
- **Forceful practices** such as Kapalabhati and Bhastrika should be learned from a qualified teacher. They are not recommended for people with heart disease, high blood pressure, hernia, ulcers, or during pregnancy.
- **Mental health:** if breath practices make you anxious, stop. They are not a replacement for treatment of anxiety or depression.
- If you have a medical condition, check with a doctor before starting.

## A short routine
Two minutes of belly breathing, five rounds of alternate nostril breathing, and five rounds of Bhramari take under ten minutes. You can follow it with [Surya Namaskar](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/) or do it before prayer.
