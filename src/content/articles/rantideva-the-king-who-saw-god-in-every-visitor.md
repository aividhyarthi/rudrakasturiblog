Imagine receiving a little food after many days without a proper meal. You are hungry, your family is hungry, and there is finally something to eat. Then a visitor arrives asking for help. What would you do?

The Bhagavatam remembers King Rantideva for the answer he gave.

## A king brought to the edge of hunger

Rantideva was known for generosity. The Bhagavatam describes a period when he and his family went without food and water for many days. When food finally became available, they prepared to eat.

But before they could finish, guests began arriving. One after another, people asked for food. Rantideva shared what he had.

At the end, only a little water remained. A thirsty person came and asked for it. Rantideva gave away that water too.

## Seeing the Divine in others

The episode is not simply about a king being generous. Rantideva's prayer reveals the way he looked at the world. He did not want special powers or a privileged place in heaven. He wished to share in the suffering of others and help them find relief.

The Bhagavatam presents the visitors as a divine test. The people who came were not ordinary strangers in the spiritual meaning of the story. The episode reveals whether Rantideva's compassion would remain steady when giving became difficult.

## What made his giving different?

Many people give when they have plenty. Rantideva gave when he had almost nothing. His generosity was not a public display or a way to earn praise. It came from a conviction that the Divine is present in all beings.

This does not mean that every person must ignore their own health or family responsibilities. The story presents an extraordinary ideal of compassion. Its everyday lesson is to avoid treating another person's suffering as someone else's problem.

## The deeper meaning

Rantideva's story asks a simple question: what does devotion look like when another person is hungry or thirsty? His answer was to serve the person before him.

In bhakti, prayer and compassion are not separate paths. Love for God should soften the heart towards God's creation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the story of Rantideva found?

It appears in the Ninth Skandha of the Srimad Bhagavatam.

### Why is Rantideva remembered?

He is remembered for sharing his food and water with people in need, even when he and his family were suffering from hunger.

### What is the spiritual lesson?

The story teaches compassion, humility and seeing the Divine in all living beings.
