*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The lines
> **asato mā sad gamaya**
> **tamaso mā jyotir gamaya**
> **mṛtyor mā amṛtaṁ gamaya**
> (Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.3.28)

## Word by word
| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| asataḥ | from the unreal |
| mā | me |
| sat | to the real |
| gamaya | lead |
| tamasaḥ | from darkness |
| jyotiḥ | to light |
| mṛtyoḥ | from death |
| amṛtam | to the immortal, to the deathless |

## In plain words
*From the unreal, lead me to the real. From darkness, lead me to light. From death, lead me to immortality.*

## Three pairs, three journeys
**Asat to sat.** *Sat* comes from the root *as*, to be. The real is that which *is*, which does not change. *Asat* is what comes and goes. The prayer is not asking to leave the world, but to see what in it is lasting.

**Tamas to jyoti.** Darkness is ignorance, light is knowledge. It is not about the sun. It is about not seeing clearly.

**Mṛtyu to amṛta.** Death to deathlessness. The Upanishads do not mean endless life of the body. They mean freedom from the fear that death brings, which they connect with knowing the Self.

## Who is asking?
Look at *mā*, "me". Each line begins with the one who prays. This is not a prayer for the world, or for a nation. It is a prayer to be *led*. The verb *gamaya* is causative: *make me go*, *cause me to reach*. The person admits that they cannot get there alone.

## Where it comes from
The verse is from the *Pavamana mantras*, chanted during the Soma sacrifice. The sacrifice involved moving from a lower to a higher offering. The prayer was part of that movement. It has been taken out of the sacrifice and into daily life, and is now sung in school assemblies across India. It is followed in the text by a peace invocation.

## Common misreading
- **"It is about heaven."** The Upanishad speaks of knowing, not of a place.
- **"It is just a lovely poem."** The line is a three-step programme of study.

## One thing to try
Say the three lines slowly, once, in the morning. Then pick one pair and ask: *what is one thing I take as lasting that is not?* Or *one thing I do not see clearly?* Write it down.
