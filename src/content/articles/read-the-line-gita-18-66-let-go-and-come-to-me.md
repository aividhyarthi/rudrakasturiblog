*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The line
> **sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja**
> **ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ**
> (Bhagavad Gita 18.66)

## Word by word
| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| sarva-dharmān | all dharmas, all duties and rules |
| parityajya | having completely given up |
| mām | me |
| ekam | alone |
| śaraṇam | refuge |
| vraja | go to |
| aham | I |
| tvām | you |
| sarva-pāpebhyaḥ | from all sins |
| mokṣayiṣyāmi | will release |
| mā | do not |
| śucaḥ | grieve |

## In plain words
*Giving up all dharmas, come to me alone for refuge. I will free you from all sins. Do not grieve.*

## The first thing to ask
What does it mean to give up *all dharmas*? If we read it literally, Krishna has just told Arjuna to abandon his duties as a warrior, a son, a friend. But the whole Gita has been about doing duty. Something else must be meant.

The classical teachers read it this way. Shankara reads it as giving up all actions and rites in the knowledge that the Self is the one reality. Ramanuja reads it as giving up all other means of reaching God and trusting in grace alone. Different roads, but a similar spirit: *put down the burden of control.*

## The shape of the verse
It begins with *letting go* (*parityajya*) and ends with *do not grieve* (*mā śucaḥ*). In between is the only instruction, *go to me alone for refuge*. Note that *śaraṇam*, refuge, is the same idea as the *sharanagati* of the Ramayana's Vibhishana.

The promise is *mokṣayiṣyāmi*, "I will release". Not "you will earn". The Gita ends not on effort but on grace.

## What Arjuna does
This is the most important check. Krishna says, in the next verses, *tell this to no one who has no devotion*, and asks Arjuna whether he has understood. Arjuna says (18.73): *my delusion is destroyed; I have regained memory by your grace; I stand firm, free of doubt; I will act according to your word.*

He gets up, and he fights. Surrender in the Gita is not retirement. It is acting without the sense that everything depends on you.

## Common misreading
- **"Give up your responsibilities."** The Gita does not say this. Arjuna returns to his.
- **"Nothing I do matters, since I am surrendered."** The surrendered person acts, with the weight lifted.

## One thing to try
Think of one burden you are carrying as if all of it depends on you: a decision, a worry, someone else's choices. Do your part today. Then say, in your own words, that the rest is not yours to carry.
