*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The line
> **vāsāṁsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya navāni gṛhṇāti naro 'parāṇi**
> **tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇāny anyāni saṁyāti navāni dehī**
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.22)

## In plain words
*As a person throws away worn-out clothes and puts on new ones, so the embodied one throws away worn-out bodies and goes into new ones.*

## What is happening in the verse
Arjuna has put down his bow. He sees his teachers and cousins across the field and cannot bear the thought of killing them. Krishna begins his answer not with duty but with a question about who is really in the body. The verse is a comparison. A coat can be worn out and thrown away without the wearer ceasing to exist. So, he says, with the body and the one who wears it.

Notice the word *dehī*. It means *the one who has a body*. Not the body itself. The verse is built on that difference.

## What it does not say
It does not say that grief is wrong. It does not say that the person who died did not matter. It does not say that you should say it to a mourner at a funeral. Krishna says it to a warrior who must now decide what to do, and who is paralysed by sorrow. For many people, it becomes a place to rest after the first wave of grief, not a first word.

## Common misreading
- **"So death is nothing."** The Gita does not say this. It says that what is essential continues, and it keeps Arjuna's sorrow in view.
- **"So I need not care for the body."** The same book tells us to eat, sleep and work in moderation (6.17).

## One thing to try
Think of one thing you are holding on to that has already worn out: a role, a grievance, an old picture of yourself. Read the verse again. What would it mean to put it down, as one puts down an old coat?
