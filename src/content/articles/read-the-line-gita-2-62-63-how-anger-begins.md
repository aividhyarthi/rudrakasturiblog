*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The lines
> **dhyāyato viṣayān puṁsaḥ saṅgas teṣūpajāyate**
> **saṅgāt sañjāyate kāmaḥ kāmāt krodho 'bhijāyate**
> **krodhād bhavati sammohaḥ sammohāt smṛti-vibhramaḥ**
> **smṛti-bhraṁśād buddhi-nāśo buddhi-nāśāt praṇaśyati**
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.62 and 2.63)

## Word by word
| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| dhyāyataḥ | of one who dwells on |
| viṣayān | objects (of the senses) |
| puṁsaḥ | of a person |
| saṅgaḥ | attachment |
| teṣu | to them |
| upajāyate | arises |
| kāmaḥ | desire |
| krodhaḥ | anger |
| abhijāyate | is born |
| sammohaḥ | delusion, confusion |
| smṛti-vibhramaḥ | muddling of memory |
| buddhi-nāśaḥ | loss of discernment |
| praṇaśyati | is ruined |

## In plain words
*When a person keeps dwelling on objects, attachment to them grows. From attachment, desire is born. From desire, anger. From anger, delusion. From delusion, the memory is muddled. From the muddling of memory, the power to discern is lost. And when discernment is lost, the person is ruined.*

## Read it as a staircase
Each phrase begins with the last word of the previous one, *saṅgāt*, *kāmāt*, *krodhāt*. It is a staircase going down, and Krishna is describing each step in order.

1. **Dwelling.** The mind keeps going back to something.
2. **Attachment.** It starts to feel necessary.
3. **Desire.** It must be had.
4. **Anger.** When something blocks it.
5. **Delusion.** Rage clouds the picture.
6. **Memory muddled.** You forget what you value, who you love, what you promised.
7. **Discernment lost.** You can no longer tell right from wrong.
8. **Ruin.**

## Where to step off
Nearly all of us only notice step 4, after the shouting has begun. By then the chain is already half-formed. What this verse tells us is that the *best* place to act is step 1: the quiet replaying of a thought. A resentment is rarely born in a rage. It starts as a thought repeated twenty times in the shower.

## One thing to try
Next time you catch yourself going over a conversation or a grievance for the third time, say silently, *dhyāyataḥ*, dwelling. Then move: stand up, get water, change the room. It does not fix the problem, but it breaks the chain at its first link.

## A line to follow
The verse after these (2.64) says that the one who moves among objects with the senses controlled, free from liking and disliking, attains peace.
