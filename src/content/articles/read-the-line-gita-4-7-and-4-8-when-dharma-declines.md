*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The lines
> **yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata**
> **abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham**
> **paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām**
> **dharma-saṁsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge**
> (Bhagavad Gita 4.7 and 4.8)

## In plain words
*Whenever dharma fades and adharma rises, then I send myself forth. To protect the good, to destroy the wrongdoers, and to establish dharma, I come into being, age after age.*

## A closer look at two words
**Ātmānaṁ sṛjāmi**, "I send forth myself". Not "I am punished into being" or "I am summoned". Krishna says he *releases himself*, as it were, into the world. In the tradition this is the idea of *avatāra*, the descent.

**Dharma-saṁsthāpana**, "setting dharma firmly in place". Notice that the purpose is not only to punish but to *establish*. The destruction of evil is the clearing of ground.

## Common misreading
It is easy to hear these lines as a promise that someone else will fix the world. But look at who is hearing them. Arjuna is in the middle of a battlefield and has just refused to fight. Krishna is not telling him to relax. Within a few chapters he is telling him to stand up and do his duty. The verse speaks of God's presence in history, and the *same Gita* then tells you to act.

## What this means for an ordinary day
You will not meet a battlefield. But you will meet small declines of dharma: a lie in the office, a bribe, a cruel remark at home. The verse invites you to ask a quieter question: *what part of it can I set right?*

## One thing to try
Think of one small place around you where something is slipping, in your family, workplace or street. Do one concrete thing about it this week, however small.
