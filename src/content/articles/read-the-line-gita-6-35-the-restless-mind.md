*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The line
> **asaṁśayaṁ mahā-bāho mano durnigrahaṁ calam**
> **abhyāsena tu kaunteya vairāgyeṇa ca gṛhyate**
> (Bhagavad Gita 6.35)

## Word by word
| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| asaṁśayam | without doubt |
| mahā-bāho | O mighty-armed one |
| manaḥ | the mind |
| durnigraham | hard to restrain |
| calam | restless, moving |
| abhyāsena | by practice |
| tu | but |
| kaunteya | O son of Kunti |
| vairāgyeṇa | by non-clinging |
| ca | and |
| gṛhyate | it is held, grasped |

## In plain words
*Without doubt, O mighty-armed one, the mind is hard to restrain and restless. But it can be held by practice and by non-clinging.*

## The conversation behind it
In the verse just before (6.34), Arjuna says something very honest. He tells Krishna that the mind is restless, turbulent, strong and obstinate, and that holding it back seems as hard as holding the wind. It is a sentence any modern person can say about their phone, their thoughts at 2 a.m., their ability to read a page without wandering off.

Krishna's reply begins with *asaṁśayam*: *no doubt*. He does not say Arjuna is wrong, or lazy. He agrees. Then, with the little word *tu*, "but", he turns.

## Two tools
**Abhyāsa, practice.** Not a single big effort, but showing up again and again. Five minutes of breathing every day does more than an hour once a month.

**Vairāgya, non-clinging.** Not hatred of the world, but the ability to let a thought go once you have noticed it. Practice without letting go becomes strain. Letting go without practice becomes drift.

The verse says both, joined by *ca*, "and". Many traditions use the image of a bird with two wings.

## One thing to try
For a week, give the mind five minutes. Sit, close your eyes, and count ten breaths. Each time the mind wanders, and it will, say *calam*, restless, and come back without scolding yourself. That is abhyāsa. The coming back is not the failure of practice. It is the practice.

## A word on patience
The Gita does not promise results in a week. The next verse (6.36) adds that yoga is hard for the one who cannot control himself, but is attained by the one who strives by right means. Think in months, not days.

## A closer look at the word calam
*Calam* means moving, shaking, unsteady. The same word is used in Sanskrit for the flicker of a flame in the wind or the ripple on water. It does not mean evil or broken. The mind is described as restless by nature, in the same way that water flows downhill. Seen this way, a wandering mind is not a personal failing. It is how minds behave, and Krishna says so plainly.

## What practice looks like
Abhyāsa is a word you will meet again in the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali, where practice and non-clinging are also named together as the two ways to still the mind. Here are a few ordinary forms it can take:
- Sitting at the same time each day, even for five minutes.
- Chanting a short mantra, such as the one you learned as a child. See our guide to [mantra japa](/mantra-japa-how-to-start-count-and-keep-going/).
- Reading one verse slowly each morning.
- Counting breaths before sleep.

## What non-clinging looks like
Vairāgya is easy to misunderstand as coldness. In daily life it can be quite small. You notice a worry arriving, you say "I see you", and you do not follow it down the road. You stay kind to the people around you. You do the job in front of you. You let the result be what it will be.

## Questions people ask
**Does everyone have this problem?**
Yes. Arjuna, a great warrior, said it first. If you feel your mind will not behave, you are in good company.

**How long before it gets easier?**
Everyone is different, and the text does not give a number. Many people find that after a few weeks of steady practice they recover from distraction a little faster. That is a good sign.

**Is this a medical solution for anxiety?**
No. This is a spiritual teaching about attention. If your restlessness comes with panic, low mood or sleeplessness that is hard to bear, please speak to a doctor or counsellor. It is not a weakness to ask for help.
