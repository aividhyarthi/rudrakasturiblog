*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The line
> **patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati**
> **tad ahaṁ bhakty-upahṛtam aśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ**
> (Bhagavad Gita 9.26)

## Word by word
| Sanskrit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| patram | a leaf |
| puṣpam | a flower |
| phalam | a fruit |
| toyam | water |
| yaḥ | whoever |
| me | to me |
| bhaktyā | with devotion |
| prayacchati | offers |
| tat | that |
| aham | I |
| bhakti-upahṛtam | brought with devotion |
| aśnāmi | I accept, I eat |
| prayata-ātmanaḥ | of the one whose heart is pure and offered |

## In plain words
*Whoever offers me a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water with devotion, I accept that offering of the pure-hearted one.*

## The surprise in the verse
Think of what Krishna could have listed. Gold. Elephants. A great sacrifice. He lists the four cheapest things in an Indian village: something green, something that blooms, something that grows, and water from the well. Almost anyone can offer these.

The key word is *bhaktyā*, "with devotion". It appears twice, once as *bhaktyā* and once in *bhakty-upahṛtam*, "brought with devotion". Krishna is repeating it so that we do not miss it. Take that out of the verse, and nothing is left. Put it back, and a leaf is enough.

## What it changed
For many Indians this verse is the answer to a quiet worry: *I am poor, I do not know the Sanskrit, I cannot afford the grand puja. Does it count?* The Gita answers, plainly: yes.

It is why the tulasi leaf on a plate, a single flower from the garden, or a glass of water in front of the picture is a complete act of worship in a village home.

## Common misreading
It does not say that nothing else matters, or that one should offer a leaf instead of acting rightly. In the very next verse (9.27) Krishna says: *whatever you do, whatever you eat, whatever you offer, whatever you give, whatever austerity you perform, do it as an offering to me.* The leaf is the beginning, not the whole.

## One thing to try
Tomorrow morning, offer one thing: a flower, a leaf, a sip of water. Before you do, say aloud in your own language what you are offering, and why. See how it feels to give something small with your whole attention.
