*How to use this page:* read the Sanskrit slowly, even if only in the English letters. Then read the word-by-word, then the meaning. Chanting and listening carry the rhythm of a verse, and that has its own power. Reading the words carries the meaning, and you can stop, go back and ask what each word is doing. Do both, but do not skip the reading.

## The lines
> **satyaṁ vada. dharmaṁ cara. svādhyāyān mā pramadaḥ.**
> **mātṛ-devo bhava. pitṛ-devo bhava. ācārya-devo bhava. atithi-devo bhava.**
> **yāny anavadyāni karmāṇi tāni sevitavyāni no itarāṇi.**
> (Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11)

## In plain words
*Speak the truth. Practise dharma. Do not neglect your studies. Treat your mother as a god, your father as a god, your teacher as a god and your guest as a god. Follow the blameless actions, not the others.*

## The setting
This is a farewell speech. In the ancient system, a student lived with his teacher for years. When the course ended, the teacher gave an address, the *samāvartana upadeśa*, to send him back into the world. It is the Upanishad's equivalent of a convocation speech. And look at what it says: not a theory of the universe, but a few plain instructions about how to live.

## Reading each line
**Satyaṁ vada.** Speak the truth. Two words. The command comes first, before anything about God or the Self.

**Dharmaṁ cara.** Practise dharma. *Cara* means *walk*. Dharma is not a thing to be believed in, but a way to walk.

**Svādhyāyān mā pramadaḥ.** Do not neglect study. Learning is a lifelong duty, not something you finish with school.

**Mātṛ-devo bhava.** The text does not say "obey your mother". It says *be one who sees the mother as a god*. It is about an attitude of reverence. The same goes for father, teacher, guest.

**Atithi-devo bhava.** The guest as a god. It is the root of the Indian ideal of hospitality, and still the phrase on tourism posters.

## The last line is the clever one
*Yāny anavadyāni karmāṇi tāni sevitavyāni no itarāṇi*: follow the blameless actions, *not the others*. Just after telling the student to honour his elders, the teacher adds a safeguard. Follow what is *blameless* in what they do, and leave the rest. The text expects the student to think.

## Common misreading
- **"The Upanishads ask for blind obedience."** This line shows the opposite. The student is told to discriminate.
- **"Guest as god means we must put up with anything."** It sets a standard for the host, and does not exempt the guest from decency.

## One thing to try
Pick one of the first three: speak one true thing you have been avoiding saying, do one right thing you have been delaying, or spend half an hour on something you want to learn. Do it today.
