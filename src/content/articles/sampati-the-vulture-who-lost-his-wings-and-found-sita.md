**Source:** Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda (chapters 56 to 63).

The search party was stuck on the southern shore. Hanuman, Angada, Jambavan and the other vanaras had come to the edge of the sea, and the sea was too wide to cross. They had missed the time limit that Sugriva had given them. They were sitting there ready to starve themselves rather than face the king. And out of the mountains above came a huge, wingless vulture.

## The brothers who flew to the sun
The vulture was Sampati, the elder brother of Jatayu. The two were sons of Aruna and nephews of Garuda. In their youth, he told the vanaras, the two brothers had a contest to see who could fly highest. They flew toward the sun. As the heat grew, Jatayu began to fall. Sampati spread his own wings over his brother and shielded him.

Jatayu was saved. Sampati's wings were burnt, and he fell to the Vindhya mountain. For years he lived on that mountain, unable to fly, not knowing what had happened to his brother.

## The sage's promise
Sampati told the vanaras that a sage called Nishakara had once found him on the mountain, and had told him this: *one day a message will come from Rama's messengers. When you help them, your wings will grow again.* He had waited, the text says, for a very long time.

When the vanaras began to speak of Jatayu's death, Sampati learned for the first time what had happened to his brother. He was devastated. He asked the vanaras to take him to the water so he could offer water for Jatayu, and they did.

## The sight of a vulture
Then came the moment that made his waiting useful. Vultures were said to have extraordinary sight. Sampati looked across the sea and said that he could see the city of Lanka a hundred yojanas away, and in a garden in it, a woman guarded by rakshasis, a woman with the signs the vanaras had described. It was Sita.

With that, the search party had what it needed: *she is alive, and she is in Lanka.* It was the first certain news in the whole story since the abduction. And as he said it, Sampati's wings began to grow again.

## What it teaches
- **Our limitations can be useful to others.** Sampati could not fly, and yet his eyes could see what no flier could.
- **Sacrifice is not always rewarded at once.** He lost his wings for his brother, and waited years for them.
- **We often do not know whom we are waiting for.** The sage's promise made sense only when the vanaras arrived.
- **The smaller roles matter.** Without Sampati's sight, Hanuman would not have known where to leap.

## The two brothers
It is moving to see Jatayu and Sampati together: one gave his life to stop Ravana, the other gave the information that led Rama to him. The Ramayana does not say so, but it invites us to notice that what each did came out of what he had lost.
