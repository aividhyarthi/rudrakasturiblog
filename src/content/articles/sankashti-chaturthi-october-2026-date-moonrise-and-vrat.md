Sankashti Chaturthi, also called Sankata Hara Chaturthi, is the Chaturthi of the dark fortnight, observed every month in honour of Ganesha. *Sankashti* means deliverance from difficulty.

## Date and timings (Hyderabad)
- **Date:** Thursday, 29 October 2026.
- **Krishna Chaturthi tithi:** present at sunrise on 29 October and ends at 10:10 PM.
- **Moonrise:** about 8:36 PM.

Since the tithi is in force at moonrise, the fast is kept on this day and broken after the Moon is seen. Moonrise differs by a few minutes between cities, so check the [panchangam for your location](/panchangam/).

In north India the same day is Karwa Chauth.

## How to observe it
1. **Fast through the day.** Many people take only fruit and water, or eat nothing until moonrise. A lighter meal is fine for those who need it.
2. **Evening Ganesha puja.** Light a lamp, offer durva grass, red hibiscus and a naivedyam of modak or laddu.
3. **Chant.** *Om Gam Ganapataye Namah* (ఓం గం గణపతయే నమః) 108 times, or *Vakratunda Mahakaya*. Meanings are on our [Ganesha mantras page](/mantras/ganesha/).
4. **After moonrise,** offer a little water (arghya) to the Moon, then break the fast with the prasad.

## Why the Moon
Ganesha cursed the Moon for laughing at him, and later relented. The Moon is therefore offered respect on this night. Whatever one makes of the story, the practical rhythm is a day of restraint ending with a calm look at the sky.

## When it falls on a Tuesday
If Sankashti Chaturthi falls on a Tuesday, it is called Angaraki Chaturthi and is considered especially powerful. This month it falls on a Thursday.

## Sankashti and Vinayaka Chaturthi
The Chaturthi of the dark fortnight is Sankashti, and the Chaturthi of the bright fortnight is Vinayaka Chaturthi. Both are Ganesha's days, and the annual festival, Vinayaka Chavithi, falls in the month of Bhadrapada.

*Traditions vary by family and region. Fasting is optional and should not be undertaken against medical advice.*
