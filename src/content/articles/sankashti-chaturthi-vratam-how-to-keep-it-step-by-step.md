The month has turned, the moon is a thin memory, and somewhere in the house an elder says, "Today is Sankashti." Do you know how the day goes? If not, here is a plain walk through it, from morning to moonrise.

The word sankashti means difficulty or trouble; Sankatahara, the other name you will hear, means the remover of trouble. In Telugu it is సంకష్టహర చతుర్థి, and in Tamil Sankatahara Chathurthi. It is observed on the Chaturthi, the fourth lunar day, after the full moon in the Krishna paksha, so it comes once a month. For this year's date and the exact moonrise, see our page on [Sankashti Chaturthi](/sankashti-chaturthi/).

## Before you begin

Decide the night before that you will keep the vrata. Clean the puja corner, and keep ready:

- A Ganesha idol or picture, with a lamp.
- Durva grass, red or white flowers, and kumkum.
- Fruit, a sweet such as modaka or jaggery sweet, and milk.
- A small vessel of water, with a little milk and flower, for the moon's arghya.

## The morning

Rise early, bathe, and in front of the lamp say a short sankalpa: that today you will fast and worship Ganesha for the removal of difficulties. Offer a flower and a prayer, such as Om Gam Ganapataye Namah.

The fast itself varies. Some keep a strict fast without food or even water until moonrise. Others take only fruit and milk, or sago and peanuts, during the day. A few eat one simple meal without salt or grains. Which is right for you? The one your family follows, and the one your body allows. Pregnant women, the elderly, children and those unwell are generally advised by elders to keep a lighter form.

## The evening puja

As dusk comes, bathe again or wash up, and sit before Ganesha. A simple order:

1. Light the lamp and incense.
2. Offer water, flowers, kumkum and durva, with the names of Ganesha or the 108 names.
3. Read the Sankashti vrata katha, the story of the day, if your family has one. Many booklets are available in Telugu, Tamil and Hindi.
4. Offer your sweet and fruit as naivedya.
5. Do aarti and bow.

If you want to know what to offer and how, our guide to [Ganesh Chaturthi puja at home](/ganesh-chaturthi-puja-at-home-step-by-step/) has the full sixteen-step form, which you can shorten.

## During the day

A fast is not only about food. Many families use the day to keep the mind quiet: less idle talk, no arguments if you can help it, a few minutes of japa whenever you pass the puja corner. Chanting Om Gam Ganapataye Namah on a mala, 108 times, is a common choice. Others read the Ganapati Atharvashirsha or a Ganesha stotra such as Sankata Nashana Stotra in the afternoon. If you have a job to go to, do not feel the vrata is lost. Carry the fast lightly, keep Ganesha in mind between tasks, and do the puja when you return.

Children can take part without fasting: let them place the durva or ring the bell at aarti. It is how most of us learned.

## Moonrise darshan and arghya

This is the heart of the day. After the puja, wait for moonrise. When the moon is visible, stand facing it and offer arghya: pour the water, with milk and a flower, slowly from a small vessel while folding your hands and thinking of Ganesha. Say a short prayer asking for relief from difficulties and for peace in the home.

If the sky is cloudy, many families offer arghya facing the direction where the moon is expected, at the time given by the panchangam. Do not worry if you cannot see it; tradition accepts that.

## How to find the moonrise time

Moonrise changes every month and from city to city. Use a panchangam for your place, such as ours for [Hyderabad](/panchangam/hyderabad/), or ask your temple. The moonrise on Sankashti is usually late in the evening, sometimes after 8 pm, so plan your dinner accordingly.

## Breaking the fast

After arghya, do a final namaskara and offer thanks. Then break the fast with simple, sattvic food: fruit, milk, a light meal of rice or rotis without onion or garlic, and the sweet you offered. Eat what the prasada gives you first. Many families avoid heavy fried food on this night.

## Angaraki Sankashti

When Sankashti falls on a Tuesday, called Angaraki after Angaraka, the name of Mars, tradition regards it as especially auspicious. Many devotees keep this one even if they miss others. It does not change the method; it only raises the day's weight in the eyes of the tradition. If a Tuesday Sankashti comes in your month, you may want to mark it on the [calendar](/calendar/).

## Why the moon?

Why does a Ganesha vrata end by looking at the moon? Tradition links it to a story in which the moon laughed at Ganesha's form and was cursed to wane, then forgiven, so that the moon of the Krishna paksha, which rises late and small, is turned toward him in respect. Versions of the story differ between regions, and you may know another. What most families take from it is simple: you finish the day by looking up, humbled, and asking for relief.

## What if you miss one?

Life interrupts. If you miss a Sankashti, simply begin again next month. Tradition does not scold. Some families keep the vrata for a fixed span, such as a year, then end it with a special puja; if you wish to do so, ask a priest how it is done in your tradition.
