There is a moment, in the life of almost every child in India, that parents remember better than the child does. A small hand is held. A finger is guided through a tray of rice. And the first letter is written.

It might be *Om*, or *Sri*, or the first letter of the child's language. Then everyone claps, and someone gives the child a sweet.

## What it is
*Aksharabhyasam* means the practice of letters (*akshara*, imperishable, *abhyasa*, practice). In Kerala and Karnataka it is called *Vidyarambham*, the beginning of learning. Telugu families call it *Akshara Abhyasam* or *Akshara Swikaram*. It is a tradition so old that it is mentioned in the Dharma Shastras, and so living that it is done in thousands of homes each year.

## When it is done
- **Vijayadashami,** the last day of Navaratri, is the most popular.
- **Vasant Panchami,** the fifth day of the bright half of Magha, is the birthday of Saraswati in many traditions and the other favourite.
- **Any auspicious day,** chosen by the family, usually when the child is between three and five.

Thursday is also associated with learning, and some families choose it.

## How to do it at home
1. **Prepare a tray** with raw rice spread out evenly, or a slate and chalk.
2. **Set a small shrine** with a picture of Saraswati and Ganesha, a lamp, flowers and books.
3. **Bathe and dress the child** in clean new clothes.
4. **Begin with a prayer to Ganesha,** the remover of obstacles.
5. **Say the Saraswati mantra:**

> **sarasvati namastubhyaṁ varade kāma-rūpiṇi**
> **vidyārambhaṁ kariṣyāmi siddhir bhavatu me sadā**

*Salutations, Saraswati, giver of boons, who takes the forms we wish. I begin my learning. May I always succeed.*

6. **A parent or elder guides the child's finger** to write *Om*, *Sri* or the first letter of the alphabet in the rice. Many also write the first letters on the child's tongue with a gold ring dipped in honey, a very old custom.
7. **Share sweets** and bless the child.

## The Saraswati of the books
In pictures Saraswati sits on a white lotus, with a veena in her hands, a rosary, a book and a swan beside her. The swan is said to separate milk from water, as a learner must separate the true from the false. The white sari says that knowledge is pure. She is often shown in a river, a reminder that she was once a river too.

A well-known prayer to her, which every schoolchild knows, begins *ya kundendu-tushara-hara-dhavala*, "She who is white as jasmine and the moon". It is recited on the first day of school.

## Basar: the great temple
In Telangana, on the Godavari river, the Gnana Saraswati temple at Basar is the best known place for Aksharabhyasam. Thousands of families bring children, especially on Vasant Panchami and during Navaratri. We have written about [Basar Saraswati temple](/basar-saraswati-temple-where-telangana-children-write-their-first-letters/), and about the story behind it.

## Beyond the first letter
The tradition says learning is not a single event. Many families honour books on the last day of Navaratri, with *Ayudha Puja* and *Saraswati Puja*: books, pens and tools are placed before the goddess and not touched until the next morning. It is a way of teaching a child that knowledge is something we revere, not only use.

## The story of Basar
Tradition says that after the Kurukshetra war, the sage Vyasa, restless and seeking peace, travelled south and sat by the Godavari at a quiet spot. He prayed to Saraswati, and she appeared. Each morning, Vyasa is said to have brought three fistfuls of sand from the river and shaped them into three images, of Saraswati, Lakshmi and Kali. The place came to be called Vyasapuri, and later Basar. This is a temple legend, not in the epics, and it explains why the temple has all three goddesses of knowledge, wealth and power.

## The river who became a goddess
In the Rig Veda, Saraswati is first a river, called the best of rivers, the best of mothers and the best of goddesses. Over time the river disappeared from the landscape, and the goddess grew in the imagination. We have a separate piece on [the Saraswati river that vanished](/saraswati-the-river-that-vanished-and-the-goddess-who-stayed/).

## Some gentle advice for parents
- **Keep it joyful.** The ceremony is a celebration, not a test.
- **Let the child choose a sweet.** It makes the first letter a good memory.
- **Do not worry about the neatness of the first letter.** The goddess is not grading.
- **Read to the child that evening.** The first letter and the first story together are a nice beginning.

## For adults
You do not need to be three to begin. Many adults mark a new beginning in their own learning by writing a first line before Saraswati. A new course, a language, a skill: put a pen in front of her picture and begin.
