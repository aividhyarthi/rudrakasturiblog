A small child sits on a grandfather's lap. A plate of rice lies in front of them, and a lamp burns nearby. The grandfather takes the child's finger and gently draws it through the grains. The child may be more interested in the sweet in the corner of the plate, but something very old is happening. This is the first letter.

## What is Vidyarambham?

Vidyarambham means the beginning of learning (vidya, knowledge; arambham, start). In Telugu it is usually called Aksharabhyasam, and in Kerala Ezhuthiniruthu or Vidyarambham. In Tamil homes it is Vidyarambham too. Whatever the name, the idea is the same: the child's first lesson is offered to Saraswati, the goddess of speech and learning, before it is given to the school.

## The days

Two days are favoured.

**Vasant Panchami.** The fifth day of the bright fortnight of Magha, around January or February, is the day of Saraswati in much of India. Many families in Telangana and Andhra choose it for the first letter.

**Vijayadashami.** The tenth day of Navaratri, usually in September or October, is the great day in Kerala and Tamil Nadu, and widely among Telugu families. On the days before it, books and tools are placed before the goddess in Ayudha Puja, and the books are taken out on Vijayadashami for the first reading. The date changes every year; please see [the festivals page](/festivals/).

## Basara and Kerala

In Telangana, the temple at Basara on the Godavari river is among the best known for Aksharabhyasam. Tradition says the sage Vyasa installed Saraswati there. Families arrive, often before dawn, and the priests help the child write the first letters in front of the goddess. You can read about it in [Basara Saraswati temple: where Telangana children write their first letters](/basar-saraswati-temple-where-telangana-children-write-their-first-letters/).

In Kerala, Ezhuthiniruthu is held on Vijayadashami at temples and in homes, with a teacher or elder guiding the child's finger over rice or sand and sometimes writing with a gold ring on the tongue as well, according to local custom. Kerala also has well-known Saraswati shrines, such as the Dakshina Mookambika temple at Panachikkad, where the ceremony is performed in large numbers.

## The verse

The best-known prayer is the one many of us learned as children:

Saraswati namastubhyam, varade kamarupini; vidyarambham karishyami, siddhir bhavatu me sada.

In paraphrase: Saraswati, I bow to you, giver of boons, taking whatever form is needed. I am beginning my learning. May success always be mine. The verse is traditional, and the wording in some families varies slightly.

## How to do it at home

You do not need to travel. A simple version at home is entirely good.

1. Choose a day: Vasant Panchami, Vijayadashami, or a day your elders recommend.
2. Bathe the child and dress them in clean clothes. In many families, yellow or white.
3. Spread rice or fine sand on a plate or a tray. Keep a lamp and a picture of Saraswati ready, with a book or a slate.
4. Offer a flower and a small sweet, and say the verse above.
5. An elder, preferably a parent, grandparent or a teacher, holds the child's finger and writes Om, or Sri, or the first letter of the alphabet in the rice.
6. Let the child write a few more letters in their own way. Do not correct them. A smudge is fine.
7. Share the sweet, and give the child a new notebook and pencil to keep.

Expect distractions. A child may laugh, cry, or walk away. Let it be. Saraswati is not strict.

## Ayudha Puja and books

On the last days of Navaratri, many families place books, pens, musical instruments and tools before the goddess and do not use them for a day. On Vijayadashami they are taken out again with a prayer. For a child, this is a wonderful way to see learning treated with respect. It also teaches, without a lecture, that a book is not an ordinary object.

## A thought for parents

Aksharabhyasam does not make a child clever. No ceremony can. What it can do is teach the child, and the parents, that learning begins with humility, with respect for the teacher, and with a small act done together.
