In the Telugu states, Saturday (Shanivaram) is the day of Venkateswara, and many families visit a Venkateswara or Hanuman temple or keep a light fast. It is also the day of Shani, the planet Saturn.

## Venkateswara
Lord Venkateswara, also called Srinivasa and Balaji, is Vishnu as worshipped at Tirumala. On Saturdays families light a lamp, offer tulasi, and say *Om Namo Venkatesaya* (ఓం నమో వేంకటేశాయ). The pilgrim's cry of *Govinda, Govinda* is the same prayer in a few syllables. See our [Venkateswara mantras](/mantras/venkateswara/) and the [Tirumala guide](/temples/tirumala-venkateswara-temple/).

## Shani
Shani stands for discipline, time and consequence. People light a lamp of sesame oil, offer black sesame and chant *Om Sham Shanaishcharaya Namah* (ఓం శం శనైశ్చరాయ నమః). His influence is described in our guide to [Sade Sati in 2026](/sade-sati-2026-which-rashis-are-affected/), for Mesha, Meena and Kumbha rashis this year. The meanings are on the [Shani mantras page](/mantras/shani/).

## Hanuman
Hanuman is the devotee of Rama and the protector of those who call on him. Saturday, with Tuesday, is his day, and the Hanuman Chalisa is often recited. See [Hanuman Chalisa meaning](/hanuman-chalisa-meaning-first-five-verses/).

## A simple Saturday routine
1. Rise early and bathe. Wear dark blue or black if you like, or simply clean clothes.
2. Light a sesame oil lamp at the shrine, and another at the doorstep at dusk.
3. Chant one of the mantras above, 11 or 108 times.
4. Eat simply. Many families avoid non-vegetarian food on Saturday.
5. Give something: food, oil, an umbrella or sandals to someone who needs it.
6. Spend the evening quietly, and finish a task you have postponed.

## What the day is really about
Saturn's lesson is patience and honest effort. The tradition says Shani is not cruel, but exact. The most useful Saturday practice is the plainest: do the dull, necessary thing well.
