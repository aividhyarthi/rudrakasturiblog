If you ask a Telugu family which day belongs to Balaji, the answer comes before the question is finished: *Saturday*. In Tirupati the lines are longest on Saturdays. In homes, a lamp is lit a little earlier in the evening, and someone starts chanting *Govinda, Govinda*.

Saturday, *Shanivaram*, is a day with three presences: Venkateswara, Shani and Hanuman. This is a plain guide to all three.

## Why Saturday is Balaji's day
Lord Venkateswara, also called Balaji, Srinivasa and Govinda, stands on the seven hills of Tirumala. The tradition holds that he appeared in this Kali Yuga for those who need help, and that Saturday is his day. Some say it is because his temple became famous on Saturdays. Whatever the reason, in the Telugu country *Shanivaram* has been *Venkateswara swamy* day for generations.

We have told his story in [Balaji of Tirumala: the seven hills, the loan and the lord who waits](/balaji-venkateswara-of-tirumala-seven-hills-one-lord/).

## A simple Saturday vratam
1. **Bathe** in the morning and wear clean clothes.
2. **A light fast,** if it suits your health, with milk and fruit until evening.
3. **Light a pindi deepam,** a lamp made of rice flour or wheat flour mixed with jaggery and ghee, in front of the Lord. Many believe this is especially dear to Venkateswara.
4. **Chant the Govinda namalu,** the names of the Lord, or the *Om Namo Venkateshaya* mantra, 108 times.
5. **Offer tulasi leaves** and a simple naivedyam such as sweet pongal or fruit.
6. **Break the fast** after the evening lamp.

If you can, read or listen to the Venkateswara Suprabhatam, the morning hymn sung at Tirumala.

## Shani and Saturday
In Vedic astrology Saturday is the day of Shani (Saturn), the slow planet of discipline, work and consequence. The traditional worship is simple:
- **A sesame oil lamp** lit at dusk.
- **Charity,** such as food, sesame, black clothes or oil given to someone in need.
- **Honest effort.** Shani is said to reward work, fairness and patience.

If you are in *Sade Sati*, the seven-and-a-half-year period linked with Saturn, read our [Sade Sati guide](/sade-sati-2026-which-rashis-are-affected/). The honest teaching is that it is a time to be steady, not a time to be afraid.

## Why Hanuman
There is a popular story that Hanuman freed Shani from Ravana's captivity and was promised that Shani would not trouble his devotees. This comes from later tradition, not Valmiki. We have told it, and marked it as folk tradition, in [Hanuman and Shani](/hanuman-and-shani-why-hanuman-devotees-are-said-to-be-safe/). Whatever its origin, it is why many families recite the Hanuman Chalisa on Saturdays.

## Narasimha on Saturday
Many families also worship Lakshmi Narasimha on Saturdays, especially in Andhra, where temples such as Ahobilam, Simhachalam and Yadadri are among the best loved.

## A plain way to spend a Saturday
If all of this feels like too much, here is a small version. Light a lamp at dusk. Say *Govinda* nine times. Do something kind for someone who cannot repay you. Spend ten minutes thinking about what you have been avoiding. That is what Saturday asks.

## The Govinda namalu
At Tirumala, pilgrims call out *Govinda, Govinda* all along the way, and the cry is the heart of Saturday worship at home too. The *Govinda namalu* are a long list of names of the Lord, each ending in *Govinda*, sung in many homes before the evening lamp. Even if you only know the first line, say it slowly. The old teachers say the name itself is the offering.

## The seven hills
The hills of Tirumala are called by seven names: Seshadri, Neeladri, Garudadri, Anjanadri, Vrishabhadri, Narayanadri and Venkatadri. Each has its own story. Seshadri is said to be the form of the serpent Adisesha, with Tirumala at its hood. Many pilgrims climb the stairway of Alipiri on foot, as a vow.

## The Saturday oil bath
In many Tamil and Telugu homes, Saturday is the day for an oil bath: warm sesame oil massaged into the head and body before bathing. It is traditionally linked with Shani, and it is a practical piece of care too, a slow start to the day.

## If you cannot visit Tirumala
Most of us cannot go often. The tradition has always said that the Lord is as present in a lamp in your home as on the hill. Many families keep a small picture of Venkateswara and light a pindi deepam every Saturday. Some set aside a small amount each week in a *hundi* at home, and take it to Tirumala when they go. It keeps the thought alive through the year.
