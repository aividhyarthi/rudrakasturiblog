Think of a house-warming day. The new rooms smell of fresh paint and turmeric, the neighbours are on their way, and someone says, "Shall we do Satyanarayana Puja?" Or think of a family evening on Pournami, the moon rising outside, when the elders say it has been a while since the vratam was done. Whichever it is, you may wonder what exactly you will need and where to begin. It is simpler than it looks.

## What is it?

Satyanarayana means Narayana as Truth. The vratam is a vow, and an act of thanks, to Vishnu in this form. Tradition holds that it comes from the Skanda Purana, in a conversation between the sage Narada and Vishnu. The heart of the puja is a set of stories that say the same thing in five ways: keep your word, and do not forget to give thanks when a wish is fulfilled.

## When to do it

- **Pournami.** The full moon day is the most common choice. See upcoming dates on our [Pournami page](/pournami/).
- **Sankranti.** Some families do it on Makara Sankranti or other Sankrantis.
- **Special occasions.** House-warming, a new vehicle, a birth, a wedding, a promotion, an exam result.
- **Any day.** If your family has made a vow, or simply wishes to, there is no day on which it is forbidden. Many prefer the morning.

## What to keep ready

- A clean place for the puja, with a low wooden plank or a small mat, and a rangoli of rice flour if you like.
- A **kalasha**: a pot or lota of water, with mango leaves and a coconut on top, decorated with turmeric and kumkum.
- A **photo or idol of Satyanarayana** or Vishnu, with Lakshmi. A picture of Venkateswara is also used by many families.
- Flowers, tulasi leaves if available, incense, a lamp with ghee or oil, camphor, betel leaves and nuts, fruits.
- **The prasadam.** Rava (sooji) roasted in ghee, cooked with milk or water, sugar and ripe banana, with cardamom. Many families use equal measures of rava, sugar, ghee and milk. Others have their own recipe. Cook it fresh and plenty, since it will be shared.
- A **book of the five stories**, in a language you can read.
- New or clean clothes for the family.

## The steps

1. **Bath and fresh clothes.** Many families fast lightly until the puja is done, but sip water if you need to. Do not strain.
2. **Invoke Ganesha.** Begin with a short prayer to remove obstacles.
3. **Sankalpa.** State the purpose in your own words, or let the priest say it for you. Name your family, your place and the reason for the vratam.
4. **Kalasha puja.** Offer flowers and kumkum to the pot, welcoming the Lord into it.
5. **Worship of Satyanarayana.** Offer flowers, tulasi, incense and the lamp. Many chant the **Ashtottara** (108 names) or simply the name "Satyanarayana" with each flower.
6. **Read the five stories.** One chapter at a time, with everyone listening. The stories are about merchants, kings and cowherds who forgot, or remembered, their vow to the Lord.
7. **Naivedyam.** Offer the prasadam, with a small piece of fruit and betel.
8. **Aarti.** Wave the camphor flame, and ring a bell if you have one.
9. **Prarthana and closing.** Bow down, ask forgiveness for any mistakes, and give thanks.

## Priest or home?

In cities, many families call a priest, who brings his own materials and a rhythm for the whole service, and who explains each step. If you do not have one, you can still do the puja. A printed booklet in Telugu, Tamil, Kannada or another language gives the sankalpa, the steps and the stories. Read slowly. If you stumble on a word, go on. A priest is a guide, and Vishnu is not counting your errors.

## Who can do it?

Anyone who wishes. In many families a husband and wife sit together, but a single person, a widow, a student or an elder can perform it too. Children can sit in, hold the bell and hand over the flowers.

## Giving the prasadam

After the puja, the prasadam is not just yours. Share it with everyone present, and carry some to neighbours and relatives who could not come. Many families make sure that at least one person outside the household receives it. Some also feed a few people. Eat it fresh, the same day if you can.

## What the stories teach

The five chapters follow ordinary people. A poor Brahmin who finds that his small vow brings him comfort. A woodcutter who is helped when he honours the Lord. A merchant who promises a vratam if he is blessed with a child, and then keeps postponing it, and meets trouble at sea. The details differ by book, and families tell them in their own words. The thread is the same: a promise made to God is still a promise, and gratitude should not be put off for a better day. You can sit with your children afterwards and ask which promise they have made, and kept.

## What not to worry about

Do not worry if the rava turns out a little thick or a little loose. Do not worry about a late start, or a small crowd, or a picture instead of a metal idol. The vratam asks for truthfulness and thanks, and both fit in a one-room home as well as a hall.

Do not feel that you must do everything perfectly. If a part of the list is missing, such as no tulasi or no priest, do what you can with a clean heart.
