Almost everyone who takes the spiritual path asks the same question sooner or later: where do I find a guru? And almost everyone who has asked it has also met someone who was happy to be the answer. So let me say a few plain things.

## What the old texts ask for
The Mundaka Upanishad says: to know the truth, one should approach a teacher, carrying fuel in the hand (a sign of humility and readiness to serve), one who is learned in the scriptures and established in truth.

Shankara, in the *Vivekachudamani*, lists the qualities a little more fully. The teacher knows the scriptures, is free from sin and not driven by desire, is the best among those who know the truth, is peaceful like a fire that has burnt out its fuel, and is an ocean of compassion with no reason for it. He is a friend of those who come to him.

Look at the list. It does not say powerful, famous, rich, or able to do miracles. It says: learned, honest, free from craving, kind.

## The student has duties too
The tradition also says what the student must bring. Humility, a real wish to know, a willingness to ask questions, and service. In the Gita, Krishna says to learn *pariprashnena sevaya*: by sincere questioning and by service (4.34). So the relationship goes both ways.

## How to tell a real one
These are questions, not rules:
- **Does his conduct match his teaching?** Watch how he treats people with no power: servants, children, the poor.
- **Is he free about money?** Teaching in return for a fair offering is old and honourable. Constant demands, pressure to give everything, expensive "initiations" and property in his name are warning signs.
- **Can you question him?** A true teacher welcomes doubt. If questions are punished, leave.
- **Does he point to himself or beyond?** A real guru turns your attention to truth. A false one makes himself the point.
- **Does he respect your family and your life?** Be wary of anyone who asks you to cut off your family or give up your work out of the blue.
- **Is there secrecy?** Teaching that must be hidden from your parents or your doctor is suspicious.
- **Do you become calmer and kinder,** or more anxious and dependent? Look at the fruit.

## Warning signs
- Claims of miracles as proof
- Claims to be God, or to be the only way
- Demands for total obedience
- Control of food, sleep, relationships or money
- Sexual advances or secrecy of any kind
- Pressure to recruit others

If any of these appear, leave, and speak to someone you trust. Many people have been hurt, and it is not a sign of foolishness to have been taken in.

## How to begin without waiting
You do not need to find the perfect guru before you start.
1. **Read.** Begin with the Gita and a good commentary. See [which chapter first](/which-bhagavad-gita-chapter-to-read-first/).
2. **Practise.** A daily routine of prayer, breath and silence.
3. **Listen to many teachers.** Observe without committing.
4. **Visit established places.** Mathas with a long tradition, like [Sringeri](/shankaras-four-mathas-sringeri-dwaraka-jyotirmath-puri/), have open doors.
5. **Be patient.** The old saying is that when the student is ready, the teacher appears.
6. **Remember the first guru.** In Hindu tradition, Dakshinamurthy teaches by silence and is the first teacher. And the inner voice you learn to trust is also a teacher.

## The three rare things
In the second verse of the *Vivekachudamani*, Shankara says that three things are very rare and come only by grace: a human birth, the desire for liberation, and the refuge of a great teacher. If you have the first two, you are already ahead.
