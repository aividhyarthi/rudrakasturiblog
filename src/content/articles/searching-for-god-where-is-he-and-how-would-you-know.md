Where is God? Children ask, and adults pretend they have stopped asking. We go to temples and mountains, to Tirumala and Kailash, to saints and sages. Some come back certain. Some come back with more questions.

What do the old texts say?

## "All this is pervaded by the Lord"
The very first verse of the Isha Upanishad says: *Ishavasyam idam sarvam*, all this, whatever moves in this moving world, is pervaded by the Lord. Therefore enjoy through renunciation, and do not covet anyone's wealth.

So the first answer is: everywhere. Not far, not high, not in a special place only. In the sense that the texts speak of, God is not an object among objects. God is the ground of everything.

## In the heart
The Gita says it more intimately. In 10.20, Krishna says: I am the Self seated in the hearts of all beings. And in 18.61: the Lord dwells in the region of the heart of all beings, and turns them around by his power, as if on a wheel.

If that is so, searching for God by travelling is a bit like looking for your spectacles while wearing them. And yet people do travel, and many say that the journey changed them. How do we put the two together?

## The one by which the mind thinks
The Kena Upanishad asks: by whom is the mind sent out to think? By whom does the breath move? By whom are the eyes and ears directed? And its answer is a famous paradox. That which cannot be thought by the mind, but by which the mind thinks, know that to be Brahman, not what people worship here.

The point is that you cannot catch it with a thought, because it is what is doing the thinking.

## Why then temples, mantras and pilgrimage?
Because we are human, and we are not always able to turn straight to the formless. The tradition is generous about this. It offers images, stories, songs and festivals as ways to turn the mind. A temple gives the restless mind a place to rest. A mantra gives it something to hold. A pilgrimage gives it time and effort. Shankara himself wrote hymns to many gods, as we saw in the [Kanakadhara story](/kanakadhara-stotram-the-golden-amla-story/).

So the temple is a door, not the house.

## How would you know?
People who have written about their experience of God say very different things. Some describe visions, but most teachers warn against taking visions as a measure. A more reliable sign is in the person:
- **Less fear,** especially of death and loss.
- **More compassion,** especially for people who are difficult.
- **Less need to prove yourself.**
- **A steadiness** that does not depend on circumstances.

It is the same list that the Gita gives for the person dear to Krishna (chapter 12).

## Where to look
- **In stillness.** Even five minutes a day. See [how to start japa](/mantra-japa-how-to-start-count-and-keep-going/).
- **In service.** Many say they met God in the face of someone they helped.
- **In places that move you.** A temple at dawn, a river, a hill. See [Arunachala](/arunachala-the-hill-that-is-shiva-and-the-walk-around-it/).
- **In questions.** The search itself is a form of devotion.

## If you have doubts
Doubt is not the enemy of faith. In the Gita, Arjuna begins with doubt. If you find that you cannot believe, you can still live well, serve and be kind, and the search can wait until you are ready. See [Gita on doubt](/gita-on-suspicion-and-doubt-when-questions-turn-into-poison/).
