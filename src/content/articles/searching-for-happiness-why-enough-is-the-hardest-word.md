We know the pattern by heart. We want a thing: a phone, a car, a job, a house. We get it. For a few weeks we are delighted. And then we begin to look at the next thing.

It is the oldest pattern in the world, and the Gita has a sentence about it.

## What the Gita says
In the fifth chapter, verse 22, Krishna says: the pleasures born of contact with sense objects are indeed wombs of sorrow. They have a beginning and an end, Arjuna. The wise do not delight in them.

Read it carefully. He does not say they are evil, or that you should not enjoy them. He says they come and go, and so they cannot be where you rest your whole weight. A chair with legs that wobble is a fine chair to sit in for a moment, and a terrible one to build a house on.

## The story of Yayati
We told the story of King Yayati in [the karma series](/yayati-borrowed-youth-and-the-limits-of-desire/). He borrowed his son's youth for a thousand years, and at the end said that desire is not quenched by enjoying it, any more than fire is quenched by pouring ghee. What he learned in a thousand years you can, if you wish, learn in a week.

## A deeper joy
The Taittiriya Upanishad has a lovely passage on bliss. It imagines the happiest young person on earth, healthy, strong, wealthy, and calls that one unit of human joy. Then it climbs, each step a hundred times greater: the joy of the heavenly musicians, of the gods, and so on, up to the bliss of Brahman. The last, it says, is the same joy that a person who is free of desire shares.

The message is that there is a kind of happiness that does not come from getting, and it is available now, in a smaller form, whenever you stop reaching for a moment.

## What is enough?
It is worth asking. Write down, once, what would be enough: enough money for a secure life, enough food, a home, time with family, work you respect. Most people find that the list is shorter than what they chase.

## Practical steps
1. **Gratitude, daily.** Write three things you have. Simple, and it works.
2. **Limit comparison.** See [the Gita on jealousy](/gita-on-jealousy-and-comparison-your-path-is-not-theirs/).
3. **Pause before buying.** Wait 48 hours for anything you don't need.
4. **Savings, not spending, for security.** Much of our craving is fear, and a modest reserve calms it.
5. **Spend on experiences and people,** not only objects.
6. **Give.** Generosity reliably increases happiness, and it is built into every festival.
7. **Be present.** Enjoy a meal, a sunrise, a conversation, with attention. Enjoyment without clinging is the practice the Gita describes.

## What not to take from this
Don't use the idea of detachment to feel superior to people who like nice things. Don't use it to avoid working for a decent life. The tradition speaks of artha and kama as proper goals. It asks you to hold them lightly.

## For days when nothing seems to help
If you feel no pleasure in anything, for weeks, it may not be a spiritual question. It can be depression, and it can be treated. Please speak to a doctor. See [yoga when you feel low](/yoga-when-you-feel-low-small-practices-for-a-heavy-mood/).
