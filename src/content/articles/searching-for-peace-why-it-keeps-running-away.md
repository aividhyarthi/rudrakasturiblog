When I first start talking about peace with someone, they usually say the same sentence: "I'll be at peace once this is over." Once the exam is done. Once the daughter is married. Once the loan is cleared. Once the promotion comes.

And then the exam is over, and another thing begins. Peace, it seems, keeps running ahead of us.

## What the Gita says
In the second chapter, verse 66, Krishna says something that sounds blunt: for the one who is not steady, there is no wisdom, no meditation, and for one with no peace, how can there be happiness?

Notice the order. Peace comes first, and happiness comes from it. Most of us think the other way round.

Then in verse 70, he gives a picture. The ocean is always being filled by rivers entering it, and yet it stays steady. In the same way, the person into whom all desires flow, and who is not disturbed by them, attains peace. The person who runs after desires does not.

Think of that picture. The ocean does not stop the rivers. It does not wait for them to stop. It is simply wide enough, and deep enough, to take them in.

## What this means
Peace is not the absence of events. It is a mind that is wide and steady enough for events. You cannot build that by arranging your life perfectly, because no life can be arranged. You build it by training attention.

## Four things that help
**1. Stop postponing.** Ask: is there one minute today in which I can be quiet? Take it. The practice begins now, not when the troubles end.

**2. Reduce the inputs.** The mind takes in more news, messages and opinions in a day than our grandparents did in a month. Choose two times a day to look at the phone, not twenty.

**3. Do honest work, and do it fully.** The Gita says peace comes to the one who acts without clinging to the results ([2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/)). A divided mind, working while thinking of rest and resting while thinking of work, never settles.

**4. Keep a daily practice.** Ten minutes of prayer, [breathing](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/) or quiet sitting each morning, for months, builds the ocean.

## A prayer for the search
The oldest peace prayers in Sanskrit are very short. One is from the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad:
*Asato Ma Sad Gamaya, Tamaso Ma Jyotir Gamaya, Mrityor Ma Amritam Gamaya.*
*Lead me from untruth to truth, from darkness to light, from death to immortality.*
It is followed by *Om Shanti Shanti Shanti*, peace thrice repeated, for the three kinds of disturbance: from within, from other beings and from nature.

## When you can't find it
Sometimes peace stays away because of something that needs attention, such as depression, anxiety, grief or a harmful situation. These are not spiritual failures. If the restlessness does not lift, please speak to a doctor or counsellor. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is free and open at all hours.

## What peace looks like
It looks very ordinary. A morning when you notice the light. A conversation in which you listen. An evening when the worry is still there but you are not its slave.
