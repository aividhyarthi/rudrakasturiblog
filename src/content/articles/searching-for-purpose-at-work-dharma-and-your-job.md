You spend about a third of your waking life at work. For many people that third feels empty. The job pays the bills, but the question lingers: is this what I am here for?

The Gita has a lot to say about work, and none of it is "quit and go to the Himalayas."

## Devotion through your own work
In the eighteenth chapter, verse 45, Krishna says that each person attains perfection by being devoted to their own work. And in verse 46, he adds: by worshipping the one from whom all beings come, through his own work, a person attains perfection.

This is a bold idea. Work itself can be worship, an offering. A teacher teaching well, a nurse caring with attention, a driver driving safely and an engineer building something that won't fall are all doing something sacred, if it is done with care and for others.

## Skill in action
In 2.50, Krishna says *yogah karmasu kaushalam*: yoga is skill in action. He doesn't ask for a special spiritual activity. He asks for excellence and full attention in whatever you do, with a mind not thrown by success or failure.

## Your own path
In 3.35, he says it is better to do your own dharma imperfectly than another's perfectly. This is not an argument for staying in a job for ever. It is a warning against comparing yourself with other people's careers. See [the Gita on comparison](/gita-on-jealousy-and-comparison-your-path-is-not-theirs/).

## Finding purpose in an ordinary job
Here are some questions:
1. **Who does this work serve?** Even a back-office job serves customers, colleagues and a family at home.
2. **What part can I do with real care?** Choose one piece and do it excellently.
3. **What do I learn here?** Skills, patience, how to work with people.
4. **Am I honest in it?** Truthfulness in work is a dharma of its own.
5. **What do I do with the money?** Providing for family and giving a part away is dharma too.
6. **Can I add a small purpose?** Mentoring a junior, improving a process, being kind.

## When to change
The Gita does not ask you to stay in harm. If a job asks you to lie, to cheat or to hurt others, or if it is damaging your health, dharma may require that you leave. If you are bored but the job is honest, you can look for more interesting work while you do this one well.

Use the process from [Gita 2.7 on confusion](/gita-on-confusion-when-you-cannot-decide-what-is-right/) to decide. Gather facts, ask advisers and test small changes before a big leap.

## A caution about passion
Many people say "follow your passion." The Gita's version is gentler: do what is in front of you with attention, and find out, step by step, what suits you. Passion often comes after skill, not before.

## When you are burnt out
If you hate your work and feel tired all the time, see [the Gita on burnout](/gita-on-burnout-the-middle-path-of-eating-sleeping-and-working/). And if it is making you low or anxious, talk to a counsellor.
