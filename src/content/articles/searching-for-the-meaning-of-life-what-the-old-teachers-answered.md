At some point most of us, usually late at night, ask: what is all this for? We work, we eat, we sleep, we watch the news. Is there a point?

Indian tradition has been asking this for three thousand years, and its answers are practical.

## The four goals
The old texts speak of four *purusharthas*, the aims of human life.
1. **Dharma:** doing what is right, your duties, truthfulness and compassion.
2. **Artha:** livelihood, security, wealth. The tradition does not despise it.
3. **Kama:** enjoyment, love, beauty, art and desire.
4. **Moksha:** freedom, the end of the sense of lack and fear.

Notice what is on the list. Wealth and pleasure are not sins. The old thinkers saw that a life without them is thin. But they placed dharma first and moksha last, as the frame inside which the others fit.

## A way to use it
When you feel lost, you can check the four.
- **Dharma:** Am I being honest, kind and responsible in my relationships and work?
- **Artha:** Do I have enough to live and a plan for the future?
- **Kama:** Do I have enjoyment and love in my life, or am I only working?
- **Moksha:** Do I have time to ask who I am, in prayer, silence or study?

Usually one is neglected. That is the one to begin with.

## What Shankara said
In the second verse of the *Vivekachudamani*, Shankara says that three things are hard to get and come only by grace: to be born human, to want liberation, and to find a great teacher. He is not trying to make you feel guilty. He is pointing out how much you already have. A human mind that can ask "why am I here?" is the rarest thing in the universe, as far as we know.

## The Gita's version
Arjuna also asked, on the battlefield, "I don't know what is right." Krishna's answer was not a single sentence. It was a way of living: do your duty, without clinging, with a mind steady in success and failure, and offer your actions to something larger than yourself. See [Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/) and [how to decide](/gita-on-confusion-when-you-cannot-decide-what-is-right/).

## Smaller answers
Not everyone finds meaning in a grand idea. Many people find it in:
- **Someone else.** Caring for a child, a parent, a friend.
- **Work that matters.** Teaching, healing, building, growing food.
- **Beauty.** Music, a temple at dawn, a garden.
- **Service.** A little time each week given freely.
- **Understanding.** Reading, asking, practising.

The tradition says that meaning is not found like a lost key. It is made, in what we choose to care about.

## If it feels empty
A feeling that life has no point can be a normal passing phase, a sign of a transition or, sometimes, depression. If it feels heavy, if it has lasted weeks, or if you think of harming yourself, please talk to a doctor or counsellor, or call Tele-MANAS on 14416.

## A question to carry
Instead of "what is the meaning of life?", try asking, "What does this day need from me?" It is easier to answer, and a life is made of days.
