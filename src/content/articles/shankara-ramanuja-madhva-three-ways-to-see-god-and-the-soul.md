It is a funny thing about Indian philosophy. Three of its greatest teachers read the same texts, the Upanishads, the Gita and the Brahma Sutras, and came away with three different answers. They did not stop respecting each other's logic. They argued, wrote and left behind schools that are all alive today.

## The question they all asked
Who is the soul, who is God, and what is the relationship between them? Are they one, different, or something in between?

## Shankara: not two
Shankara, whose life we looked at earlier in [this series](/adi-shankaracharya-the-boy-from-kaladi-who-walked-across-india/), said that the soul and Brahman are one and the same. Our sense of being separate comes from ignorance. Seeing the truth is liberation. This school is **Advaita**, non-dual.

## Ramanuja: one, but with parts
Ramanuja, who taught in the 11th and 12th centuries from the temple of Srirangam, said that the soul, the world and God are all real, but the souls and the world are like the body of God. They are different from God, but cannot exist without him, as your body cannot exist without you. This school is **Vishishtadvaita**, qualified non-dualism.

For Ramanuja, devotion and surrender to Vishnu is the main path. He opened the temple gates to all who came to hear the teaching and is remembered, according to tradition, for climbing the temple tower at Thirukoshtiyur and calling out a sacred mantra to the crowd below, so that everyone could hear it. Followers of this school are the Sri Vaishnavas, and their centre in the South is Srirangam.

## Madhva: always different
Madhvacharya, born in the 13th century near Udupi in Karnataka, taught that God and the soul are eternally different. There are five eternal differences, he said: between God and the soul, between God and matter, between one soul and another, between souls and matter, and between one piece of matter and another. This school is **Dvaita**, dualism.

For Madhva, Vishnu is the supreme lord, and the soul is his servant. Liberation is not merging, but living in his presence forever. Madhva founded the Krishna temple at Udupi, and eight mathas still manage the worship there in turn.

## A way to remember
Think of a drop of water and the sea.
- Shankara: the drop is the sea.
- Ramanuja: the drop is part of the sea and cannot be without it.
- Madhva: the drop and the sea are always two, though the drop is held by it.

## Why it matters to ordinary people
You may meet these schools without knowing it. A Smarta family in Andhra will usually follow Shankara. A Sri Vaishnava family will follow Ramanuja. Many Kannada Madhva families follow Madhva. They worship in similar ways, but their explanations differ.

## Which one is right?
There is no easy answer, and the tradition does not ask you to choose in a hurry. Many people find that devotion comes first and philosophy follows. If you would like to start with the most radical of the three, read [Advaita in plain words](/advaita-vedanta-in-plain-words/).
