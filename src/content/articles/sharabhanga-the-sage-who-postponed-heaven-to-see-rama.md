**Source:** Valmiki Ramayana, Aranyakanda (chapters 4 and 5).

Here is a scene that is easy to skip because it comes just after the great exile begins. The dust of the road hangs in the air, and in a clearing in the forest we find a chariot in the sky and an old man at his fire.

## Indra at the hermitage
As Rama, Sita and Lakshmana enter the forest of Dandaka, they see a divine chariot in the sky, and a great being standing beside a sage's hermitage. It is Indra, king of the gods. Seeing Rama approach, Indra withdraws for the moment.

The sage is Sharabhanga. He had earned, through long austerities, a place in the world of Brahma. Indra had come to take him there. And Sharabhanga had said: *not yet.*

The text says that he had learned Rama would be coming to Dandaka, and he chose to stay on earth until he could see him. That is the whole drama of this scene. A man who could walk into heaven at any moment decided to stay a little longer for a single meeting.

## The meeting
When Rama arrives, Sharabhanga welcomes him warmly. Then he does something touching. He says that by his austerities he has earned the worlds of Brahma and others, and offers them all to Rama. Rama thanks him, and says that he would rather win such worlds himself, by his own conduct. He asks only where to find a place to live in the forest.

The old sage does not argue. He advises Rama to go to the hermitage of Sutikshna, a sage nearby, who will guide him further. Then, in the sight of Rama, Sita and Lakshmana, he lights a fire and enters it. Out of the flames, the text says, he rises in a young and radiant form and ascends to the world of Brahma.

## Why did he wait?
There are different ways to read it.

*The devotional reading:* Sharabhanga had loved Rama long before they met, and would not leave without seeing him. This reading is the most common one. The tradition values this longing: the wish to see the beloved before the end.

*The practical reading:* He had a job to do. He was the first of a series of hermits who would tell Rama about the plight of the Dandaka forest, and guide him from one hermitage to the next.

*The human reading:* He was an old man who wanted to meet a person he had heard about, and he would not be hurried by heaven.

All three are in the text, and none excludes the others.

## What it teaches
- **Some meetings are worth waiting for.** Even for someone who could leave right now.
- **To give is also to be generous in receiving.** Rama's refusal was courteous. He did not insult the old man's gift.
- **Passing the torch matters.** Sharabhanga's last act was to send Rama to the next person who could help.
- **A good end is part of a good life.** The text treats his departure with great calm.

## Where to think of it
The forest of Dandaka covered a vast area of central and southern India, and traditions attach Rama's stay to many places, from Panchavati near Nashik to Bhadrachalam on the Godavari, where the Rama temple draws crowds at Sri Rama Navami.
