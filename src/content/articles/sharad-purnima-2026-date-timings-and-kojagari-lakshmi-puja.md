Sharad Purnima, the full moon of the Ashwayuja month, is considered the brightest and most beautiful full moon of the year, at the end of the monsoon when the sky is clear. It is a night for moonlight, Lakshmi and kheer.

## Date and timings (Hyderabad)
- **Pournami tithi begins:** Sunday, 25 October 2026, at 11:56 AM.
- **Pournami tithi ends:** Monday, 26 October 2026, at 9:41 AM.
- **Moonrise on 25 October:** about 5:03 PM, close to sunset (5:48 PM). The moonlit night of Sharad Purnima is therefore the night of **Sunday, 25 October**.
- **Daytime Pournami vratams** such as Satyanarayana puja are usually kept on Monday, 26 October, when Pournami is present at sunrise. Some temples and families follow Sunday instead, so check with yours.

See the full [Pournami dates and timings](/pournami/october-2026/).

## Kojagari: the night of Lakshmi
In many parts of the country, the night is called Kojagari. The name is traced to the words *ko jagarti*, "who is awake?", the question Lakshmi is said to ask as she moves through the night, blessing those who are awake and worshipping. Families clean the house, light lamps, worship Lakshmi, and stay up with prayer, song or games.

## Kheer in the moonlight
The best-known custom is to cook milk and rice (kheer or payasam), place it in the moonlight through the night and eat it in the morning as prasad. The tradition describes the autumn moon as especially cooling. Keep the vessel covered with a thin cloth against insects.

## Other associations
- **Raas Purnima:** in the Vaishnava tradition, this is the night of Krishna's Raas with the gopis.
- **Valmiki Jayanti:** many calendars observe the birth anniversary of the sage Valmiki, author of the Ramayana, on this Purnima.
- **Kumara Purnima:** in parts of Odisha and Karnataka, unmarried girls worship the Moon for a good husband.

## How to observe it
1. Take a morning bath and keep a light fast if you wish.
2. In the evening, light a lamp and worship Lakshmi with *Om Shreem Mahalakshmyai Namah* ([Lakshmi mantras](/mantras/lakshmi/)).
3. When the Moon rises, offer arghya (water) to it, and look at it for a while.
4. Share the kheer the next morning.
