The Puranas tell the story in several ways. In the commonly told version, Brahma had five heads, and the fifth spoke with arrogance, claiming that he alone was supreme. Shiva, in his form of Bhairava, cut that head off.

## The skull that would not fall
The act brought the weight of Brahmahatya, the sin of killing a Brahmin, and the skull of Brahma stuck to Shiva's hand. He wandered the world as Bhikshatana, a naked beggar with the skull as his bowl. In some tellings the food placed in it did not fill it, and his wandering continued through holy places and forests.

At last he came to Kashi, where the skull fell from his hand at a place still called Kapala Mochana (the release of the skull), and the burden lifted.

## What the story teaches
- **Anger acts first and answers later.** The head spoke in pride, and the answer was swift, but the cost followed.
- **Not even the lord of destruction is exempt.** Whatever one thinks of the act, the story insists that it left a mark which had to be carried.
- **Penance is a journey, not a moment.** Shiva does not talk himself out of it. He walks.
- **Release comes when the work is done.** The skull falls only at the end, and in a place of surrender.

Versions of this story differ among the Shiva Purana, Skanda Purana and regional traditions. The details change, but the teaching is the same.

## For today
If you have lost your temper and caused harm, the first step is to carry it honestly instead of denying it. Apologise, make it right in a practical way, and give it time. Release comes slowly, and it comes.
