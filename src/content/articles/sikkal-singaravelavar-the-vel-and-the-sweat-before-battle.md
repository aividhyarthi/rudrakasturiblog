At Sikkal, the story of Murugan's battle begins with a gift from his mother.

Every year during the Skanda Sashti observances, devotees remember the moment Goddess Parvati gives her son the Vel, the divine spear with which he will defeat Surapadman. But Sikkal is known for one more detail. After the ceremony, devotees say that drops of moisture appear on the image of Singaravelavar, as though Murugan himself is perspiring before the battle.

The temple's tradition gives this moment a powerful meaning: even the divine warrior is shown at the threshold of a difficult task.

## Sikkal and Singaravelavar

Sikkal Singaravelavar temple is in Tamil Nadu's Nagapattinam region. The presiding form of Murugan is closely associated with the Vel, the weapon given by Goddess Parvati.

The temple's Skanda Sashti celebrations focus on the sequence before the battle with Surapadman. The Vel is not simply a weapon. In the devotional story, it is the power and blessing of the Goddess placed in her son's hands.

## The mother gives the Vel

In the familiar account of Skanda, Surapadman becomes a force of adharma and the devas seek help. Murugan is sent to confront him. Before the battle, Goddess Parvati gives Murugan the Vel.

At Sikkal, this episode is enacted as a central festival moment. The giving of the Vel is remembered with ritual, music and the gathering of devotees. The weapon represents divine knowledge and the power to cut through ignorance as much as it represents the force needed to defeat an adversary.

The battle is not treated as a random act of violence. It is the restoration of dharma.

## Why devotees speak of sweat

The unusual part of Sikkal's tradition comes after the Vel ceremony. Devotees describe the image of Singaravelavar as developing beads of moisture, which they interpret as sweat.

In the story, Murugan is about to go to war. The perspiration is understood as the sign of the effort and intensity of the moment. The deity is not seen as distant from the event. The festival makes the story present, and the image becomes part of that living remembrance.

It is important to describe this carefully. Moisture on a temple image can have physical explanations connected with temperature, humidity, ritual liquids or the surroundings. The devotional meaning belongs to the temple tradition; it should not be presented as scientific proof of a miracle.

## The deeper meaning of the Vel

The Vel is often understood as the sharpness of wisdom. It removes confusion. It cuts through arrogance. It helps a person see what must be confronted.

There is another detail worth noticing. Murugan receives the Vel from his mother. Strength is not shown as something he claims by himself. It is entrusted to him. The story begins with a blessing and a responsibility.

That makes the Sikkal festival more than a dramatic prelude to battle. It is a reminder that power must be guided by wisdom and used in the service of dharma.

## Skanda Sashti at Sikkal

Skanda Sashti is observed over six days in the Tamil month of Aippasi. The festival remembers Murugan's campaign against Surapadman and culminates in the celebration of victory. Sikkal's Vel-giving ceremony is one of the distinctive moments associated with this observance.

Festival dates follow the Tamil calendar and change each year. Check the temple's current announcements for the exact schedule, crowd arrangements and darshan information before travelling.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens at Sikkal during Skanda Sashti?

The temple commemorates Goddess Parvati giving the Vel to Murugan before his battle with Surapadman. This is the defining ritual moment associated with Singaravelavar.

### Does the Murugan idol really sweat?

Devotees describe moisture appearing on the image after the Vel ceremony and interpret it as sweat. The observation and its devotional meaning should be distinguished from a scientifically verified explanation.

### What does the Vel represent?

The Vel is the divine weapon given by Parvati to Murugan. In devotional interpretation it also represents wisdom that cuts through ignorance and restores dharma.
