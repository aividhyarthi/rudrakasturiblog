Most temples show you the deity as he is. Simhachalam shows you the deity as he *hides*.

On the hill of Simhachalam, a few kilometres above Visakhapatnam, the Lord of the temple stands beneath a thick coating of sandalwood paste, shaped into an almost abstract form. For most of the year, that is all you see. Only on one day, the festival of Akshaya Tritiya, is the covering lifted for a few hours, and the true form appears.

## The hill
Simhachalam, the "lion hill", rises about 240 metres above the Bay of Bengal coast. It is known in Telugu as *Simhadri*, and the deity as *Simhadri Appanna*, "the father of Simhadri". Locals call him Appanna with the same affection you would have for a grandfather. The temple is among the oldest and richest in Andhra Pradesh.

## The story of Prahlada
According to the temple tradition, this is the place where Vishnu, after killing Hiranyakashipu in the form of Narasimha, came to rest. The boy Prahlada, the demon's devoted son, built the first shrine here. After a flood buried it, the Lord is said to have been rediscovered in later ages, and the temple built again. This is the temple's own tradition, repeated in the Puranas and stories. The oldest inscriptions on the walls date from the eleventh century, and the temple shows the hand of many dynasties.

## The deity
The deity is called **Varaha Lakshmi Narasimha**. The form is a unique blend: a human body with the head of a boar and the head of a lion, a combination of two of Vishnu's avatars, Varaha, the boar who lifted the earth, and Narasimha, the man-lion who saved Prahlada. The Lord is accompanied by Lakshmi.

The form is only an outline under the paste. The sandalwood is applied, layer on layer, for the whole year, and pilgrims see only a smooth pillar.

## Chandanotsavam
On **Akshaya Tritiya**, in the month of Vaishakha (usually April or May), the temple holds the *Chandanotsavam*. The layers of paste are carefully removed, and for a few hours, devotees may see the original form, the *Nijaroopa darshanam*. Hundreds of thousands come. It is said that the tradition of keeping the deity covered is to cool the fierce Narasimha, who came with anger, and also that the sandalwood is a treasured offering. By the end of the day the paste is applied again, and the Lord is hidden for another year.

## Giri Pradakshina
On Ashadha Pournami (the full moon of the month of Ashadha, usually July), more than a hundred thousand people walk around the hill, a distance of about 32 km, in the night, calling *Govinda*. It is among the biggest annual walks in Andhra, and a lovely sight.

## The Kappa Stambham
Inside the temple, a pillar called the *Kappa Stambham* is held to be sacred. Devotees embrace it, especially those who are childless or ill, believing that it brings relief. The hold of such beliefs is part of why the temple is so loved.

## Who built it up
The temple was patronised by the Eastern Gangas of Kalinga, the Kakatiyas, and later, in 1516, by Krishnadevaraya of Vijayanagara, who came here after his campaign in Odisha and made rich gifts, recorded on the walls. It is one of the finest examples of the Kalinga style in the South.

## Visiting
- **From Visakhapatnam,** Simhachalam is about 15 km by road. Buses and taxis run all day.
- **Dress modestly,** and expect queues on weekends and festivals.
- **Prasadam:** a sweet made of rice and jaggery, and the famous Simhachalam *pulihora*.
- **If you are planning Chandanotsavam,** go early and check the dates on the temple site.

## Why sandalwood?
Sandalwood paste is a cooling substance. Narasimha is the fierce form of Vishnu, who emerged in anger and is shown with claws and a roaring face. The old explanation for the paste is that it soothes him: a god who has just torn open a demon's chest is given something cold and sweet-smelling to rest in. Another explanation is more devotional: the Lord is so splendid that he can be seen only rarely, and the long hiding makes the day of seeing him unforgettable. Whichever you prefer, the practice is lovely. Imagine a year of devotion, of walking up the hill to bow before a smooth, fragrant shape, and then on one day the covering is lifted.

## The sculpture of the temple
Beyond the sanctum, the temple has beautifully carved pillars, a hall with a hundred pillars, and sculptures of every period. The sculpted figures, among them dancers and musicians, are an education in the art of Kalinga. If you go, spend an hour walking around the outer corridors, not only in the queue.

## What to eat and take home
The temple offers prasadam such as *pulihora* (tamarind rice) and *chakkera pongali* (sweet pongal). Many pilgrims also buy small sandalwood paste packets from the temple counters. Check that you buy them from the official stalls.

## A thought
There is something very Indian about a god who is shown only once a year. It says that *seeing* is not the only way to know, and that a thing you have waited for all year is worth more. For many families, the waiting is the prayer.
