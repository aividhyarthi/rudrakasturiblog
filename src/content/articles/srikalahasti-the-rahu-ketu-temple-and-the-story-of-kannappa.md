Almost every South Indian family knows two things about Srikalahasti. It is where you go if your horoscope shows Rahu and Ketu trouble. And it is where a hunter once gave his eyes to Shiva. The second story explains the first.

## Where it is
Srikalahasti is a temple town on the bank of the Swarnamukhi river, in Tirupati district of Andhra Pradesh, about 36 km from Tirupati. The temple is built against a rocky hill, and some parts are cut into the rock. It is one of the five Pancha Bhoota temples, the one linked with *vayu*, the air.

## The name
The name is a story. *Sri* means spider, *kala* means snake and *hasti* means elephant. According to tradition, each of the three worshipped Shiva here in their own way and attained liberation. The spider spun a web over the lingam, the snake placed a gem on it, and the elephant poured water. They quarrelled over who was the greater devotee, and each died in the fight, and all three were freed.

## The air lingam
Inside the sanctum, there is a lamp that is said to flicker constantly, even though there is no wind. Devotees say it shows the presence of Vayu, the air, in the lingam. The lingam is not touched by priests during abhishekam. Instead the ritual is done on a different surface.

## The story of Kannappa
The best-loved story is of the hunter Thinnadu, in Tamil, also called Kannappa Nayanar and in Telugu *Bhakta Kannappa*.

Thinnadu was a tribal hunter in the hills near the temple. One day, he found a lingam on the hill and felt a pull toward it. He did not know the rituals. He brought water in his mouth, flowers in his hair and meat from the hunt as an offering. A Brahmin priest, who worshipped properly each morning, was horrified.

One night, the priest hid and watched. Thinnadu was keeping vigil, and he saw that one eye of the lingam was bleeding. He took out his own eye and placed it on the lingam. The bleeding stopped, but then the other eye of the lingam began to bleed. He prepared to offer the other eye too. Placing his foot on the lingam to mark the spot, he raised his knife. Shiva stopped him.

The story is told to say that what the god sees is the heart, not the ritual. It is retold in the Tamil Periya Puranam, in a famous Telugu poem, and in Telugu cinema.

## Rahu-Ketu pooja
Today most visitors come for the Rahu-Ketu pooja. In astrology, Rahu and Ketu are two shadow points, linked with obstacles, delays and sudden changes. People who are told that their chart shows a Rahu-Ketu problem, or *kala sarpa dosha*, come to Srikalahasti and have the pooja done in a special hall. Devotees wear specific clothes and offer a ritual with a silver or copper serpent image.

As with other beliefs of this kind, it is entirely a personal choice. Astrology cannot be verified, and nobody should accept a diagnosis out of fear. Many people go for the peace of mind and the beauty of the place.

## Visiting
- The temple opens early and has several ticket levels for rituals. Use the official counter and be careful with agents.
- Dress modestly. Footwear stays outside.
- Avoid festival days and weekends if you want quiet.
- Combine with Tirupati, Tiruchanur and the Tirumala temple.

## A closer look at Kannappa's lesson
Many temple stories praise perfect ritual. This one praises something else. Thinnadu knows no mantras, no purity rules, no proper offerings. What he has is a child's directness: he sees a god who seems to be in pain, and he cannot leave.

Look at how the story sets up the contrast. The priest has done everything right, and he is shocked. The hunter has done everything "wrong", and he is the one the god calls his own. The tradition does not say the priest was bad. It says that love without form can reach as far as form without love. Both have a place, and the story asks us to remember which one is the root.

If you read the tale with a young person, it makes a good starting point for a talk about what we really give when we give something: a gift, a favour or a prayer.

## The festival to know
Srikalahasti is especially lively around Maha Shivaratri, when the temple holds a long festival with processions and large crowds. If you prefer a quiet visit, avoid this period. If you enjoy atmosphere, it is memorable.

## A calm way to think about Rahu and Ketu
In Vedic astrology, Rahu and Ketu are not planets in the usual sense. They are the two points where the Moon's path crosses the Sun's path in the sky, and tradition treats them as shadowy influences. You can read more in [Navagraha: the nine planets in Vedic astrology](/navagraha-the-nine-planets-in-vedic-astrology/).

If you have been told that these two are troubling you, remember three things.

- **Astrology is a belief,** not a proven science, and you are free to take it or leave it.
- **Nobody should frighten you** or ask for large sums to put you right.
- **Ordinary effort helps most.** Work, health care, patience and kind relationships matter far more than any ritual.

## Questions people ask
**Do I have to do the Rahu-Ketu pooja to visit?**
No. Many pilgrims come only for darshan of Shiva and Gnanaprasunambika, the goddess of the temple, and for the story of Kannappa.

**Is it only for people with a chart problem?**
No. Families of all kinds visit, some for the pooja, some for the Pancha Bhoota circuit, some for the hill and the river.
