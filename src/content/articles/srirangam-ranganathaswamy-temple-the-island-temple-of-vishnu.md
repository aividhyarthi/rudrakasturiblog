Imagine a temple that is a small town. The streets inside it have shops, houses and temples, and the whole thing is surrounded by seven walls. That is Srirangam, on an island between the Kaveri and Kollidam rivers, near Tiruchirappalli.

## What it is
The temple of Ranganathaswamy, Vishnu in his reclining form on the serpent Adisesha, is counted as the first of the 108 Divya Desams, the most sacred Vishnu temples in the tradition of the Sri Vaishnavas. It covers around 156 acres. There are seven concentric walls, and 21 gopurams. The tallest, the Raja Gopuram, is about 73 metres and has thirteen levels. It was completed in the 1980s after centuries of waiting.

## How the Lord came to Srirangam
The popular story says that Brahma worshipped a divine image of Vishnu, which passed down the Ikshvaku family until Rama gave it to Vibhishana, Ravana's brother, who had helped him in the war. Vibhishana set off for Lanka with the image, but on the banks of the Kaveri, he put it on the ground to rest. When he tried to lift it, the image would not move. Vishnu spoke, and said he wished to stay here. Vibhishana accepted, and asked that Ranganatha face south, toward Lanka. That is why the Lord at Srirangam faces south, which is unusual.

## Ramanuja and Srirangam
The great teacher Ramanuja lived and taught here, and he organised the temple's worship and administration in a form that is largely followed today. Tradition says that his body was kept in the temple after his death and can be seen in a shrine inside the complex, a belief that draws many pilgrims. ## Vaikuntha Ekadashi
The temple's biggest celebration is Vaikuntha Ekadashi, in the Tamil month of Margazhi (December to January). The festival is called Adhyayana Utsavam and lasts for twenty-one days. It features the recitation of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, the collection of four thousand hymns by the Alwar saints. On the day of Vaikuntha Ekadashi, a special northern door, Paramapada Vasal, is opened before dawn, and the deity's procession passes through it, followed by thousands of devotees. In 2026, Vaikuntha Ekadashi falls around 20 December.

## What else to see
- The **Ranga Vilasa Mandapam**, with fine carved pillars.
- The **Sesha Mandapam**, with its rearing horse pillars.
- A **museum and the roof view**, from which you can see the whole complex and the Kaveri.
- The nearby **Jambukeswarar temple at Thiruvanaikaval**, one of the five Pancha Bhoota Shiva temples, is only a few kilometres away.

## Planning a visit
- Early morning is best, and the temple has a long midday break.
- Large areas have to be walked barefoot. In summer, the stone is hot, so wear socks until the entrance if permitted, and carry water.
- Several ticketed darshan options exist. Use the official counter.
- Allow half a day, or more.

## A closer look at the reclining Lord
Ranganatha lies on the coils of Adisesha, facing south. For many devotees the south-facing posture carries a special meaning, and the old tradition says the Lord looks toward Lanka out of kindness to Vibhishana. A beloved idea in the Sri Vaishnava tradition is that Vishnu rests here not out of tiredness, but as a host who stays available to anyone who comes.

## The Alwars and Srirangam
Many of the twelve Alwar saints sang about Srirangam. Their Tamil hymns are gathered in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, and the Margazhi recitations in this temple are among the most important in the tradition. Andal, the girl-poet of Srivilliputtur, is remembered for her love of Ranganatha, and her Tiruppavai is sung across Tamil Nadu in the same month. Thondaradippodi Alwar is also known for his hymns here. If you are curious about the Alwars, listen to a recording of the Tiruppavai before your visit. It will make the temple feel familiar.

## Questions people ask
**Why do people call it the first Divya Desam?**
Because in the traditional list of 108 sacred Vishnu temples, Srirangam comes first. It is often called Bhuloka Vaikuntha, meaning Vaikuntha on earth.

**Can non-Hindus enter?**
Rules differ between parts of large temples, and they can change. Check at the entrance or the official office before you go.

**What should I wear?**
Simple, modest clothes. Many temples in Tamil Nadu ask men and women to follow a dress code, so ask on arrival.

**Is it crowded?**
It can be, especially on festival days and around Vaikuntha Ekadashi. On an ordinary weekday morning it is far calmer, and the sheer size of the place means you can find quiet corners.

## Seeing it with patience
Do not try to see every gopuram. Walk slowly, sit for ten minutes in a pillared corridor, and watch families come and go. Srirangam is more a place to be in than a list of things to tick off.
