At Srisailam, Kartika Purnima is marked not only by rows of lamps but also by a ritual involving fire. It is known as *Jwala Thoranam*. The name describes a ceremonial arch of flame, and the observance is associated with the worship of Lord Mallikarjuna during the sacred month of Kartika.

A report on the Srisailam festival programme lists Jwala Thoranam for **Tuesday, 24 November 2026**, on Kartika Purnima, at Gangadhara Mandapam. A separate news report also describes the temple's Kartika celebrations. Confirm the latest timings and arrangements with the Devasthanam before travelling.

## What does Jwala Thoranam mean?

In Telugu, *jwala* means flame and *thoranam* refers to an arch or festoon. A thoranam is commonly seen at the entrance to a home or temple during auspicious occasions. In this observance, the idea of the arch is expressed through a ceremonial arrangement of fire.

The ritual belongs to the wider tradition of lighting lamps during Kartika. Across the month, devotees light deepams at home, in temples and near water bodies. At Srisailam, the fire arch is one of the distinctive observances associated with the full moon.

The fire should not be treated as a performance or public stunt. It is part of worship, and the ritual is conducted within the temple's arrangements. Visitors should watch only from areas permitted by the authorities.

## Why is Kartika associated with lamps?

Kartika is a month in which lamp lighting has a special place in many Hindu traditions. A lamp is a simple but powerful symbol. Its flame represents awareness, knowledge and the effort to move away from ignorance.

In homes, a small clay lamp is enough to mark the evening. In temples, the same idea can become a collective act, with many lamps lit together. The repeated act of lighting a lamp gives the month its rhythm: a moment of prayer at the start or close of the day.

Different regions explain the merit of Kartika lamp worship through Puranic stories and local temple traditions. Jwala Thoranam should be described as a Srisailam observance unless a specific textual source is identified for a wider origin story. It is better not to attach an unverified legend to the ritual.

## Why is Srisailam important?

Srisailam is home to Sri Mallikarjuna Swamy and Goddess Bhramaramba. Mallikarjuna is counted among the twelve Jyotirlingas, while Bhramaramba is revered as a Shakti Peetha in traditional lists.

The temple stands in the Nallamala hills, close to the Krishna River. Kartika is a major pilgrimage period here. Devotees visit for Shiva worship, lamp offerings and the special programmes arranged through the month.

Jwala Thoranam is part of this larger calendar. It should not be confused with the Maha Deepam lit on Arunachala hill at Tiruvannamalai. Both involve sacred flame, but they belong to different temple traditions and have different ritual settings.

## What pilgrims should plan for

Kartika Purnima is one of the busy days at Srisailam. If you plan to visit, check the temple's official website or current public notice before leaving. Confirm darshan timings and whether any sevas are suspended or moved. Plan transport and accommodation early. Follow police and temple staff instructions around the mandapam and temple roads. Keep children and older family members close in crowded areas. Do not enter a restricted area to get a better view of the fire ritual.

Festival arrangements can change according to crowd conditions. A social media post from a previous year is not a reliable guide to this year's access.

## The meaning of light in Kartika

The lamp is one of the simplest symbols in Hindu worship. It represents knowledge, attention and the effort to move away from ignorance. In Kartika, the repeated act of lighting a lamp gives the month its distinctive rhythm.

Jwala Thoranam brings that symbolism into a public temple observance. The fire is not a spectacle to be approached carelessly. It is part of worship, and the people gathered around it should follow the ritual and safety instructions.

## Questions people ask

**Is Jwala Thoranam held every Kartika Purnima?**

It is a traditional observance associated with Srisailam's Kartika celebrations. Confirm the current year's programme with the Devasthanam.

**Is it the same as Koti Deepotsavam?**

No. Koti Deepotsavam is a large lamp-lighting event. Jwala Thoranam is the ceremonial fire arch. They are separate observances.

**Can devotees pass under the fire arch?**

The ritual arrangements and permitted participation should be confirmed with the temple. Do not assume that everyone can pass through it.
