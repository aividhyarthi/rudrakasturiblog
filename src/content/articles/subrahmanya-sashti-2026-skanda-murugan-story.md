A mother places a shining spear in her son's hand. It is not an ordinary weapon. It is the Vel, the divine gift of Goddess Parvati to her son Murugan. With it, he will face Surapadman, whose power has become a threat to the worlds.

This is the story at the heart of many Murugan festivals. Subrahmanya Sashti is observed in honour of Lord Subrahmanya, also known as Murugan, Skanda and Kartikeya. In the Hyderabad calendar consulted for this guide, the 2026 observance falls on **Tuesday, 15 December**. The exact practice and date should be checked with the local temple and panchangam.

## Who is Lord Subrahmanya?

Subrahmanya is the son of Shiva and Parvati and the commander of the divine forces. In Tamil tradition he is widely loved as Murugan, the youthful god of wisdom, courage and beauty. In Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, devotees also worship him as Subrahmanya or Skanda.

His vehicle is the peacock, and his most recognisable symbol is the Vel. The spear is often understood as the power of clear knowledge that cuts through ignorance.

## The story of the Vel and Surapadman

The Skanda Purana and regional Kanda Puranam traditions tell of Surapadman and the conflict between the asura forces and the devas. The gods seek help from Shiva. Murugan is born for the purpose of restoring balance and becomes the commander of the divine army.

Before the battle, Parvati gives Murugan the Vel. In the Tamil Kanda Puranam tradition, the weapon carries the power of the Goddess. Murugan faces Surapadman and defeats him. In a compassionate turn in the story, Surapadman is transformed into the peacock and the rooster associated with Murugan.

The story is not only a battle narrative. Devotees understand it as the victory of wisdom over confusion, courage over fear and discipline over destructive pride.

## What is the difference between Skanda Sashti and Subrahmanya Sashti?

Sashti is the sixth lunar day. Several observances dedicated to Murugan occur on Sashti days through the year. The major six-day Skanda Sashti festival is observed in the month of Aippasi in the Tamil calendar and culminates in Soorasamharam, the enactment of Murugan's victory over Surapadman.

Other monthly or regional Sashti observances may also be called Subrahmanya Sashti. Names and prominence differ between calendars and temple traditions. The December 15 date in the Hyderabad calendar is the Margashirsha Shukla Sashti observance. It should not be confused with the six-day Aippasi festival.

## How devotees observe the day

Practices vary, but many devotees:

1. Visit a Murugan or Subrahmanya temple.
2. Offer flowers, fruit and a lamp.
3. Recite *Kanda Sashti Kavasam*, Subrahmanya Bhujangam or the names of Murugan, according to their family tradition.
4. Observe a fast or take a simple meal if health permits.
5. Spend time in prayer and avoid anger or harsh speech.

At temples in Tamil Nadu, devotees may offer milk, sandal paste and other customary offerings. At Kukke Subrahmanya in Karnataka and other Subrahmanya temples, local temple procedures guide the worship.

## The meaning of the Vel

The Vel is a symbol of sharp understanding. A spear has one clear point; in the same way, spiritual practice asks the mind to become steady and focused. Murugan's victory is therefore remembered as an inner victory too: the effort to overcome ignorance, pride and impulses that harm others.

## Questions people ask

**Is Murugan different from Kartikeya and Subrahmanya?**

They are names of the same deity, though regional traditions emphasise different stories, forms and places of worship.

**Can I observe Sashti without fasting?**

Yes. Devotion does not require a difficult fast. A prayer, temple visit or recitation is a valid way to mark the day.

**Is Kanda Sashti Kavasam a prayer for this day?**

Many devotees recite it as a hymn of protection and devotion to Murugan. Families may also follow other prayers.

Murugan's story asks for courage, but it also asks for clarity. The Vel is powerful because it is guided by wisdom. That is the lesson devotees carry from Subrahmanya Sashti.
