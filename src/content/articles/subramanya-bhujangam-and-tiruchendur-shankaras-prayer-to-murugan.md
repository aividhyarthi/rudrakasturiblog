Stand at Tiruchendur on a morning in the winter, and you will understand why this temple has been loved for centuries. It is built on the seashore, with waves breaking against the base of the sanctum. The sound of the sea and the sound of the chant blend into one.

It is here, the tradition says, that Adi Shankara sat and sang to Murugan.

## The story of the hymn
According to the tradition, Shankara, on his travels, came to Tiruchendur in the course of an illness. He prayed to Subramanya, who is also called Murugan, Kartikeya and Skanda, and was healed. In gratitude, he composed a hymn of thirty-three verses in a metre called *bhujanga-prayata*, "the gait of a snake", whose rhythm is said to resemble the movement of a serpent.

This is the **Subramanya Bhujangam**. Scholars do not agree on the story, and the question of authorship has been debated, but the hymn is sung in every Murugan temple, and it is deeply loved.

## The first verse
> **sadā bāla-rūpāpi vighnādri-hantrī**
> **mahā-dantī-vaktrāpi pañcāsya-mānyā**
> **vidhīndrādi-mṛgyā gaṇeśābhidhā me**
> **vidhattāṁ śriyaṁ kāpi kalyāṇa-mūrtiḥ**

It begins not with Murugan but with Ganesha, the brother. *Always a child, yet destroyer of the mountain of obstacles, with the face of a great elephant yet honoured by the five-faced Shiva...* The hymn opens by honouring the one who begins everything, then moves to Subramanya. Notice how the hymn mixes humour, affection and reverence in a few lines.

## Who Murugan is
Murugan is the younger son of Shiva and Parvati, and the commander of the army of the gods. His weapon is the *vel*, the spear given to him by his mother. He is shown with a peacock as his vehicle, one or six faces, and, in the South, two wives: Valli, a hunter's daughter, and Devasena, the daughter of Indra. He is a god of youth, courage and wisdom. We have written about [Murugan, Kartikeya and Subramanya](/murugan-kartikeya-subramanya-one-god-many-names/) and the [six abodes of Murugan](/arupadai-veedu-the-six-abodes-of-murugan/).

## Tiruchendur and Surasamharam
Tiruchendur is the place, in the tradition, where Murugan defeated the demon Surapadman. The festival of **Skanda Shashti** in the month of Aippasi (October to November) lasts six days and ends with *Surasamharam*, the destruction of the demon, enacted on the beach. Hundreds of thousands gather. It is among the great sights of Tamil Nadu.

## Subramanya on Tuesdays
Tuesday is a day of Subramanya, as well as Hanuman, and the sixth day after the new moon, *Shashti*, is especially his. Many families visit a Murugan temple on those days, light lamps and offer milk or fruit. In the Telugu country he is called Subrahmanyeswara Swamy, and is often worshipped as the lord of serpents, as at Mopidevi and Kukke Subramanya ([Kukke Subramanya and sarpa dosha](/kukke-subramanya-temple-sarpa-dosha-and-the-snake-worship-of-the-south/)).

## How to use the Bhujangam
- **Read it aloud once** on a Tuesday or Shashti, even slowly. It takes about fifteen minutes.
- **Follow with a translation.** The Sanskrit rewards slow reading.
- **Light a lamp** before a picture of Murugan with his vel.
- **Say a short mantra:** *Om Saravanabhavaya Namah*, 108 times. The word *Saravanabhava* is a famous name of Murugan, "born in the reed-forest".

## The mood of the hymn
One of its recurring ideas is humility: the poet says he is weak and Murugan is strong. The verses ask for relief from troubles, and some have been traditionally recited by those who are ill. Whatever you believe about the story of its writing, the tone is comforting, like a child singing to a protective older brother.

## Kanda Sashti Kavacham
Another hymn that many Tamil families know by heart is the *Kanda Sashti Kavacham*, a protective hymn to Murugan composed by Devaraya Swamigal in the nineteenth century, and often sung on Shashti days and during Skanda Shashti. It is in Tamil, and it is almost a chant: each line asks for the protection of a part of the body by the Lord's vel. Listening to it on a Tuesday evening is part of the tradition of many families.

## Palani and the six abodes
Palani, with its hilltop temple of Dandayudhapani, the Murugan who holds a staff, is the third of the six abodes. Tiruchendur is the second. Swamimalai is where, in the tradition, Murugan taught his father Shiva the meaning of *Om*. Each of the six has its own story and its own mood. If you cannot visit them all, reading about them is a start.
