It is half past six. The sky has turned from grey to a washed orange, and somewhere in your house a kettle is already on. For most of us, the first thing we do on a morning like this is reach for the phone.

There is another way to begin, and it takes less time than scrolling.

## What it is
Surya Arghya is the offering of water to the Sun. You stand facing east as the Sun rises, hold a small vessel of water in both hands, and pour it slowly in a thin stream so that you can see the light through the water. As you pour, you say a few words. That is all.

It is one of the oldest practices in India. The Rig Veda has hymns to Surya, and the Gayatri mantra, which is said to the Sun as the giver of light and understanding, is part of the daily sandhya of millions of families. In many homes it is a quiet habit, done at the tulasi plant or on the terrace, without any announcement.

## How to do it
1. **Wake before or at sunrise.** Bathe if you can. If not, wash your face and hands.
2. **Take clean water** in a small vessel. Copper is traditional, but any clean cup will do.
3. **Face east.** Stand with your feet together and hold the vessel at the level of your chest or forehead.
4. **Pour in a thin stream,** slowly, three times, watching the Sun through the water. Do not look straight at the Sun when it is bright, and stop looking once it is above the horizon.
5. **Say a mantra:** the Gayatri, or *Om Suryaya Namah*, or just a sentence of thanks in your own language.
6. **Stand for one breath** with your hands folded.

## What people say it gives them
Ask someone who has done it for years and you will hear something like this: *the day feels different when it starts this way.* Part of that is simple. You are outside, awake, with the light on your face, away from a screen. Doctors who study sleep say that morning light helps set the body's clock. A habit of watching the sunrise is also, quietly, a habit of getting up.

We do not claim that water poured to the Sun cures illness. The tradition does not say that either. It says that the practice builds *discipline*, *gratitude* and a sense of belonging to something larger than your to-do list.

## For Sunday
Sunday is the day of Surya. Many people do the arghya with a little more care on Sundays, and read the [Aditya Hridayam](/aditya-hridayam-sunday-surya-worship-and-meaning/), the hymn Sage Agastya taught Rama before the battle with Ravana. If you want a few gentle stretches after the offering, [Surya Namaskar](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/) is the natural next step.

## If you cannot be outside
You can do it from a window or a balcony. If clouds cover the Sun, face east anyway. On days you cannot manage it, do not feel guilty. Try again tomorrow. A practice is something you return to, not something you pass or fail.

## The twelve names of Surya
In the Surya Namaskar, each of the twelve steps carries a name of the Sun: Mitra (friend), Ravi (shining), Surya (the one who moves), Bhanu (the radiant), Khaga (the one who goes through the sky), Pusha (the nourisher), Hiranyagarbha (the golden womb), Marichi (the ray), Aditya (the son of Aditi), Savitr (the impeller), Arka (the praiseworthy) and Bhaskara (the maker of light). Saying one name for each pour is a lovely way to deepen the practice. Even twelve slow breaths at sunrise, with those names in mind, is a full morning meditation.

## Questions people ask
**What time should I do it?** At sunrise or within the first hour after. If you miss it, do it later in the morning and do not worry.

**Can anyone do it?** Yes. Men, women and children can offer arghya. There is no initiation needed.

**Why a thin stream of water?** The old teachers say the stream lets you see the sunlight break through the water into colours. It is a gentle way to look at the Sun without staring at it.

**Is it safe to look at the Sun?** Never look straight at a bright Sun. Look at the water, or at the sky near the Sun, and stop once the Sun is above the horizon.

**What if I am traveling?** Do it from a train window or a hotel balcony. A moment of thanks counts.
