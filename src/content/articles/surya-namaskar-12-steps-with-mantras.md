Surya Namaskar, the sun salutation, is a sequence of twelve poses done in one flowing round. It is traditionally practised at sunrise, facing the Sun, and each step has a mantra that names the Sun.

## The rule of breath
Breathe **in** when you open the chest (arms up, cobra pose, stepping back). Breathe **out** when you fold forward or bring the body together. Keep the breath smooth and let it set the pace.

## The twelve steps
1. **Pranamasana (prayer pose).** Stand straight, palms together at the chest. *Om Mitraya Namah.*
2. **Hasta Uttanasana (raised arms).** Breathe in, raise the arms and arch back gently. *Om Ravaye Namah.*
3. **Padahastasana (forward fold).** Breathe out and fold forward, hands beside the feet, knees bent if needed. *Om Suryaya Namah.*
4. **Ashwa Sanchalanasana (equestrian pose).** Breathe in, step the right leg back, knee down, chest open, look up. *Om Bhanave Namah.*
5. **Parvatasana (mountain, or plank).** Hold the breath or breathe out, step the other leg back so the body forms one line. *Om Khagaya Namah.*
6. **Ashtanga Namaskara (eight limbs).** Lower knees, chest and chin to the floor, hips up. *Om Pushne Namah.*
7. **Bhujangasana (cobra).** Breathe in, slide forward and lift the chest, elbows slightly bent. *Om Hiranyagarbhaya Namah.*
8. **Parvatasana (downward dog).** Breathe out and lift the hips into an inverted V. *Om Marichaye Namah.*
9. **Ashwa Sanchalanasana (other side).** Breathe in, bring the right foot between the hands, left knee down, look up. *Om Adityaya Namah.*
10. **Padahastasana.** Breathe out, bring the left foot forward and fold. *Om Savitre Namah.*
11. **Hasta Uttanasana.** Breathe in, rise with arms overhead and arch back. *Om Arkaya Namah.*
12. **Pranamasana.** Breathe out and return to prayer pose. *Om Bhaskaraya Namah.*

This is half a round. For a full round, repeat with the left leg stepping back first in step 4, and the left foot forward in step 9.

## How much to do
Begin with three or four rounds, slowly. Add a round every week or two. It is better to do fewer rounds with good form than many rounds in a hurry.

## Before you begin
- Practise on an empty stomach, ideally in the morning, on a non-slip mat.
- Warm up the wrists, shoulders and back for a minute.
- **Check with a doctor first** if you have a back, neck or wrist injury, high blood pressure, a heart condition, a hernia, or if you are pregnant or have had recent surgery.
- Never push into pain. Bend your knees in forward folds and lower your knees in the plank if it feels strong.

## Why the Sun
In the Indian tradition the Sun is the source of light and life, and morning practice facing the Sun is a way of giving thanks. You do not need to hold any belief to enjoy the exercise itself, which moves the whole body and sets a calm start to the day.

Pair it with a few minutes of [pranayama](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/) at the end for a short complete practice.
