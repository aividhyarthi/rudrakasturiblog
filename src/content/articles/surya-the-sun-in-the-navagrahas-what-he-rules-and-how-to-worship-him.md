Think of a grandmother who gets up before the house does, steps out to the courtyard, and pours a little water toward the east before she touches anything else. Nobody ever explained it to you. It was just part of the morning. That small gesture is, in a way, the whole worship of Surya.

In the system of the nine grahas, the Navagrahas, Surya is where everything begins. He comes first in the usual order, he sits at the centre of the Navagraha shrine in most temples, and the other eight are arranged around him. If you are new to the nine, our guide to [the Navagrahas in Vedic astrology](/navagraha-the-nine-planets-in-vedic-astrology/) gives the full picture. Here we sit with Surya alone.

## Who is Surya in the tradition?

Surya, called Sooryan in Tamil and Suryudu in Telugu (సూర్యుడు), is both the visible Sun and a deity. Tradition holds that he rides a single chariot with seven horses, driven by Aruna, the charioteer who is the dawn itself. The seven horses are often said to stand for the seven days of the week or the seven colours of light. Families differ on the exact explanation, and that is fine.

He is the Sun that every human being can see, which makes him a rare god: you do not need an image, a priest or a temple. The sky provides all three.

## What Surya is said to govern

When astrologers in this tradition read a birth chart, they treat each graha as a kind of symbol. Surya is said to stand for:

- the atma, the inner self or soul
- authority, dignity and the capacity to lead
- the father, elders and people in positions of responsibility
- clarity, confidence and a steady sense of purpose

Notice that these are ideas, not predictions. A person with a prominent Sun in their chart is described as having certain themes, not a fixed destiny. Honest astrologers say this too, and some will tell you plainly that the chart shows tendencies and nothing more. Please treat anyone who frightens you with a chart reading with caution.

## Day, colour, grain and mantra

Here is what families commonly associate with him.

- **Day:** Sunday, Ravivaram in Tamil and Telugu, Adityavara in Sanskrit.
- **Colour:** red, saffron or deep orange.
- **Grain:** wheat (godhumai in Tamil, godhuma in Telugu). In many homes, a sweet or pongal made with wheat is cooked on Sundays.
- **Flower:** red lotus or red hibiscus where available.
- **Direction:** east, where he rises.
- **Mantra:** Om Suryaya Namah, or the longer beeja mantra that your family elder may teach.

For a fuller prayer, many people recite the Aditya Hridayam, the hymn the sage Agastya is said to have taught Rama before the battle with Ravana. It has its own place in our article on [the Aditya Hridayam and Sunday Surya worship](/aditya-hridayam-sunday-surya-worship-and-meaning/). You do not have to read it all in one sitting. Many people take a few verses and add more as they grow comfortable.

## How families actually worship him

There is no single method, and that is part of the beauty. In many families the practice looks like this.

1. **Wake before or around sunrise** and bathe if you can.
2. **Stand facing east** with a small copper or clean steel vessel of water.
3. **Pour the water slowly** as an offering, saying Om Suryaya Namah or your family's mantra, and watch the stream catch the light. The full method is in our guide to [offering arghya to the Sun at sunrise](/surya-arghya-how-to-offer-water-to-the-sun-at-sunrise/).
4. **Fold your hands** for a quiet minute. That is enough.

Another widely loved practice is Surya Namaskar, the twelve-step sequence of postures done facing the Sun, each step paired with a name of Surya. Our page on [Surya Namaskar with mantras](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/) lists them. If your body does not allow the full sequence, the mantras alone, said standing with folded hands, are considered a worthy offering by many families.

## Temples to Surya

Surya temples are fewer than temples to other gods, which makes each one special. Two are well known. The Surya temple at Arasavalli in Andhra Pradesh is famous in Telugu-speaking homes, and tradition holds that on certain days of the year the first rays of the morning Sun touch the deity's feet. The Sun Temple at Konark in Odisha is a huge stone chariot, with carved wheels and horses, and is now mostly a monument. The Surya sthalam among the nine Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam is Suryanar Koil; you can see how the nine temples are ordered in our guide to [the Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam](/navagraha-temples-near-kumbakonam-the-nine-in-order/).

## What you can leave alone

Some websites and sellers will tell you that a weak Sun in your chart needs a costly ring, a stone, or an expensive ritual performed by a stranger. The tradition does not require any of that to honour Surya. Water, a mantra, a little honest attention to the morning: this is the core. If someone presses you for money, you are free to say no and still be a devoted person.

Equally, astrology is not a guarantee, and Surya worship is not a bargain in which one offering buys one result. A grandmother pouring water at dawn is not trading. She is saying thank you to the light.

## A simple practice for this Sunday

Tomorrow, or this coming Sunday, step outside a few minutes after sunrise. Stand still. Say Om Suryaya Namah three times, slowly, and pour a little water on a plant. That is all. See how the morning feels afterwards.
