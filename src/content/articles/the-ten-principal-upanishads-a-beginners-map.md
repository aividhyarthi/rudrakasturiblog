The word *Upanishad* is usually explained as sitting down near a teacher. That is what they are: private conversations between teachers and students about the biggest questions. Who am I? What happens at death? What is real?

There are over a hundred Upanishads, but ten are considered the main ones, because Shankara wrote commentaries on them. Here is a short map.

## The ten, one line each
1. **Isha.** Everything in the world is pervaded by the Lord, so enjoy by letting go. It has only eighteen verses.
2. **Kena.** Who makes the mind think and the eyes see? The answer is the one that the mind cannot grasp.
3. **Katha.** The best story of the lot. A boy named Nachiketa meets Yama, the lord of death, and asks him what happens after we die. Yama tries to offer him wealth and long life instead, but the boy insists.
4. **Prashna.** Six students ask a sage six questions, about creation, the senses, sleep and the sacred syllable Om.
5. **Mundaka.** It separates two kinds of knowledge: the lower one about the world and the higher one about the self. The phrase *Satyameva Jayate*, truth alone triumphs, is from here.
6. **Mandukya.** The shortest of the ten, only twelve verses, which explains the sound Om as the key to four states of awareness: waking, dreaming, deep sleep and a fourth, beyond them.
7. **Taittiriya.** The five sheaths of the self, from the body to bliss. It also contains the teacher's advice to students on graduation: *Satyam vada, dharmam chara*, speak the truth, follow dharma.
8. **Aitareya.** About creation and the self as the source of awareness.
9. **Chandogya.** One of the oldest and longest. It has the story of Shvetaketu and his father, where the line *Tat tvam asi*, you are that, comes from.
10. **Brihadaranyaka.** The largest, with the teaching of Yajnavalkya, including his conversation with his wife Maitreyi on what lasts, and the method of *neti neti*, not this, not this.

## How to start
If you try to read them cold, the language can feel strange. Here is a gentler way.
- Start with the **Katha Upanishad**, because it is a story.
- Then read the **Isha** or **Kena**, which are short.
- Look for a translation with a short commentary, from a traditional teacher or a modern one you trust.
- Read a few verses at a time and think about them.

## How they connect to what you may already know
The Bhagavad Gita is often described as the essence of the Upanishads. You will find its teaching on the self, on action and on steadiness of mind echoed in them. If you have read [Gita 2.14](/gita-2-14-heat-and-cold-pleasure-and-pain-come-and-go/), the Katha Upanishad will feel familiar.

## Where they lead
The ten are the base of Vedanta, the school that Shankara and later [Ramanuja and Madhva](/shankara-ramanuja-madhva-three-ways-to-see-god-and-the-soul/) developed in different directions. If you want to see the whole picture, start with [Advaita in plain words](/advaita-vedanta-in-plain-words/).
