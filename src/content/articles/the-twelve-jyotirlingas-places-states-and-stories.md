Maybe there is a small framed picture at home with twelve lingams in a ring, and your mother knows the names by heart: Somnath, Mallikarjuna, Mahakala. You have always meant to visit them, and you have never known where to begin. Let us put the twelve in a list, with the stories, as the tradition tells them.

## What is a Jyotirlinga?

Jyotir means light. The Shiva Purana tells of a time when Brahma and Vishnu argued over which was greater, and Shiva stood before them as an endless column of fire, whose top and foot neither could reach. The twelve Jyotirlingas are the places where that light is said to have chosen to remain. They are not ordinary shrines of Shiva; they are the ones where the Lord is held to be present as light itself.

There is a short Sanskrit hymn, the Dwadasha Jyotirlinga Stotra, which names all twelve in a line each. Tradition attributes it to Adi Shankara. Many families recite it at dawn.

## The twelve, one by one

**1. Somnath, Gujarat.** On the sea at Prabhas Patan. The moon god Soma, cursed to fade, is said to have worshipped Shiva here and been restored. The temple has been broken and rebuilt many times.

**2. Mallikarjuna, Srisailam, Andhra Pradesh.** On the Nallamala hills above the Krishna. Tradition says Shiva and Parvati came here to Kartikeya in his sulking, and Shiva stayed with Parvati as Bhramaramba. Read more in [the hills of Andhra](/hills-of-andhra-where-the-gods-live-simhachalam-ahobilam-srisailam/).

**3. Mahakaleshwar, Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh.** On the Shipra. Shiva is said to have appeared to save a devotee from a demon, and stayed as the Lord of Time. The lingam faces south, which is unusual.

**4. Omkareshwar, Madhya Pradesh.** On an island in the Narmada shaped like the sign Om. Tradition links it to a rivalry of Vindhya mountain with Meru.

**5. Kedarnath, Uttarakhand.** In the high Himalaya. The story says the Pandavas sought Shiva to atone for the war, and he hid as a bull; the hump remains here.

**6. Bhimashankar, Maharashtra.** In the Sahyadri hills, linked to the demon Bhima, Kumbhakarna's son, defeated by Shiva.

**7. Kashi Vishwanath, Uttar Pradesh.** At Varanasi on the Ganga. Tradition holds that Shiva never leaves Kashi, and that those who die here receive his whispered mantra of liberation.

**8. Trimbakeshwar, Maharashtra.** Near Nashik, at the source of the Godavari. Gautama's penance brought the river here, and Shiva stayed as the three-eyed Lord.

**9. Vaidyanath, Deoghar, Jharkhand.** Linked to Ravana, who offered his heads to Shiva and received healing. Here the claim is shared: Parli in Maharashtra and Baijnath in Himachal Pradesh also say they are the true Vaidyanath. Different traditions follow different towns.

**10. Nageshwar, Gujarat.** Near Dwarka. It remembers the merchant Supriya, saved from the demon Daruka. Claims are shared with other places too, including Aundha in Maharashtra and Jageshwar in Uttarakhand.

**11. Rameshwaram, Tamil Nadu.** On the island where Rama is said to have worshipped Shiva before crossing to Lanka. See [Ramanathaswamy temple](/rameswaram-ramanathaswamy-temple-where-rama-prayed-to-shiva/).

**12. Grishneshwar, Maharashtra.** Near the Ellora caves, remembered through the devotion of a woman named Ghushma and her son brought back to life.

## Which are in the South?

Two lie in the far South by common reckoning: Mallikarjuna at Srisailam and Rameshwaram. Bhimashankar, Trimbakeshwar and Grishneshwar are in Maharashtra, on the Deccan plateau, and are often combined with a trip south by those who start from Hyderabad or Pune. If you live in Telangana or Andhra, Srisailam is closest, and many families make it their first.

## Planning a visit

Do not try all twelve at once. Many families spread them over years, one or two at a time, and count that as the pilgrimage. A few honest tips:

- Check temple timings and any dress rules before you travel; they change.
- Book abhishekam or darshan tickets in advance when the temple allows it.
- The Himalayan shrine at Kedarnath is open only in the warmer months, and requires a trek or other transport. Ask your doctor if you have a heart or breathing condition.
- Carry a small bottle of Ganga or river water if the family tradition is to offer it, but follow the temple's rules on what may be brought in.

For other temples, see [our temples page](/temples/).

## Reading the list as a pilgrim

Look at the spread of the twelve on a map. They run from the Himalaya to the southern tip, from the Arabian Sea to the plains of the east. Some teachers say this is deliberate: the Lord is not kept in one corner of the land, and a person who walks from shrine to shrine comes to know the whole of Bharat. Whether or not that was the intention, it is what happens to many pilgrims. They return knowing a language of food, hills, rivers and customs they had only heard about.

Each shrine has its own way of doing things. In some, you may touch the lingam yourself after a bath; in others, you may only see it from a distance. In some, the abhishekam is done by priests on your behalf. Do not be disappointed if a visit is crowded or short. Many devotees say that a few quiet seconds of darshan, with the eyes closed, are enough.

A practical suggestion for families: write the twelve names on a card and keep it in the puja room. As each visit is made, mark it with a small tick and the date. Years later, children enjoy reading it.

## Do you need to see all twelve?

No one says you must. The Purana says that even to remember the twelve names at dawn and dusk is a merit. Some people never leave their own town and say the names every morning. Others make a lifetime of walking. Both are dear to the Lord.
