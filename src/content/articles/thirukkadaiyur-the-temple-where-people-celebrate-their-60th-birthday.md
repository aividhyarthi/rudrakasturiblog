In a Tamil or Telugu family, a sixtieth birthday is a big event. It is called *Shashtiabdapoorthi*, the completion of sixty years. And for many families, there is only one place to hold it: Thirukkadaiyur.

## Where it is
Thirukkadaiyur, also called Tirukkadavur, is a small town in Mayiladuthurai district of Tamil Nadu, near Tharangambadi on the Kaveri delta, about 20 km from Sirkazhi. The temple is dedicated to Shiva as Amritaghateswarar and to Parvati as Abhirami Amman.

## The story of Markandeya
The temple is tied to one of the best-known stories in the Puranas. A sage, Mrikandu, and his wife Marudvati prayed to Shiva for a child. They were offered a choice: a clever son who would die at sixteen, or a dull son who would live long. They chose the first, and the boy Markandeya was born.

As his sixteenth year came, Markandeya, a devotee of Shiva, spent his days worshipping a lingam. When Yama, the god of death, came to take him, the boy clung to the lingam. Yama threw his noose, which fell around both the boy and the lingam. Shiva emerged from the linga in anger and kicked Yama away, saving the boy and declaring him sixteen forever. In that form, Shiva is called Kalasamhara Murti, the destroyer of death.

That is why people who want to celebrate long life come here.

## What happens at the ceremonies
- **Ugra Ratha Shanti** at 55 or 59.
- **Shashtiabdapoorthi** at 60: the couple, dressed as bride and groom, take part in a series of fire rituals and renew their marriage vows. The wife is again tied with a *mangalsutra*.
- **Bhima Ratha Shanti** at 70.
- **Sathabhishekam** at 80, which marks 1,000 full moons seen in a lifetime.

Many couples also hold the ceremony close to the husband's birth star. Families often come in large groups, and the town has many lodges and priests who arrange the rites.

## The goddess Abhirami
A much-loved story belongs to the shrine of the goddess. A devotee named Subramania Iyer, later known as Abhirami Bhattar, was so absorbed in worshipping the goddess that when the king asked him what day it was, he said "it is the full moon", although it was the new moon day. The king was angry and said he would be punished if the moon did not appear that night.

The tradition says that Bhattar sat in a fire pit, in a sling over the fire, and began to sing in praise of the goddess. At the seventy-ninth verse, the goddess threw her earring into the sky, and it shone like a full moon. The verses became the *Abhirami Anthadi*, a hundred verses that are among the best-loved Tamil hymns to the goddess.

## Planning a ceremony or visit
1. **Book through the temple office** or its recognised priests well ahead. Dates near auspicious days fill up.
2. **Ask about the exact cost** and what it includes.
3. **Check the dress code.** Many ceremonies require traditional clothes.
4. **Allow a full day.** The rites take several hours.
5. **Invite the family, but plan for the heat.**

## If you cannot go
Many families hold Shashtiabdapoorthi at home or at a local temple. The meaning is gratitude: for sixty years, for a partner and for the people who made it possible.
