*Karma* means action, and also the result of action. Almost every person has heard the word, but few know that Indian philosophy does not treat karma as a single fixed bill. It describes three layers.

## The three kinds
1. **Sanchita karma** is the whole stored account: everything done in this and earlier lives, waiting to bear fruit.
2. **Prarabdha karma** is the portion of that account that has begun to ripen. It explains the starting conditions of this life: your family, your body, your era, many of the people who come into your life.
3. **Kriyamana (or agami) karma** is what you are doing right now, with free choice. It shapes the future and feeds the stored account.

## The archer
The tradition's favourite picture is an archer. The arrows in the quiver are sanchita. The arrow that has already left the bow is prarabdha: it cannot be called back, and it will land. The arrow you are drawing now is kriyamana, and where it goes is your choice.

## Why this matters
This explains why two people who work equally hard can see different results, and why a good person can go through a hard stretch. It is also not fatalism. The Gita says that the course of action is hard to understand (4.17), which is a warning against both easy blame and easy pride. And it says that nobody can remain without action even for a moment (3.5). The choice is not between acting and not acting, but between acting well and acting badly.

## What to do with this
- **Do not read every setback as punishment.** Prarabdha explains why something happens, not that you are bad.
- **Take responsibility for the present.** What you say and do today is the only karma entirely in your hands.
- **Repair what you can.** Apologise, repay a debt, return what you took. Tradition holds that sincere correction lightens the weight of the old action.
- **Add good karma without keeping score.** Feeding someone, teaching, keeping a promise: do it because it is right, as [Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/) asks.
