Think of the person who taught you something that you still use. It might be a schoolteacher who stayed after class. It might be your grandmother, who never read a book and understood people better than anyone you know. It might be someone you have not thanked, and now cannot.

Thursday is their day.

In the Hindu week, Thursday belongs to Brihaspati, the planet Jupiter, who is the guru of the gods. That is why in Telugu it is *Guruvaram* and in Hindi *Brihaspativar*. It is the day families remember their teachers, and the day many people go to the temples of the great gurus.

## Four teachers people turn to on Thursday
**Dakshinamurthy.** This is Shiva as a teacher, a young man sitting under a banyan tree, facing south, with old disciples around him. Adi Shankara wrote a hymn about him, and one verse is worth reading slowly: *the wonder is that the disciples are old and the teacher is young, and the teacher teaches in silence, and the disciples' doubts are gone.* It is a picture of a lesson that cannot be put into words.

**Dattatreya.** The teacher who is said to have learned from twenty-four teachers, including the earth, the river, the pigeon and the bee. He is the son of Atri and Anasuya, whose story we told in the [Ramayana series](/anasuya-and-sita-the-gift-of-an-old-sage-couple/). His birthday falls on Margashira Pournami.

**Sai Baba of Shirdi.** The saint who lived in a mosque in Shirdi until 1918, and whose message was *shraddha and saburi*, faith and patience. Thursday is the day his devotees gather for the evening aarti.

**Raghavendra Swami.** The Madhva saint (1595 to 1671) whose brindavana is at Mantralayam on the Tungabhadra. Thousands visit on Thursdays, and many in Andhra and Karnataka count him as the family guru.

## A simple Thursday
You do not need an elaborate puja.
- **Light a lamp** at the shrine in the morning.
- **Yellow flowers or a yellow cloth** are traditional for Jupiter. If you have neither, do not worry.
- **Offer chana dal or jaggery** as the naivedyam, and share it.
- **Say one verse.** The most common is this:

> **gurur brahmā gurur viṣṇuḥ gurur devo maheśvaraḥ**
> **guruḥ sākṣāt paraṁ brahma tasmai śrī gurave namaḥ**

*The guru is Brahma, the guru is Vishnu, the guru is the lord Maheshwara. The guru is the Supreme itself. Salutations to that guru.*

- **Then do the part that matters most:** think of one real teacher in your life and say thank you, in your own words, to them or for them. If you can, send a message to someone who is alive.

## What if I do not have a guru?
Most of us do not have a guru in the sense of a spiritual master, and that is fine. The tradition does not ask you to rush. It says the teacher appears when the student is ready. Until then, honour the ones you have had. If you are looking, we have written about [how to find a real guru and how to know one](/searching-for-a-guru-how-to-find-one-and-how-to-know-a-real-one/).

## More about the four teachers
**Dakshinamurthy** is worshipped in a special way at the Shiva temples of Tamil Nadu, where his image faces south on the outer wall of the sanctum. Students go to him before exams. The Dakshinamurthy Stotram, by Adi Shankara, is read on Thursdays: its gist is that the real teaching is silence, and the doubts of the student fall away in that presence.

**Sai Baba of Shirdi,** who lived from the 1800s until 1918, asked his devotees for only two things, *shraddha* (faith) and *saburi* (patience). Thursday is his day in Hyderabad, Chennai and across the South, and many temples hold an evening aarti.

**Raghavendra Swami** (1595 to 1671) is regarded by devotees as a living presence at Mantralayam on the Tungabhadra. The Madhva tradition honours him as a saint who combined scholarship and devotion. Mantralayam is one of the most visited places in Andhra and Karnataka on Thursdays.

**Dattatreya** is told through the story of the [24 gurus](/dattatreya-and-the-24-gurus-what-the-avadhuta-learned/).

## What to do on a Thursday if you have a guru
If you have a guru, visit them, or call them, or sit in front of their picture for five minutes. Offer yellow flowers and a simple sweet. If your guru has passed, light a lamp for them. If you have no guru, honour your first teacher: the one who taught you to read, or to cook, or to wait.

## Questions people ask
**Why yellow?** Yellow is the colour of Jupiter in the tradition, and of turmeric, which is auspicious. Many families use yellow flowers and yellow rice on Thursdays.

**Is it a fasting day?** Some families fast. It is entirely optional.

**What if I am not religious?** Then simply thank a teacher. It is the same offering.
