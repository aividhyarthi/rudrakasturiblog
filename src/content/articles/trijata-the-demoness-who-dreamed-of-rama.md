**Source:** Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara Kanda (chapter 27). That she was Vibhishana's daughter is from later traditions.

Imagine being Sita in the Ashoka grove. You are alone in an enemy city. Day and night you are surrounded by fierce guards who threaten you, mock you and try to wear you down. In that crowd, there is one person who does not.

## The old woman at the edge of the circle
Valmiki describes the rakshasis who guarded Sita as terrible in appearance and speech, many of them with animal faces. They were ordered to persuade or frighten her into marrying Ravana. One of them, however, was different. Her name was Trijata. She is described as an old woman (a *vriddha*) who spoke wisely and loved dharma.

When the others turned on Sita, threatening to eat her if she refused, Trijata woke from sleep and spoke up.

## The dream
She told them she had seen a dream at the end of the night. In it, Rama and Lakshmana, dressed in white, rode in a heavenly vehicle. Sita, in white, was reunited with Rama. Lanka itself sank. Ravana, in the dream, was shaved, smeared with oil, dressed in red and riding an ass toward the south, the direction of Yama, the god of death. Vibhishana alone stood under a white umbrella, with a white garland.

To the ear of an Indian listener, the omens are clear: white for victory, the south for death, the one honest rakshasa under the royal umbrella.

## What she said
After telling it, Trijata did not just say that she was afraid. She told the others to stop harassing Sita, to ask her pardon and, if they were wise, to ask her protection. *She will save us when the time comes,* she said, in effect. It is an unusual thing for a guard to say to a prisoner, and it is even more remarkable that she said it in front of the others.

## The effect
The other guards were afraid, and for the moment they held back. The dream also gave Sita something she needed badly: a reason to hold on a little longer. The Ramayana says that her heart, which had been about to break, was lifted. It was around this time that she sat under the tree and nearly gave up. Hanuman arrived soon after.

## What it teaches
- **Kindness can come from the unlikeliest place.** She was a guard, in the enemy's service, and she was kind.
- **A single voice can change a room.** Trijata did not fight. She told her dream.
- **Hope can be given by someone who has little else to give.** She had no weapons. She had a story.
- **Not everyone on the "other side" is the enemy.** Vibhishana and Trijata are the two lights in Lanka.

## The daughter of Vibhishana?
Some later traditions, including Kamban's Tamil *Ramayana* and the *Adhyatma Ramayana*, make Trijata the daughter of Vibhishana, which would explain her good heart. In Valmiki, she is simply an old rakshasi. You may hear either version, and it is fair to say that the tradition is not unanimous.
