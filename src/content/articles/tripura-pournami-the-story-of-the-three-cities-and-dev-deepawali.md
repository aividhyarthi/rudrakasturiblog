On the full moon night of Karthika, lamps appear on riverbanks, temple walls and rooftops across India. In Varanasi, a million lamps light up the ghats. The reason takes us back to a story about three cities that flew.

## The story of Tripura
The Puranas tell of three demon brothers, the sons of the demon Taraka, who did great penance to Brahma. They asked for immortality. Brahma refused, and they asked instead for three cities that could fly, and a condition on their death: they could only be killed by a single arrow that struck all three cities when they came into a line, which would happen only once in a thousand years.

The brothers ruled their cities of gold, silver and iron. At first they were righteous, but with time they grew arrogant and began to harass the gods and sages. The gods went to Shiva for help.

Shiva took Mount Meru as his bow, the serpent Vasuki as the string and Vishnu as the arrow. The Earth became his chariot, the Sun and Moon the wheels, and Brahma his charioteer. When the three cities came into line, Shiva shot the arrow and burnt them all to ashes. This is why he is called **Tripurantaka**, the ender of the three cities.

The day was the full moon of Karthika. The gods lit lamps to celebrate, which is the origin of the lamp festival.

## What the story means
Teachers often read the three cities as the three states of the mind: waking, dreaming and deep sleep, or the three qualities of nature. They are all conquered when attention becomes one-pointed, like one arrow. It is an old image for the way a focused mind sees through illusion.

## What happens on Kartika Pournami
- **Lamps.** Devotees light lamps in Shiva temples, at the doorstep and on the banks of rivers. Many float lamps on leaf boats in a river or pond.
- **Dev Deepawali in Varanasi.** The festival of the gods' Diwali is celebrated on the ghats of the Ganga with lakhs of lamps and the river aarti.
- **Jwala Toranam.** In some Shiva temples, such as at Srisailam, a ceremonial fire called Jwala Toranam is lit at dusk.
- **Karthika Deepam at Arunachala.** Around this time, the Maha Deepam is lit on the hill at Tiruvannamalai. See [our Arunachala piece](/arunachala-the-hill-that-is-shiva-and-the-walk-around-it/).
- **Guru Nanak Jayanti.** The birth anniversary of Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, falls on the same full moon.
- **Pushkar Fair.** In Rajasthan, the famous Pushkar mela peaks on this day.
- **Matsya Jayanti** in some traditions, remembering Vishnu's fish incarnation.

## In 2026
Kartika Pournami is on **Tuesday, 24 November 2026**. The tithi begins on 23 November at 11:42 PM and ends on 24 November at 8:23 PM (Hyderabad). See [the November 2026 Pournami page](/pournami/november-2026/).

## How to observe it at home
1. Bathe early, and fast lightly if you wish.
2. Light a lamp at dawn and at dusk. In the evening, light 11, 21 or 365 small lamps.
3. Place lamps on the Tulasi, at the door and on the compound wall, and, if you can, float one on running water.
4. Chant *Om Namah Shivaya*.
5. Give food or oil to someone in need.

## A safety note
Lamps are lovely, but they are also fire. Keep them away from curtains and dry leaves, don't leave small children near them, and use a tray.
