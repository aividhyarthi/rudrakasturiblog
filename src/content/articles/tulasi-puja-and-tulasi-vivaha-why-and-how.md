A Tulasi (holy basil) plant on a raised platform, called Tulasi katte or brindavan, stands in front of many South Indian homes. It is worshipped as a form of Lakshmi and as the beloved of Vishnu.

## The story
According to the Puranas, Tulasi was Vrinda, the devoted wife of the demon king Jalandhara. Her faithfulness made him invincible, until Vishnu intervened and the war ended. Vrinda, in grief, cursed Vishnu to become a stone, which is why he is worshipped as the Shaligrama stone. She gave up her life, and from her pyre the Tulasi plant grew. Vishnu promised that he would not accept any offering without Tulasi leaves, and that she would be married to him each year.

## Daily worship
- **At dawn,** pour a little water at the base, and walk around the plant (pradakshina) once or three times with folded hands.
- **At dusk,** light a small lamp near the plant.
- **Chant:** *Vrindayai Tulasi Devyai Priyayai Keshavasya Cha, Vishnu Bhakti Prade Devi Satyavatyai Namo Namah.* It means, "salutations to Vrinda, Tulasi Devi, beloved of Keshava, giver of devotion to Vishnu."

## Plucking rules
Tradition suggests plucking leaves in the morning after a bath, and avoiding Ekadashi, Dwadashi, Sunday, Amavasya, Pournami and the time after sunset. Pluck gently with the fingers and say a small prayer. Leaves already fallen can be used.

## Tulasi Vivaha 2026
Tulasi Vivaha is a ceremonial wedding of the Tulasi plant to Vishnu, represented by a Shaligrama, or by an amla branch, and sometimes Krishna. It is held on any day from Prabodhini (Utthana) Ekadashi, when Vishnu is said to wake from his four-month sleep, to Kartika Pournami. In 2026 Prabodhini Ekadashi falls on 20 or 21 November, and Kartika Pournami is on **24 November**, so the ceremony is held between those dates. Check your family's or temple's date.

It marks the end of Chaturmasya and the start of the marriage season in many families. Read about [Karthika Masam 2026](/karthika-masam-2026-dates-deepam-and-fasting/).

## How the ceremony goes
1. Clean the Tulasi platform and decorate it with a canopy, rangoli and flowers.
2. Dress the plant like a bride, and place the Shaligrama or Vishnu image next to it.
3. Perform the puja, with a thread (mangalsutra) tied round the plant, and sing the marriage songs.
4. Share prasad and sweets with everyone.

*Traditions vary by region and family.*
