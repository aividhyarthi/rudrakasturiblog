Rivers, in the Hindu way of thinking, are often born from the meeting of two. The Tungabhadra is a very literal example, and her banks are full of history.

## Two rivers, one name
The Tunga and the Bhadra both rise in the Western Ghats in Karnataka. They join at Koodli, near Shivamogga, and become the Tungabhadra. She flows east for about 530 km and joins the Krishna near Alampur. According to the Puranas, she is also called Pampa, from the goddess Pampa who gave her name to Hampi.

## Hampi
The ruins of Hampi sit among boulders on the south bank, in Karnataka. This was Vijayanagara, the capital of the great empire that ruled most of South India from 1336 to 1565. Its kings, such as Krishnadevaraya, built temples, markets and water systems, and supported Kannada, Telugu, Tamil and Sanskrit poets. The city fell after the Battle of Talikota in 1565.

The Virupaksha temple, dedicated to Shiva as Pampapati, has been in worship continuously for over a thousand years, even through the fall of the city. The Vittala temple, with its stone chariot and musical pillars, is a World Heritage Site. The river runs beside them, and a boat crossing to the other bank is a good way to see the sunset.

## Kishkindha
Across the river, near Anegundi, is a landscape of boulders that local tradition identifies with Kishkindha, the monkey kingdom of the Ramayana. The hill known as Anjanadri is considered by many in Karnataka to be Hanuman's birthplace. Pilgrims climb about 570 steps to the small temple at the top.

## Mantralayam
Further downstream, in Andhra Pradesh, on the bank of the Tungabhadra, lies Mantralayam. This is the Brindavana, the resting place, of the saint Raghavendra Swami, a 17th-century teacher of the Dvaita tradition. Thousands of devotees come, especially on Thursdays. Many go around the Brindavana, and the annual Aradhana festival in the month of Shravana is very large. ## Pushkaralu
The Tungabhadra has her own Pushkaralu festival once every twelve years, when Jupiter enters Makara rashi. It was held in 2020, and the next will be in 2032. Kurnool and Mantralayam are among the main bathing places.

## The dam
The Tungabhadra Dam, near Hosapete, was built in the 1950s and irrigates parts of Karnataka and Andhra. It is a major source of water for the region. In 2024 one of its gates broke and caused a flood alert, which reminded people of how much depends on it.

## Planning
Hampi is about 350 km from Bengaluru and a few hours from Hyderabad by train and road. The best months are October to February. Allow two days. Mantralayam is about 50 km from Raichur and Adoni.
