There are days when a temple's worship brings two traditions together. Vaikuntha Chaturdashi is one such observance. It is associated with Lord Vishnu and Lord Shiva, and it falls on the fourteenth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Kartika, just before the full moon.

For New Delhi, Drik Panchang lists **Monday, 23 November 2026** as Vaikuntha Chaturdashi. The Chaturdashi tithi begins early on 23 November and ends late that night. Check your own city's panchang for the local observance.

## Why are Shiva and Vishnu worshipped together?

The central message of the day is the closeness of the two forms of the divine. In many traditions, Shiva and Vishnu are not treated as rivals. They are understood as different forms through which devotees approach the same ultimate reality.

A well-known story explains the observance through an offering made by Vishnu to Shiva. Vishnu wishes to worship Shiva with a thousand lotuses. As he counts the flowers, one is found missing. Vishnu offers one of his own eyes in its place, because his eyes are often compared to lotus petals. Shiva is moved by the devotion and blesses him.

The story is presented in devotional traditions in more than one form. Drik Panchang also identifies the day with Vishnu offering a thousand lotuses to Shiva. Its focus is not the number of flowers. It is the willingness to offer what is most precious.

## What does the name mean?

*Vaikuntha* is the abode of Vishnu. *Chaturdashi* is the fourteenth tithi of the lunar fortnight. The name connects the day with Vishnu, while the worship of Shiva is central to the shared ritual tradition.

The observance is particularly noted in places such as Varanasi, where the relationship between Shiva and Vishnu has a long place in the city's sacred stories and temple life. This is a broad devotional association, not a claim that every temple follows the same ritual. Local temple practices are not identical, so follow the instructions of the shrine you visit.

## How devotees observe the day

Common practices include:

- Visiting a Shiva or Vishnu temple and offering flowers.
- Reciting the Vishnu Sahasranama, Shiva prayers or the stotras followed by the family.
- Lighting lamps and offering a simple meal or fruit.
- Giving food or other help to someone in need.
- Keeping a fast only if it is part of the family's practice and safe for the person observing it.

Some traditions describe worship at a particular night-time period. Do not use a generic muhurta from another city. The local panchang and temple notice should guide the timing.

## Vaikuntha Chaturdashi and Kartika Purnima

Vaikuntha Chaturdashi comes immediately before Kartika Purnima in the lunar calendar. The next day is marked by several observances, including Kartika Purnima, Dev Deepawali in Varanasi and Guru Nanak Jayanti.

## What the story teaches

The offering of the eye is a story about wholehearted devotion. It also reminds us that different paths of worship need not become arguments about who is greater. For a devotee, the important question is whether prayer makes a person more humble, truthful and compassionate.

## Questions people ask

**Is Vaikuntha Chaturdashi the same as Vaikuntha Ekadashi?**

No. Vaikuntha Chaturdashi is observed in Kartika, on the fourteenth tithi before Purnima. Vaikuntha Ekadashi is a different observance later in the year.

**Is the day dedicated only to Vishnu?**

No. The distinctive feature of the observance is the worship of both Vishnu and Shiva.

**Can I observe it at home?**

Yes. A simple prayer, flowers and a lamp are enough for many households. Follow the custom you have inherited.
