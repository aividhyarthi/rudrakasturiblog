Before sunrise, a long queue forms outside a Vishnu temple. Families have travelled through the night. Some carry flowers, some hold small children, and many have been waiting for hours. They have come for one special moment: to pass through the Vaikuntha Dwaram, the symbolic gate associated with the abode of Lord Vishnu.

Vaikuntha Ekadashi is among the most important annual observances for Vishnu devotees. It is also known in many South Indian traditions as Mukkoti Ekadashi. The Hyderabad calendar consulted for this guide lists it on **Sunday, 20 December 2026**. Because Ekadashi observance can differ by sampradaya and local panchang, confirm the date and parana time with your temple.

## What is Vaikuntha Ekadashi?

Ekadashi is the eleventh tithi of the lunar fortnight. Vaikuntha Ekadashi is the important Shukla Paksha Ekadashi associated with Margashirsha or the Dhanurmasa period, depending on the calendar tradition.

Vaikuntha is the abode of Vishnu. Devotees observe this Ekadashi with prayer, recitation, temple visits and fasting according to their capacity. The day is associated with the hope of spiritual release and the grace of Vishnu.

## Why is the northern gate opened?

Many Vishnu temples create a special entrance called Vaikuntha Dwaram, Paramapada Vasal or Sorga Vasal. Devotees pass through it as a symbolic act of seeking the Lord's grace and the path to Vaikuntha.

The practice is especially prominent at major temples such as Tirumala and Srirangam. At Srirangam, the opening of Paramapada Vasal is part of the celebrated Adhyayanotsavam period. At Tirumala, the Vaikuntha Dwara Darshan draws large numbers of pilgrims. The exact arrangements, access rules and number of days are decided by each temple.

The gate is not a magical shortcut. It is a ritual symbol. Passing through it expresses the devotee's longing to move from worldly confusion towards the presence of Vishnu.

## The story and spiritual meaning

Vaishnava traditions preserve several explanations for the importance of this Ekadashi. One popular account describes the Lord opening the gates of Vaikuntha for devotees who observe the day with faith. Another devotional explanation connects the observance with the destruction of demonic forces and the protection of dharma.

The stories are told in different forms across Puranic and regional traditions. Rather than treating every local telling as one identical scriptural account, it is better to understand the shared message: sincere devotion, restraint and remembrance of Vishnu are central to the day.

## How devotees observe Vaikuntha Ekadashi

Common practices include:

1. Waking early, bathing and offering prayers to Vishnu.
2. Reciting the Vishnu Sahasranama, Divya Prabandham or other prayers followed by the family.
3. Visiting a Vishnu temple and following its queue and darshan instructions.
4. Avoiding grains and pulses, or observing a stricter fast if that is the family tradition and health permits.
5. Staying engaged in prayer, reading and service rather than treating the day only as a food restriction.

The fast is broken on Dwadashi within the parana period given by the local panchang. Do not rely on a generic internet time for parana.

## Vaikuntha Ekadashi in South India

The observance has a special place in the temple life of South India. At Tirumala, devotees seek Vaikuntha Dwara Darshan. At Srirangam, the Paramapada Vasal is central to the festival. Other Vishnu temples in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu and Karnataka hold special worship and recitations.

In Telugu households, the day is often called Mukkoti Ekadashi. Many families read Vishnu-related texts, sing devotional songs and visit a nearby temple. The scale of the celebration may differ, but the emphasis on remembrance of Vishnu is shared.

## Who should be careful with fasting?

A strict fast is not suitable for everyone. Children, older adults, pregnant women and people with diabetes, heart disease, kidney disease or other medical conditions should not attempt a demanding fast without advice from their clinician. A simple prayer or a meal that follows medical needs is better than risking health.

## Questions people ask

**Is Vaikuntha Ekadashi the biggest Ekadashi?**

It is among the most important Ekadashis for many Vaishnava communities, especially in South India. Other Ekadashis also hold great importance in different traditions.

**What is Mukkoti Ekadashi?**

Mukkoti Ekadashi is a name commonly used in Telugu and some South Indian traditions for this major Vishnu observance.

**When should the fast be broken?**

The fast is generally broken on Dwadashi during the prescribed parana period. Check the local panchang and temple guidance.

**Does every Vishnu temple open a Vaikuntha gate?**

No. The practice and the name of the special entrance vary by temple.

The northern gate is a powerful image because it gives a physical form to an inner wish. Devotees pass through it asking for the grace to live with greater humility, steadiness and devotion. That is the heart of Vaikuntha Ekadashi.

*Temple schedules, darshan arrangements and tithi observance vary. Check official temple announcements before travelling.*
