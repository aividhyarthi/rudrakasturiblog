**Source:** The local legend of Vaishno Devi, as told by the Shri Mata Vaishno Devi Shrine Board and local tradition. It is a legend and not a record.

The most visited goddess shrine in the north starts with a girl from the south.

## The girl who wanted Rama
According to the legend, in the south of India, in the time of the Treta Yuga, a couple named Ratnakar Sagar and his wife had a daughter, Vaishnavi, after long prayer. She was a child of unusual gifts. From her earliest days she wished to be united with Vishnu, and as she grew older, to marry Rama.

When Rama passed through the south, searching for Sita, she met him and told him her wish. Rama, who was bound by his vow of one wife, said that he could not marry her in that age. But he promised that in the Kali Yuga, in the form of Kalki, he would come to her. In the meantime she was to go to the hills of the north, to Trikuta, and do penance there.

## The hills of Trikuta
Vaishnavi went north. In a cave in the Trikuta mountain, she began to meditate. There, the legend says, she became more than a mortal and was joined by the powers of Maha Kali, Maha Lakshmi and Maha Saraswati, so that the three goddesses are said to be present in her.

## Bhairon Nath
In the same story, a tantric named Bhairon Nath saw her and became obsessed. He tried to seize her. She ran, up the mountain, to a cave called Adhkuwari, where she stayed for nine months, hidden, like a child in the womb. Bhairon found her there, and she broke out through the back.

At last she reached the main cave, turned, and struck off his head. As he died, he repented and begged for forgiveness. The goddess granted that no pilgrimage to her would be complete without visiting his temple, which stands higher up the hill.

## The shrine
In the cave at Bhawan, there is no statue. There are three natural rock formations, *pindis*, which the tradition honours as Maha Kali, Maha Lakshmi and Maha Saraswati. A stream flows through the cave. The pilgrimage is a trek of about 13 km from Katra, a small town at the base of the hills.

Millions of pilgrims take the walk every year, many calling out *Jai Mata Di* as they go. The way is lined with shops, rest-stops and langars. A shrine board runs the pilgrimage, and numbers are controlled because of the crowds.

## A story with a southern root
Many visitors from the south are surprised to learn that the goddess of this northern hilltop was born, in the legend, in the south. The story ties the two ends of India together in a way few other places do.

## What it teaches
- **A vow is a path.** Vaishnavi's wish took her over a mountain.
- **Waiting is part of devotion.** Rama's reply is a promise for a later age.
- **Fierceness protects.** The goddess did not run forever.
- **Forgiveness can follow justice.** Bhairon Nath's end is a lesson in repentance.

## Visiting
Register at Katra before the climb. The best months are March to October, though winters are also popular and cold. Carry water and rain protection, and take your time. Elderly pilgrims can take ponies or palanquins. The shrine is also reachable by helicopter from Katra.
