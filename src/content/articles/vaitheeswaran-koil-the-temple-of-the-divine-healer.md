Whenever someone in a South Indian family falls seriously ill, an older relative says, "Let's make a vow to Vaitheeswaran." The name means "the god who is the physician," and the temple has been a place of hope for the sick for over a thousand years.

## Where it is
Vaitheeswaran Koil is in Mayiladuthurai district of Tamil Nadu, close to Sirkazhi, about 110 km from Thanjavur and 235 km from Chennai. It is one of the nine Navagraha temples near Kumbakonam, the temple for Angaraka (Mars). See [our guide to the Navagraha temples](/navagraha-temples-near-kumbakonam-the-nine-in-order/).

## The god
The main deity is Shiva as Vaidyanatheswarar, worshipped together with Thaiyalnayaki, a form of Parvati. The name Vaidyanatha, "lord of physicians," points to the idea of the god as a healer of the body and the mind.

## The story
According to the temple's tradition, Angaraka (Mars) was afflicted with leprosy and was cured by worshipping Shiva here. That is how the temple came to be the place for Mars. Another legend says that Jatayu, the vulture in the Ramayana, who fought Ravana to save Sita, was cremated here by Rama, and the place is also known by the old name Pullirukkuvelur.

## The sacred tank
The Siddhamritham tank in the temple is believed to contain the nectar of the gods. Devotees bathe in it before darshan. Many drop a small amount of jaggery, pepper or salt into it, as part of a vow.

## The temple's medicine
One of the best-known customs of the temple is the *thiruchandu urundai*, a small ball made of sacred earth mixed with the sandal paste and ash from the shrine, given as prasadam. Devotees believe it heals diseases. Others take sandalwood paste, the temple's vibhuti and the water of the tank.

Please treat this as a symbol of faith. It is not a medicine in the medical sense. If you are ill, you need a doctor.

## Ways of praying
- **Archana** with the name of the sick person.
- **Salt and pepper in the tank,** as a vow.
- **A ritual for Angaraka,** especially on Tuesdays.
- **Special days:** Tuesday, Krittika nakshatra days, and the Thai Poosam festival in January or February.

## Faith and treatment together
The honest teaching of the tradition is that prayer and medicine are not rivals. Even the name Dhanvantari, the divine physician of Hindu tradition, belongs to a healer who gave knowledge of medicine to humans. A good approach is:
1. **See a doctor first,** and follow the treatment.
2. **Pray alongside.** It brings peace to the patient and to the family.
3. **Do not stop treatment** because of a vow or a ritual.
4. **Be careful of anyone who claims a cure** in exchange for money.

For health prayers, see [which god to pray to for health](/which-god-to-pray-to-for-health-and-recovery-dhanvantari-and-shiva/) and the [Shiva mantras](/mantras/shiva/).

## Visiting
The temple is open from early morning, closes for several hours in the afternoon and reopens in the evening. It is crowded on Tuesdays. Dress modestly. Use the official counter and beware of agents.

*Beliefs and customs differ from family to family and temple to temple. Check timings, rules and costs on the temple’s official channels before you travel, and be careful with agents who offer to arrange rituals for large sums.*
