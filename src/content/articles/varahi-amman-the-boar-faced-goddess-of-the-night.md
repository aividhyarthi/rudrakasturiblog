Among the goddesses of South India, Varahi has become one of the names people search for most. She is not a gentle mother figure. She is the guard at the gate, and the one who is called when there is a fight to be won.

## Who she is
Varahi has the head of a boar and the body of a woman. She is the shakti, the power, of Varaha, the boar avatar of Vishnu who lifted the earth out of the cosmic ocean. She is also one of the Saptamatrikas, the seven mothers, who are the powers of Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Indra, Kartikeya, Varaha and Yama.

In the Lalita tradition, she is Dandanatha, the commander of the army of Lalita Tripurasundari, and her sister is Mantrini, the minister. When Lalita goes to war with the demon Bhandasura, Varahi leads the charge.

## What she looks like
Dark in colour, with a boar's face, riding a buffalo or sitting on a lotus, she carries a plough, a pestle, a goad and a noose in different traditions, and gives protection with another hand. The plough is a link to her boar form, which digs the earth.

## Why night and dawn?
Varahi worship is traditionally done at night, or in the hour before dawn, in quiet. The reason given is that she is the goddess of the hidden and the unseen. Her devotees speak of a quiet strength which does not make noise, like a guard who stands silent at the door.

## Panchami and Ashadha Navaratri
Panchami, the fifth tithi, is linked with Varahi. The nine nights of **Ashadha Navaratri**, also called Varahi Navaratri or Gupta Navaratri, are in the month of Ashadha (June to July). In the south, many homes and temples hold special worship for Varahi on those nights.

## What people ask her for
Devotees pray to her for protection from enemies and from fear, for success in disputes and for strength in difficult times. The honest teaching is that she is not a tool for harming people. Her devotion is about the inner enemies as much as outer ones: laziness, fear and doubt.

## The temples
There are Varahi shrines throughout India. The Chaurasi Varahi temple in Odisha is a very old temple dedicated to her, and in Varanasi there is a Varahi Devi temple which, by local accounts, opens only in the early morning hours. In Tamil Nadu and Andhra she appears among the Saptamatrika figures in many large temples, and some towns have a Varahi shrine of their own. If you plan a visit, check the exact name and timings for the particular place, since many small shrines keep unusual hours.

## A simple prayer
Many people simply recite her name with a lamp. Varahi's own mantras are traditionally taken from a teacher, and we have not printed them here for that reason. If you are new, it is better to start with the common prayers to the Devi, such as the [Durga mantras](/mantras/durga/).
