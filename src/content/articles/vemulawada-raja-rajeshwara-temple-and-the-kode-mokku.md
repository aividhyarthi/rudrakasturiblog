Telangana has many Shiva temples, but the one many families mention first is Vemulawada. Every year, around Maha Shivaratri, more than a lakh of people come to this small town. They walk, they bathe in the pond, and they bring bulls.

## Where it is
Vemulawada is in Rajanna Sircilla district of Telangana, about 35 km from Karimnagar and 150 km from Hyderabad. The Sri Raja Rajeshwara Swamy temple is the main attraction, and the town is often called **Dakshina Kashi**, the Varanasi of the South.

## The god
The Lord here is Shiva as Raja Rajeshwara, with Raja Rajeshwari Devi and Lakshmi Sahita Siddhi Vinayaka as other deities. The temple is old, with a Chalukya-period history, and has been a place of pilgrimage for many centuries. A well-known story links the name with a king, Narasimha, who was cured of leprosy by bathing in the temple tank, and with the ancient name Lembulavada.

## The Dharmagundam
In the temple complex there is a large pond called the Dharmagundam. Devotees take a dip in it before going in for darshan. It is believed that bathing here washes away sins and cures skin ailments. The pond is the first stop for most visitors, and there are separate arrangements for men and women.

## Kode Mokku
The custom that makes Vemulawada special is the **Kode Mokku**. *Kode* means a bull or a young bull, and *mokku* means a vow. A devotee who has made a wish, such as for a child, a job, a recovery or a good harvest, promises to bring a bull to the temple if it is fulfilled.

On the day of the vow, the devotee leads the bull around the temple, usually decorated with flowers, and gives an offering. The bull is treated as a representative of Nandi, Shiva's vehicle. The animals are not slaughtered. They are often donated to the temple's goshala or returned to the family.

It is a tradition that ties together farming, devotion and gratitude, and it is common in rural Telangana.

## Other things to do
- **Maha Shivaratri.** The temple's biggest festival. Devotees keep an all-night vigil with abhishekam and bhajans.
- **Karthika Masam.** Mondays in the month are especially busy. See [Karthika Masam 2026](/karthika-masam-2026-dates-deepam-and-fasting/).
- **Nearby shrines:** the Bheemeswara temple and a very old Narasimha temple on the hill, as well as the Bhadrakali shrine.
- **A tradition of harmony.** The town also has a dargah of Hazrat Bade Sahib, and local people of both faiths visit each other's festivals.

## Visiting
1. **Go on a weekday.** Festivals and Mondays are crowded.
2. **Dress modestly.**
3. **Don't bring a bull unless** you are doing the vow, and check with the temple on current rules.
4. **Use the official counters** for darshan and puja tickets.
5. **Plan a half-day** and add a stop at Karimnagar's other temples if you have time.

## A closer look at the idea of a vow
A *mokku* is a promise made to God, and it sits at the heart of many village traditions in Telangana and Andhra. The pattern is simple: you ask, you wait, and when the wish is fulfilled, you return to give thanks. The Kode Mokku adds something special: you bring something living and valuable to farmers, a bull, and offer it with respect.

It is worth noticing what the vow does for the person who makes it. It gives a worry a place to go. And it ties a family to a place and a time of return. Many families make the trip together, so that the vow becomes a story the children remember.

## Why the bull?
The bull is Nandi, the faithful companion of Shiva, who sits facing the sanctum in almost every Shiva temple. In a farming society the bull is also the strength of the farm: it ploughs the field and pulls the cart. To offer a bull is to offer something close to the heart of the family's livelihood. It is also a reminder of how closely the land and worship were bound.

## Questions people ask
**Is Vemulawada connected to Kashi?**
The town is called Dakshina Kashi, meaning "Kashi of the South". The name expresses how much the people hold the temple in esteem.

**Do I need to bring a bull to visit?**
No. Most visitors do not. The bull is part of a specific vow. You may come for ordinary darshan.

**Is it a good place for children?**
Yes, but festival days are very busy. Visit on a weekday if you have small children or elderly relatives.

**Does the town welcome people of other faiths?**
The town has a dargah as well as the temple, and local people of both faiths visit each other's festivals. Visitors are welcome so long as they come with respect.
