**Source:** Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda (chapters 9 to 10, 16 to 19).

It is one of the most difficult choices in the epic. Your brother is the king, and he is wrong. You have said so, politely and then not so politely. He has called you a coward and thrown you out. What do you do?

## The advice that was refused
Vibhishana, the younger brother of Ravana, was a man of dharma in a house that had lost it. When Hanuman burned Lanka and Rama's army began to gather, Vibhishana told Ravana in open council that the wise course was to return Sita. He told it again, more bluntly. Ravana's reply was cruel. The text says that he struck his brother with his foot in the court and told him to go, if he loved the enemy so much.

Vibhishana left. He did not go alone. Four ministers went with him. They rose into the air and travelled to the shore, where Rama's army was camped.

## The doubts in Rama's camp
What happened next is one of the most human scenes in the epic. The vanaras were suspicious. Here was a rakshasa, Ravana's own brother, arriving unannounced at their camp. Sugriva said that he could be a spy and that he should be killed. Several others spoke in the same way.

Hanuman argued in his favour, pointing to the way Vibhishana had come: openly, and in the manner of someone seeking refuge, not of someone hiding.

Rama listened to everyone, and then he said something that has echoed for thousands of years.

## The vow of refuge
> **sakṛd eva prapannāya tavāsmīti ca yācate**
> **abhayaṁ sarva-bhūtebhyo dadāmy etad vrataṁ mama**
> (Valmiki Ramayana 6.18.33)

*To the one who comes to me just once, saying "I am yours" and asking for protection, I give freedom from fear from all beings. This is my vow.*

He went further, in the verse that follows: whether it is Vibhishana, or Sugriva, or even Ravana himself who comes, he will protect him. And he reminded them of the old story of a dove who sheltered the hunter who had caught his mate, and gave even his own body as food. The point was that the one who gives refuge does not ask whether the refugee deserves it.

This is the verse on which the idea of *sharanagati*, surrender, rests in the tradition of Sri Vaishnavism.

## The crowning
Rama accepted Vibhishana at once. The text says that Lakshmana was asked to bring water from the sea and Vibhishana was consecrated king of Lanka, even before Lanka was won (Yuddha Kanda 19). He then told Rama about the strengths of Ravana's army and the defences of Lanka.

Tradition places the coronation at Dhanushkodi, near Rameswaram, where the temple of Kothandaramaswamy stands. Many people visit it on the way to Rameswaram.

## What it teaches
- **Dharma sometimes means leaving what you love.** Vibhishana did not leave out of ambition. He left because he could not stay in a house that was doing wrong.
- **Refuge is given without conditions.** Rama's vow is a promise that no one is beyond the reach of grace.
- **Doubt is reasonable, and can be answered.** The vanaras were right to be cautious, and Hanuman and Rama answered them.
- **Kindness and prudence can live together.** Rama listened to every argument first.

## A note for those who have had to leave
Many people have had to walk away from a family business, a home or a way of life that was no longer right. Vibhishana is sometimes called a traitor by those who find it comforting. The tradition does not. It remembers him as the man who chose truth at a price.

## A closer look at where Vibhishana began
Vibhishana was born into a family of great power. In the Uttara Kanda, the three brothers, Ravana, Kumbhakarna and Vibhishana, perform austerities and are granted boons. According to the text, when Brahma offered Vibhishana a boon, he asked that his mind always remain fixed on dharma, even in the hardest times. It is a beautiful request from a man whose life was to be tested in exactly that way.

That is why his later choice is not a sudden break. He was the same person all along. The family around him changed, and he did not.

## Questions people ask
**Was he a traitor?** Some people say so, and the phrase "a Vibhishana in the house" is sometimes used for a betrayer. The epic itself does not treat him that way. He spoke first, in open council, and only left when he was thrown out. Compare him with [Kumbhakarna](/kumbhakarna-the-brother-who-knew-it-was-wrong-and-fought-anyway/), who agreed with him and stayed to fight.

**Why did Rama trust him so quickly?** The text shows that Rama weighed the advice of his friends, and decided by the principle he stated. Hanuman's judgement of the way Vibhishana came also counted.

**What is the Srirangam connection?** A well-known tradition says that Rama gave Vibhishana an image of Vishnu as Ranganatha, and that it came to rest on the island of Srirangam, where it faces south, toward Lanka. This comes from later temple tradition and not from Valmiki. See [Srirangam](/srirangam-ranganathaswamy-temple-the-island-temple-of-vishnu/).

## Facing your own Ravana
Most of us will never face a king. But there are smaller versions: a workplace that cuts corners, a relative who wants us to help hide something, a group that is doing something unkind. Vibhishana teaches a clear order of steps.
1. Speak the truth first, plainly and with respect.
2. Try again, if you can.
3. If you are cast out, go without hatred.
4. Look for a place where you can still do right.
