**What our past deeds, the Mahabharata and the nature of time teach us about success, pride, karma and responsibility.**

## Sometimes, It Is Not You. It Is Your Yogam.

Have you ever noticed something about life?

There are periods when everything seems to work in your favour.

You say something, and people listen. You make a decision, and it turns out right. You meet the right people at the right time. Opportunities come looking for you. Even situations that appear difficult somehow turn in your favour.

People begin to admire you. Your influence grows. Your words carry weight. What you once struggled to achieve now seems to happen naturally.

And slowly, something changes inside you.

You begin to believe that this is happening because of you.

Your intelligence. Your capability. Your experience. Your power. Your ability to control people and situations.

But what if it is not entirely you?

What if this is your Yogam?

In the way many Indian spiritual traditions understand life, Yogam can be seen as a favourable period, a combination of circumstances, timing and the fruits of one's past actions. It is a time when doors open and opportunities come your way.

Some people understand this through the idea of Punya, the accumulated fruits of good deeds. Others understand it through destiny, planetary combinations or divine grace.

The interpretations may differ, but there is a powerful thought worth reflecting on.

**When life gives you the opportunity to rise, do not mistake the opportunity for proof that you are greater than everyone else.**

Perhaps life is giving you a chance to do something bigger. Something better. Something that benefits people beyond yourself.

And that is where the real test begins.

## The Biggest Mistake We Make When Success Comes

When things go right for us, we rarely stop to examine why.

We enjoy the attention. We enjoy the recognition. We enjoy the authority.

If people agree with us, we believe we are right. If our decisions succeed, we believe we cannot make mistakes. If people follow us, we assume we deserve their unquestioning loyalty.

But there is a difference between being successful and being right.

A person may be enjoying a favourable period in life. Their decisions may be producing good results. Their influence may be growing.

But that does not mean every decision they make is correct.

Sometimes, circumstances are favourable. Sometimes, people around us are making our success possible. Sometimes, we are receiving the fruits of actions performed long before the success became visible.

Yet we claim all the credit.

This is where gratitude disappears and ego takes its place.

And when ego takes over, we begin to misuse the very opportunities that life has given us.

A position that could have helped hundreds becomes a tool to control people. Wealth that could have created opportunities becomes a reason to look down on others. Knowledge that could have guided people becomes a weapon to prove superiority.

We forget that Yogam is not a certificate of greatness.

It is an opportunity that comes with responsibility.

## Duryodhana: When Yogam Becomes Ahamkara

The Mahabharata gives us one of the most powerful illustrations of this idea through Duryodhana.

Duryodhana was born into a royal family. He had access to power, wealth, influence and an entire kingdom. He had the support of powerful warriors, experienced elders and a formidable army.

He was not a person without opportunities.

But what did he do with them?

Instead of recognising the Pandavas as people with their own rightful claims, he allowed jealousy and entitlement to dominate his thinking.

His desire to retain power became more important than justice. His pride became more important than family. His ego became more important than the future of his kingdom.

Even when he had opportunities to avoid war, he refused to step back.

Consider the contrast.

Duryodhana had power, but he could not control his jealousy.

He had wealth, but he could not find contentment.

He had powerful people around him, but he could not listen to wise counsel.

He had opportunities to prevent destruction, but he chose pride over peace.

The Mahabharata does not present his downfall as a simple story of a king losing a war. It shows how one person's attachment to power can bring suffering to an entire family, a kingdom and generations of people.

Think about the people who stood behind him.

The warriors who fought for him. The elders who tried to guide him. The families who lost their sons. The people whose lives were destroyed by a conflict they had not created.

His decisions did not affect only Duryodhana.

They affected everyone who depended on the world he controlled.

**This is the danger of ego during a favourable period. You may think you are making decisions for yourself, but the consequences can fall on thousands of people who trusted you.**

## Ravana: When Knowledge and Power Stop Making You Wise

Ravana offers another lesson.

He was a learned ruler, a powerful warrior and a figure associated with extraordinary knowledge and devotion.

Yet, his knowledge did not prevent his ego from taking control.

His actions, particularly the abduction of Sita, set in motion the events that led to the destruction of his kingdom and his own death.

Ravana had the ability to understand. He had access to wisdom. He had people around him who could advise him.

But having knowledge and accepting wisdom are two different things.

When a person begins to believe that nobody can question them, even the best advice starts sounding like an insult.

That is when power becomes dangerous.

A person can be educated and still be arrogant. A person can be wealthy and still be insecure. A person can command an entire organisation and still lack the wisdom to treat one employee with dignity.

The real question is not how much power you have.

It is whether you have the humility to understand its limits.

## We Can See This Around Us Today

This is not merely a lesson from ancient kingdoms.

Look at the world around us.

We see people in positions of authority forgetting that the position is temporary. We see leaders confusing public trust with personal ownership. We see powerful individuals treating people who work under them as though they have no dignity of their own.

Every few weeks, a story reaches us of someone in authority humiliating a person who works under them, in an office, in a meeting, in a shop or at home. Most of us have seen some version of it, or felt it ourselves.

The point is not to judge any one person.

It is to ask a larger question.

When someone receives authority, does that authority give them the right to humiliate another human being?

A designation may give someone the power to make decisions. It does not give them the right to destroy another person's dignity.

And this is where the ancient lesson becomes relevant to modern life.

Your position may be giving you influence today. Your decisions may be accepted today. People may be afraid to disagree with you today.

But none of that means the world will always remain the same.

The people who are silent today may not remain silent tomorrow. The organisation that supports you today may change. The circumstances that protect you today may disappear.

Power is never a permanent personal possession.

It is a responsibility attached to a particular moment in time.

## The People Behind You May Pay for Your Ego

There is another part of this lesson that deserves more attention.

When a person rises, many people rise with them.

Their family enjoys the benefits. Their employees receive opportunities. Their supporters gain confidence. Their colleagues build their careers around the person's leadership.

But when that person begins to misuse power, the consequences do not necessarily stop with them.

Employees may lose their jobs. Families may face financial difficulties. Supporters may lose their credibility. Businesses may collapse. People who trusted the leader may find themselves paying for decisions they never made.

This is why ego is not merely a personal weakness.

When you occupy a position that influences other people's lives, your ego can become their burden.

The Mahabharata shows this through the destruction that followed Duryodhana's refusal to make peace.

He was not the only person who lost everything.

A war born out of the pride and ambitions of powerful people brought suffering to countless others.

The lesson is uncomfortable but necessary.

**When you are blessed with influence, remember that other people's lives may be connected to the way you use it.**

## Yogam Can Lift You. But Do Not Challenge Time.

Indian philosophical thought gives enormous importance to Kala, or time.

The Bhagavad Gita, in Chapter 11, presents Krishna's universal form and the overwhelming nature of time. In that revelation, Krishna declares himself to be Time, the destroyer of worlds.

This is a reminder of the limits of human control.

A person may rise today. Another may rise tomorrow. Kingdoms may become powerful and eventually disappear. Wealth may accumulate and then pass into other hands.

The person who believes that today's success will continue forever has misunderstood the nature of life.

Think of Yogam as a favourable season.

When the season is good, you can plant, build, grow and create something meaningful.

But if you spend that season insulting people, exploiting opportunities and building your identity around ego, what happens when the season changes?

You may discover that the very people who once stood behind you have been hurt by your actions.

You may find that the relationships you neglected cannot be repaired.

You may realise that the position you believed was permanent was only temporary.

And the painful part is that you may not have imagined such a day would ever arrive.

This is not a prediction that every successful person will face a dramatic downfall. Nor should every difficulty in life be treated as punishment for past actions.

It is a spiritual reminder to remain humble, responsible and aware of the consequences of our choices.

## What Should You Do When Yogam Favours You?

Perhaps the most important question is not how to recognise Yogam, but how to behave when life is favourable.

When people listen to you, use your words to guide rather than dominate.

When you have money, create opportunities for others.

When you have authority, protect the dignity of those who work with you.

When your decisions succeed, remember the people who helped make them possible.

When you receive recognition, do not assume that you are beyond criticism.

And when someone questions you, do not immediately treat them as an enemy.

A favourable period should make you more generous, not more arrogant.

It should make you more responsible, not more careless.

It should encourage you to do greater good, not give you permission to behave as though you are above everyone else.

## The Throne Is Temporary. The Responsibility Is Real.

There is a beautiful way to look at life.

Perhaps the success you are enjoying today is not entirely a reward for who you are. Perhaps it is also an opportunity to become someone better.

Perhaps the influence you have received is meant to help people who do not have the same opportunities.

Perhaps the position you occupy today is asking something of you.

The Mahabharata reminds us what happens when pride becomes more important than responsibility. The Ramayana shows us how knowledge and power can be destroyed by ego. The Bhagavad Gita reminds us that time is greater than individual ambition.

So, the next time everything goes right for you, pause for a moment.

Before taking all the credit, remember that life, circumstances, people and perhaps your own past actions may have brought you to that position.

Do not mistake favourable Yogam for permanent superiority.

Do not hurt people because you believe nobody can question you.

Do not assume that the people standing behind you will never suffer because of your decisions.

**The same Yogam that gives you the opportunity to rise may one day cease to support you.**

And when that day comes, the real question will not be how powerful you once were.

It will be what you did with the power you were given.

Because the greatest achievement is not reaching the throne.

It is leaving behind a world that is better because you once occupied it.
