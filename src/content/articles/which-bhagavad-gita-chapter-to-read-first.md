The Bhagavad Gita is a conversation of about 700 verses in 18 chapters, set on the battlefield of Kurukshetra just before war begins. Arjuna, the warrior, is confused and tells Krishna he will not fight. Krishna's reply is the Gita.

Many people begin at Chapter 1 and give up after the battlefield scene. You do not have to read it in order.

## A simple reading order
1. **[Chapter 2, Sankhya Yoga](/bhagavad-gita/chapter-2/).** The heart of the Gita: the self is not born and does not die, and how to act without being ruled by the result. [Verse 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/) is here.
2. **[Chapter 3, Karma Yoga](/bhagavad-gita/chapter-3/).** Why action cannot be avoided, and how to turn work into a spiritual practice.
3. **[Chapter 12, Bhakti Yoga](/bhagavad-gita/chapter-12/).** The way of devotion, and a famous description of a person dear to Krishna. It is short and easy to read.
4. **[Chapter 6, Dhyana Yoga](/bhagavad-gita/chapter-6/).** Meditation: how to sit, how to steady the mind, and how to balance effort and rest.
5. **[Chapter 18, Moksha Sanyasa Yoga](/bhagavad-gita/chapter-18/).** A summary of the whole teaching and Krishna's final advice.

## Then, by theme
- **Chapters 4 and 5** continue the theme of knowledge and action.
- **Chapters 7 to 11** speak of the nature of the divine, ending with the vision of the universal form in Chapter 11.
- **Chapters 13 to 17** explore the field and the knower, the three qualities (gunas) of nature, and the divine and demonic tendencies. Chapter 15 is read daily in many homes.

## How to read it
- **Take a chapter at a time.** A summary first, then the verses, makes a chapter easier to follow.
- **Pick a translation you can read.** Several good translations exist. Our chapter guides are written in our own words, so use them to find your way, and read a translation you trust alongside.
- **Read with a question.** "What should I do when I am afraid of failing?" is a good question to take to Chapter 2.
- **Go slowly.** One verse a day, thought about, is better than a chapter skimmed.
