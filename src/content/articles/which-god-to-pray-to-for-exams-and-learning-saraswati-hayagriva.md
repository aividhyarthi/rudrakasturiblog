It is eleven days before the exam. Your notes look like a mountain. Your mother has already been to the temple and tied a red thread to your wrist. In a South Indian home, exam season is also a prayer season, and two gods are most often asked for help.

## Saraswati
Saraswati is the goddess of knowledge, music and speech. She is shown in white, on a white lotus, holding a veena and a book. Students put their books at her feet on Saraswati Puja during Navaratri. Read about the temple in [Basar](/basar-saraswati-temple-where-telangana-children-write-their-first-letters/).

**The verse for beginning study:**
*Saraswati Namastubhyam Varade Kamarupini,*
*Vidyarambham Karishyami Siddhir Bhavatu Me Sada.*
*Meaning:* Salutations to Saraswati, who gives boons and takes any form. I begin my study. May I always succeed.

**The name mantra:**
*Om Aim Saraswatyai Namah* (ఓం ఐం సరస్వత్యై నమః). *Aim* is her seed sound. 11 or 108 times.

## Hayagriva
Hayagriva is a form of Vishnu with a horse's head, said to have retrieved the Vedas from demons. Many in South India, especially Tamil and Telugu students, pray to him for clear thinking and memory. There are famous temples at Thiruvaheendrapuram near Cuddalore in Tamil Nadu and in Assam.

**The well-known verse:**
*Jnananandamayam Devam Nirmala Sphatikakritim,*
*Adharam Sarvavidyanam Hayagrivam Upasmahe.*
*Meaning:* We worship the god of Hayagriva, full of knowledge and bliss, pure as crystal, the support of all knowledge.

## Others who help
- **Ganesha**, for a clear start. See [the Ganesha guide](/which-god-to-pray-to-for-obstacles-and-new-beginnings-ganesha/).
- **Dakshinamurthy**, the silent teacher, on Thursdays. See the [mantras page](/mantras/dakshinamurthy/).

## A simple routine
1. **Thursday or any morning,** after a wash, sit before a lamp.
2. **Say the Saraswati verse** and the Hayagriva verse once each.
3. **Open your book** and begin with the hardest topic while your mind is fresh.
4. **End the day** with a minute of thanks.

## The night before and the morning of
- Pack your things the evening before.
- Sleep. A rested brain remembers more than a tired one that studied until 3 a.m.
- Say the verse in the morning, and then forget about it. Don't use the time at the exam hall to recite.
- Eat a light breakfast.
- Breathe out slowly three times before the paper starts.

## An honest word
A mantra is not a substitute for preparation. Teachers often say that Saraswati helps the person who has already opened the book. And the Gita's advice is relevant: do your best and let go of the outcome. See [Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/).

## If you feel anxious
For nerves, see [yoga for anxiety and stress](/yoga-for-anxiety-and-stress-a-ten-minute-reset/). If anxiety about exams is overwhelming, talk to a teacher, a parent or a counsellor.

## A closer look at the two verses
The Saraswati verse above is the one many children are taught before they open a new book. It is short enough to say in a breath. The word *vidyarambham* means "the beginning of learning", and it is the same word used for the ceremony in which a small child writes the first letters. If you have a little one at home, read [Saraswati and the first letter](/saraswati-and-the-first-letter-aksharabhyasam-at-home/).

The Hayagriva verse is widely sung and is associated with the Hayagriva Stotra of the great teacher Vedanta Desika. Its picture is of a god pure as crystal, which suggests a clear, quiet mind. Many students find it helps to picture that clarity for a moment before they begin.

## Questions people ask

**Do I need to fast for the exam?**
No. Fasting can leave you light-headed. Eat well and drink water. A prayer will be just as welcome.

**Which day is best?**
Thursday is dear to teachers and Friday to Saraswati in some homes, but any morning is fine. Hayagriva Jayanti is kept on the full moon of the month of Shravana, and many students make a point of praying then. Saraswati Puja falls during Navaratri.

**What if I do not feel anything when I pray?**
That is common. Prayer is not a feeling you have to produce. Say the verse slowly, and give your full attention to the words for a minute. That is enough.

**What about children who are not good at exams?**
Marks measure one kind of ability. The old tradition honours singers, craftspeople, healers and farmers as much as scholars. Please be gentle with a child who is struggling, and look for help with the subject itself.

## Study habits that go with prayer
- Study in short blocks of 25 to 45 minutes, with five minutes of rest.
- Keep your phone in another room.
- Write answers out by hand once; it shows you what you really remember.
- Tell someone what you have learned. Explaining is the best test.

A small ritual and a good habit work together. Ask a parent to keep the house quiet for an hour. That is a form of seva too.
