A property dispute in the family. A court case that has run for years. A neighbour who will not stop. A boss who is unfair. Conflict is draining in a way few things are, because it keeps showing up in your thoughts.

People in these situations have long prayed to a few gods.

## Durga: the protector
Durga rides into battle against injustice. She is the goddess of protection for the one who is wronged.

*Sarvamangala Mangalye Shive Sarvartha Sadhike,*
*Sharanye Tryambake Gauri Narayani Namostute.*
*Meaning:* Salutations to Narayani, the auspiciousness in all that is auspicious, Shiva's consort, who grants all aims, the refuge of all, three-eyed and fair.

See [Durga mantras](/mantras/durga/) and also [Varahi](/varahi-amman-the-boar-faced-goddess-of-the-night/), the commander of the Mother's army, who is worshipped by those facing opposition. Note that her own mantras are traditionally taken from a teacher.

## Narasimha: the protector of the innocent
Narasimha burst from a pillar to save the child Prahlada. See [the Narasimha verse](/which-god-to-pray-to-for-fear-and-courage-hanuman-narasimha-durga/).

## Rama: dharma and truth
Rama is the model of someone who stood by truth even when it cost him. A prayer such as *Sri Rama Rama Rameti* is said for peace of mind and steadiness. See the [Rama mantras](/mantras/rama/).

## What to ask for
This is important. Most teachers say:
- **Ask for fairness,** not for victory at any cost.
- **Ask for strength** to face it.
- **Ask for a clear mind** to make good decisions.
- **Ask for the other person's mind to soften.**
- **Do not ask for harm to come to anyone.** Mantras for harming people are condemned in the tradition, and they tend to trouble the person who uses them more than their target. Be careful of anyone offering to do "black magic" for a fee. It is a fraud.

## What to do alongside
1. **Get a good lawyer,** and follow their advice. Keep every document.
2. **Don't talk about the case** to people who may pass information on.
3. **Stay calm in writing.** What you write can be shown to a judge.
4. **Consider mediation.** Many family and property disputes end faster by settlement than by judgement, and with less damage.
5. **Set limits.** Decide how much time and money this dispute is allowed to take.
6. **Look after yourself.** Sleep, exercise, friends.

## For the mind
Conflict makes the mind loop. [Yoga for anger](/yoga-for-anger-cool-the-body-and-the-mind/) and [the Gita on anger](/bhagavad-gita-on-anger-krishna-teaching/) help.

## A thought from the epics
The Mahabharata is a long story of a family dispute that went to war, and nobody comes out of it happy. Krishna himself went to the Kauravas as a peace envoy before the war. The epic does not say that conflict can always be avoided. It does say that it should be the last resort.

## Questions people ask

**Is it wrong to pray that I win?**
Many teachers suggest asking for justice and a fair outcome rather than for victory over a person. You can also ask that the dispute end soon and with the least damage on both sides.

**What if the other person is clearly in the wrong?**
Then let the facts and the process speak. Anger gives the other side a weapon. A calm record is your best friend.

**Can a mantra help me in court?**
A mantra can help you stay steady while you wait and while you speak. It cannot change the evidence. Please be wary of anyone who promises a result in exchange for money.

## A line from the Gita
Krishna's advice to Arjuna as he prepares for battle is to treat gain and loss, victory and defeat alike, and then take up the work (Gita 2.38). It is not an instruction to be careless. It means to do what is right without letting the outcome control your mind. That is a great help in a long case.

## Talking to family in a dispute
When the conflict is within your family, try a few things:
1. Speak one to one, not in a group.
2. Begin with what you value in the person.
3. Write down what you want and what you can give up.
4. Ask a respected elder or neutral relative to mediate.
5. Agree on a time to talk again, so no one feels brushed off.

Not every dispute can be solved this way, but many soften when people feel heard.

## On the day of a hearing
Sleep early, eat a light meal and carry your papers in order. Light a lamp, say a short prayer to the deity you love, and leave home with time to spare. In the court, speak politely and briefly. Afterwards, whatever the outcome, take a walk and have some food. For truthfulness as a strength, read [Satyameva Jayate](/read-the-line-mundaka-upanishad-satyameva-jayate/).
