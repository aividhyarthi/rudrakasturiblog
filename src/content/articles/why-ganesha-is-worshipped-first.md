Whatever festival or ceremony you are at, one thing never changes. The first prayer goes to Ganesha. A wedding, a housewarming, a child starting school, even a new notebook. His is the first name.

## Why first?
Ganesha is Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles. He is also Vighnakarta, the one who can place obstacles, which is why people want him on their side before they begin. A prayer at the start is like asking the doorkeeper to let you in.

There is also a story about how he earned the place. Shiva and Parvati set a race between their sons, Ganesha and Murugan: whoever circled the world first would win a prize. Murugan flew off on his peacock. Ganesha simply walked around his parents, saying that they were his whole world. He won.

## How he got the elephant head
The most popular story goes like this. Parvati made a boy out of the paste she used for her bath and set him to guard the door while she bathed. Shiva returned, and the boy, who did not know him, refused to let him in. In the argument that followed, Shiva cut off the boy's head.

Parvati was devastated. To console her, Shiva promised to restore the boy and sent his attendants to bring the head of the first creature they met facing north. It was an elephant. Shiva fitted it on the child and declared that he would be honoured before all other gods.

Other Puranas tell it differently, but this is the version most Telugu families grew up with.

## The broken tusk
Ganesha has one tusk because of the Mahabharata. When Vyasa wanted a scribe for the epic, he asked Ganesha, who agreed on one condition: he would not stop writing. Vyasa agreed on his own condition: Ganesha must understand every verse before writing it. When Ganesha's pen broke in the middle of dictation, he broke off his own tusk to keep writing. That is why you often see him holding the tusk.

## The small mouse
It always surprises people that this big god rides on a tiny mouse. The usual explanation is that the mouse stands for restless desire and the sharp mind that can slip through anywhere. Ganesha riding it shows the mind brought under control.

## Modak, durva and red flowers
His favourite sweet is the modak, a sweet dumpling. His favourite offerings are durva grass, in bunches of twenty-one, and red hibiscus. If you are doing a short puja, a lamp, some durva and the mantra *Om Gam Ganapataye Namah* is enough.

## The days to remember
- **Chaturthi**, the fourth tithi, every fortnight.
- **Sankashti Chaturthi**, in the dark fortnight, with a fast broken after moonrise. See [this month's date and moonrise time](/sankashti-chaturthi-october-2026-date-moonrise-and-vrat/).
- **Vinayaka Chavithi**, the big festival, in the month of Bhadrapada.
