If you ask a Telugu grandmother which is the holiest month, a good number will say Karthika. The streets of villages fill with small lamps at dawn and dusk, temples are crowded on Mondays, and a feeling of devotion settles on the whole month. What is it about Karthika?

## What the name means
The Hindu lunar months are named after the star, the nakshatra, that the Moon is near at the full moon. In this month, the full moon sits near the star group **Krittika**, the Pleiades. So the month is Karthika.

This is the same star group that gave Murugan his name Kartikeya. In the story, the six Krittika women raised the six babies who became Subramanya. So Karthika is also linked with him.

## The dates in 2026
Karthika Masam, as counted in Andhra, Telangana and the amanta calendar, runs from **10 November to 8 December 2026**. It starts the day after Deepavali Amavasya and ends with Kartika Amavasya. The main days are the four Mondays (16, 23, 30 November and 7 December) and Kartika Pournami on 24 November. For all dates see the [Karthika Masam 2026 guide](/karthika-masam-2026-dates-deepam-and-fasting/).

## Why is it holy?
Two gods share this month, and that is unusual.
- **Shiva.** Mondays are Karthika Somavaram. The month is linked with the defeat of the demon Tripurasura on the full moon. Shiva temples glow with lamps.
- **Vishnu.** In the north, the month is called Damodara, after Krishna being tied with a rope by Yashoda. Vishnu is worshipped with the Tulasi plant, and the month includes the Ekadashi on which Vishnu wakes from his four-month sleep.

Both are honoured with the same simple act, the lighting of a lamp.

## Who to worship
| God | Why in Karthika | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Shiva** | Mondays, Tripura story | Abhishekam, bilva leaves, *Om Namah Shivaya* |
| **Vishnu (Damodara)** | The month of Damodara | Tulasi, lamp, *Om Namo Narayanaya* |
| **Subramanya** | The Krittika link, Skanda Shashthi | Red flowers, *Om Saravanabhavaya Namah* |
| **Tulasi Devi** | Tulasi Vivaha | Water and lamp each day |
| **Ayyappa** | Mala vow begins | The 41-day vratam |

Choose the one you feel closest to. You don't have to do all.

## A simple daily plan
1. **Rise early** and bathe, if you can before sunrise.
2. **Light a lamp** at the home shrine. Many families also light one at the Tulasi plant, and one at the door at dusk.
3. **Chant** a short mantra, 11 or 108 times.
4. **Eat simply.** Many families avoid onion, garlic and non-vegetarian food for the month.
5. **Give something:** a lamp, oil, food or time.
6. **Read** a little of the Karthika Puranam or the Gita each day.

## Why lamps?
The lamp stands for knowledge driving out darkness, as well as the hope that light will win even when the days are getting shorter. In the dark half of the year, in cool mornings and cool evenings, a lamp is also just beautiful. We look at this more closely in [the story of Karthika Deepam](/karthika-deepam-why-we-light-lamps-and-how-to-do-it-well/).

## What to avoid
Don't turn it into a competition or a source of stress. If you cannot rise at five, rise at six. If you are ill, pray from your bed. The month is meant to lift you, not to make you feel guilty.
