Someone you studied with has bought a flat. A cousin has been promoted again. A friend's wedding photographs come up on your phone while you are looking at the electricity bill. None of them has done anything to you. And still, you feel a small, hard weight settle in your chest.

This is not wickedness. It is one of the most ordinary pains in the world. And the old teachers had a good deal to say about it.

## The two birds
The Mundaka Upanishad has a lovely image that you may have seen on a bookmark and not stopped to think about.

> **dvā suparṇā sayujā sakhāyā samānaṁ vṛkṣaṁ pariṣasvajāte**
> **tayor anyaḥ pippalaṁ svādv atty anaśnann anyo 'bhicākaśīti**
> (Mundaka Upanishad 3.1.1)

*Two birds, close companions, sit on the same tree. One eats the sweet fruit. The other does not eat, and only watches.*

The commentators read it as a picture of the person. One bird is the part of us that is busy tasting, comparing, wanting more. The other is the quiet witness that has nothing to gain and nothing to lose. In the next verse, the bird that eats is said to be sad and confused; when it looks at the other bird, it is freed of its sorrow.

You do not have to accept the whole philosophy to see what it says about envy. When you are comparing, you are the bird eating the fruit. Even when you win, the next tree has a sweeter fruit. There is another part of you that is not in the race.

## What Krishna tells Arjuna about your road
The Gita has a line that is easy to miss.

> **śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt**
> (Bhagavad Gita 3.35)

*Better one's own duty, even if imperfectly done, than the duty of another well done.*

It is not telling you to settle for less. It is saying that your road has its own contours, shaped by your gifts, your family, your circumstances. A life lived by comparison is always someone else's life, done a little worse.

## Karna, the great comparer
If any figure in the Mahabharata knew this ache, it was Karna. He was as skilled as Arjuna, and was famed for his generosity. But he was raised by a charioteer, and was refused a place at the tournament. From that day his life was shaped by a single thought: *I must prove I am the equal of Arjuna.*

He found a friend in Duryodhana, who gave him a kingdom when no one else would. But the need to be better than Arjuna bound him to a side he knew was wrong. The tragedy of Karna is not that he lacked talent. It is that he let another man's life become his measure.

## Some small, practical things
- **Notice what triggers you.** If scrolling does it, scroll less.
- **Write down what you actually want,** not what others have. Many people find that half of what they envy is not what they want.
- **Compare with yourself of a year ago.** Are you kinder, more skilled, calmer? That is the only fair comparison.
- **Be glad in the way you can.** Sending a sincere message of congratulations to a friend, even a small one, loosens envy more than any argument.
- **Remember what you cannot see.** Behind every photograph is a life with its own storms.
