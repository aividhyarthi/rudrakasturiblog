It was an ordinary day. A scan, a blood test, a doctor looking at the screen for a little longer than usual. And then the sentence that divides a life into *before* and *after*.

And it comes again, the same question: *Why me? I did not smoke. I walked every morning. I was careful.*

Let me say first what I will not do. I will not tell you that your illness is the result of something you did in a past life. No one can know that, and it helps no one. What I can do is show what the old teachers said about the body and about the person who lives in it, and how two of the greatest saints of the last two centuries faced exactly this.

## The body is not the whole of you
The Gita opens its teaching on this very point. Krishna says:

> **dehino 'smin yathā dehe kaumāraṁ yauvanaṁ jarā**
> **tathā dehāntara-prāptir dhīras tatra na muhyati**
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.13)

*As the embodied one passes through childhood, youth and old age in this body, so it passes to another body. The steady person is not confused by this.*

Read it slowly. It begins with something we all know from our own lives. You were a child, then a young person, now older. The body changed completely. Yet you still say *I*. Something stayed. That is the first step of the teaching: *you are not only your body*.

A few verses later he adds: *na jāyate mriyate vā kadācit*, it is never born and it never dies (2.20). This does not make pain unreal. It gives you a place to stand while the pain is there.

## It is not a reason to skip treatment
A word of caution. Some people read verses like these and say, *then why take medicine?* That is a mistake, and the tradition does not make it. Ayurveda is called the *upaveda*, a branch attached to the Vedas. Dhanvantari, the divine physician, is a form of Vishnu, and he rose from the ocean holding the pot of nectar. The physician is treated as God's hand.

So the rule is simple: *pray, and follow your doctor*. Faith that tells you not to treat an illness is not faith, only fear wearing a saffron cloth.

## Two lives to hold on to
**Sri Ramakrishna** lived for a year and a half with cancer of the throat in 1885 and 1886. Great saint though he was, he could barely swallow. His disciples were distraught. When one of them said that he could cure himself if he only asked the Mother, he is said to have replied that he could not ask her for such a thing. He was cared for by Sarada Devi and the young disciples, and they remember that even then he went on teaching.

**Ramana Maharshi** developed a sarcoma on his arm in 1948. He had operations that did not stop it. In his last weeks, people who came to see him found him calm, and when his devotees wept he told them, in effect, that he was not going anywhere. He died in April 1950 and the account of that evening, the light that people saw moving across the sky over Arunachala, is among the best known stories of the ashram.

Neither was spared the body's trouble. Neither was a sinner. And neither ever said that illness is a measure of a person. That is the most important thing these two lives say to us.

## What helps, practically
- **Let yourself feel it.** Fear and anger are not failures of faith.
- **Get the best care you can,** and ask your doctor everything.
- **Keep one small daily practice,** such as three minutes of breathing, a few verses, or a lamp. When much is taken from you, a small steady habit holds.
- **Do not let others hand you a theory.** Not every relative who says *it is karma* is helping.
- **Let people help you.** Allowing others to care for you is also a form of dharma for them.

## A verse to keep near
The Mahamrityunjaya mantra is chanted for health and healing across India. It says *do not let me be cut from life as the ripe cucumber is cut from its vine, but let me be free of death into the deathless*. It is not a bargain with God. It is a way to hold the fear in words.
