Few questions cause as much quiet pain as this one. At every wedding, someone looks at you and then looks away. At every festival, a relative asks if there is any news. You smile and change the subject, and then at night you ask yourself *why me?*

If you are in that wait, this is for you. I will not tell you that it is a punishment, because it is not. I will not tell you it will end by a certain date, because no one knows. What I can do is show you that this is an old pain, and that the people who bore it in the old stories were not found wanting.

## Dasharatha and the long wait for sons
King Dasharatha of Ayodhya had everything: a kingdom, three queens, the respect of sages. What he did not have was a son. In the Balakanda of the Valmiki Ramayana (chapters 8 to 16) we are told that he worried about it for years. He consulted his ministers and sages, and the sage Rishyasringa was brought to perform the *Putrakameshti* sacrifice. At the end of it, the story says, the four sons were born.

There are two things worth noticing. First, that the epic treats his wish as a good and natural one, not as a failing. Second, that he is not told he did something terrible. He is told what to do next.

## What the tradition does not say
You may hear, from well-meaning relatives or the person selling a ritual, that your delay is the result of a terrible sin or a *dosha*. I want to say plainly: the great texts do not teach this. They do not tell a woman waiting for a child that she is cursed. The great weddings and births in the epics come in their own time, for the best and the worst of people.

Astrology and rituals are part of many families' practice, and if they bring you comfort that is fine. But if someone is frightening you, or asking for large sums of money, stop. Genuine religion does not make a person feel more ashamed.

## Some ways to carry the wait
- **Pray in the way that moves you.** Many families pray to Katyayani and Gauri for marriage and to Santana Gopala or Subramanya for children. Prayer calms the one who is praying, whatever else it does.
- **Do the practical things too.** Where children are concerned, see a doctor, and see one early. A couple has the right to know what is going on, and many problems are treatable. For marriage, let friends and family help in good faith.
- **Have a short answer ready.** *We will let you know when there is news* works for most. You owe no one an explanation of your life.
- **Choose your confidants.** Tell one or two people who can listen without advising.
- **Keep a life that is not on hold.** Work, study, music, service. Waiting is not the same as stopping.

## What the Gita adds
The Gita does not tell us to stop wanting. It tells us not to make our peace hostage to the result.

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana**
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

*Your right is to the action, never to its fruits.* In practice, this can mean that you do all that you reasonably can, and then you let the day be a day. That is not giving up. It is the only way many people find they can go on.

## When it weighs on your mind
Years of waiting can bring low mood, anger at your own body or your family, even a feeling that life has no direction. If you notice that, please speak to a counsellor or doctor. Tele-MANAS (14416) is free in India. This article is not medical or astrological advice.

## Parvati's long wait
There is another waiting story that is often told to people in your position. In the tradition, the goddess Parvati longed to marry Shiva, who was deep in meditation and did not turn towards her. She did not give up and she did not complain. She took up austerities in the forest and kept at them for a very long time. In the Puranic accounts, Shiva finally came, and they were married.

You do not need to copy her penance, and no one is asking you to. What is worth taking from the story is the steadiness. She went on being herself, and she did not treat the delay as a verdict on her worth.

## Questions people ask
**Is it wrong to feel angry about this?** No. Anger and grief are normal when something you want deeply does not come. Let yourself feel them, and speak to someone you trust.

**Should I do every ritual that is suggested?** Choose the ones that give you peace and that your family can do without strain. If a ritual costs more than you can afford, or leaves you more afraid, put it down.

**Does a delay mean something is wrong with me?** No. People marry and have children at very different times for reasons of health, work, circumstance and plain chance. Only a doctor can tell you about your health, and it is wise to ask.

**What do I say to a relative who keeps asking?** You can say, kindly, that you would rather not discuss it, and change the subject. You are allowed to protect your privacy.

## For the family around you
If you are the parent, aunt or friend of someone who is waiting, you matter more than you know. Please do not count the years out loud, compare, or offer remedies uninvited. Ask how they are, and then simply listen. A calm home is the best help a person can have in a long wait.
