There are some questions that have no answer, only company. *Why did she go so early? Why him? Why us?* If you are reading this after a loss, I am sorry. I will not try to fix it. No verse can. But the old texts have walked this road before you, and it may help to know that you are not the first to stand here.

## Dasharatha, who could not live without his son
When Rama left for the forest, King Dasharatha could not bear it. In the Valmiki Ramayana (Ayodhya Kanda), he calls Rama's name again and again in his last nights, and dies of grief. He was a king, a warrior, a man who had waited decades for his sons. Grief did not care about any of that.

It is worth remembering when you feel ashamed of how deeply you feel. The *Ramayana* itself holds up grief as the sign of love, not weakness.

## Arjuna and the loss of a son
Abhimanyu, Arjuna's sixteen-year-old son, was killed in the war, trapped in the chakravyuha. In the Mahabharata (Drona Parva), Arjuna is struck dumb. Yudhishthira cannot look at his face. Even Krishna, who has all the knowledge there is, had to console a grieving father and a grieving mother. He did not tell them to stop grieving.

## What the Gita says, and what it does not
Krishna's first great teaching to Arjuna is about this:

> **na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin nāyaṁ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ**
> **ajo nityaḥ śāśvato 'yaṁ purāṇo na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre**
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.20)

*It is never born and never dies. It does not come to be and cease to be. Unborn, eternal, ancient, it is not killed when the body is killed.*

Many people read this as a rule that you must not grieve. That is not what it is for. Krishna says it to Arjuna on a battlefield, where Arjuna must act. For a family sitting at a funeral, the same teaching is a place to rest, not a command to stop crying. Arjuna himself wept again after Abhimanyu's death, and no one corrected him.

## The question of the yaksha
In the forest, the five Pandavas come to a lake. A yaksha, a spirit, forbids them to drink until they answer his questions. Four brothers drink anyway and fall dead. Only Yudhishthira stops to answer. The yaksha asks him, *What is the most wonderful thing in the world?*

> **ahany ahani bhūtāni gacchantīha yamālayam**
> **śeṣāḥ sthāvaram icchanti kim āścaryam ataḥ param**

*Every day, beings go to the house of Yama, yet those who remain wish to live forever. What could be more wonderful than this?*

He is not scolding. He is describing something strange about all of us: we know, and yet we live as if we did not. That is why a death shakes us so. It shows us the thing we have been quietly not looking at.

## What helps
- **Give grief time.** There is no deadline. Months, even years, are normal.
- **Keep the rituals if they comfort you.** The thirteen days, tarpanam, the shraddha are old ways of letting a family do something with its love.
- **Talk about the person.** Say their name. Tell the stories.
- **Look after the body.** Eat, drink water, sleep. Grief is physical.
- **Do not rush to be the strong one.** Some families need someone to cry first.

## If it does not ease
If weeks pass and you cannot eat, sleep or work, if you feel that life has no point, or if you have thoughts of harming yourself, please see a doctor or counsellor. This is not weakness. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is free, and open day and night.

## A thought
The Gita does not promise that the pain will leave. It says that the one you lost was never only a body, and that love does not end where the body does. You may not believe it today. Keep the verse. Some day it may come back to you in a different voice.
