One of the hardest things to bear is not hardship itself, it is being misunderstood. A rumour travels faster than the truth, and by the time you hear it, it has already reached your relatives, your office, your street. You did not do it. Yet people look at you differently, and you cannot walk around explaining.

*Why me?* is a question with a particular ache in it when it is about your name.

## The god who was falsely accused
There is a story in the Bhagavata Purana (Canto 10, chapters 56 and 57) that very few of us hear as children, and it is worth hearing as adults.

A Yadava named Satrajit had a jewel called Syamantaka, given to him by the Sun god, which yielded gold every day. Krishna suggested that it should be given to the king for the good of everyone, but Satrajit refused. One day Satrajit's brother Prasena wore the jewel into the forest, and a lion killed him. The jewel was then taken by Jambavan, the old bear king of the Ramayana.

When Prasena did not return, people began to whisper that Krishna had killed him for the jewel. The accusation was false, and Krishna was hurt by it. The text says he decided to *clear his name*. He went into the forest, followed the trail to a cave and found the jewel in the hands of Jambavan's child. He fought Jambavan for many days. In the end Jambavan recognised him, bowed to him, and gave him the jewel along with his daughter Jambavati. Krishna returned the jewel to Satrajit, who, ashamed of his own words, gave his daughter Satyabhama in marriage.

There are a few things in this story. Krishna did not ignore the rumour. He went after the facts. He did not fight the people who spoke it. He found the proof. And the story ends not with revenge, but with the jewel returned to its owner.

In Andhra and Telangana this is the story that is told on Vinayaka Chaturthi, when people recall the tradition of avoiding the sight of the moon on that night.

## Sita and Draupadi
Sita, after the war, was asked to prove herself in the fire (Yuddha Kanda). Later, in the Uttara Kanda, which scholars regard as a later addition to the Ramayana but which is deeply rooted in the tradition, she is sent away because of a rumour. Draupadi was dragged into the court and insulted before the elders. Neither of them was at fault. Both of them are among the most honoured women in the tradition.

The honour was not for the accusation. It was for the way they carried it: with dignity, and without letting it turn them into something smaller.

## What the Gita says
> **samaḥ śatrau ca mitre ca tathā mānāpamānayoḥ**
> **śītoṣṇa-sukha-duḥkheṣu samaḥ saṅga-vivarjitaḥ**
> (Bhagavad Gita 12.18)

*The same to enemy and friend, the same in honour and dishonour, in cold and heat, in pleasure and pain, free from clinging.* This is the devotee Krishna says is dear to him. It is not a person who feels nothing. It is a person who is not owned by the opinion of others.

## What to do
- **Do not rush to defend yourself in anger.** Pause for a night.
- **Gather the facts.** Messages, dates, witnesses, documents. Be calm and specific.
- **State your side once, clearly,** to the people who matter. Repeating it in every corridor makes it look like panic.
- **Ask for help from the right place.** A senior person, an HR office, a lawyer. If someone has made a false complaint, the law has remedies.
- **Let the people who know you speak for you.** A good name is held up by many hands.
- **Look after yourself.** Sleep, eat, walk. Shame is physical.

## If you are in distress
Being publicly blamed can lead to dark thoughts. If that is happening to you, please speak to someone now. Tele-MANAS (14416) is free in India, and 112 is for emergencies. This article is not legal advice.
