It usually happens on an ordinary day. A calendar invite with no title. A meeting with a person from HR you have never met. A few polite sentences, and then a box, a form, a handshake. By afternoon you are on the road with a bag, wondering what to say at home.

If this has happened to you, or to someone you love, I am sorry. This is not a piece to tell you that it is a blessing. It is to sit beside you for a little, and then to show what the old stories offer, and what practical steps may help.

## First: it is allowed to hurt
You may feel a hundred things at once: shock, anger, shame, relief, fear. Many people say the shame is the worst. We have been taught that who we are is what we do, so when the work goes, a piece of us feels gone.

It helps to know that this is a very old pain. The Gita begins with a warrior, Arjuna, who loses his footing in a different way. The Mahabharata and the Puranas are full of people who lose their work, their place and their status: kings who become servants, queens who become cooks. And they are not shamed for it.

## Harishchandra
The story most of us know is that of King Harishchandra, who lost his kingdom, his wealth and, in the end, even his family. He ended up working at a cremation ground, collecting fees. He did the job without complaint, and without lying. When the test was over, the gods restored all. We have told this in [Harishchandra: truth under trial](/harishchandra-truth-and-karma-under-trial/).

It is a story of dignity. What it says is not *you will get it all back* but *you can keep your name, wherever you work*. If the only job you can find for now is a humble one, it does not lessen you.

## The Pandavas
For thirteen years, the Pandavas lost everything: their kingdom, their comforts, their names. They spent the last year in disguise as servants in the court of King Virata. Arjuna, the greatest archer, taught dance. Bhima cooked. Yudhishthira, the king, served as a courtier. They did the job in front of them, did it well, and waited. When their time came, they were ready.

## What the Gita says
You will have heard the famous verse:

> **karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana**
> (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

*Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits.* In a time like this, it is easy to hear that as a cold slogan. It is better to read it as an offer. *You cannot control whether a company keeps you. You can control what you do today.* It does not say stop wanting a job. It says do not let your whole peace depend on the result of an application.

We have written about [what to do when the result does not come](/why-me-when-hard-work-brings-no-result/).

## A plain plan for the first weeks
Please take this as a friend's suggestion, not as professional advice.
1. **Tell someone you trust,** today. Do not carry it alone.
2. **Check the basics.** Notice period, severance, leave pay, insurance, loan dates. Make a short list of what is due and what is owed.
3. **Make a small budget** for the next three months. Knowing the number often reduces fear.
4. **Rest for a few days** before you rush. Sleep, eat, walk.
5. **Update your résumé** and tell your network. Many jobs are found through people who know you.
6. **Pick one skill** to learn or refresh. Even an hour a day gives a sense of movement.
7. **Keep a routine.** Get up, bathe, dress and begin at the same time. Many people find that the worst part of unemployment is the loss of structure.
8. **Keep your small practice.** A lamp, five minutes of breathing, a verse. When much is taken, a small steady thing helps.

## What not to do
- **Do not decide your worth from this.** A company's decision is about a company.
- **Do not hide it from your family** for long. It usually costs more to keep up the story.
- **Do not rush into a bad commitment** because of fear: a high-interest loan, an investment promise.
- **Do not compare.** Others' posts show only the good news.

## When it is heavy
If you are sleeping poorly, losing appetite, feeling hopeless for more than two weeks or thinking of harming yourself, please speak to a doctor or counsellor. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is free and open round the clock, and 112 is for emergencies. This is not weakness. It is the practical step of a person who is taking care of himself or herself.

## One more thought
Many people who have been through this say that it was the beginning of something they did not expect: a different career, a business, a move, a closer family. Some say it was the worst thing that ever happened to them. Both are true. The tradition does not tell you which it will be for you. It tells you how to walk in the meantime: honestly, patiently, with others.
