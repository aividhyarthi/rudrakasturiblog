Some wounds you can explain to anyone. A stranger cheats you, a boss is unfair. You can be angry and you know whom to be angry with. But when the wound comes from your own family, from the person who held you when you were small or the one you promised your life to, there is something else mixed in: disbelief.

*How could they? Of all people, them.*

If that is you, I want to say first: it is a real pain, and it is allowed to be as large as it is. Now let us see how the old stories deal with it. They do not pretend that family is always gentle. They are full of brothers, mothers and fathers who fail.

## Rama and a mother who asked for the wrong thing
On the morning that Rama was to be crowned, Kaikeyi, his stepmother, asked her husband for two boons: that Bharata be king and that Rama go to the forest for fourteen years. Dasharatha was broken, and Rama, who could have refused, did not.

What is quietly remarkable is how Rama speaks of her. In the Valmiki Ramayana (Ayodhya Kanda), he does not curse her, and when Lakshmana burns with rage he says that destiny is stronger and that no one should blame the queen. He is not saying it did not hurt. He is saying that he will not let the hurt turn into hatred.

## Prahlada and a father who wanted to destroy him
In the Bhagavata Purana (Canto 7), Prahlada is the son of Hiranyakashipu, who hated Vishnu. The boy loved Vishnu from childhood. His father tried to have him poisoned, thrown from a cliff and trampled by elephants. Prahlada never raised a hand against his father. But he also did not give up his faith to please him. He kept both his heart and his dharma.

There is a lesson here for many of us: *you can refuse to obey and still refuse to hate*.

## Vibhishana and a brother who would not listen
Vibhishana warned Ravana again and again to return Sita. Ravana insulted him in open court and threw him out. In the Yuddha Kanda Vibhishana leaves Lanka with a few loyal followers and goes to Rama. It cost him his home and his family. The tradition does not call him a traitor. It calls him a man who chose dharma when the people he loved would not.

Sometimes the answer to a family that harms you is distance. That is not a failure of love. It is a recognition that you cannot save someone who does not want to be saved.

## The Pandavas and cousins who cheated
The Pandavas lost their kingdom in a rigged game of dice, were sent to the forest for thirteen years, and were refused even five villages when they returned. Yudhishthira asked for peace until there was no peace left. Krishna himself went as an envoy. Only then did the war begin, and the Gita begins with Arjuna's reluctance to fight his own relatives.

What this shows is that dharma does not tell you to stay silent and let injustice happen just because the wrongdoer is family. It tells you to try everything first, and to do what has to be done without hatred.

## What can I take from this?
- **Your pain is not a sign of weakness.** Rama grieved. Prahlada was afraid. Vibhishana wept at leaving.
- **Do not let the wound decide who you become.** Rama's way was to keep his courtesy.
- **You may love and still keep your distance.** Dharma does not require you to stand where you are harmed.
- **Forgiveness is for you.** It does not mean saying that what happened was right. It means putting down a burden you were carrying for them.
- **Find one person who listens.** A friend, a teacher, a counsellor.

## A line of Krishna
> **uddhared ātmanātmānaṁ nātmānam avasādayet**
> (Bhagavad Gita 6.5)

*Lift yourself by yourself. Do not let yourself sink.* No one else can do your healing for you, but you are allowed to have help while you do it.
