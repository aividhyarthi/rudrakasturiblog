You are standing in the queue, the curtain opens, and the lamp is lit in front of the deity. And suddenly your eyes fill. You didn't plan it. You may not even know what you are crying about. The person next to you looks at you kindly or looks away. What is going on?

## You are in good company
Crying in temples is very common. Some people cry at the sight of the deity, or at a particular song. Some cry at the first hymn of the morning, and others, after years of holding it in, at a time of loss.

## What the devotional traditions say
In the Bhakti traditions, tears are not a failure of control. They are a sign of the heart's softening.
- The **Alvars**, the Tamil Vaishnava saints, and the **Nayanmars**, the Shaiva saints, wrote hymns in which they weep, call and complain to God like a lover separated from the beloved.
- Saints such as Meera and Tukaram describe being in tears before their Lord.
- The Bhagavata Purana lists tears and horripilation, hair standing on end, among the signs of devotion.

In these traditions, if you feel tears in front of the Lord, it is a gift, not an embarrassment.

## What psychology says
A temple has many of the things that help feelings come up:
- **A feeling of safety.** You are in a place that is meant for letting go.
- **Music and chanting.** Rhythm and voice go deep in the brain.
- **Attention.** In a queue, you stop and slow down, and what you have been avoiding catches up.
- **Ritual.** The familiar sequence loosens control.
- **Memory.** Smells and songs remind you of childhood and of people you have lost.

So the tears may be for something you have been carrying: grief, stress, loneliness, a hope that you've not dared to speak. Letting them out is usually healthy.

## What to do if it happens
- **Let it.** Don't push it away. Breathe slowly.
- **Don't be embarrassed.** Everyone in the temple understands.
- **Stay a little longer.** Sit in a corner or in the prakaram until it passes.
- **Be kind to yourself afterward.** Drink some water. Walk. Sit with a friend.
- **Ask what it was about.** Sometimes the tears point to something that needs attention.

## If someone near you is crying
Don't ask questions or tell them to stop. A quiet presence or a glass of water is enough.

## When to seek help
Tears in a temple are a good thing. But if you are crying often and without cause, feel sad or empty most days, or have lost interest in things, please speak to a doctor or counsellor. If you have any thoughts of harming yourself, call Tele-MANAS on 14416, free in India, or 112 in an emergency.

## A thought
Many people say that they went to the temple with a heavy heart and came out lighter, without a word being said. Whatever one believes about God, that is the gift of the place. See [what the Gita says about grief](/gita-on-grief-what-krishna-says-to-someone-who-has-lost-someone/).
