Some people you meet, and within ten minutes it feels as if you have known them for years. You finish each other's sentences. You trust them quickly. Or the reverse: you meet someone and feel a strange tension, as if there is unfinished business. People often say, "It must be a karmic connection."

What does that actually mean, and what should you do with the feeling?

## The Indian idea: rinanubandha
In many Indian families, the word you will hear is *rinanubandha*. *Rina* means debt, and *anubandha* means bond. The idea is that the people who come into our lives, parents, children, spouses, friends, even pets and neighbours, are connected to us by a balance of give and take from earlier lives. Some bonds are of love, some of duty, some of learning.

It is a way of understanding why you are close to some people and not others, why some bonds feel easy and others heavy. It is not a statement found in one particular text. It lives in family speech, in sayings and in the broader belief in karma that we have been writing about in our [karma series](/three-kinds-of-karma-sanchita-prarabdha-kriyamana/).

In astrology, the nodes Rahu and Ketu are called the karmic axis, and some astrologers read the seventh house of the chart for relationship bonds. That is a belief system, not something that can be proved.

## What psychology adds
Whatever one believes about past lives, there are also everyday reasons why a connection can feel instant:
- **Familiarity.** A voice, a face, a way of laughing that reminds you of someone from your childhood can make you feel safe before you know why.
- **Shared values and humour.** When two people see the world the same way, they feel understood quickly.
- **Timing.** If you are lonely or at a turning point, you may bond fast.
- **Attachment style.** For some people, an intense pull is actually anxiety. It feels like a deep connection because it stirs old needs, and that is not always a healthy sign.

None of this cancels the other view. Many people hold both: that the feeling is real, and that it has ordinary causes too.

## How to tell a good bond from a draining one
A karmic label can make a relationship feel fated. These questions help:
- After you spend time with this person, do you feel calmer, or more anxious?
- Are they consistent, or hot and cold?
- Do they respect your boundaries?
- Can you be yourself?
- Does it feel like love, or like a craving you cannot control?

A bond that is warm, steady and safe is a gift. A bond that is intense, confusing and painful is a signal to slow down, whatever name you give it.

## What the tradition actually asks of you
Hindu thought never says that because a bond is karmic, you must accept harm. The Gita asks you to act according to dharma and not to be driven by feeling alone ([Gita 2.47](/bhagavad-gita-2-47-meaning-karmanye-vadhikaraste/)). Dharma includes compassion for others and also protection of yourself. Rinanubandha itself suggests that a bond is something to settle, with kindness, repayment and forgiveness, and then to release when it is done.

If the person is hurting you, whether emotionally, physically or financially, "karmic" is not a reason to stay. Please talk to someone you trust, a friend, a counsellor, a helpline. You can be kind and still leave.

## What to do with a good connection
- Be grateful for it. Say so.
- Give back what you receive, in time, care and honesty.
- Do not rush it. A real bond survives patience.
- Do not make it responsible for your whole life.

## A last thought
Maybe we do meet some people for a reason. Whether or not that is true, the useful question is the same: given that this person is in my life, how do I act with honesty, care and good sense? That, as the Gita would say, is the part that is yours.
