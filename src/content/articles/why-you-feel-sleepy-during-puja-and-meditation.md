You light the lamp, sit down to pray and within a few minutes your head nods. You jerk awake, feel guilty, and try again. Almost everyone has been there.

## The ordinary reasons
- **You are tired.** If you pray at night after a long day, the body takes the first chance to rest.
- **You've just eaten.** Digestion pulls energy to the stomach. A heavy meal and a quiet room are a perfect recipe for a nap.
- **The room is warm and dim.** Lamps, incense and closed windows make a cosy sleep chamber.
- **Slow breathing relaxes you.** The same thing that calms the mind also lowers alertness, so a tired person may drop off.
- **You've missed sleep.** If you are short of sleep generally, the prayer is the first place it will show.

## What the tradition calls it
In the language of the three gunas, dull, heavy drowsiness is *tamas*, and the old teachers distinguish it from sattva, the calm alertness that meditation aims at. See [the three gunas](/gita-three-gunas-why-you-feel-restless-dull-or-calm/). The two feel different: the calm of sattva is bright. The drowsiness of tamas is thick.

The teachers also say, very practically, that if you are sleepy, you should sleep. An hour of rest will do more than an hour of struggle.

## What helps
1. **Sit upright.** Not on the bed or a very soft seat. A mat or a firm chair, with the spine straight.
2. **Splash cold water** on your face and eyes before you begin.
3. **Pray at a better time.** Early morning, after a bath and before breakfast, is traditional for this reason. If you pray at night, make it a short prayer before dinner, not after it.
4. **Keep the room cool and lit.** A bright lamp, an open window.
5. **Open your eyes.** Look at the deity, the lamp or a picture, rather than closing your eyes. It is a valid form of prayer called *darshan*.
6. **Stand or walk.** You can do your prayer standing, or do *pradakshina*, walking around the shrine.
7. **Chant aloud** instead of silently, or sing. It keeps the mind awake.
8. **Do a few rounds of breathing** that are energising, such as slow Surya Namaskar, before sitting.
9. **Eat lightly,** and leave two hours between a meal and meditation.
10. **Fix your sleep.** Most of the time, this is the real answer. See [yoga for sleep](/yoga-for-sleep-an-evening-routine-that-helps-you-wind-down/).

## Is it a bad sign?
No. Falling asleep in meditation is nearly universal. It doesn't mean that you lack devotion. Many saints are said to have struggled with it. A teacher's advice would be: don't scold yourself, don't give up, and change the conditions.

## When to see a doctor
Please get checked if:
- You feel very sleepy in the daytime, even after a full night.
- You snore loudly, or someone has seen you stop breathing in sleep. This can be sleep apnoea, which is treatable.
- You fall asleep suddenly at unusual times.
- You feel low or have lost interest in things. Oversleeping can be a sign of depression.

## A different way to see it
There is something touching about falling asleep in front of God. Many families say that the god of the house is like a parent, and a child falls asleep in a parent's presence without fear. Fix the practical things, and don't make it a source of shame.

## Questions people ask
**Is it disrespectful to doze off in front of the deity?** The tradition does not treat it so. Teachers speak of dull drowsiness as something to work with, not as an insult to God. If it happens, wake gently, sit up, and go on.

**Can I pray lying down if I am unwell?** Yes. Illness changes the rules. A prayer in bed, with the name of God in your mind, is fine.

**Does it help to have a cup of tea first?** Some people find a warm drink helps them feel alert. If you are sensitive to caffeine or have trouble sleeping, you may prefer plain water. Do what suits your body.

**Why am I fine while chanting, but sleepy in silence?** Voice and movement keep the body engaged. Silence gives a tired body nothing to do. That is why many teachers suggest beginners chant or sing before they sit in quiet.

## A short routine for an evening prayer
If your only time is at night, here is a way to make it kinder to a tired body.
1. Wash your face and hands, and drink a glass of water.
2. Sit on a firm seat or stand for the first minute.
3. Light the lamp and look at it for a few breaths.
4. Chant aloud, softly, for five minutes, with the beads or on your fingers.
5. Sit quietly for a minute or two, and then say thank you.
6. End before you are too tired. A short, awake prayer is better than a long, sleepy one.

## A closer look at the morning hours
Many traditions recommend the early morning for prayer and study, and the reason is partly practical. The mind is fresh, the house is quiet and you have not yet been pulled in many directions. You can read more in [Brahma muhurtham: time and meaning](/brahma-muhurtham-time-and-meaning/). But if early mornings do not work for you because of your work or family, do not feel you have failed. A prayer you can actually stay awake for is better than a perfect time you cannot keep.

## Resting on purpose
Some people find that a few minutes of deliberate rest before prayer helps them stay awake during it. Our piece on [yoga nidra](/yoga-nidra-the-practice-of-resting-while-awake/) explains a practice of resting while staying aware, which is a different thing from falling asleep.
