Period days are different for every woman. Some carry on as normal. Some curl up on the sofa with a hot water bottle. Whichever you are, gentle movement can make the days easier, and it is much kinder than pushing through.

## What research and teachers say
Small studies suggest that regular yoga can reduce period pain and the heaviness of the mood swings before periods. Yoga teachers in the Indian tradition also give a simple rule: before and during periods, practise softly. Think of rest and ease, not of effort.

## Poses that many women find soothing
**Child's pose (Balasana).** Kneel and rest your forehead on the floor, arms forward. It relaxes the lower back and belly. Stay for a minute or two.

**Reclined butterfly (Supta Baddha Konasana).** Lie on your back with the soles of your feet together and the knees falling out. Support the knees with pillows if it pulls. Rest your hands on your belly and breathe. This is a favourite for cramps.

**Cat and cow.** Slow rounds on hands and knees ease the lower back.

**Knees to chest (Apanasana).** Hug both knees in and rock gently.

**Easy seated forward fold.** Sit cross-legged and walk your hands forward, resting your head on a cushion or your hands. Keep it soft.

**Gentle supine twist.** Drop both knees to one side while lying on your back. Hold for five breaths each side.

**Rest pose (Shavasana).** Lie flat for five minutes and let your breath settle.

## Practices that many teachers suggest skipping on heavy days
- **Strong inversions** such as headstand, shoulderstand and handstand. The traditional advice is to avoid turning the body upside down while the flow is on. Some modern teachers say it is fine for some women, but there is no urgency to try.
- **Intense abdominal work** like many crunches and boat pose repeated.
- **Hot yoga or very vigorous flows**, especially if you feel weak.
- **Forceful breathing** such as Kapalabhati and Bhastrika.
- **Long, deep twists** if the belly feels sore.

Many women find the legs-up-the-wall pose (Viparita Karani) comforting even though it is mildly inverted. Traditions differ on this one, so use your own judgement and your teacher's advice.

## Breathing that helps
Slow breathing with a longer exhale than inhale, for example four counts in and six out, calms the nervous system. Humming bee breath (Bhramari) is also gentle. See [our pranayama guide](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/).

## Before the period: the week of mood swings
In the days before your period, a short walk, slow Surya Namaskar and regular sleep help many women. Cut down on very salty snacks and too much caffeine if you notice bloating. Moving daily matters more than any particular pose.

## When pain is more than ordinary
Period pain that stops you from going to work or school, pain that is getting worse with each cycle, very heavy bleeding that soaks through pads quickly, or pain with fever should be checked by a doctor. Conditions such as endometriosis and fibroids are common and treatable, and women are often told for years that the pain is normal when it is not.

## A gentle fifteen-minute sequence
If you want something simple to follow on a heavy day, try this order and stop at any point if it does not feel right.
1. Child's pose, one to two minutes.
2. Cat and cow, eight slow rounds.
3. Knees to chest, rocking softly for a minute.
4. Reclined butterfly, three to five minutes, with pillows under the knees.
5. Gentle twist, five breaths each side.
6. Rest pose, five minutes, with a warm cloth on the belly if you like.

## Questions people ask
**Should I stop exercising altogether on my period?**
Not necessarily. Many women feel better with light movement such as walking. Listen to your body: if you are tired, rest is a good choice too.

**Does warmth help?**
Many women find a hot water bottle or a warm drink soothing. Keep it comfortably warm and not hot on the skin.

**Can I do Surya Namaskar?**
Some women do a slow, gentle version, and others prefer to skip it on heavy days. Let your comfort guide you.

**Is rest lazy?**
No. Rest is part of care for the body. A tradition that values balance has always included it.

## Being kind to yourself
Take the pressure off. Some months your body will ask for more rest, and some months you will feel strong. Neither is wrong.
