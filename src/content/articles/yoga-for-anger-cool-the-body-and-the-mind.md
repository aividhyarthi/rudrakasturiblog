Anger is quick. By the time you realise you are angry, your heart is racing, your jaw is tight, your face is hot and the words are already out. This is why people say things they regret.

The good news is that anger gives a few seconds of warning in the body, and yoga trains you to notice them.

## The body signs
- A hot face or neck
- Clenched jaw and fists
- Fast, shallow breath
- A pounding heart
- Tight shoulders

When you feel two or three of these, you have a window of a few seconds. Use it.

## The emergency pause
1. **Breathe out slowly,** like blowing out a candle, for six counts.
2. **Drop your shoulders** and unclench your jaw.
3. **Press your feet into the floor.**
4. **Don't speak yet.** Say, "I need a minute," and step away.

## Cooling breath (Sheetali)
Roll your tongue into a tube if you can, and breathe in through it as if sipping through a straw. Close your mouth and breathe out through the nose. Do ten rounds. If you can't roll your tongue, press your teeth together lightly and breathe in through them (Sheetkari). Avoid this practice if you have a cold, asthma, or in very cold weather.

## Humming breath (Bhramari)
Breathe in, then hum on the exhale. Five rounds. The vibration is quietening.

## Move the heat out
Anger is energy. Giving it somewhere to go helps.
- **Walk fast** for ten minutes.
- **Slow Surya Namaskar,** three to six rounds. See [the twelve steps](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/).
- **Forward fold,** letting the head hang, for ten breaths.

## Rest afterwards
When the heat has dropped, lie down in Shavasana for five minutes. Notice how different the body feels.

## Build the gap over time
A daily practice of ten minutes of breathing and gentle movement makes you more aware of your body and gives you more of a gap before you react. This is the real gift of yoga for anger. It does not make you passive. It gives you a choice.

## What Krishna says
In the Gita, Krishna traces the path from a thought, to attachment, to desire, to anger, to confusion and ruin (2.62 and 2.63). The earlier you catch it, the easier it is. See [our article on this](/bhagavad-gita-on-anger-krishna-teaching/).

## When anger is a bigger problem
If you often lose control, if your anger has led to violence or harm, or if people around you are afraid of you, please seek help from a counsellor or doctor. Anger can come from stress, pain, sleep problems, trauma or other causes, and there are good ways to treat it. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) can point you to help.

*This is general information, not medical advice. If you have an injury, a long-term condition or you are pregnant, check with your doctor before you start.*
