Your heart is pounding, your chest feels tight and your thoughts are racing ahead to the worst possible outcome. Anxiety is a body event as much as a mind event, and that means the body is a good place to begin.

Here is a ten-minute routine you can do at home, at your desk or in a quiet corner.

## Minute 1 to 2: ground yourself
Stand with your feet flat and hip-width apart. Press your feet into the floor and feel your weight. Look around and name five things you can see, four you can touch, three you can hear. This pulls attention from imagined danger to real surroundings.

## Minute 3 to 5: breathe out
Sit comfortably. Breathe in for four counts and out for six. Keep your shoulders loose. After ten rounds, try humming on the out-breath for five more.

Stay away from fast, forceful breathing when you are anxious. It can make you dizzy.

## Minute 6 to 7: fold forward
Stand or sit and fold forward with soft knees, letting your head hang. Forward folds slow the heart and calm the mind. Stay for five or six breaths. Rise slowly.

## Minute 8 to 9: legs up the wall (Viparita Karani)
Lie on your back with your hips close to a wall and your legs resting up it. Rest your hands on your belly. Breathe slowly. This pose is a favourite for a tired, wired body. If it makes you dizzy or you have glaucoma or serious back problems, skip it and lie flat with your knees bent instead.

## Minute 10: rest
Lie down in Shavasana, with arms by your sides, palms up, eyes closed. Let go of effort. If thoughts come, let them pass like traffic.

## Add these in the day
- **Walk** outdoors for fifteen minutes.
- **Cut down caffeine,** which can feed an anxious body.
- **Sleep at the same time** each night.
- **Talk** to someone. Anxiety shrinks when it is spoken.

## Signs it needs more than yoga
See a doctor or counsellor if:
- You have panic attacks, with chest pain, trembling and a feeling of doom.
- You avoid places or people because of fear.
- Your sleep or appetite has changed for weeks.
- You have thoughts of harming yourself.

In India, call Tele-MANAS on 14416, free and open at all hours. If you or someone near you is in immediate danger, call the emergency number 112.

Treatment for anxiety works well, and many people use yoga alongside it.

## Why the out-breath matters
Many people find that a longer out-breath helps the body feel slower and safer, which is why this routine asks you to breathe out for six counts. You do not need to force it. If six feels too long, use three in and four out, and let it grow.

## Questions people ask

**What if I cannot lie on the floor at work?**
Skip the wall pose and the final rest. Do the grounding, the breathing and a seated forward fold with your hands on your thighs.

**How often should I do this?**
Once a day is plenty, and many people like to do it at the same time each day. A short routine you actually repeat is better than a long one you skip.

**Can I do it when I am panicking?**
Start with the grounding and slow breathing only. Keep the poses for later. If panic keeps coming back, please see a doctor.

**Does it replace treatment?**
No. It is a gentle support alongside whatever your doctor or counsellor advises.

## Two quick resets at your desk
- **Shoulder drop.** Breathe in and lift the shoulders to the ears, then breathe out and let them fall. Do it five times.
- **Hands on the heart.** Place one hand on your chest and the other on your belly, and take three slow breaths. Feel the body move.

Anxiety often gets louder at night. ## On the days it does not work
Some days you will do the whole routine and still feel jittery. That is normal and does not mean you did it wrong. Anxiety is not a test you pass. Be as kind to yourself as you would be to a friend, drink some water, step outside for a few minutes of daylight, and try again tomorrow. Small, repeated visits to calm do more than one perfect session.
