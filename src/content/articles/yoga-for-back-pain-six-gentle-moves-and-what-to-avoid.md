If you sit at a desk, drive a lot or spend the evening on a sofa with a phone, there is a good chance your back has complained to you recently. For most people, the cause is not dramatic. The muscles of the lower back are tired from holding you up for hours, and the muscles of the belly and hips that should share the work have gone lazy.

The good news is that slow, gentle movement helps many people. Here are six moves that yoga teachers commonly use for ordinary lower back stiffness.

## Before you start
- Move slowly. Back pain does not like speed.
- Breathe out as you go into a pose, and breathe in as you come back.
- A mild stretch is fine. Sharp pain is a signal to stop.
- Do it on a mat or a folded blanket, not on a slippery floor.

## 1. Cat and cow (Marjaryasana and Bitilasana)
Go onto your hands and knees. As you breathe in, let the belly drop and look up a little. As you breathe out, round the back like a cat and tuck the chin. Do ten slow rounds. This loosens the whole spine.

## 2. Knees to chest (Apanasana)
Lie on your back, bring both knees toward your chest and hold them gently with your hands. Rock a little from side to side. Stay for five to eight breaths. It stretches the lower back and calms it.

## 3. Child's pose (Balasana)
From hands and knees, sit your hips back toward your heels and rest your forehead on the floor with your arms stretched out in front. If your hips don't reach your heels, place a pillow under them. Stay for a minute.

## 4. Bridge (Setu Bandhasana)
Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, hip-width apart. Press into your feet and lift your hips a little, squeezing the buttocks. Hold for three breaths, lower slowly. Repeat five or six times. This strengthens the back of the body, which is what holds you up.

## 5. Gentle cobra (Bhujangasana)
Lie on your belly with your palms by your chest. Lift just the head and chest a few inches, keeping your elbows bent and your belly on the floor. Hold for three breaths. Keep the lift small. If this hurts, skip it.

## 6. Lying twist (Supta Matsyendrasana)
Lie on your back, hug one knee to the chest and drop it across your body to the opposite side, with the arms out wide. Look the other way if your neck feels fine. Stay for five breaths on each side. Keep the shoulders on the floor.

## What to avoid
- **Straight-leg forward bends**, such as touching your toes with locked knees. Bend your knees if you do forward folds.
- **Fast, forceful twists.**
- **Deep backbends**, like the full wheel.
- **Lifting both legs straight off the floor** while lying on your back, which can strain the lower back.

## A simple routine
Ten minutes a day is enough: cat and cow, knees to chest, bridge, child's pose and a lying twist. Walk for ten minutes afterwards. Every hour at your desk, stand up and walk for two minutes.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor soon if you have numbness or tingling in a leg or foot, weakness in a leg, pain that follows a fall or accident, fever with back pain, or any trouble controlling your bladder or bowels. Pain that is getting worse or that does not improve after a few weeks also needs a check.

## A word on the mind
Back pain and stress often travel together. Slow breathing during the poses does as much good as the stretch. If you want to add a breathing practice, read [our beginner guide to pranayama](/pranayama-for-beginners-three-safe-breathing-practices/).

*This is general information, not medical advice. If you have an injury, a long-term condition or you are pregnant, check with your doctor before you start.*
