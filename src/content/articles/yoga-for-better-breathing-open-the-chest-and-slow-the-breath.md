Notice how you are breathing right now. Chances are your shoulders are lifted a little and your breath is short and high in the chest. Most of us breathe like this when we are busy, stressed or hunched over a screen.

That is not dangerous, but it means we use only part of our lungs. A few simple habits help.

## Why posture matters
The main muscle of breathing is the diaphragm, a dome-shaped sheet under the lungs. When you slump, the ribs and belly are squeezed and the diaphragm has less room. So the first step to better breathing is to sit and stand taller, not to take bigger breaths.

## Five postures that open the chest
**1. Mountain pose (Tadasana).** Stand tall with feet hip-width apart, shoulders loose, chin level. Take five slow breaths. Feel the ribs widen.

**2. Arms-up stretch (Urdhva Hastasana).** Breathe in and raise your arms, breathe out and lower them. Ten times. It gently lengthens the sides of the body.

**3. Cat and cow.** Slow rounds on hands and knees move the ribs and spine together.

**4. Gentle cobra (Bhujangasana).** Lying on your belly, lift your chest a little with the elbows bent. It opens the front of the chest.

**5. Supported bridge (Setu Bandhasana).** Lie on your back with a cushion under the hips. It lifts the chest and lets the breath settle.

## Three breathing habits
**Belly breathing.** Put one hand on your belly and one on your chest. Breathe in through the nose so that the belly rises more than the chest. Breathe out slowly. Five minutes, twice a day.

**Longer exhale.** Breathe in for four counts and out for six. A longer out-breath slows the heart and calms the mind.

**Humming bee breath (Bhramari).** Breathe in and hum gently on the exhale. Five rounds. The vibration is soothing and most people find it easy.

## Everyday habits that help
- Take a walk every day. Brisk walking is the simplest lung exercise.
- Take a short break from your screen every hour and stand up.
- Sleep with your head slightly raised if you often feel blocked at night.
- Avoid smoke, including incense smoke in closed rooms.

## What yoga cannot do
Yoga is not a treatment for asthma, COPD, infections or heart problems. If you are short of breath on mild effort, wheezing, coughing for more than three weeks or coughing up blood, see a doctor.

If you have asthma and want to try yoga, do these things:
- Keep your inhaler with you.
- Skip forceful breathing such as Kapalabhati and Bhastrika.
- Avoid holding the breath for long.
- Tell your doctor and your yoga teacher.

## A word on air
In big cities during winter, the air itself can be a problem. On very polluted days, practise indoors, and wait for the air to clear before outdoor exercise.

## A small test
Stand with one hand on your belly. If it moves a little when you breathe, you are doing it right. If only your chest and shoulders move, you may be a shallow breather, and the belly breathing practice is a good place to start.

## A two-minute reset at your desk
You do not need a mat for this.
1. Sit on the front half of your chair with your feet flat on the floor.
2. Let your shoulders drop away from your ears.
3. Put your hands on the sides of your lower ribs and breathe in through the nose. Feel the ribs widen under your hands.
4. Breathe out slowly, as if fogging a mirror, and feel them close.
5. Repeat for ten breaths.

Doing this once or twice a day teaches your body what a fuller breath feels like.

## Questions people ask

**Should I breathe deeply all day?**
No. Your body breathes on its own, and forcing big breaths can make you dizzy. The aim is a relaxed, quiet breath through the nose, with the belly softly moving. Practise for a few minutes, then let it go.

**Is mouth breathing a problem?**
Many people breathe through the mouth when the nose is blocked, which is common with colds and allergies. If your nose is blocked for weeks, ask a doctor. For daily rest, breathing through the nose is usually more comfortable.

**Why does my chest feel tight when I am anxious?**
Stress makes the muscles around the ribs tighten, and the breath becomes quick. Slow, longer out-breaths and an easy stretch often help the feeling ease. If chest tightness is new, severe or comes with pain, get medical help straight away.

**How long before I notice a difference?**
Some people feel calmer after the first session. Changes in posture and habit usually take a few weeks of regular practice.

## Breathing and the mind
The old yoga texts treat the breath as a bridge between body and mind. When the breath is rough and fast, the mind tends to be too. When it is slow and even, the mind often follows. You can use this in a small way during the day: before a phone call, before opening an email you dread, or when you climb a flight of stairs, take one slow breath out first.
