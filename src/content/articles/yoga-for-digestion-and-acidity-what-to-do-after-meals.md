In a South Indian household, the afternoon nap after a big lunch is a tradition. It is also a common reason for that heavy, bloated, acidic feeling by evening. Yoga has some simple suggestions that fit into an ordinary day.

## After meals: Vajrasana
There is only one pose that traditional teachers recommend after eating: **Vajrasana**. Kneel and sit back on your heels, keeping the back straight and the hands on the thighs. Stay for five to ten minutes. The posture is said to help digestion, and it is also a calm way to sit. If your knees or ankles hurt, skip it.

Follow it with a **ten-minute walk**. A slow walk after meals helps move the food along, and a short one is better than none.

## Poses for bloating and wind
Do these at least two hours after eating, or before a meal.

**Wind-relieving pose (Pavanamuktasana).** Lie on your back, hug your knees to your chest and rock gently. Stay for five breaths.

**Seated twist (Ardha Matsyendrasana, gentle).** Sit with legs crossed, place the right hand behind you and the left on the right knee, and turn slowly. Five breaths each side. Keep the twist soft.

**Cat and cow.** Slow rounds on hands and knees massage the belly.

**Gentle cobra (Bhujangasana).** On an empty stomach, lift the chest a little and hold for three breaths. It stretches the front of the abdomen.

**Child's pose.** A minute of rest with the belly gently pressed against the thighs.

## For constipation
Drink water first thing in the morning. Do Pavanamuktasana and a gentle twist, then go for a walk. Eat enough fibre: vegetables, fruit, whole grains. If constipation lasts, see a doctor.

## What to avoid
- **Strong forward bends and deep twists** right after a meal.
- **Inversions** such as shoulderstand and headstand on a full stomach, and if you have acid reflux.
- **Advanced cleansing practices** such as Nauli and Agnisara without a teacher.
- **Lying flat straight after eating.** Wait at least two hours, and raise the head of the bed a little if you get night-time reflux.

## Eating habits that matter more than poses
- Eat at fixed times, and finish dinner at least two to three hours before bed.
- Eat slowly and stop before you are completely full.
- Limit very spicy, fried and sour food if they trigger acidity.
- Drink water between meals, not in big amounts during.
- Sleep enough. Poor sleep makes digestion worse.

## The mind and the gut
Stress is a big cause of acidity and bloating. Slow breathing with a longer exhale, even for two minutes before a meal, helps the gut. See [yoga for anxiety and stress](/yoga-for-anxiety-and-stress-a-ten-minute-reset/).

## A simple daily rhythm
A few small habits, kept regular, tend to help more than any single pose.

- **Morning:** a glass of water, a few minutes of gentle stretching, a walk if you can.
- **Before lunch:** two minutes of slow breathing, with a longer out-breath.
- **After lunch:** Vajrasana for a few minutes, then a short, slow walk. If you need a nap, keep it short and sit up a little before lying flat.
- **Evening:** an early, lighter dinner, and a gentle stroll afterwards.
- **Bedtime:** wait at least two hours after dinner before lying down.

## A closer look at the afternoon nap
The afternoon nap after a big lunch is a warm part of family life, so there is no need to feel guilty about it. If you find that it leaves you bloated, try a few small changes: eat a slightly smaller lunch, walk for ten minutes first, and keep the nap to twenty minutes or so. Many people find they feel clearer when they wake.

## Foods and habits to notice
Everyone's body reacts a little differently, so the best guide is your own diary. For a week, note what you ate and how you felt an hour or two later. You may find a pattern, such as a particular sour dish, late-night tea or a fried snack. Make one change at a time, so you can see what helps.

Traditional kitchens use a few gentle ways to ease the stomach, such as ginger, cumin, ajwain (carom seeds) or buttermilk with a pinch of salt. Many families swear by them. Treat these as home habits and not as treatment, and stop if something upsets you.

## Questions people ask
**Can I do yoga on an empty stomach?**
Gentle practice is usually better on an empty or nearly empty stomach. Leave at least two hours after a full meal for anything beyond a slow walk and sitting in Vajrasana.

**Is Vajrasana safe for everyone?**
No. If you have knee or ankle problems, skip it. You can sit on a chair with your back straight for a few minutes instead.

**Does drinking water after a meal cause acidity?**
Large amounts with a meal may make you feel heavy. Small sips are fine. Many people prefer to drink between meals.

**What if the acidity is worse during stress?**
That is very common. Slow breathing, a short walk, and regular meals help. If it continues, see a doctor.

## When to see a doctor
Do not self-treat if you have:
- Heartburn most days or at night for more than two weeks
- Difficulty swallowing
- Unexplained weight loss
- Black or bloody stools, or vomiting blood
- Severe pain in the upper belly

*This is general information, not medical advice. If you have an injury, a long-term condition or you are pregnant, check with your doctor before you start.*
