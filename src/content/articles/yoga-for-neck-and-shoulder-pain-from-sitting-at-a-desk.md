Look at yourself the next time you are working at a laptop. Your head is probably a few inches ahead of your shoulders, your shoulders are rounded and your wrists are bent. The head is as heavy as a bowling ball. Hold it forward all day and the muscles at the back of your neck do heavy overtime.

You do not need an hour of yoga to fix that. You need five small moves and a few new habits.

## Five moves you can do at your desk
**1. Chin tucks.** Sit tall. Slide your chin straight back as if making a double chin, without tilting the head. Hold for three seconds, release. Ten times. This trains the neck to sit over the shoulders.

**2. Shoulder rolls.** Roll your shoulders up, back and down in big, slow circles. Ten times each way.

**3. Cow-face arms (Gomukhasana arms).** Reach one arm up and bend it behind your head, bring the other arm behind the back, and try to join the hands. Use a scarf or a towel if they do not meet. Hold for five breaths each side. This opens the shoulders.

**4. Thread the needle.** On hands and knees, slide one arm under the chest to the opposite side, resting the shoulder and ear on the floor. Hold for five breaths each side. It releases the upper back.

**5. Cat and cow.** Slow rounds on hands and knees loosen the whole spine.

## A gentle neck stretch
Sit tall. Drop your right ear toward your right shoulder, keeping the left shoulder down. Rest there for five breaths. Switch sides. Do not pull on your head with your hand, and do not roll the neck in full circles if you have any pain or dizziness.

## Habits that keep it from coming back
- **Raise the screen.** The top of the screen should be at eye level. Put the laptop on a stack of books and use a separate keyboard.
- **Hips back, feet flat.** Sit with your back supported and your feet on the floor.
- **Take a break every hour.** Stand up, walk for two minutes and stretch.
- **Rest your eyes.** Every twenty minutes, look at something twenty feet away for twenty seconds.
- **Carry bags evenly.** Switch shoulders or use a backpack.
- **Phone at eye level.** Looking down at a phone is a major cause of neck strain.

## Breathing helps too
Stress tightens the neck and shoulders. A minute of slow breathing, with a longer exhale, makes it easier to drop the shoulders. See [yoga for better breathing](/yoga-for-better-breathing-open-the-chest-and-slow-the-breath/).

## Warning signs
See a doctor if you have:
- Tingling, numbness or weakness in an arm or hand
- Severe pain after a fall, accident or whiplash
- A headache that keeps coming back, or neck stiffness with fever
- Pain that wakes you at night or does not improve in a few weeks

## A final tip
The best stretch is the one you actually do. Set a reminder on your phone every hour, and use it for a minute.

## Two more easy stretches
**Wrist and forearm release.** Typing all day tightens the forearms, and the tension travels up to the shoulders. Hold one arm straight in front of you with the palm facing up. With the other hand, gently draw the fingers down and back. Hold for five breaths, then switch. Keep the stretch mild.

**Doorway chest opener.** Stand in a doorway with your forearm against the frame and the elbow a little below shoulder height. Step forward slowly until you feel a gentle stretch across the chest. Hold for five breaths each side. Most desk workers have a tight chest and weak upper back, and this helps to balance it.

## A five-minute routine for the middle of the day
You can string the moves together:
1. Chin tucks, ten times.
2. Shoulder rolls, ten each way.
3. Gentle side neck stretch, five breaths each side.
4. Doorway chest opener, five breaths each side.
5. Three slow breaths, with the exhale longer than the inhale.

Do it slowly. If something pinches or burns, stop. A good stretch feels like pulling, not like pain.

## Questions people ask
**How often should I do these?** A little and often is better than a long session once a week. Many people like once in the morning and once after lunch.

**Will this fix my posture?** It can help you notice and change habits, and many people feel looser. But posture is also about your chair, screen and how long you sit. Treat the stretches as one part of the answer.

**Can I do them if I already have a diagnosed neck problem?** Please ask your doctor or physiotherapist first. They can tell you which moves are right for you and which to avoid.

**What about pregnancy?** Gentle moves are often fine, but check with your doctor, and skip the floor moves if they do not feel comfortable.

## A small mindful habit
Several times a day, ask yourself: *where are my shoulders?* Most of the time they are up near the ears. Let them drop, and unclench the jaw. That costs nothing and takes two seconds. If you add a slow breath, you have done a little yoga in the middle of a meeting.
