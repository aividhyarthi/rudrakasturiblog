It is eleven at night and you are still turning over something you said at work three days ago. Or it is the future: what if this goes wrong, what if they think that, what if. The mind loops, and the harder you try to stop it, the louder it gets.

If that sounds like your life, you are in huge company. Here is what the yoga tradition offers, in practical terms.

## Why the mind loops
An unoccupied mind drifts to whatever is unresolved. A tired mind is worse. A fast, busy day with no pause leaves the loops running in the background. The old texts compare it to a monkey, restless, jumping from branch to branch. The point of yoga is not to kill the monkey. It is to give it something steady to hold.

## Start with the body
Overthinking lives in the head. The body is where you can start to loosen it.

**Mountain pose.** Stand with your feet flat and press them gently into the floor. Feel your weight. Take five slow breaths. This is grounding.

**Tree pose (Vrikshasana).** Balance on one foot and place the other foot on the calf or thigh, hands together at your chest. Balance needs attention, and attention pulls the mind out of the loop. Thirty seconds each side.

**Child's pose.** Rest your forehead on the floor for a minute and let your breath slow.

**Forward fold.** Let your head hang, knees bent, for five breaths.

## Change the breath
The fastest lever you have is the exhale. Breathe in for four counts, out for six to eight. Do ten rounds. A long out-breath tells the body that the danger has passed.

**Humming bee breath** also works well: hum as you breathe out, with a gentle vibration in the head.

## Give the mind one thing to look at: trataka
Place a candle or a small lamp at eye level, an arm's length away. Gaze at the flame without blinking too much for one or two minutes, then close your eyes and see the glow behind the lids. Trataka is described in the old texts on hatha yoga as a way to steady the mind. Stop if your eyes water a lot or if you have eye problems.

## Yoga nidra and the body scan
Lie down. Move your attention slowly from the toes to the head, naming each part: right foot, left foot, calves and so on. It takes ten minutes, and many people fall asleep. It works because it gives the thinking mind a task that is too gentle to feed on.

## Use a mantra
Repeating a short mantra such as *Om Namah Shivaya* or *Om Namo Narayanaya* gives the mind a rhythm to ride. See [how to begin japa](/mantra-japa-how-to-start-count-and-keep-going/).

## Habits that help beyond the mat
- **Write the worry down.** Put it on paper with a time to deal with it tomorrow.
- **Walk.** Ten minutes in daylight resets many minds.
- **Cut late-night screens and caffeine.**
- **Ask what you can do today.** If the answer is nothing, practise letting go. If there is something, do it.

## What Krishna says
Arjuna told Krishna that the mind is restless and stubborn, harder to hold than the wind. Krishna agrees, and says that it can be held by practice and detachment. We look at this in [our piece on the Gita and overthinking](/gita-on-overthinking-the-restless-mind-and-how-to-hold-it/).

## When it is more than overthinking
If you cannot sleep for weeks, if worry affects your work or your relationships, if you have panic attacks or feel hopeless, please talk to a doctor or counsellor. In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is a free helpline. Needing help is not a failure of discipline or faith.

## A ten-minute evening routine
If you want something you can follow without thinking, try this order.
1. **Two minutes of standing.** Mountain pose, feet pressed down, five slow breaths.
2. **Two minutes of tree pose**, one minute on each side. If you wobble, smile and start again.
3. **Two minutes of forward fold or child's pose**, whichever is kinder to your back.
4. **Three minutes of long exhales.** In for four, out for six.
5. **One minute of silence**, or a mantra repeated in the mind.

Do it at about the same time each day. The regularity matters more than how well you do each pose.

## Questions people ask
**Why does the mind get louder when I try to stop it?**
Because trying to push a thought away keeps your attention on it. It helps more to give the mind something else to hold, such as a breath, a flame or a word.

**Should I do this in the morning or at night?**
Both work. Morning practice sets the tone. Evening practice helps you put the day down. Choose the time you can actually keep.

**What if I fall asleep during the body scan?**
That is fine, especially at night. If you want to stay awake, try it sitting up or in the daytime.

**Is it all right to do this with a back or knee problem?**
Use a chair, skip the standing balance or keep your hand on a wall. Go gently and ask a qualified teacher if you are unsure.

## One small change to try this week
Pick the hour in which your mind is loudest, and place one of these practices just before it. If it is the bus ride home, do your long exhales on the bus. If it is bedtime, do your body scan in bed. You are not fixing your mind. You are giving it a softer place to land.
