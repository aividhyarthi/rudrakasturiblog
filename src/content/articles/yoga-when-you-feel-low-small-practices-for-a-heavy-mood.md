Some days the heaviness does not have a reason. You wake up and everything feels slightly grey. The idea of a long yoga session is laughable. You can barely brush your teeth.

If that is you today, here is the smallest possible version of a practice. Do one thing. That is enough.

## Step 1: let in light
Open the curtains. Stand near a window or step outside for five minutes. Morning daylight helps regulate sleep and mood, and it costs nothing.

## Step 2: three slow breaths
Sit up, and breathe out slowly, longer than you breathe in. Three times is fine.

## Step 3: a few slow Surya Namaskars
Three rounds of the sun salutation, done slowly, wake up the body. See [the twelve steps](/surya-namaskar-12-steps-with-mantras/). Skip it if you feel dizzy.

## Step 4: open the chest gently
Lying on your belly, lift your chest a little in gentle cobra and hold for three breaths. Or lie on your back with a rolled towel under the upper back and arms out wide. Opening the front of the body is said to lift the mood, and many people feel it.

## Step 5: walk
Ten minutes of walking, ideally outside, is one of the best things you can do for your mood.

## Step 6: one small task
Make the bed. Wash a cup. Water a plant. Small completed tasks give the mind a quiet win.

## Keep the routine simple
- **Sleep and wake at the same time.**
- **Eat regular meals,** even small ones.
- **Talk to one person** each day, even briefly.
- **Limit alcohol,** which makes low mood worse.
- **Ask for company** rather than staying alone all day.

## A mantra for the heavy days
Repeating a short name such as *Om Namo Narayanaya* or *Om Namah Shivaya* gives the mind something steady to lean on. See [how to start japa](/mantra-japa-how-to-start-count-and-keep-going/).

## A gentle morning order
If you want to string the steps together, here is one order that keeps the effort low.

1. Drink a glass of water.
2. Open the curtains and stand in the light for a few minutes.
3. Three slow breaths, with the out-breath longer.
4. A short walk, even around the building.
5. One small task, then a small breakfast.

If you do only the first two, that still counts. On the heaviest days, the aim is not to do more. It is to do something kind for yourself.

## A closer look at why small things help
When the mood is low, the mind tells you that nothing is worth doing. Small tasks answer this quietly. Each one is a small piece of evidence that you can still act. Light, movement and regular meals also support the body's own rhythms of sleep and waking, which low mood tends to disturb. None of this cures anything, but it can give you a little steadier ground to stand on.

The old traditions also valued small, regular acts: lighting one lamp at dusk, offering one flower, saying one name. You may find comfort in a tiny ritual of this sort. A lamp lit in a dim room can lift the feeling of the evening more than you expect. See also [how to keep a mantra going](/mantra-japa-how-to-start-count-and-keep-going/).

## Words to be gentle with
- Try not to say to yourself, "I should be fine by now."
- Try not to compare your slow day with someone else's busy one.
- Try saying, "This is a heavy day. I will do one small thing."

## Questions people ask
**Can I do this if I have no energy at all?**
Yes. Begin with the water and the light. Many people find that the rest follows, a little at a time.

**Is it okay to rest?**
Yes. Rest is not laziness. If you are exhausted, rest first, and add small actions as you are able.

**Should I tell someone?**
If the heaviness stays, yes. Tell someone you trust, and think about seeing a doctor. Asking for help is a sensible step, and it is a sign of care for yourself.

## What this is not
Yoga, prayer and walks are good support. They are not a treatment for depression, and being unable to pull yourself out of it with willpower is not a weakness.

## When to get help
Please talk to a doctor or counsellor if:
- Low mood lasts more than two weeks
- You have lost interest in things you used to enjoy
- Your sleep or appetite has changed a lot
- You feel hopeless, worthless or guilty most of the time
- You have thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life

In India, Tele-MANAS (14416) offers free help, any time of day, in many languages. In an emergency, call 112. Depression is a medical condition and it is treatable.

## If you are worried about someone else
Sit with them. Listen without fixing. Encourage them to see a doctor. Do not tell them to just be positive.

*This is general information, not medical advice. If you have an injury, a long-term condition or you are pregnant, check with your doctor before you start.*
