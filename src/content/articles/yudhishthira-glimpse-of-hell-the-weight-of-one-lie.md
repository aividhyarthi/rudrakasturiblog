Yudhishthira was known as Dharmaraja, the king of dharma, and his chariot was said to move a hand's width above the ground because of his truthfulness. The Mahabharata does not let one moment in the war pass without consequence.

## The half-truth
In the war, the Pandavas could not defeat Drona, their teacher and the commander of the Kaurava army, because he fought with unmatched skill. Krishna advised that Drona would lay down his weapons only if he heard that his son Ashwatthama had died. Bhima killed an elephant named Ashwatthama, and Yudhishthira was asked to confirm the news, since Drona would believe only him. Yudhishthira said, loudly, that Ashwatthama was dead, and added softly, "the elephant". Drona heard only the first part, laid down his weapons, and was killed.

## The glimpse of hell
In the Svargarohana Parva, at the end of the great journey, Yudhishthira alone enters heaven in his own body. There he finds Duryodhana seated in glory, and not his brothers and Draupadi. He is taken to see hell, where he hears his own family calling to him to stay and comfort them. He chooses to stay, and then the scene dissolves: it was an illusion arranged by Indra. Indra tells him that kings must see hell once, and that Yudhishthira's was brief because of the deceit in the war.

## What the story teaches
- **No one is above accountability.** Not even the king of dharma.
- **A half-truth is still a lie.** Words chosen to mislead are judged by what they were meant to do.
- **Compassion is the real test.** What frees Yudhishthira is that he chooses to stay with those in pain, even in hell.

## For today
The half-true statement in a meeting, the selective report, the message that leaves something out: these are our small Ashwatthama moments. Say the whole thing, or say nothing.
