The Sri Durga Malleswara Swamy Varla Devasthanam, better known as the Kanaka Durga Temple, stands on Indrakeeladri hill on the bank of the Krishna river in Vijayawada. The goddess is regarded as self-manifested, and her consort Shiva is worshipped on the hill as Malleswara.

## Legends and the record
Tradition links the hill to a Yaksha named Keela, who is said to have meditated on Durga, and to the sage Indrakila. The name Vijayawada is commonly connected with Durga's victory over the demon Mahishasura at this site. Temple tradition credits Adi Shankaracharya with installing the Sri Chakra. These are traditional accounts. The oldest documentary evidence cited is a Telugu inscription of 1229 CE, found in Prakasam district, which names the deity "Bejawada Malleswaradeva at Indrakiladri".

## Festivals
Dasara (Navaratri) is the major festival. The goddess is decorated in a different alankaram each day, and the festival ends with the Teppotsavam, a float festival on the Krishna. Devotees also take the 41-day Bhavani Deeksha vow, and large crowds in red clothing come in November and December.

Dates change every year with the lunar calendar.

## Before you visit
Timings, ticket prices, dress rules and booking systems change often, and festival days bring very large crowds. Check the temple's official channels for current details before you travel. Legends on this page are traditional beliefs, reported as such, not verified history.
