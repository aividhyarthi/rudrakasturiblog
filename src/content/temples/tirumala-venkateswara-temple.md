The Tirumala Venkateswara Temple stands on the Tirumala Hills, about 853 metres above sea level, in Tirupati district, Andhra Pradesh. It is dedicated to Venkateswara, regarded as a form of Vishnu. The hills have seven peaks, which tradition links to the seven heads of the serpent Adisesha.

## History
The earliest recorded endowment to the temple dates to 966 CE, from a Pallava queen named Samavai, according to the sources we reviewed. The temple is associated with worship practices organised according to the Vaikhanasa tradition, and Ramanujacharya is linked with its worship. In the colonial period administration passed through the East India Company and then the mahants of the Hathiramji Math. The Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD) took over under a law passed in 1933 (one source gives 1932).

## Festivals
The nine-day Srivari Brahmotsavam is the main annual festival. Crowds are very large during it, and guides advise booking darshan and accommodation well ahead for that season.

## Darshan
Special entry darshan is booked online through the TTD portal. One guide says slots open about 90 days ahead and that free darshan can involve a wait of 12 to 24 hours or more. Fees, quotas and rules change, so confirm on the official TTD site.

## Before you visit
Timings, ticket prices, dress rules and booking systems change often, and festival days bring very large crowds. Check the temple's official channels for current details before you travel. Legends on this page are traditional beliefs, reported as such, not verified history.
