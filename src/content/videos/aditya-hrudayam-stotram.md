faq:
- q: Who taught the Aditya Hrudayam to Rama?
  a: In the Valmiki Ramayana, the sage Agastya taught it to Rama on the battlefield, just before his last fight with Ravana.
- q: When is the best time to chant Aditya Hrudayam?
  a: At sunrise or in the early morning, facing east. Sundays and Ratha Saptami are especially associated with the Sun.
- q: How many verses are there in the Aditya Hrudayam?
  a: The text above has 31 verses. Some editions count the closing verses a little differently.
- q: How many times should I chant it?
  a: Once a day is common. The story in the text mentions reciting it three times.

## Meaning in English
The verse numbers below match the numbers in the Telugu text above.

- **Verses 1 to 5: the setting.** Rama stands tired and anxious in battle, with Ravana in front of him. The gods have gathered to watch. Agastya comes up to Rama and says: listen to an ancient secret that will let you defeat every enemy. It is the Aditya Hrudayam, a holy hymn that destroys enemies, brings victory, removes worry and grief, and lengthens life.
- **Verses 6 to 15: who the Sun is.** Worship the rising Sun, lord of the world, honoured by gods and asuras. He is the essence of all the gods: Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Skanda, Indra, Kubera, Yama, Soma and Varuna, the ancestors, the Vasus, the Ashvins and the Maruts. The verses then give a long list of his names, among them Aditya, Savita, Surya, Pusha, Bhanu, Divakara, Martanda, Ravi and Bhaskara. He drives away darkness, he is the master of the Rig, Yajur and Sama Vedas, and he is the lord of the stars and planets.
- **Verses 16 to 21: salutations.** Bows to the eastern mountain where he rises and the western mountain where he sets, to the lord of all lights and the lord of the day, to the one who removes darkness, cold and enemies, and to the witness of the whole world.
- **Verses 22 to 25: what he does.** He creates and withdraws beings, and with his rays he gives warmth and rain. He stays awake while all beings sleep. He is the sacrificial fire and its reward. A person who remembers him in hardship, in the forest or in fear does not sink in sorrow.
- **Verses 26 to 31: the promise.** Worship him with a steady mind and recite this three times, says Agastya, and you will win. Then he leaves. Rama's grief is gone. He recites the hymn while looking at the Sun, sips water three times to purify himself, takes up his bow and goes to face Ravana. In the last verse the Sun, glad to see him, says: "Hurry."

## How this helps
The hymn itself says it removes fear, worry and grief, gives courage in hardship and brings success in a hard task. Devotees have recited it for the same reasons for centuries: before an exam, a long journey, an interview or a difficult day, and simply to start the morning with steadiness. It is a prayer and not a medical treatment, so it is no substitute for a doctor or for your own effort.

## How and when to chant it
- **Time:** at sunrise or in the early morning, facing east, after a bath. Sunday is the Sun's day, and Ratha Saptami is his festival ([Ratha Saptami](/festivals/ratha-saptami/)).
- **Preparation:** sit calmly, sip a little water as Rama did, and look at the rising sun if you can do so comfortably.
- **How many times:** once a day is common. The text itself mentions reciting it three times.
- **Listening:** if you do not know the verses yet, play the video above at dawn and read the Telugu text along with it. It is about six minutes long.

## The story behind it
The hymn is part of the Valmiki Ramayana, in the Yuddha Kanda (chapter 107 in the commonly cited edition). Rama has fought Ravana for days and is weary. Agastya, who has come with the gods to watch the battle, gives him this hymn. Rama follows the sage's instructions, regains his spirit and goes on to face Ravana.
