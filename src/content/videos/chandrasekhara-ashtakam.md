## Meaning in English
1. **The refrain.** "Chandrasekhara, Chandrasekhara, Chandrasekhara, protect me. Chandrasekhara, Chandrasekhara, Chandrasekhara, guard me."
2. **The warrior.** He took the golden mountain (Meru) as his bow and lives on the silver mountain (Kailasa). He made the king of serpents his bowstring and Vishnu and fire his arrow, and he burned the three cities of the demons in an instant. Gods bow to him. "I take refuge in Chandrasekhara: what can Yama do to me?" This last line ends every verse.
3. **The destroyer of desire.** His two lotus feet are fragrant with flowers of heaven's trees. The fire from his third eye burned the body of Kama, the god of desire. He is smeared with ash, and he is the end of the cycle of birth and the imperishable one.
4. **The lord with the Ganga.** He wears an elephant's hide as his cloak. Brahma and Vishnu worship his feet. His matted locks are wet with the spray of the heavenly river.
5. **The friend of Kubera.** He destroyed the eyes of Bhaga at Daksha's sacrifice, wears snakes as ornaments, and holds Parvati on the beautiful left half of his body. His throat is blue from the poison, and he holds the axe and the deer.
6. **The conqueror of Yama.** Serpents are his earrings and the bull is his mount. Narada and the great sages sing his glory. He killed the demon Andhaka, he is the wish-giving tree for the gods, and he is the one who ended Yama.
7. **The healer.** He is the medicine for the disease of worldly life and removes all calamities. He destroyed Daksha's sacrifice, is three-eyed, gives both enjoyment and liberation, and destroys the whole mass of sin.
8. **The treasure of devotees.** Kind to those who worship him, an inexhaustible treasure, lord of all beings, beyond the highest and without equal. He is present in the moon, water, sky, fire and air.
9. **The creator and destroyer.** He makes the universe, then protects it, then takes it back. He lives in all the worlds and plays day and night with his host of attendants.
10. **The promise.** "This hymn was composed in Shiva's presence by Markandeya, son of Mrikandu, when he was afraid of death. Whoever recites it, wherever they are, will have no fear of death. Chandrasekhara himself gives them a full life, good health, all wealth and, without effort, liberation."

## How this helps
Devotees recite this hymn for protection and courage in times of fear, for peace of mind during illness or worry, and for long life and health. It is often recited on birthdays and on milestone birthdays such as the sixtieth. The hymn itself promises freedom from the fear of death. That is a promise of faith and not a medical claim, so it never replaces a doctor's care.

## How and when to chant it
- **Days:** Mondays, Pradosham ([Pradosham dates](/pradosham/)), Maha Shivaratri ([Maha Shivaratri](/festivals/maha-shivaratri/)) and Karthika Mondays ([Karthika Somavaram](/karthika-somavaram-the-monday-fast-and-the-worship-of-shiva/)).
- **How:** after a bath, with a lamp lit, before a Shiva lingam or picture. Chant the ten verses calmly, and repeat the refrain line at the end.
- **For others:** many families recite it for a sick relative or on a loved one's birthday. Please do this alongside proper medical care.

## The story behind it
Markandeya was born to the sage Mrikandu and his wife Marudmati, who had prayed to Shiva for a son. Shiva offered them a choice: a wise son who would live only sixteen years, or a dull son who would live long. They chose the wise son. When Markandeya reached sixteen, Yama's messengers came for him. The boy went to the Shiva lingam and held on to it. When Yama threw his noose, it fell around the lingam as well, and Shiva came out of it in anger and struck Yama down. Then he restored Yama to life, and he gave Markandeya the boon of staying sixteen forever. This is why Shiva is called Kalantaka, the one who ended Death. The tradition links the story with the temple at Tirukkadaiyur in Tamil Nadu. The verses above are the hymn Markandeya is said to have sung at that moment. The story varies slightly between the Puranas, so treat it as the broad outline. See also our [Shiva page](/gods/shiva/).
