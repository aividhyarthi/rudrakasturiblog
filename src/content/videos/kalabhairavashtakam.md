## Meaning in English
1. Whose lotus feet are served by Indra, the king of the gods; who wears a snake as a sacred thread and the moon on his head; compassionate; praised by Narada and other yogis; clothed in space.
2. Shining like crores of suns; he carries us across the ocean of worldly life; blue-throated, three-eyed, the giver of wished-for fruit; the death of death; imperishable.
3. He holds the trident, axe, noose and staff; the first cause; dark-bodied, the first god, free from disease; mighty and fond of his wondrous dance.
4. The giver of enjoyment and liberation; beautiful in form; kind to his devotees, steady; the form of all worlds; golden bells ring at his waist.
5. The protector of the bridge of dharma and the destroyer of the path of adharma; he frees us from the bonds of karma and gives well-being; his hair is golden and his form spotless.
6. Jewelled sandals on his shining feet; eternal, one without a second, the chosen deity; he crushes the pride of death, with fierce fangs.
7. His loud laughter shatters the egg of the universe; his glance destroys the net of sin; a stern ruler who gives the eight siddhis and wears a garland of skulls.
8. Leader of all spirits and giver of wide fame; he purifies the merit and sin of the people of Kashi; a master of the path of right conduct; ancient lord of the world.
9. The closing verse: those who recite this eight-verse hymn gain knowledge and liberation and increase in merit; sorrow, delusion, poverty, greed, anger and distress are destroyed, and they reach the feet of Kalabhairava.

## How this helps
Kalabhairava is worshipped as the lord of time and the protector of those who call on him. Devotees recite the hymn for courage, protection from fear and a steady mind in hard times, and for the strength to follow what is right. The final verse lists its promised fruits. As with any prayer, treat these as matters of faith and keep taking practical steps in your life.

## How and when to chant it
- **Days:** the Ashtami (eighth tithi) of the waning moon is the day most linked with Bhairava. Kalabhairava Jayanti, his birth festival, comes in the waning fortnight of the Karthika to Margashira period.
- **How:** after a bath, with a lamp lit, facing Bhairava's image or at his shrine. Many temples of Shiva keep a small Bhairava shrine.

## The story behind it
In the Puranic tradition, Brahma once spoke with pride and claimed to be the highest. Shiva's anger took the form of Bhairava, who cut off Brahma's fifth head. The skull stuck to his hand until he reached Kashi, where it fell away, and he stayed there as the guardian of the city. This is why Kalabhairava is called the Kotwal, the city guardian, of Kashi. The details differ between Puranas, so treat this as the broad outline.
