## Meaning in English
1. **Before birth.** In the mother's womb, because of past deeds, I suffered. Who can describe it? Forgive my fault.
2. **Childhood.** In childhood I was dirty and wanted milk. I could not control the senses, creatures troubled me, and I was helpless with illness. I did not remember Shankara.
3. **Youth.** In the prime of life I was bitten by the five senses, which are like poisonous snakes. I lost my sense of judgement and was lost in the pleasure of children, wealth and women. My heart did not think of Shiva and was proud of itself.
4. **Old age.** In old age the senses weakened, worries, sins, illnesses and separations weighed on me. My mind wandered after false hopes and did not meditate on Shiva.
5. **Rituals.** I cannot keep up the detailed rituals the scriptures lay down, so how could I follow the path of Vedic study? Forgive me.
6. **Missing worship (1).** I did not bring Ganga water for the bath of the lingam, or bilva leaves, or lotus garlands.
7. **Missing worship (2).** I did not bathe your lingam with milk, honey, ghee and curd, or anoint it with sandal paste, or offer flowers, incense, camphor lamps and food.
8. **Missing worship (3).** I did not give wealth to the learned, offer oblations to the fire with the bija mantras, or do penance, japa and the Rudra recitation on the banks of the Ganga.
9. **The yogi's way.** I do not remember Shankara in the yogi's way: seated in the lotus of the heart, in the sound of Om, on the subtle path.
10. **Beyond the three qualities.** I have not remembered you as the pure one, free from attachments and the three qualities, who is seen with the gaze fixed at the tip of the nose.
11. **A prayer.** O Shiva, with the moon on your crest, destroyer of Kama, bearer of the Ganga, with snakes at your throat and a fire in your eye: make my mind turn to liberation. What are all other actions for?
12. **What is worth having.** What use are wealth, horses, elephants, a kingdom, children, wife, friends, cattle, a body or a house? Knowing that all this passes in a moment, give them up and, by the teacher's word, worship the beloved of Parvati.
13. **Time.** Life ebbs away as we watch, youth wears down by the day, days that have gone do not return, time devours the world. Wealth is as fickle as a ripple, life as quick as lightning. So protect me, who have come to you for refuge.
14. **The great request.** "Whatever wrong I have done with hands, feet, speech or body, by action, by what I heard or saw, or in my mind, whether permitted or forbidden: forgive it all. Victory, victory to you, ocean of mercy, Great God, Shambho."

## How this helps
Devotees recite this hymn to make peace with their own mistakes: harsh words, neglected duties, rituals missed or done carelessly. It is a way of letting go of guilt and starting again, and of remembering that what truly counts is love and remembrance and not perfect ritual. It does not replace making amends to people you have hurt.

## How and when to chant it
- **Days:** Mondays, Pradosham ([Pradosham dates](/pradosham/)), Maha Shivaratri ([Maha Shivaratri](/festivals/maha-shivaratri/)) and at the end of a puja when you feel you have fallen short.
- **How:** quietly, after bathing, with a lamp lit. The last verse alone is often recited at the close of any puja, to ask forgiveness for what was missed.
- **Listening:** the video above is about six and a half minutes long. Read the Telugu text along with it.

## The story behind it
The hymn is attributed to Adi Shankaracharya, the eighth-century teacher of Advaita Vedanta. Its verses follow a human life in order, from the womb to old age, and then turn to the lessons of impermanence. Its last verse, the one asking forgiveness for every fault of body, speech and mind, is one of the best known verses in Shiva worship. See also our [Shiva page](/gods/shiva/).
