## Meaning in English
1. **Na.** The Lord whose garland is the king of serpents, the three-eyed one, smeared with sacred ash, the great Lord, eternal, pure, clothed in space. I bow to him as the syllable Na.
2. **Ma.** Worshipped with water of the Ganga (Mandakini) and sandalwood, the lord of Nandi and the host of attendants, honoured with Mandara and many other flowers. I bow to him as the syllable Ma.
3. **Shi.** Shiva, who is like the sun to the lotus-face of Gauri, the destroyer of Daksha's sacrifice, blue-throated, with the bull on his banner. I bow to him as the syllable Shi.
4. **Va.** Worshipped by sages like Vasishtha, Agastya and Gautama and by the gods, with the moon, the sun and fire as his three eyes. I bow to him as the syllable Va.
5. **Ya.** The embodiment of sacrifice, with matted hair, holding the Pinaka bow, eternal and divine, clothed in space. I bow to him as the syllable Ya.

The closing lines say that whoever recites this hymn near Shiva reaches the world of Shiva and rejoices with him.

## How this helps
The five syllables Na-Ma-Shi-Va-Ya are the heart of Shiva worship. Reciting this hymn is a way of slowing the mantra down and giving each syllable its own verse, which many people find steadying. Devotees use it for calm, for focus and as part of Monday and Shivaratri worship. The promise in the last verse is one of faith, not a guarantee.

## How and when to chant it
- **Days:** Mondays, Pradosham ([Pradosham dates](/pradosham/)) and [Maha Shivaratri](/festivals/maha-shivaratri/).
- **How:** after a bath, with a lamp lit. Recite the five verses slowly, and finish by repeating Om Namah Shivaya on a mala of 108 beads if you wish.
- **Listening:** the video above can be played in the morning or evening while you follow the Telugu text.

## The story behind it
The five syllables appear in the Shri Rudram of the Yajur Veda, where the words "namah shivaya" are recited as part of the long hymn to Rudra. Adi Shankaracharya, the eighth-century teacher of Advaita, is said to have composed this hymn that takes the five syllables one by one. See our [Shiva page](/gods/shiva/) for more stories and hymns.
