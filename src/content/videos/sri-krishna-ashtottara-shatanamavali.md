faq:
- q: How many names does the Krishna Ashtottara have?
  a: 108 names (ashtottara shatanama).
- q: What is the difference between ashtottara shatanama stotram and ashtottara shatanamavali?
  a: The stotram lists the 108 names in verse. The namavali puts each name in a form that ends with "namah" ("Om Krishnaya Namah"), so a flower can be offered at each name in puja.
- q: When should I chant the Krishna Ashtottara?
  a: Janmashtami is the great day. Many people also chant it on Ekadashi days or in the morning prayers.

## Meaning in English
Each name of Krishna is a short description, and many of them point to a story of his life:

- **Birth and childhood.** Vasudevatmaja (son of Vasudeva), Devakinandana (son of Devaki), Yashoda-vatsala (dear to Yashoda), Nandagopa-priyatmaja (beloved son of Nanda). Navanita-nata and Navanita-chora (the butter-thief), who stole butter from the cowherd women's homes.
- **Victories over demons.** Putana-jivita-hara (who took the life of the demoness Putana), Shakatasura-bhanjana (who broke the cart-demon), Trinavarta, Dhenukasura-bhanjana, Kaliya, Mushtika and Chanura (the wrestlers), Kamsari (enemy of Kamsa), Narakantaka (who ended Naraka), Shishupala-shirashchetta (who cut off Shishupala's head).
- **Pastoral name.** Govardhana-uddharta (who lifted Mount Govardhana), Vanamali (who wears a forest garland), Venu-nada-visharada (master of the flute), Gopala, Govinda, Damodara (the one tied with a rope by Yashoda).
- **The friend and teacher.** Parthasarathi (Arjuna's charioteer), Gitamrita-mahodadhi (the great ocean of the nectar of the Gita), Vishvarupa-pradarshaka (who showed his universal form), Yudhishthira-pratishtata (who established Yudhishthira), Jagadguru (teacher of the world).
- **Names that point to the Supreme.** Sachchidananda-vigraha (whose form is being, knowing and bliss), Parabrahma, Paramatma, Para-jyoti (the supreme light), Narayana, Sanatana (the eternal).

The closing lines say: "This Ashtottara of 108 names of Sri Krishna is the nectar of the name of Krishna, giving supreme joy, destroying great troubles and faults and increasing life."

## How this helps
Devotees chant these names to remember Krishna through his stories: his mischief, his protection of his devotees and his teaching. It is recited at Krishna worship for peace of mind and for the protection of children, and by those who want to remember the Gita's teacher through the day. The text promises joy and long life, which is a matter of faith.

## How and when to chant it
- **Days:** Janmashtami ([Krishna Janmashtami](/festivals/janmashtami/)) is the great day, and many people also chant the names on Ekadashi days.
- **How:** after a bath, with a lamp, flowers and tulasi leaves if you have them. Offer a little butter or sweet at the end if you wish. In puja, a flower or tulasi leaf is offered at each name of the namavali form.
- **Listening:** the video above is about seven minutes long, so you can chant along.

## The story behind it
Krishna's stories run through the Bhagavata Purana, the Mahabharata and the songs of the poet-saints. The names above are a quick tour of that life, from the prison where he was born, to Gokula, to Mathura, Dwaraka and the battlefield of Kurukshetra.
