faq:
- q: Who wrote the Rama Raksha Stotram?
  a: It is attributed to the sage Budha Kaushika. The hymn itself says that Shiva gave it to him in a dream and that he wrote it down in the morning.
- q: When should I recite the Rama Raksha Stotram?
  a: In the morning, before starting any important task, and with special devotion on Rama Navami.
- q: What does "raksha" mean?
  a: It means protection. The hymn asks Rama to protect each part of the body and the whole life of the person reciting it.

## Meaning in English
- **The opening.** The hymn begins with a short declaration of the sage (Budha Kaushika), the deity (Sitaramachandra), the metre (Anushtup) and the purpose, and then a meditation on Rama: tall, long-armed, bow in hand, seated in lotus posture, dressed in yellow, with lotus-like eyes, cloud-dark in colour, wearing matted locks and many ornaments, and with Sita on his left.
- **The story of Rama.** The hymn says the story of Raghunatha is spread over a hundred crore verses, and that each single syllable destroys great sin.
- **Protection of the body.** The central verses ask for Rama's protection by name: "May Raghava protect my head, the son of Dasharatha my forehead, the son of Kausalya my eyes, Vishwamitra's favourite my ears..." and so on down the body: nose, mouth, tongue, throat, shoulders, arms, hands, heart, waist, thighs, knees, calves and feet. Each limb is guarded by a title of Rama.
- **The fruit.** Whoever recites this protection, strengthened by Rama's power, will be long-lived, happy, blessed with children, victorious and humble. Those who move in the netherworld, on earth or in the sky, or in disguise, cannot see the one who is protected by the name of Rama.
- **Surrender.** The later verses are a prayer of refuge: "Rama is my mother and my father, Rama my lord and my friend. Rama, the merciful, is everything to me. I know no other." They include the well-known verse about Rama with Lakshmana on his right, Sita on his left and Hanuman in front.
- **The closing.** The last verses praise the name of Rama as the destroyer of the seeds of worldly life and of the fear of death. The final line says that the name of Rama equals the thousand names of Vishnu.

## How this helps
Devotees recite the Rama Raksha Stotram for a sense of protection and steadiness: before a journey, an exam, an operation or a difficult day, and as part of the daily morning prayer. The hymn is a prayer for protection and not a promise of safety. Combine it with care and sensible precautions.

## How and when to chant it
- **Time:** in the morning after a bath, or at the start of any new task. It is recited with special devotion on Rama Navami ([Rama Navami](/festivals/rama-navami/)).
- **How:** recite the meditation verse first, then the main verses, and end with the last verses on the name of Rama. In many homes it is recited once a day.
- **Listening:** the video above is about six and a half minutes long, so you can play it as you get ready or sit quietly.

## The story behind it
The hymn gives its own origin in a closing verse: "As Hara (Shiva) instructed this Rama Raksha in a dream, so Budha Kaushika woke up in the morning and wrote it down." So the tradition is that Shiva himself gave the hymn to the sage in a dream, and the sage wrote it at dawn. The last verse also quotes a line that Shiva is said to have spoken to Parvati: that the single name "Rama" is equal to a thousand names of Vishnu.
